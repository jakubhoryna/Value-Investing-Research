### Verdict — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**Campari is the best operating performer in Bucket G by a distance.** Five consecutive quarters of organic top-line growth — *"the only listed spirits player"* with that record `[claim]` — H1 2026 organic EBIT **+8.5%** with **130bp** of organic margin expansion, FY2025 free cash flow **up 96.7%** to €340.3m, net debt down **17.6%** to €1,958.0m, and a dividend raised **54%** `[sourced]`. While Pernod's profit fell 18% and Rémy's fell 24%, **Campari's grew.**
**But the direction is the opposite of Pernod's, and that is what makes this the one to watch.** FY2024 ROIC computes at **~7.3%**; FY2025 at **~8.2%** `[computed]` — **roughly 90 basis points of improvement in one year**, achieved by *shrinking the denominator*: goodwill fell from €2,420.1m to €2,233.4m and brands from €1,388.2m to €1,231.4m as non-core brands were sold, while EBIT rose 5.3% `[sourced]`. **At that rate the 10% line is roughly two years away, and the company is getting there by selling the acquisitions that put it below the line — which is precisely the correct response to the problem MODEL question 8 identifies.** `[inference]`

### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Yes on the business. One governance item is unverified and it is the memo's main gate-1 gap.**

Campari owns and markets aperitifs and spirits — **Aperol, Campari, Espolòn, Wild Turkey, SKYY, Grand Marnier, Courvoisier, Sarti Rosa**. **The economics are simpler and better than a cognac house's**: aperitifs are low-cost liquid sold at high margin into a serve — the spritz — that the company itself created and controls the ritual of. **FY2025 gross margin was 60.3%, up 190 basis points** `[sourced]`, against Rémy's cognac at 24.7% *operating* margin and Pernod's 25.8%. Aperol is a branded serve, not an aged inventory position, so **the balance sheet does not carry the multi-year barrel float that dominates both other spirits names.**

**FY2025 geography** `[sourced]`: Americas 43.8% of net sales (+2.1% organic), EMEA 49.6% (+2.3%), APAC 6.5% (+4.0%). **H1 2026** `[sourced]`: North America +2.6%, Europe +1.9% (**+2.9% excluding Germany**), Developing Markets +9.1%, APAC/GTR flat.

**Germany is the identifiable soft spot** — *"subdued consumer confidence and wallet pressure,"* described by management as consumer weakness rather than retailer destocking `[claim]`.

**The gate-1 gap: the control structure was not verified this run.** Davide Campari-Milano is a **Dutch-domiciled N.V. listed on Euronext Milan**, and it is widely understood to operate a **special / loyalty voting share structure giving the Garavoglia family holding company (Lagfin) majority voting control**. **No figure is asserted here because none was obtained** `[open item]`. **For a framework that weights capital allocation this is not a detail** — the disposal programme and the deleveraging that drive this memo's central finding are decisions made by a controlling shareholder. **Verify before any upgrade.**

#### Portfolio streamlining — the mechanism behind the ROIC improvement — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
Disposals announced or completed: **Rhum Agricole (Trois Rivières, Maison La Mauny), Cabo Wabo Tequila, Bisquit & Dubouché Cognac**, following **Cinzano and Averna** — combined pro forma revenue of approximately **€130m** `[sourced]`. Management describes the latest as *"EBIT accretive"* and *"gross margin accretive,"* targeting brands with limited growth and margin contribution `[claim]`. **Streamlining is expected to complete by end-2026** `[claim]`.
**FY2026 carries a stated perimeter drag of ~€70m of top line and ~€30m of EBIT from these disposals** `[sourced]`. **That is a real 4.7% subtraction from FY2025 adjusted EBIT** `[computed]` and it must not be netted out of the "organic growth" story.

#### Tariffs — a rare instance of the number improving — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
FY2025 guidance assumed a tariff headwind of **~€30m**; by the H1 2026 call it was **revised to €20m**, and the company recorded a **€15m contingent asset** for potential 2025 tariff refunds, with management declining to assume the benefit until confirmed `[sourced]`. **US rates ran at 10% rather than the expected 15% between 20 February and quarter-end** `[sourced]`.

#### Leverage and cash
**Net debt €2.0bn at 30 June 2026, leverage 2.6x, average cost of debt 4.3%** `[sourced]`, after a **€600m Eurobond issuance and a liability-management transaction** to extend the maturity profile `[sourced]`. FY2025 leverage was 2.5x.

**One number deserves care.** H1 2026 recurring free-cash-flow conversion was **64% before working capital and 4% overall**, the difference being seasonal inventory build `[sourced]`. **A 4% conversion figure is meaningless out of context and would be alarming if quoted alone** — the FY2025 full-year figure of €340.3m, nearly double the prior year, is the one that matters. ### Open items and data gaps
- **ROIC is computed, not disclosed.** Campari publishes none. Method validated against three peers; rests on a 25% assumed effective tax rate.
- **The Lagfin / Garavoglia voting structure was not verified**, and no figure is asserted. **Gate 1 gap.**
- **The ready-to-serve gross-profit-per-serve claim is management's and unverified.**
- **Aperol's share of group net sales and EBIT was not obtained** — only its +3.3% H1 growth and the aperitifs portfolio's +4.0%. **For a company this concentrated, that is a material gap.**
- **H1 2026 reported net sales and adjusted net profit in euros were not obtained** — only growth rates and the €358m adjusted EBIT. LTM figures are therefore not computed and FY2025 is used as the valuation anchor.
- **Disposal proceeds not obtained**, only the ~€130m of pro forma revenue disposed.
