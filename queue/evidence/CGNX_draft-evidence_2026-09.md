### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Pass.**

Cognex sells machine vision: cameras with integrated optics and lighting, barcode and 2D-code readers, 3D sensors, and the software that turns an image into a pass/fail decision, a measurement, or a location on a moving production line. The customer is a factory or a logistics operator that needs a machine to see — checking that a part is present and correctly seated, reading a code on a parcel travelling at 3 m/s, guiding a robot to a bin, gauging a dimension to a hundredth of a millimetre. **It is the sensory layer of the automation stack, sitting directly on the thesis, and it is the one part of the stack that is a component business rather than a systems business.**

**Structurally the most attractive profile in the register** `[all sourced]`:

**A 70.6% gross margin with zero debt is closer to Mips' royalty economics than to any capital-goods business in the register — and unlike Mips, Cognex has a computable return on capital.**

**What the disclosure does NOT give, and it is a real gate-1 limit.** The Q2 release states *"broad-based strength across most major end markets"* and provides **no revenue breakdown by end market or by geography** `[sourced absence, both the Q2 2026 and Q4 2025 releases]`. The fiscal 2025 release says growth was *"driven by increased year-end spending across many of our Factory Automation end markets, while Logistics continued to deliver steady growth"* `[sourced, verbatim]`. **So an investor knows there are two things — Factory Automation and Logistics — and cannot size either.** **Cognex's cyclicality is the cyclicality of consumer-electronics, automotive and e-commerce warehouse capex, and the company does not disclose the mix.** That is a materially worse disclosure position than any other row analysed this run.

**A share-count reconciliation exact to 0.01% on a page four months out of date. **Two hypotheses for the staleness, neither asserted.** **(a) The provider serves stale snapshots for some instruments.** **(b) This session's outbound agent proxy served a cached copy** — both stale reads followed a run of HTTP 429 rate-limit rejections from the proxy on those specific URLs, and the Cognex page was only obtained by varying the URL to sidestep the rejection. **Hypothesis (b) would make this an infrastructure artefact rather than a provider defect, and it changes the remedy entirely.** **Test at a PC: read the same two URLs from a normal connection. If they return current prices, the defect is this environment's and every price this corpus has taken from this route needs its stamp re-checked, not its arithmetic.** **This is the highest-priority item this run produces and it should go to the top of Step 7.**
