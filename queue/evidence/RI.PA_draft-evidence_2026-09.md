### Verdict — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**6.9% is below the 7.7% at which KION was failed on gate 2 and given a PASS** `[sourced, register]`. **Consistency with a decision the register has already made requires the same outcome here**, and this memo takes it. It is not a judgement that Pernod Ricard is a worse business than KION — on margin, brand durability and cash conversion it is plainly a better one. **It is a judgement that the framework cannot apply a bar at 7.7% to one name and waive it at 6.9% for another because the second has nicer brands.**

### Why the file remains worth reading despite the PASS — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**The dividend consumes 98.8% of free cash flow.** DPS held flat at **€4.70** `[sourced]` across **251.67m shares** `[sourced]` = **€1,183m of distribution** `[computed]` against **free cash flow of €1,197m** `[sourced]`. **Net debt is €10,662m at 3.7x EBITDA, and management's stated target is below 3x by FY29** `[sourced]`.
Those three facts cannot all hold. Deleveraging from 3.7x to below 3x cannot come from retained cash, because there is none after the dividend. **It must come from EBITDA growth — in a year in which profit from recurring operations fell 5.2% organically and 17.9% as reported** `[sourced]`. **Either the earnings recover on schedule, or the dividend gives, or the deleveraging target does.** Nothing in the FY26 release says which. `[inference]`
**Against that, the price is doing real work.** At **€61.76** the shares are on **10.8x EV/FY2026 profit from recurring operations**, **10.6x recurring EPS**, a **7.7% free-cash-flow yield** and a **7.6% dividend yield** `[computed]`, having fallen **~36% over twelve months** `[sourced]` to within **5.4%** of the 52-week low `[computed]`.
**The framing that decides this file:** €61.76 is **10.8x FY2026 profit** — or **9.0x FY2025 profit** `[computed]`. **The entire question is whether FY2026 is a trough or a new base**, and it resolves into two country lines: **US −14%, China −19%, everything else +0.5%** `[sourced]`.

### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Yes on the business; a caution on the reporting.**

Pernod Ricard distils, ages, blends and markets spirits and wine — Jameson, Absolut, Martell, Chivas Regal, Ballantine's, Havana Club, Beefeater, Mumm, Perrier-Jouët. It buys agricultural inputs, holds them in barrel for years, and sells branded liquid at a **25.8% recurring operating margin** `[sourced]`. Long inventory, long brand life, high gross margin, distribution scale. **This is an easier business to describe than any brewer in the same bucket.**

Two reporting cautions:

1. **Organic and reported diverge enormously this year.** Net sales **−3.9% organic against −14.2% reported**; PRO **−5.2% organic against −17.9% reported** `[sourced]`. **A 10-14 point gap is disposals and currency, not trading.** Any multi-year series must state which basis it is on, and this memo values the company on **reported** figures because that is what an owner receives.
2. **"Profit from recurring operations" is the company's own measure.** Group share of net profit from recurring operations was **€1,476m**; group share of **reported** net profit was **€1,203m** `[sourced]` — a **€273m gap, 18.5% of the recurring figure** `[computed]`. Every multiple below is stated on the basis it uses.

**FY2026 by region, organic** `[sourced]`: Americas **−10%**, Asia-RoW **flat**, Europe **−3%**. **By country: US −14%, China −19%, India +7% (+9% excluding Imperial Blue), and net sales excluding the US and China +0.5%.**

**That last figure is the most important disclosure in the release.** The rest of the world — two-thirds or more of the business — grew, marginally. **The decline is two countries.** Whether that is reassuring or alarming depends entirely on whether those two countries are cyclical or structural, and the framework has no gate that answers it. `[inference]`

#### What is disclosed and good — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
- **PRO margin 25.8%** `[sourced]`, down only **35bp organically** in a year sales fell 3.9% organically. **Holding margin within 35bp on a 3.9% organic sales decline is evidence of real pricing power**, and it is a materially better operating performance than the headline suggests. `[inference]`
- **Free cash flow rose 6% to €1,197m** in a year profit fell, with **cash conversion of 91%, up 17 points** `[sourced]`. **Working capital — which for a spirits company means barrel inventory — was released rather than built.** That is the correct management response to a demand slowdown, and it is the strongest single piece of evidence for the bull case.
- **India +7%** `[sourced]`, described as *"strong momentum reflecting underlying consumer demand and premiumisation trends"* `[claim]`. A genuine long-duration growth market where the company already has scale.

#### What is disclosed and bad — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
- **The earnings base is falling, not flat.** PRO −5.2% organic, **−17.9% reported**. EPS **−19%**. Group reported net profit **−26%** `[sourced]`.
- **Leverage is 3.7x** `[sourced]` — the highest of any name examined in Bucket G, higher than Carlsberg's 3.0x — **with a target of below 3x by FY29**, i.e. three more years.
- **FY27 guidance is for organic net sales "broadly stable"** in a *"contrasted and uncertain environment,"* with **declines still anticipated in the US and China** `[sourced]`. **Two more years of the same in the two problem markets is management's own base case.**
- **The medium-term FY27–29 target was set at "close to the lower end of the +3% to +6% range"** `[sourced]` — i.e. ~3% organic sales growth. **A business guiding to 3% top-line growth while deleveraging from 3.7x is a business with very little room.**

#### Return on capital employed — computed, because the company discloses none
**Pernod Ricard publishes no ROCE or ROIC figure** in its FY26 results release or in its FY25 consolidated financial statements `[sourced absence — both checked]`. It is computed here.

**Inputs** `[sourced]`: net financial debt **€10,662m** (30-06-2026); total shareholders' equity **€16,226m** at 30-06-2025 (group share 15,212 + non-controlling interests 1,014); goodwill **€6,406m**; trademarks **€11,515m**; other intangible assets **€579m** — all at 30-06-2025, the most recent balance sheet obtained.

**Method and its validation.** Invested capital is taken as total equity plus net debt; NOPAT as operating profit less tax at an assumed effective rate. **The method was checked against two peers that disclose the answer:**

| | Method gives | Company discloses |
|---|---|---|
| Heineken FY2025 | **9.4%** | **9.4%** `[sourced]` |
| Royal Unibrew FY2025 | 13.8% | 13% `[sourced]` |
| Carlsberg FY2025 | 9.9% | 10.8% `[sourced]` |

**Two of three reproduce to within a point and one is exact.** `[computed]` That is enough to use it here, and not enough to treat the output as a disclosed number.

**Applied to Pernod Ricard, FY2026** `[computed]`:

- FY2026 equity is estimated at **~€16,000m** — FY2025's 16,226 plus reported net profit of 1,203 less a declared dividend of ~1,183, less currency `[assumption; FY2026 equity was not obtained]`.
- Invested capital ≈ 16,000 + 10,662 = **€26,662m**.
- NOPAT = PRO 2,423 × (1 − 24% assumed effective tax) = **€1,842m** `[assumption on the rate]`.
- **ROCE including goodwill and brands ≈ 6.9%.** At a 20% tax rate it is 7.3%; at 30%, 6.4%. **Below 10% on any plausible tax assumption, and below 8% on all of them.**
- Goodwill, trademarks and other intangibles total **€18,500m** `[sourced, 30-06-2025]`, so invested capital excluding them is ≈ €8,162m and **ROCE excluding goodwill and brands ≈ 22.6%.**
- **Tangible equity ≈ €16,226m − €18,500m = −€2,274m** `[computed, 30-06-2025]` — negative, but modest relative to the balance sheet, and far less extreme than Carlsberg's.

**This is the widest inclusive/exclusive spread in Bucket G after Carlsberg's, and it is decisive rather than cosmetic:** 6.9% fails gate 2 outright; 22.6% passes it comfortably. **The `!!` flag on this row is not a formality.**

**One caveat that cuts in Pernod's favour and should be stated.** A spirits company's invested capital is dominated by **inventory that appreciates while it ages**, carried at cost. A ROCE computed on book capital therefore understates the economic return more here than at a brewer, whose inventory turns in weeks. **This does not rescue 6.9%** — the gap to 10% is too wide — but it is a real reason the figure flatters the brewers by comparison, and it belongs in the MODEL question 8 discussion.

### The answer, first — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**The structural threat to US spirits is not a volume threat. It is a revenue-per-case threat.** In calendar 2025, US spirits **volume rose 1.9%** while **supplier revenue fell 2.2%** `[sourced — DISCUS, briefing 2026-02-05]`. Americans bought **more** cases of spirits and paid **less** for them.
**And it reframes Pernod Ricard's number.** Pernod's US net sales fell **14% organically** in FY2026 `[sourced]` against a US spirits market whose supplier revenue fell **2.2%** in calendar 2025 `[sourced]`. **The periods do not match — DISCUS is calendar 2025, Pernod's year ends June 2026 — so the gap cannot be read as a precise share loss.** But a roughly twelve-point gap is far too large to be timing alone. **Most of Pernod's US decline is company-specific — destocking, portfolio mix, share — not the market.** `[inference]` The market is a slow structural headwind; the −14% is not the market.

### 1. What the US data actually says — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
`[sourced — Distilled Spirits Council of the United States, annual economic briefing, 2026-02-05, covering calendar 2025]`
| | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|
| **US spirits supplier revenue** | **US$36.4bn** | **−2.2%** |
| **US spirits volume** | **318.1m nine-litre cases** | **+1.9%** |
| Spirits' share of total US beverage alcohol | 42.4% | share lead maintained |
| Category | Revenue | Rev. change | Volume | Vol. change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vodka | $7.0bn | −3.0% | 72.5m cases | −2.2% |
| Tequila / mezcal | $6.4bn | **−4.1%** | 32.1m cases | **−0.3%** |
| American whiskey | $5.1bn | −0.9% | 30.0m cases | −1.0% |
| Cordials | $2.7bn | −3.2% | 19.5m cases | −2.7% |
| **RTD cocktails** | **~$4.0bn** | **+16.4%** | **85.6m cases** | **quadrupled since 2020** |
**Tequila is the cleanest single illustration:** volume essentially flat at **−0.3%**, revenue down **−4.1%** `[sourced]`. **Nobody stopped drinking tequila. They stopped buying the expensive one.**

### 2. The generational thesis is not supported by the data — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**The "Gen Z rejects alcohol" narrative, which underpins most of the structural bear case in the press, is contradicted by the industry's own tracking data.** `[sourced — IWSR Bevtrac, reported 2026-07]`
- A second source records the same reversal: **66% of legal-drinking-age Gen Z drank in March 2023, rising to 73% by March 2025** `[sourced — IWSR, via FoodNavigator 2026-02-13]`.
**Gen Z's low participation was a 2020–2023 phenomenon that has substantially normalised, and the evidence points to affordability rather than values as its cause** `[sourced — Euromonitor commentary]`. **A framework that has been discounting spirits terminal values on generational rejection has been discounting them for a reason the data does not support.** `[inference]`
**But moderation is real, and IWSR calls it structural in terms the framework should take seriously.** Drinkers across *all* generations report drinking **less frequently and less per occasion**, with average consumption falling to **3.9 drinks per occasion from 4.4** — an **11% decline** `[computed on sourced figures]`. IWSR's president: the trend *"increasingly appears to be driven by lifestyle choices, resulting in a structural, rather than cyclical, change"* `[sourced, verbatim]`.
**So: participation is stable, intensity is falling.** That is a volume headwind of a specific size — global alcohol volume declined at a **−1% CAGR from 2019 to 2024**, with the **US at −1%** and China at −3% `[sourced — IWSR]`.

### 3. GLP-1 — the largest genuine uncertainty, and it should be sized rather than asserted — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**Prevalence is now material and rising fast** `[sourced — Gallup, survey 28 May – 5 June 2026, n=5,065 US adults, ±1.5pp]`:
**Effect on drinking, where the estimates are much softer** `[sourced — Morgan Stanley research, via FoodNavigator]`: GLP-1s can reduce alcohol consumption *"by as much as 75%, and 50% per occasion."* Euromonitor's caution is recorded alongside it: *"it's difficult to measure the exact effect of GLP-1s on the alcohol market."*
| Assumed reduction among users | Population-level volume effect at 11% penetration |
|---|---|
| 30% | **−3.3%** |
| 50% (the per-occasion figure) | **−5.5%** |
| 75% (the upper bound quoted) | **−8.3%** |
**The check that matters: US spirits volume grew 1.9% in 2025** `[sourced]`. **Whatever GLP-1s are doing to alcohol, it is not currently visible as an aggregate volume decline in the largest spirits market.** Either the effect is smaller than the arithmetic implies, or it is offsetting growth that would otherwise be visible, or it lags. **All three are possible and the data cannot separate them.** `[inference]`

### 5. Honest limits of this note — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*

### ⟪ `claude_2026-09-07_review-session_due-reviews_v1.md` → 4. Pernod Ricard — a 2026 event the memo does not carry ⟫
**Found while checking row 9 for an evidence trigger. It is not a trigger — it is a gap in a committed verdict, and it is the most consequential single item this run produced.**

`[both sourced, both primary company releases]`

- **26 March 2026 — Pernod Ricard confirms discussions with Brown-Forman** regarding a potential business combination, characterised in contemporaneous coverage as a **"merger of equals"**.
- **28 April 2026 — terminated.** Verbatim: *"Pernod Ricard today announces that these discussions have ended and did not result in an agreement as the companies were unable to reach mutually acceptable terms."* **No financial terms were disclosed in either release.**

**Why this matters to a committed PASS.** The memo's gate-2 failure is basis-dependent (6.9% incl. goodwill / 22.6% excl.) and the register passes it on the KION precedent. **But the memo's bear case, its capital-allocation section and register note (p) all rest on the same argument: a company at 3.7x leverage against a sub-3x FY29 target, holding a flat EUR 4.70 dividend that consumes 98.8% of free cash flow, offering the final payment in scrip, with deleveraging that "must come entirely from EBITDA growth".** **A management team that, five months into that position, attempted a transformational combination with a major US competitor is evidence directly on that argument** — and the direction it cuts is genuinely ambiguous, which is exactly why it belongs in the file rather than out of it:

- **Against the row:** a board pursuing a merger of equals while at 3.7x leverage and 98.8% payout is not behaving like a board whose stated first priority is deleveraging. **It also means the sub-3x-by-FY29 target was, for at least a month, not the operative plan.**
- **For the row:** attempting a defensive combination is a rational response to a structurally impaired US market, and **walking away over terms rather than completing a bad deal is the capital discipline the framework asks for.** The termination is arguably the better datapoint than the approach.
- **And a third reading that is neither:** a failed merger of equals between the two most US-exposed large spirits companies is **sector evidence**, not company evidence. It bears on the structural-versus-cyclical question the memo names as its own priority research task, and on Diageo via cross-read (u).

**Confidence that the memo omits it: moderate-to-high, not certain.** The memo's Open items and data gaps section was read in full and lists the Imperial Blue disposal, buyback status, the 52-week-high disagreement and the FY26 URD assumptions — **but no M&A item of any kind, which is where a terminated combination would be flagged.** A full-text search of project knowledge for the counterparty returned the memo only via its unrelated sections. **Treat as a gap to verify at the merge session, not as a proven absence.**

**Recommended handling.** **The verdict does not change** — PASS on gate 2 stands on the return, and nothing here touches the 6.9%. **The memo needs a capital-allocation paragraph and an open item, and register note (p) needs one sentence.** Proposed note (p) addition: *"Management confirmed discussions with Brown-Forman on a potential merger of equals 2026-03-26 and terminated them 2026-04-28 for want of mutually acceptable terms `[sourced, both primary]`. No terms disclosed. Bears on the deleveraging commitment in both directions and is sector evidence for the structural-vs-cyclical question; do not read it as a verdict change."*

**And it raises a process question the corpus should answer once: none of the twenty-four memos records a systematic check for announced-and-terminated corporate transactions in the trailing twelve months.** That class of event is invisible to a results-driven review trigger — it is not a result, not a filing, and not a price move — **and it is the class most likely to contradict a capital-allocation judgement.** Proposed as a memo requirement, §5.
