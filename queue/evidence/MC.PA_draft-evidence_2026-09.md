### Verdict — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
LVMH at €429.10 trades at **12.6x EV/LTM recurring operating profit excluding leases, 13.5x including them, 19.6x earnings, on a 5.4% free-cash-flow yield and a 3.0% dividend yield** `[computed]`, having touched a five-year low of €428.35 on 3 September 2026 `[sourced]`. The free-cash-flow figure is unusually trustworthy because **LVMH's headline operating free cash flow is already struck after lease principal repayments** — I verified the arithmetic across four periods `[computed]` — so it is a conservative measure that does not need adjusting downward the way Moncler's does.

### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Pass on the business, marginal on the disclosure.**

Seventy-five Maisons across five business groups, sold largely through 6,283 directly operated stores `[sourced, 30.06.2026]`. FY2025 revenue €80,807m; recurring operating profit €17,755m `[sourced]`.

H1 2026 organic growth by division `[sourced]`: Fashion & Leather Goods €18,146m (−1%), Wines & Spirits €2,598m (+5%), Perfumes & Cosmetics €3,914m (0%), Watches & Jewelry €5,225m (+9%), Selective Retailing €8,406m (+5%).

**The disclosure caveat is real and should be held against the name.** LVMH does not break out Louis Vuitton or Dior separately, so brand-level dependence inside a division that is three-quarters of group profit **is unquantifiable from filings**. It also does not disclose capex by division, brands and goodwill by business group, or a clean store count by business group `[all sourced absences]`. For a company of this size that is thin.

---

#### Returns
**LVMH publishes no ROIC, ROCE or ROE.** The only ratio it prints is net financial debt to equity: 13.3% (2024) against 17.1% (2023) `[sourced]`. The following are mine, on FY2025 figures and a 32.8% effective tax rate `[computed from sourced lines; NOPAT = €17,755m × 0.672 = €11,931m]`:

| Capital base | Amount | Return |
|---|---|---|
| Equity €68,949m + net financial debt €6,857m | €75,806m | **15.7%** |
| Same, plus lease liabilities €16,018m | €91,824m | **13.0%** |
| Same, less brands and goodwill €41,444m | €34,362m | 34.7% |

**15.7% including the brand and goodwill carry is the number this framework should use.** It is respectable and it is not exceptional — and it is the arithmetic consequence of having bought Tiffany for **$15.8bn** `[sourced]` and adding LVMH's structurally lowest-margin large division: Watches & Jewelry earned a 14.4% recurring margin in FY2025 `[computed]` against Fashion & Leather Goods' 35%.

Note also a permanent-looking haircut: **the effective tax rate rose from 28.5% to 32.8%** between FY2024 and FY2025 `[computed]`, unexplained in the release.

#### The Fashion & Leather Goods margin — the whole question
| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FLG revenue `[sourced]` | €38,648m | €42,169m | €41,060m | €37,770m | €18,146m |
| FLG recurring operating profit `[sourced]` | €15,709m | €16,836m | €15,230m | €13,209m | €6,195m |
| **FLG margin** `[computed]` | **40.6%** | **39.9%** | **37.1%** | **35.0%** | **34.1%** |

Six consecutive years of decline, 650bps cumulative, on a division that is 74.4% of group profit.

**Management's counter deserves a fair hearing** and it is partly persuasive: the CFO states that the H1 2026 60bp contraction is *"more than entirely attributable to currencies"* and that at constant currency *"EBIT margin improved modestly, and operating income was flat"* `[commentary]`. FX is a genuine and quantified headwind — **~€1bn to EBIT expected in FY2026, broadly similar to FY2025** `[claim]`. Group gross margin did improve 30bps to 67.1% in H1 2026, and marketing and selling expenses fell 2% with G&A flat `[sourced]`.

**But the constant-currency defence does not explain 2023 through 2025**, when the decline was 39.9% → 35.0% and revenue fell in reported terms two years running.

#### Financial strength
**Pass, comfortably.** Total equity €69,694m against net financial debt of €8,245m at 30 June 2026, with total lease liabilities of €16,261m separately `[sourced]`. Gearing in the mid-teens. There is no balance-sheet question here.

Note LVMH's net-financial-debt definition deducts cash *plus current available-for-sale financial assets* (€13,587m at end-2024 against a balance-sheet cash line of €9,631m) and **excludes leases** `[sourced]`.

#### Capital allocation
Disciplined and, in one respect, quietly revealing.

**The dividend has been frozen at €13.00 for three consecutive years** (2023, 2024, 2025) while EPS fell from roughly €30 to €21.86 — **the payout ratio rose 43% → 52% → 59% purely through earnings decline, not through a raise** `[sourced]`. That is the single most informative capital-allocation fact in the file: management has chosen to hold the dividend flat rather than cut it or grow it, which is a statement about how they see the next few years.

Buybacks are modest and, unusually, actually cancelled: a €1,000m programme announced February 2025 completed on 28 November 2025 with **1,899,397 shares repurchased and stated as intended for cancellation** `[sourced]`. Earlier programmes: €300m (Dec 2021), €1,500m (Mar 2023). No 2026 programme has been found `[sourced absence]`.

Capex runs at **5.7% of revenue in FY2025**, described by the CFO as *"in line with our long-term historic average"* `[commentary]`, and ~5.3% in H1 2026 `[computed]`. FY2023 was an outlier at €7,478m (+50% on 2022) and is the sole reason operating free cash flow dipped to €8,104m that year `[computed]`.

**Tiffany is the live capital-allocation question.** Management's framing is that high jewellery *"has tripled in four years"* and that new-concept stores are *"about a third in terms of number of stores"* but **42% of sales**, with *"15-20 margin point differences favoring renovated locations"* `[commentary]`. The bear reading is that after five years only ~40% of the network is renovated, renovation is still an explicit margin headwind, and $15.8bn bought the division that now earns 14.4%.

### Gate 3 — Is it cheap? — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
Inputs `[sourced]`: price €429.10 (2026-09-04); weighted average basic shares 497,650,238 (FY2025); market capitalisation €211,590m; net financial debt €8,245m and lease liabilities €16,261m, both at 30.06.2026.
| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| EV excluding leases | €219,835m `[computed]` |
| EV including leases | €236,096m `[computed]` |
| LTM recurring operating profit (17,755 − 9,012 + 8,691) | €17,434m `[computed]` |
| **EV / LTM recurring operating profit, excl. leases** | **12.6x** `[computed]` |
| **EV / LTM recurring operating profit, incl. leases** | **13.5x** `[computed]` |
| LTM net profit, Group share | €10,877m → EPS €21.86 `[computed]` |
| **P/E** | **19.6x** `[computed]` |
| **FY2025 operating free cash flow €11,333m** | **5.4% yield** `[computed]` |
| Dividend yield (€13.00 / €429.10) | **3.0%** `[computed]` |
A 5.4% owner's yield plus a 3.0% dividend on the best collection of luxury brands in existence, at a five-year low, is a serious proposition. **What stops it is that both revenue and recurring profit have now declined for two consecutive years** — €86,153m (2023) → €84,683m → €80,807m, and €19,571m → €17,755m `[sourced]` — with no evidence yet that the FLG margin has found a floor.
Management gives **no formal guidance** and this is explicit, not inferred. Bernard Arnault, FY2025 call: *"I am optimistic in the medium term, but short term, it's very difficult to provide a serious forecast."* CFO Cabanis, H1 2026: *"once we reach 3%-4% growth, we are starting to get operating leverage"* `[commentary]`. **No numeric margin target exists** `[sourced absence]`.

### What would need to be true — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
2. **Chinese demand stabilises rather than structurally re-rates downward.** CFO, H1 2026: Chinese local and touristic demand *"has been flattish"* and is increasingly *"clustered around shopping event[s]"* `[commentary]`. Bernstein's 3 September 2026 note reports the H1 2026 recovery pausing, with preference shifting toward prestige beauty rather than leather goods — and names **Richemont, not LVMH, as its top sector pick** `[sourced]`.

### Control structure
Not a governance risk in the ordinary sense, but a fact a minority holder should price.

The Arnault family holds **50.01% of capital and 65.94% of voting rights**, confirmed by an AMF filing on **19 February 2026**, held through Christian Dior SE and Financière Agache `[sourced]`. The family bought more than 1.1m shares in early 2026, including roughly 757,000 shares for **€407m** between 28 January and 4 February, and Bernard Arnault characterised the purchases as *"buying the dip"* `[commentary]`. He had stated on 27 January 2026 an intention to cross 50% during the year.

The capital/voting gap is only explicable by France's double-voting-right regime for long-held registered shares, but **I could not source the specific LVMH bylaw provision** — the URD governance chapter was unreachable, so this is inference, not disclosure.

On succession: Alexandre and Frédéric Arnault joined the board (announced January 2024) and Frédéric runs Financière Agache `[sourced]`. **No publicly disclosed succession plan names a single successor** `[sourced absence]`, and no succession announcement was made on the H1 2026 call. The bear reading is straightforward — control is being consolidated ahead of a transition, and minority holders now face an owner with an outright capital majority and near two-thirds of votes.
