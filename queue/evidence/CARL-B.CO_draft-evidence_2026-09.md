### Verdict — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
- **Returns on the capital shareholders actually committed have fallen through the framework's bar.** ROIC including goodwill: **12.5% → 15.2% → 14.5% → 13.8% → 10.8%** across FY2021–FY2025 `[sourced]`. The FY2025 collapse is the Britvic purchase premium entering invested capital, which rose from 66,059 to **104,097** `[sourced]`.
- **Leverage went with it.** NIBD **27,357 → 61,617**; NIBD/EBITDA **1.73x → 3.28x** `[sourced]`. At 30-06-2026 the ratio is **3.0x** `[sourced]` — improved, and the improvement is partly financing rather than deleveraging (below).

### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Marginal pass, and weaker than Royal Unibrew's on every dimension except disclosure quality.**

Carlsberg brews and bottles beer and, since January 2025, sells soft drinks at scale. **FY2025 volumes were 148.0 million hectolitres against Royal Unibrew's 18.1** `[sourced]` — an eight-fold difference in operational surface area. Three reporting regions: **Western Europe**, **Asia** (China, Vietnam, Laos), and **Central & Eastern Europe and India** (Poland, Kazakhstan, Ukraine, India). Soft drinks and non-beer are now *"about one-third of volumes"* group-wide and *"almost 60%"* in Western Europe `[sourced, H1 2026 call]`.

Things a gate-1 assessment has to absorb here that it does not for Royal Unibrew:

- **Hyperinflation accounting.** Goodwill is disclosed *"after hyperinflation adjustments"* `[sourced, 30-06-2025 statement]`. Any multi-year comparison of balance-sheet items carries a restatement the reader cannot reconstruct.
- **Minorities.** Total equity at 30-06-2025 of 27,769 comprises shareholders' equity **25,021** and non-controlling interests **2,748** `[sourced]`. Per-share arithmetic and group EBIT are not on the same base.
- **A hybrid.** **EUR 1.8bn of hybrid notes issued in May 2026** `[sourced]`. Hybrids are typically part-equity in the issuer's own leverage measure and full debt to an enterprise-value calculation. This matters — see gate 2.
- **Foundation control.** The Carlsberg Foundation held **30% of share capital and 76% of votes** `[sourced, but dated 31-12-2021 — the company's shareholder page does not surface a machine-readable current figure]`. The structure is durable and the staleness is a disclosure gap, not a change.
- **Two profit measures.** Guidance is given on a constant-perimeter measure ("CPM"): 2026 growth is guided against a **2025 CPM operating profit of DKK 13,686m**, while the reported FY2025 operating profit before special items is **13,356m** `[sourced]`. **A 330m gap between the base for guidance and the base in the accounts.** Not large, but it must not be crossed accidentally when computing a forward multiple, and this memo keeps them separate.

None of this is disqualifying. Together it means Carlsberg is a business one can follow but not easily *know*, which is the honest reading of gate 1.

**The FY2025 column is the Britvic year** — completed 16 January 2025 `[sourced]`. Revenue +18.8%, volumes +17.7%, operating profit +17.0%, and **invested capital +57.6%** `[computed]`. Free cash flow of **−21,667** is the purchase price passing through the statement.

**The ROIC spread is enormous and it is the whole argument.** 10.8% including goodwill against **30.9%** excluding it `[sourced]`. The operating business earns roughly 31% on the capital it runs. Shareholders earn **10.8%** on the capital they have committed, because the difference — approximately **DKK 68bn of goodwill** `[sourced: goodwill 68,464m at 30-06-2025]` — is what was paid to acquire those operations rather than build them.

**This is now the fourth file in the register carrying the `!!` gate-2 basis flag** (Krones, KION, Royal Unibrew, Carlsberg). **In every case the two bases give a different answer, and the framework does not say which one gate 2 is asked on.** Carlsberg is the most extreme instance yet: a 20-point spread that moves the name from "outstanding" to "marginal." **MODEL question 8 should be treated as the highest-priority framework question in the queue, ahead of question 4.** `[inference]`

#### Tangible capital
At 30-06-2025 `[sourced]`: total equity **27,769**; goodwill **68,464**; brands **10,734**; other intangibles **1,842**. **Intangibles of 81,040 against equity of 27,769 gives tangible equity of approximately −53,271 mDKK** `[computed]`.

For comparison, Royal Unibrew's equivalent figure is roughly **−3,489** `[computed]` on a business one-sixth the size. **Scaled to revenue, Carlsberg's negative tangible equity is about 2.7x Royal Unibrew's** `[computed]`.

#### Leverage, and one thing to be careful about
Reported deleveraging is real but not entirely operational. NIBD/EBITDA at 30-06-2026 is **3.0x** `[sourced]`, against **3.78x** at 30-06-2025 `[sourced]` and **3.28x** at 31-12-2025 `[sourced]`. Management attributed the fall to strong operating cash flow **and the EUR 1.8bn hybrid issuance** `[sourced, call]`.

**A hybrid that the issuer treats as part-equity reduces a reported leverage ratio without reducing the cash the group owes.** EUR 1.8bn is roughly **DKK 13.4bn** `[computed at ~7.46 DKK/EUR, the ERM II central rate — FX convention flagged as an open item]`; if half of it is credited as equity in the company's own measure, the reported ratio improves by roughly **0.35x on unchanged economics** `[computed on an assumption]`. Free operating cash flow of 3,694 in H1 2026 against 2,918 `[sourced]` is genuine and is doing real work; the point is that **the headline 3.9x → 3.0x narrative from the call cannot be taken at face value**, and in fact the "3.9x at year-end 2025" figure quoted in the call summary **contradicts the annual report's 3.28x** `[sourced discrepancy]`. Do not use the call figure.

#### What is genuinely good, and it is a lot — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
- **26 consecutive years of dividends** `[claim]`, raised to DKK 29.0 in FY2025 `[sourced]`.
- **Britvic is working ahead of plan.** Cost synergy target **GBP 110m**, raised from GBP 100m in October 2025 `[sourced, AR2025]`, with *"approximately 80% of total synergies by the end of this year"* `[claim, H1 2026 call]`. **Note: an earnings-call summary rendered this target as "DKK 110 million"; the annual report says GBP. The annual report governs.** `[sourced discrepancy]`
- **H1 2026 was a good half:** revenue 47,053, organic revenue +2.7%, operating profit 7,448 (+4.5% reported, **+5.9% organic**), margin **15.8% (+30bp)**, net profit 4,288 (+6.0%), EPS 32.4 `[sourced]`. **Guidance was narrowed upward from 2–6% to 4–6% organic operating profit growth** `[sourced]`.
- **Western Europe organic operating profit +8.7% with 80bp of margin expansion, supported by Britvic synergies** `[sourced]`.
- **The Pepsi wins compound an existing route to market.** Denmark, Finland and the Baltics from 2029 — CEO Jacob Aarup-Andersen: *"we can create a Nordic Pepsi cluster"* — plus Azerbaijan from 1 January 2027, expected to *"double our volumes in the market"* `[sourced]`. Both are described as leveraging existing distribution with minimal incremental capital `[claim]`.

#### What is not — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
- **Asia volumes were flat organically and China declined mid-single-digit**, attributed to *"severe weather conditions in China specifically"* with *"heavy rainfalls and floodings"* `[sourced]`. **Weather is the explanation management offered; it is not an explanation the framework can verify, and Q3 is already guided soft on distributor destocking** `[claim]`. A peer, China Resources, grew volumes 2% in the same half `[sourced, analyst question]`.
- **UK beer volumes declined slightly despite the Britvic combination** `[sourced, analyst question]`.
- **CFO Ulrica Fearn flagged 2027 input inflation**, while arguing *"cost push will be significantly less than it was back in 2022, 2023"* `[sourced]`. **This is the same 2027 packaging-and-aluminium risk that cost Royal Unibrew 7.8% in a day**, and Carlsberg has framed it more calmly. The framework should hold both companies to the same standard here rather than accepting the calmer framing.

### Open items and data gaps
- **NIBD at 30-06-2026 not obtained.** Only the 3.0x ratio is disclosed in the announcement reached. EV is computed on the 31-12-2025 figure — conservative, and overstated by ~1.5% if the ratio implies what it appears to.
- **Hybrid accounting treatment not obtained.** Whether the EUR 1.8bn is inside or outside the company's NIBD measure, and at what equity credit, changes both the leverage read and the EV.
- **FX convention.** The EUR/DKK conversion used above is the ERM II central rate, not a dated market rate. **Item 15 of the register's verification debt applies: state the convention or do not convert.**
- **Goodwill and brands are at 30-06-2025, not 31-12-2025 or 30-06-2026.** The tangible-equity figure mixes a FY2025 equity base with H1-2025 intangibles and is therefore approximate. Direction is not in doubt; magnitude is ±.
- **Carlsberg Foundation holding is dated 31-12-2021** (30% capital / 76% votes). The A/B share counts and the A-share price were not obtained, which is also why the market-cap discrepancy against the provider could not be resolved.
- **Britvic purchase price not disclosed in the annual report's five-year section**, so the premium is inferred from the DKK 38bn rise in invested capital rather than sourced directly.
- **Two secondary sources produced materially wrong figures this run** — an earnings-call summary rendering GBP 110m as "DKK 110 million," and the same summary quoting year-end 2025 leverage as 3.9x against the annual report's 3.28x. **Both were caught only because a primary document was available to check against.** See the process note in the session's queue update.
- Price is single-source, `u1`.
- No named published bear thesis or short report found. No analyst price target used as a valuation input. No screener fair-value model was used.
