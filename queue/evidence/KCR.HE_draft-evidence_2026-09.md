**The business is one good third and two mediocre ones.** Industrial Service — inspections, spare parts, modernisations and maintenance contracts on an installed base of overhead cranes — earns 20.8%. **Industrial Equipment earns 5.7%, which is barely above a cost of capital, and Port Solutions earns 10.4% on lumpy multi-year terminal projects.** Group margin is the weighted average of a franchise and two contractors.

**The mechanism that makes the good third good is the right one for this file:** an overhead crane in a working factory is a piece of safety-critical lifting equipment under statutory inspection, the customer cannot switch supplier without re-certifying, and the installed base outlives several equipment cycles. **That is brownfield productivity with a compliance moat, and it is the part of Konecranes the thesis actually wants.** It is one third of revenue.

**Contracted recurring revenue, stated properly.** The **Industrial Service agreement base is EUR 351.5m, +6.1%** `[sourced]` — the annual value of maintenance contracts. Against annualised Industrial Service sales of ~EUR 1,508m and fiscal 2025 group net sales of EUR 4,187.8m, that is **23.3% of service revenue and 8.4% of group revenue** `[both computed]`. **Everything else — spares, modernisations, call-outs — is transactional, and every euro of equipment and ports revenue is a project.**

**Capital structure.** **79,221,906 shares at 31 December 2025, of which 7,637 in treasury** `[sourced]`. **The Annual General Meeting of 26 March 2026 resolved a share issue without payment in which "two (2) new shares were issued for each existing share"** `[sourced, verbatim]` — **a three-for-one split.** Post-split: **237,665,718 issued, 237,642,807 net of treasury** `[computed]`. **Single share class, no controlling block identified** — a cleaner structure than Kalmar's unlisted class A.

### Verification — one new finding
**28. NEW — after a share split, providers do not split-adjust every per-share figure, and the dividend is the one they miss.** Google Finance's Konecranes page displays **shares outstanding, market capitalisation and earnings per share all correctly adjusted for the three-for-one split of 26 March 2026** — the share count is exactly 237.67M and the P/E reconciles to 0.6% — **while displaying a dividend yield of 7.63%, which is the PRE-split EUR 2.25 dividend against the POST-split EUR 29.50 price.** **The true yield is 2.53%: an error of a factor of three, in the direction that would make this the highest-yielding name in the register by a wide margin** `[all computed]`.

**Why it matters more than an isolated slip: the page passes the mandatory reconciliation.** Verification item 21's check — price times share count against displayed market capitalisation — **passes to 0.03%, and so does the P/E cross-check.** **A page can satisfy every existing internal check and still carry a per-share figure that is wrong by 200%.** This is item 20's family in a new guise: internal consistency is not evidence of correctness, and here three fields are consistent while a fourth, drawn from a different vintage of the same database, is not.

**Rule to add: where a corporate action has changed the share count — a split, a reverse split, a consolidation, a scrip issue — every provider per-share figure is re-derived from a company-disclosed absolute amount and a company-disclosed count before use. The reconciliation of item 21 does not detect this failure and must not be relied on to.** **Concretely: a dividend yield is checked against DPS × count = total distribution, not against the displayed yield.**

**And a companion note for the register's own hygiene: Konecranes' 52-week range of EUR 22.12–34.57 is split-adjusted and its trigger, its price and its low are all on the post-split basis. Any figure carried forward from a pre-2026-03-26 source for this name is out by a factor of three.** **Flag it in the row.**
