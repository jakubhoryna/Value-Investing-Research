### Verdict — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
MonotaRO passes gates 1 and 2 cleanly: a catalogue-distribution model any analyst can explain, a ten-year sales CAGR of 19.0% `[computed]`, ROE of 28.7% `[sourced]`, net margin at a decade high, a net-cash balance sheet, and a genuine structural runway (claimed 3–4% share of a ¥8–10tn market `[claim]`).
The reason it earns a TRACK rather than a PASS is the single most useful finding in this file: **the 2026 growth acceleration is borrowed, both lenders have been repaid, and this is verifiable from disclosure rather than inferred.** Growth ran +14.1% through 9M 2025, jumped to ~+20–21% from Q4 2025 through Q2 2026, and fell back to **+15.1% in June and +15.4% in July 2026** `[sourced, monthly disclosures]`. Two dated, disclosed, non-company-flattering events cover exactly that window: Askul's ransomware shutdown, and a Middle East prebuy that Grainger quantified at ~USD 45m and described as having **"fully subsided"** `[sourced]`.
An investor extrapolating H1 2026's +20.6% is extrapolating a competitor's ransomware outage. The shares are already ~24% below their January 2026 high `[sourced]`, and 35% of covering analysts are at Sell `[sourced, data provider]`. **If the market completes that repricing, a business of this quality enters the cheapness range — and the framework should be ready with a pre-agreed trigger rather than deciding in the moment.**

### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Pass — the most straightforwardly understandable business in the queue.**

MonotaRO sells indirect materials — maintenance, repair and operating supplies — to Japanese businesses through a catalogue and website. 28.7m SKUs listed, ~731k stocked, ~761k same-day-ship, same-day delivery across 42 prefectures `[sourced]`. Two channels: MonotaRO.com (small and mid customers, self-serve) and Enterprise (systems-integrated procurement for large accounts).

The economics are distribution economics: gross margin on merchandise, less the cost of picking and shipping small orders, less customer-acquisition spend, against a fixed distribution-centre asset base. Every one of those lines is disclosed.

Mix, H1 2026 `[sourced]`: standalone sales ¥187,293m, of which Enterprise ¥64,131m (34.2%) and MonotaRO.com ¥123,162m `[computed]`. Private brand ~18% of sales across ~20,000 SKUs `[sourced]` — the margin lever, and it is working.

Overseas is small and currently a drag `[sourced, H1 2026]`: Korea ¥4.91bn with ¥0.03bn operating profit, Indonesia ¥0.83bn at −¥0.18bn, India ¥0.32bn at **−¥0.44bn** and shrinking. Combined drag roughly ¥0.6bn per half `[computed]`. Immaterial to the thesis but should not be capitalised as optionality.

#### Returns — and what the ROE decline actually is
Reported ROE `[sourced]`: 43.0% (2016), 42.2%, 36.1%, 33.0%, 32.9%, 33.1%, 28.4%, 27.5%, 27.7%, **28.7% (2025)**.

The three-factor decomposition matters, because the headline decline is widely misread:

| FY | Net margin | × Asset turnover | × Leverage | = ROE `[computed]` | Reported |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 | 9.58% | 2.231 | 1.977 | 42.3% | 42.2% |
| 2021 | 9.25% | 2.143 | 1.670 | 33.1% | 33.1% |
| 2025 | 9.71% | 1.974 | 1.495 | 28.7% | 28.7% |

Every year reconciles to the company's reported figure within 0.1 point `[computed]`. **The 43% → 29% decline is not margin erosion — net margin is the single most stable line in the company (8.3%–9.7% across a decade, and at a decade high in FY2025).** The entire decline is deleveraging as retained earnings accumulated (leverage 1.98x → 1.50x) plus asset turnover falling from 2.23x to 1.97x as distribution-centre capital went onto the balance sheet.

That is a materially better fact pattern than "returns are eroding". A 28.7% ROE earned with **1.5x leverage and net cash** is a higher-quality 28.7% than a 43% earned with 2.0x leverage. Management's stated target is ROE ≥30% `[claim]`.

#### Financial strength
**Pass, comfortably.** Net cash of **¥20,371m** at 30.06.2026 `[computed: cash 41,334 less short-term borrowings 220, long-term borrowings 20,500, lease liabilities 243]`, equity ratio 63.2%, total equity ¥121,164m `[sourced]`. The company took on ¥13.0bn then ¥20.5bn of long-term debt to fund the Mito distribution centre and is *still* net cash mid-build.

#### Capital allocation
Sound, and recently reset in shareholders' favour `[sourced]`: the payout ratio sat at ~36% for four straight years and was permanently lifted to a stated policy of **≥50%** (FY2025 actual 50.6%, DPS ¥33.00 from ¥19.00). A ¥10bn buyback was authorised in February 2026 and **fully executed and cancelled in H1** — 5,209,200 shares for ¥9,999m. Prior-year buybacks were trivial by comparison (¥94m, ¥44m), so this is a change of policy, not a routine.

The offset is the Mito distribution centre: **¥50.4bn budgeted, roughly ¥30bn still unspent** `[computed: 50,400 less ¥20,033m construction in progress at 30.06.2026]`, operations not starting until **May 2028**, with depreciation beginning then `[sourced]`. FY2025 capex was ¥23.9bn on an accrual basis against ¥33.7bn of operating cash flow. The company is spending most of its cash generation on an asset that produces no revenue for two more years — defensible for a distributor whose whole edge is fulfilment density, but it is why the free-cash-flow yield looks poor today.

Askul was hit by RansomHouse ransomware on 2025-10-19; November 2025 sales fell ~95% YoY; service levels did not normalise until after February 2026; FY2026 (to May 2026) sales fell 16.8% to ¥400.2bn with a ¥17.4bn operating loss `[sourced]`. ### Evidence that would change the verdict — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*

### Strongest honest bear case — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
The market is paying ~26x and 7.8x book for a growth rate that has been flattered by a competitor's ransomware outage and a one-off wartime prebuy, both of which are now over and both of which the companies themselves have dated. Underlying growth had already decelerated to 12–13% in 2023–24, below the company's own ">15%" policy target, and June and July 2026 are back at ~15%. Meanwhile ¥30bn of capex remains to be spent on a facility that generates no revenue until May 2028 and then brings depreciation with it, asset turnover has already fallen from 2.23x to 1.97x, the ROE decline has been running for eight years, and net margin at a decade high leaves limited operating-leverage runway. Amazon Business is structurally larger, better capitalised and opaque in Japan; Askul is recapitalising and guiding a 22% recovery, and will price to win back whatever it lost. On top of all that sits a permanent governance discount: Grainger owns **50.34%** `[sourced]`, so minority holders cannot influence outcomes and cannot be taken out at a control premium against the parent's will.
