### Verdict — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
KION is the world's second-largest forklift manufacturer and, through Dematic, a top-three warehouse-automation integrator `[sourced]`. It is a genuinely good operating business trapped inside a balance sheet that paid too much for half of itself. On the company's own definition it earned **ROCE of 7.7% in FY2025** and guides **8.4–9.4% for FY2026** `[sourced]` — below any defensible cost of capital for a levered, cyclical capital-goods group. Strip the €3,450.9m of goodwill and the return on the remaining capital employed is **11.6%**; strip the acquired brand and technology intangibles as well and it is **15.1%** `[computed]`.
**First, the leading indicator has broken while the reported numbers improve.** H1 2026 revenue rose 3.5% and adjusted EBIT rose 11.6% `[sourced]` — and **Q2 group order intake fell 19.7% year on year, with the automation segment down 39.6%** `[computed from sourced]`. The order book of €5,070m covers roughly **5.2 months** of guided revenue `[computed]`. A capital-goods company converting backlog into margin while orders fall by a fifth is showing you next year, not this year.
**Second, two alternative performance measures were redefined in the same reporting period, both in the flattering direction.** Free cash flow was broadened from FY2026 to include cash flows from additions to and repayments of financial liabilities used to fund the lease and short-term rental business; industrial net operating debt was adjusted at 30 June 2026 to deduct **€495m** of financial debt used to refinance the lease business `[sourced]`. Neither change is illegitimate on its face. Both make the current period look better than the comparative, and **the company's own press release and its own investor deck give H1 2026 free cash flow figures I could not reconcile** (§ Open items). Until they reconcile, the free-cash-flow-based case for the shares is not assessable.

### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Pass. Two segments, both physical, both explainable in a paragraph.**

FY2025 `[sourced]`:

| Segment | Order intake | Revenue | Adjusted EBIT | Adj. margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Industrial Trucks & Services (ITS) | €8,104.0m | €7,558.0m | €531.2m | 7.0% |
| Supply Chain Solutions / Intelligent Automation Solutions (IAS) | €3,601.5m | €3,739.2m | €257.4m | 6.9% |
| **Group** | **€11,705.5m** | **€11,297.2m** | **€788.6m** | **7.0%** |

**ITS** builds and rents counterbalance and warehouse trucks under Linde, STILL, Baoli, Fenwick and Voltas, and services the installed fleet. Roughly two-thirds of a forklift maker's economics over a cycle sit in the service and rental annuity rather than in new-truck sales — that is the standard structure of the industry `[inference]`, and it is why ITS margins (9.0% in H1 2026) hold up better than the order book does.

**IAS** — renamed from Supply Chain Solutions with effect from 2026 `[sourced]` — is Dematic: conveyors, sorters, automated storage and retrieval, goods-to-person picking, and the software that runs them, sold as multi-year warehouse projects to e-commerce, grocery and general merchandise operators.

The two fit together in the way the company describes: the trucks move goods inside a building, the automation decides where they go. **This is squarely inside the reader's professional circle of competence — brownfield industrial productivity and repeatable rollouts in DACH — which is precisely the condition Marshall's first gate is asking about, and it is the main reason this name enters the bucket at the top rather than on a screen.**

**What complicates gate 1, and it is not trivial:** the reported group accounts blend an industrial manufacturer with a captive leasing bank. Lease business liabilities were **€4,540.1m** at 30 June 2026 `[sourced]`, against net financial debt of €1,244.1m and industrial net operating debt of €2,299.1m `[sourced]`. Three different debt measures, one of which was redefined this year. The company does the reconciliation, but a reader who takes "net financial debt €1,244m" as the leverage of the business has misunderstood it by a factor of two, and a reader who takes total lease liabilities as debt has misunderstood it in the other direction. **Gate 1 passes on the business and only just passes on the accounts.**

#### The number that decides it
| | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 guided |
|---|---|---|---|
| ROCE, company definition `[sourced]` | 8.7% | **7.7%** | **8.4% – 9.4%** |

The company defines ROCE as annualised adjusted EBIT over average capital employed across five quarters, where capital employed includes **goodwill and other intangibles** `[sourced]`. That is the right definition for this question, and the answer it gives is that KION does not earn its cost of capital.

#### The capital wedge, measured
Implied capital employed at the FY2025 ROCE: €788.6m ÷ 7.7% = **€10,241.6m** `[computed]`.

| Basis | Capital employed | Return on it |
|---|---|---|
| As reported (incl. goodwill and PPA intangibles) | €10,241.6m `[computed]` | **7.7%** `[sourced]` |
| Less goodwill of €3,450.9m `[sourced, 31-12-2025]` | €6,790.7m `[computed]` | **11.6%** `[computed]` |
| Less brands €939.0m and technology €634.0m `[sourced, 30-06-2026]` | €5,217.7m `[computed]` | **15.1%** `[computed]` |

**This is the sixth instance of the same pattern in the current cycle of work, and the widest in absolute euros.** Alimak 22.7% ex-goodwill against 10.0%; Verisure ~20% claimed against 7.5%; LVMH 34.7% against 15.7%; Moncler 33.3% against 18.2%; Shimano ~12% on operating assets against a 3.9% ROE. KION's wedge is 15.1% against 7.7% — **a factor of two, on €5.0bn of intangible capital.**

**The honest reading of the wedge cuts against the name, not for it.** Ex-goodwill returns tell you what the business would earn for an owner who acquired the operations at replacement cost. Nobody can. KION's shareholders funded the 2016 Dematic purchase, and the 7.7% is what that decision actually produced. A wedge is a reason to understand a business better; it is not a reason to add back capital that was genuinely spent.

**The counter, stated fairly:** the shares trade at **0.97x book** `[computed]`, so the market is already declining to pay for the goodwill. An investor buying at book is, in a sense, buying the tangible business at the price at which it earns 15.1%. That argument is real and it is the reason this memo runs to a full gate 3 rather than stopping here. It fails only because book value and capital employed are not the same thing, and because a business that has never demonstrated it can earn a double-digit consolidated return gives you no evidence that it will.

#### The order book has broken and the income statement has not caught up
| | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 | Change `[computed]` |
|---|---|---|---|
| ITS order intake | €2,070m | €1,941m | **−6.2%** |
| IAS order intake | €1,445m | €873m | **−39.6%** |
| **Group order intake** | **€3,500m** | **€2,809m** | **−19.7%** |
| Group revenue | €2,708m | €2,916m | +7.7% |
| Group adjusted EBIT | €189m | €224m | +18.4% |

H1 2026: order intake **€5,794m against €6,206m, −6.6%** `[computed from sourced]`; revenue €5,687m, +3.5%; adjusted EBIT €429.6m at a 7.6% margin against 7.0% `[sourced]`.

Order book at 30 June 2026: ITS €2,137m, IAS €2,953m, **group €5,070m** `[sourced]` — about **0.43x** the midpoint of guided FY2026 revenue, roughly **5.2 months** `[computed]`.

**The IAS number is the one to sit with.** Warehouse automation orders are large, lumpy and won in competitive processes, so a single quarter down 40% is not by itself a thesis. But IAS revenue grew 23.3% in the same quarter and its margin expanded to 6.5% from 5.7% `[sourced]` — the segment is converting a backlog it is not replacing. Management narrowed FY2026 guidance in July, taking the top of the IAS revenue range down from +6.0% to +2.8% and the top of its adjusted EBIT range from +23.0% to +15.6% `[sourced]`, which is the company acknowledging the same thing.

#### Margin direction is genuinely better, and the reason matters
Adjusted EBIT margin went 8.0% (FY2024) → 7.0% (FY2025) → 7.6% (H1 2026) `[sourced]`. The company attributes the Q2 improvement to its efficiency programme **and to lower long-term incentive expense because the share price was lower at the Q2 close** `[sourced]`. The second of those is not operating improvement, and it is a reminder that a rising share price would partly reverse it.

#### Adjusted EBIT versus EBIT — a fourth instance of a pattern already flagged — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
| | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Adjusted EBIT `[sourced]` | €917.2m | €788.6m |
| EBIT `[sourced]` | €777.8m | €500.9m |
| **Adjustments** `[sourced]` | **€139.4m** | **€287.7m** |
| Adjustments as % of adjusted EBIT `[computed]` | 15.2% | **36.5%** |
The adjustments comprise purchase price allocation effects and non-recurring items including restructuring, reorganisation and M&A `[sourced]`. **They doubled year on year and now consume more than a third of adjusted EBIT.** PPA amortisation is the accounting cost of the same Dematic goodwill that sinks the ROCE; restructuring at KION has recurred across an efficiency programme spanning multiple years, which is the definition of an operating cost.

#### Financial strength — adequate, not strong, and hard to read
Net financial debt **€1,244.1m** at 30 June 2026, up from €584.0m at year-end, driven by a €500m corporate bond issued in March 2026 whose proceeds repaid lease-business liabilities `[sourced]`. Industrial net operating debt **€2,299.1m** against €2,158.3m, leverage stable at **1.2x adjusted EBITDA** `[sourced]`. Net defined benefit pension obligation **€481.7m**, down from €527.5m on a €16.8m special funding and higher discount rates `[sourced]`. Equity €6,123.0m at FY2025 on total assets of €18,294.9m — a **33.5% equity ratio** `[computed]`.

1.2x is comfortable. The difficulty is that the metric it is struck on was redefined in the same period.

#### Ownership — a fact that belongs in gate 2, not a footnote
**Weichai Power holds 46.5%; free float is 53.4%; treasury 0.1%** `[sourced, 30-12-2025]`. A single strategic shareholder just below the 50% line controls this company in practice. That is not disqualifying — Krones' founding family holds 51.9% and that memo treats it as a positive — but a Chinese state-linked industrial group controlling a German maker of automation for European supply chains carries governance, capital-allocation and geopolitical considerations that a family holding does not, and none of them were assessable from the material reviewed.

### Open items and data gaps
- **Unresolved conflict on H1 2026 free cash flow.** The company press release of 30 July 2026 states **€22.2m against €161.9m** in H1 2025. A reading of the same day's update-call deck returned **€137m against €66m**, together with a statement that the definition change "would have had no impact on the free cash flow reported for FY 2025 / Q1 2026", and a separate reading of the interim report returned an assertion that the change increased reported H1 2026 free cash flow by **€494.7m**. **These three cannot all be true.** The press-release figures are used in this memo because they are the primary company statement in plain text; **no free-cash-flow-derived conclusion is drawn.** This must be resolved before any figure enters the register.
- **Whether industrial net operating debt already includes the net pension obligation is unresolved.** The FY2025 management report describes the net defined benefit obligation as a component of "industrial net debt"; the H1 2026 report gives "industrial net operating debt" without restating the components. Both enterprise values are shown for that reason.
- **Interest-bearing debt is not separable from lease-business funding** in the material reviewed with confidence. Three measures — net financial debt €1,244.1m, INOD €2,299.1m, lease business liabilities €4,540.1m — were obtained; the bridges between them were not.
- **Goodwill is taken at 31-12-2025 (€3,450.9m) for the ROCE decomposition** because capital employed is a FY2025 average; the 30-06-2026 figure is €3,505.8m. Brand (€939.0m) and technology (€634.0m) intangibles are 30-06-2026 figures applied to a FY2025 denominator — **a minor basis mismatch, flagged rather than corrected.**
- **No goodwill split by cash-generating unit was obtained**, so the ITS and IAS shares of the €3.5bn cannot be separated. This matters: if most of it sits in IAS, the automation business earns far less on committed capital than the 6.9% segment margin suggests.
- **FY2026 reported EBIT is not guided**, only adjusted EBIT. The 16.5x reported-EBIT multiple is therefore trailing while the 8.9x is forward — **not a like-for-like comparison**, and stated as such.
- **Service and rental share of ITS revenue and gross profit was not obtained.** The claim that the aftermarket annuity carries the segment is an inference from industry structure, not from KION disclosure.
- **No pre-2024 ROCE series was obtained.** The judgement that low returns are structural rather than cyclical rests on two years plus guidance.
- **Weichai Power's intentions, board representation and any related-party arrangements were not examined at all.**
- No named third-party bear thesis was obtained. The bear case is built from the company's own disclosures.
