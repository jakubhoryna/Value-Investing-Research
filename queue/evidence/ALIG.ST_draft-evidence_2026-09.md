### Verdict — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
Alimak discloses service *revenue* share — 39.6% in Q2 2026, 42% of order intake `[sourced]` — and **discloses no service gross profit, no service EBITA and no service margin under the current five-division structure** `[sourced absence]`. The last time aftermarket profitability was visible was FY2019, when After Sales was a reported segment: 27% of revenue producing **53% of group adjusted EBITA at a 26.3% margin** `[sourced]`. Anyone underwriting "39% of revenue at 26%+ margins" today is extrapolating a seven-year-old disclosure across a group whose composition has since changed completely — Tractel alone doubled it.
Layered on top: **intangibles and goodwill are ~60% of total assets and exceed total equity — tangible equity is negative ~SEK 660m** `[computed, 30.06.2026]`. ROCE including goodwill is **10.0%**; excluding goodwill the company cites **22.7%** `[sourced]`. The gap is the acquisition premium, and there is no disclosed goodwill-by-CGU split to test impairment headroom.

### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Marginal pass — the machines are simple, the accounts are not.**

Rack-and-pinion hoists and platforms that climb a fixed mast: construction hoists, permanent industrial elevators, building maintenance units for facade access, service lifts inside wind turbines, plus height-safety equipment and fall protection from the Tractel acquisition. Sold as equipment, then serviced over a long life. That is a genuinely explainable business.

The accounts are the problem. Alimak has **reorganised its reporting segments twice** — in 2021 (Construction / Industrial / BMU / Wind) and again in 2023 post-Tractel (Facade Access / Industrial / Construction / Height Safety & Productivity Solutions / Wind) — and **restated per-share history for the March 2023 rights issue** `[sourced]`. Cross-year divisional comparison is not clean, and at least three reconciliation problems remain unresolved in the source documents themselves:

- FY2015/FY2016 EPS reads 3.12/4.48 in the 2016 annual report and 2.86/4.10 in the 2019 annual report. **Neither document explains the restatement** `[sourced discrepancy]`.
- The FY2022 dividend reads SEK 3.65 proposed pre-rights-issue, SEK 1.82 restated, and MSEK 176 actually paid — and 1.82 × 107.57m shares = 196, not 176 `[computed]`. Does not reconcile.
- FY2025 divisional revenue sums to 6,929 against a group figure of 6,874; the difference is eliminations, not quantified in the source `[sourced]`.

None of these is fatal on its own. Together they mean the historical series requires more care than a first-pass screen implies.

FY2025 divisional mix `[sourced]`: Facade Access 1,919 revenue / 12.2% adj. EBITA margin; Industrial 1,544 / 25.4%; Construction 1,533 / 14.0%; HS&PS 1,292 / 17.7%; Wind 641 / 19.3%.

#### The aftermarket question — the crux, and it is unauditable — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
Service share of revenue `[sourced]`: FY2023 35%, FY2025 38.7% (2,657 of 6,874), Q2 2026 39.6% (699), H1 2026 38.8%. Service order intake was **42% of the total in Q2 2026, +9% organic** `[sourced]` — genuinely strong while equipment orders were weak.
Service share of revenue by division, FY2025 `[sourced]`: Industrial 56%, Facade Access 42%, Construction 38%, Wind 38%, HS&PS 14%.
The historic evidence, where it exists, is genuinely impressive: FY2019 After Sales 1,263 revenue (27% of group) generating 332 adjusted EBITA at **26.3%**, equal to **53% of group adjusted EBITA**; FY2016 After Sales 680.3 (33% of revenue) at a **32.9%** margin against a group margin of 16.1% `[sourced]`. If those economics survive at 39% of a doubled revenue base, this is a much better business than the group margin suggests. **I cannot show that they do.**

#### Returns
**ROCE 10.0% FY2025 (9.8% FY2024)** on a capital base including goodwill `[sourced]`; **22.7% excluding goodwill** `[sourced, Q2 2026 slides]`.

Both numbers are true and they say different things. The operating business earns well on the capital it actually runs. The *company* earns 10% on the capital shareholders have actually committed, because SEK ~3.4bn of goodwill was created buying Tractel (3,261) and Tall Crane (154) `[sourced]`. For a value framework the second number is the one that governs: 10% is at or near cost of capital.

#### Cyclicality — the 2020 natural experiment — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
The best evidence in the file, and it supports the aftermarket thesis directionally `[sourced, FY2020 vs FY2019]`:
The service-heavy and installed-base-linked divisions held margin; the project and equipment divisions did not. **That is the strongest single argument for the aftermarket thesis — and it is being repeated right now:** in Q2 2026, Construction revenue fell 18% and its margin **halved to 8.3% from 16.7%**, while Industrial held 25.3% and Wind improved to 22.5% `[sourced]`. Group adjusted EBITA margin held at 16.9–17.4% because the mix is now far more service- and Tractel-weighted than in 2020.
**No 2009 data exists.** Alimak was Triton-owned from 2007 and listed only in June 2015; the earliest year in any Alimak IR key-figures table is 2012 `[sourced]`. The 2012 EBIT margin of 9.2% against 17.6% in 2013 is the earliest visible stress episode, unexplained in the source.

#### Financial strength
**Adequate, and deteriorating at the wrong point in the cycle.** Net debt/EBITDA: 1.76x (Dec-2025) → 1.85x (Mar-2026) → **2.00x (Jun-2026)** `[sourced]`, against a 2.5x target that the Pro-Bel acquisition "will temporarily increase net debt slightly above" `[sourced, company statement]`. Equity ratio 55.2%. Total equity 7,421 against intangibles and goodwill of 8,081 → **tangible equity −660** `[computed]`.

#### Capital allocation
Acquisitive, and the record is mixed to unknowable. Tractel (Nov 2022) at ~EUR 500m EV was transformative and created SEK 3,261m of goodwill; the stated ~150 MSEK synergy target by 2026 `[claim]` — **I could find no report confirming whether it was delivered** `[sourced absence]`. Purchase prices for Century Elevators, Interlift, Tall Crane, Cento, Verta, Dataline, Avanti and Facade Access Group were **not disclosed** `[sourced absence]`. Pro-Bel (July 2026) is the exception and looks sensible on the disclosed terms: CAD 200m EV for CAD 24m of adjusted EBITA at a 34.6% margin = **8.3x** `[sourced]`.

Ownership is a positive: Investment AB Latour holds **29.78%** `[sourced, 31.12.2025]` — a long-duration Swedish industrial holding company with a board seat, replacing the previous PE sponsor. One share class, one vote per share, no shareholder agreements known to the board `[sourced]`.

### Gate 3 — Is it cheap? — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
Inputs `[sourced]`: price SEK 128.40 (2026-09-04, MarketScreener); 107,573,111 shares issued, ~105,835k basic average ex-treasury; net debt SEK 2,550m (30.06.2026); market capitalisation SEK 13,590m.
| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Enterprise value | SEK 16,140m `[computed]` |
| **EV / LTM adjusted EBITA (1,150)** | **14.0x** `[computed]` |
| EV / LTM EBIT (905) | 17.8x `[computed]` |
| P/E on LTM reported net income (550) | 24.7x `[computed]` |
| P/E on LTM adjusted EPS (~6.85) | **18.7x** `[computed]` |
| FY2025 FCF (OCF 829 − capex 185) | SEK 644m → **4.7% yield** `[computed]` |
| Dividend yield (3.30 proposed / 128.40) | 2.57% `[computed]` |
| ROCE including goodwill | 10.0% `[sourced]` |
**14x EV/EBITA and 18.7x adjusted earnings, for a business earning 10% on total capital with negative tangible equity, rising leverage, and its largest division's margin halving, is not a value entry.** The 4.7% free-cash-flow yield is the most attractive number here and it deteriorated sharply in 2025: operating cash flow fell 28% (1,148 → 829) while adjusted EBITA fell only 2%, and capex rose from 126 to 185 `[sourced]`.
One permanent haircut to note: the CFO stated on the Q2 2026 call that the 29.2% effective tax rate "likely won't return to 25%" `[sourced]`, partly because Pro-Bel adds US and Canadian earnings.
