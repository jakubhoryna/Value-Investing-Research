### 5. The bear case, and what would change the verdict — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**The cycle objection, in its Epiroc-specific form.** **Orders received grew 19% organically in H1 2026 with large orders more than tripling to MSEK 720** `[sourced]`. **A jump in "large orders" — the company's own >MSEK 150 category — is the most cyclical revenue there is: single mine developments, booked years ahead, converting at whatever margin they were priced at.** **The 64% aftermarket share is the damper, and it is a real one; but 36% of revenue is equipment sold into a capex boom.** `[inference]`
**And a specific, checkable divergence.** **Revenues grew 7% organically in the half against orders received up 19%** `[sourced]`. **The book is building well ahead of the P&L — which is the GEA shape and a good sign — but it means the margin being capitalised at 25.5x enterprise value is being earned on a revenue base that has not yet absorbed the boom-priced orders.** **When it does, the margin either holds (and the multiple was right) or it does not (and the return falls with it).** **That is the whole question on this row and it resolves over the next four quarters, not sooner.**
