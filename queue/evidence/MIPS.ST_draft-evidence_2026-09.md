### Verdict — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**First, the moat is now unauditable in the one dimension that matters.** Mips discloses ~60 patent families and 400+ granted patents `[sourced]` and **no expiry dates whatsoever**. The widely-circulated "core patents lapse around 2030" claim is third-party assertion, not company disclosure. For a business whose entire economic engine is a per-unit royalty on a patented mechanism, the term profile is the single most important fact about it — and it is missing.
**Second, in December 2025 Mips bought Koroyd, one of its named competing technologies, for SEK 682m.** That converted a debt-free asset-light licensor carrying SEK 69m of intangibles into a business carrying ~SEK 564–569m of goodwill and net debt, and the dividend was cut from 6.50 to 2.50 to help pay for it `[sourced]`. That is either shrewd category consolidation or an admission that organic defensibility was insufficient. The file does not settle which, and at 63x the buyer is paying for the first reading.

### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Pass, with a caveat.**

Mips licenses a low-friction slip-plane layer that lets a helmet shell rotate slightly relative to the head during angled impact, reducing rotational energy transfer to the brain. Helmet brands pay a per-unit royalty. Revenue is units × royalty rate; costs are largely fixed (roughly 110 employees, SEK 41m of R&D in 2025 `[sourced]`). That is a two-variable business and easy to model.

The caveat is that **the per-unit royalty rate is not disclosed** — the "$8 per unit" figure in circulation is an unsourced blog claim `[claim]`. Without it, unit volumes cannot be derived from revenue, and the "is a brand's next product manager going to keep paying this?" question cannot be tested quantitatively.

Vertical mix `[sourced]`: FY2025 Sports 478, Motorcycle 31, Safety 23. H1 2026 Sports 313, Motorcycle 23, Safety 47. Safety is the growth story management is selling — SEK 100m targeted for 2026 `[claim]` — but Q2 2026 Safety grew 604% reported and only **80% organically** `[sourced]`; the rest is Koroyd.

#### Returns
**Mips discloses no ROE or ROCE in any filing reviewed** `[sourced — absence confirmed across the 2025 annual report, the 2025 year-end report and both 2026 interim reports]`. Only the equity ratio is given: 87% (Dec-2024) → 43% (Dec-2025) → 46% (Jun-2026).

The structurally important observation, stated without inventing a ratio: **before Koroyd, Mips carried SEK 69m of intangibles, SEK 382m of cash, zero credit-institution debt and SEK 641m of equity while earning SEK 174m of EBIT (FY2024)** `[sourced]`. Invested capital excluding cash was a small fraction of equity, which is why the pre-2026 business screened as an extreme-return compounder. **After Koroyd, intangibles are SEK 768m of which ~SEK 564–569m is goodwill** `[sourced, 30.06.2026 / 31.12.2025]`. Post-deal ROIC is therefore an entirely different number depending on whether goodwill is included, and the answer swings from "exceptional" to "ordinary". **This is the biggest analytical fork in the file and it cannot be resolved from disclosure.**

#### Financial strength
Still fine, but no longer pristine. Net debt SEK 124m against equity of 621m at 30.06.2026 `[sourced]`; SEK 311m of debt to credit institutions where there was none twelve months earlier. Cash SEK 187m. This is not a leverage problem; it is a change of character.

#### The moat, weighed honestly — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**For:** ~60 patent families, 400+ granted patents `[sourced]`; 70%+ gross margin held through a volume depression; POC's competing SPIN technology faced infringement claims and was discontinued in favour of re-adopting Mips `[sourced, third-party]`; more than 150 customers `[sourced]`; eleven consecutive quarters of bike growth `[sourced]`.
**Against:** no disclosed patent expiry profile at all; **top-five customer concentration rose to 43% of sales in 2025 from 40%** `[sourced]`; at least one credible brand defection (Lazer to in-house KinetiCore) `[claim, unverified]`; and in July 2026 Mips **paid USD 3.25m to settle patent-infringement litigation** brought by BrainGuard Technologies against its customer Revelyst `[sourced]` — which shows the IP position is contested even where Mips is not the primary defendant.

#### Quality of the 2026 growth — this matters for the multiple — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
H1 2026 revenue was SEK 382m against 251m, headline +52%. Decomposition `[sourced]`:
- The Q2 2025 comparator was **depressed by US tariffs imposed during that quarter** `[sourced]`, and North America's +91% in Q2 2026 partly reflects easier comparators `[sourced]`.
So the 72% headline overstates the run-rate on two counts. **In fairness to the bull case, the restocking objection is weaker than it looks:** the company's own Q4 2025 report notes bike inventories at their lowest in a decade excluding 2020–21, and management argued on the Q2 2026 call that channel stock cannot be the driver because there is none `[sourced]`. Bicycle Retailer attributes the expansion to premiumisation and e-bike penetration `[sourced]`.
One flag for the reviewer: management quoted an **adjusted** EBIT margin of 46.7% for Q2 2026 against a **reported** 41.0% — a 5.7-point gap with **no disclosed bridge** `[sourced absence]`. Likely acquisition amortisation and transaction costs, but that is `[assumption]`, not disclosure. Do not use the adjusted series until the bridge is obtained.
