### 1. The business
**GEA builds and services the equipment that processes liquids and solids into food, beverages, dairy products and pharmaceuticals** — separators and homogenisers, spray dryers and freeze dryers, brewing and beverage lines, milking robots and barn equipment. **The customer is a food, dairy, beverage or pharmaceutical producer; the purchase is a production line or a component of one; and roughly two fifths of the revenue is what happens afterwards.**

**One division earns the group's return.** **Pure Flow Processing is 35.4% of gross divisional revenue and 47.6% of gross divisional EBITDA before restructuring** `[computed]`, at a **27.0% margin against 9.8% for Nutrition Plant Engineering** — a 17-point spread inside one company. **The components-and-separation business is a components business; the plant-engineering business is a projects business; and the framework has repeatedly found that those two structures earn differently.** `[inference]` **The Bucket K sweep's sub-sector map predicted exactly this ordering, and GEA is the first name in the corpus where both layers sit inside one issuer and can be compared without a cross-company adjustment.** **That is the most useful thing this memo produces after the returns themselves.**

**Service is disclosed and it is large. 40.3% of H1 2026 group revenue, against 40.9% in H1 2025** `[sourced]`. **That is the highest disclosed service share of any name in the register's capital-goods-with-service band** — Konecranes discloses an agreement base of EUR 351.5m against H1 net sales of EUR 1,927.4m `[carried]`, which is a different and narrower measure, and Kalmar discloses services sales of EUR 158m in Q2 against EUR 480m of sales, or 32.9% `[carried, computed]`. **GEA's number is the cleanest and the biggest, and it is the mechanism gate 2 is measuring.**

**Gate 1 verdict: PASS.** **The caveat, stated rather than buried: four divisions is more than any name the register holds except the conglomerates it dropped.** **What separates GEA from them is that all four sell process equipment into overlapping food, beverage and pharma customers, and all four are disclosed with revenue and profit — so the thesis-relevant business does not have to be extracted from a disclosure that will not give it up.** **That is the recovered rule's actual test and GEA passes it.** **Bucher Industries, the other four-way group in the screen's band, does not** `[inference — see the recovered-reasons file §Verdict]`.

### 3. Gate 2 — the returns, and what MODEL question 8 does and does not do here
**GEA's disclosed ROCE definition, verbatim and in full** `[sourced — company key-financial-data page]`:

> *"EBIT before restructuring expenses of the last 12 months. Capital employed average of the last 4 quarters and excluding goodwill from the acquisition of the former GEA AG by former Metallgesellschaft AG in 1999."*

**Read that carefully, because the natural first reading is wrong and it is wrong in GEA's favour twice and against it once.**

**It is an adjusted numerator** — MODEL question 8's numerator half, in its purest form: the excluded cost recurs every period. **It is a four-quarter average denominator**, which flatters a growing capital base. **But the goodwill exclusion is far narrower than "excluding goodwill" sounds: it removes only one 1999 acquisition block, not the EUR 1,497.8m of goodwill the balance sheet carries** `[sourced]`. **The company's capital employed of EUR 1,949.3m sits EUR 482.8m below equity less net liquidity of EUR 2,432.1m** `[computed]` — **so roughly a billion euros of goodwill is INSIDE the disclosed denominator.** `[inference — the EUR 482.8m gap also contains averaging and definitional differences and is not a clean read of the excluded block]`

**The disclosed figure replicates.** **Reconstructing GEA's own formula gives 36.74% against a disclosed 36.8%** `[computed]` — **a 0.06 percentage-point difference, and the corpus's SECOND exact replication of a company-disclosed return after Konecranes' 22.15% against 22.1%.** **It also independently validates the derived restructuring add-back**, which was built from the gap between EPS and EPS-before-restructuring and is the only derived input in the whole numerator chain.

### 5. The bear case, stated at its strongest — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**The Q2 EBITDA margin before restructuring of 17.4% is a record** `[claim, and the company's own framing]`. **The order backlog of EUR 3,540.4m is up 13.1% to what is very likely a high.** **ROCE guidance was raised to 36–40% from 34–38%, having been 33.8% two years earlier** `[sourced]`. **Revenue growth accelerated from 3.7% organic in FY2025 to 8.2% in H1 2026 and 11.0% in Q2** `[sourced]`. **A business measured at the top of every one of those series is being underwritten at a peak, and the framework's own restock document opened by flagging exactly this failure mode for the six names it queued.**
**Second: the return is flattered by a denominator that grew slower than the business.** **Capital employed was EUR 1,700.6m in FY2024 and EUR 1,709.8m in FY2025 — essentially flat — while EBIT before restructuring rose** `[sourced]`. **A return that improves because the numerator grows on a static capital base is a real improvement; it is also the shape that reverses fastest when a capex cycle arrives.** `[inference]`
**Third, and this is the one that would change the verdict rather than shade it: the corpus has never seen GEA through a downturn.** **No return series before FY2024 was obtained this run** — **the company's key-financial-data page returned only two years** `[sourced absence]`. **Krones failed to establish gate 2 on a three-year window for precisely this reason, and its bear case was that FY2019 produced a 1.1% pre-tax margin** `[carried]`. **GEA is a peer of Krones. The FY2019–FY2023 series is the single highest-value cheap research item on this row and it was not obtainable from this run.** §6.

**MODEL question 13 — NEW, and it is a denominator question that question 8 does not cover.** ***Are employee-benefit and pension obligations part of invested capital?*** **On GEA the choice moves the post-tax return from 19.3% to 14.5% — 480 basis points, on a 12.3% break.** **The framework has silently used equity-plus-net-debt everywhere, which excludes them.** **That convention has never been stated and it is the third unstated convention this corpus has found in two days, after the trigger multiple (question 12) and the conglomerate boundary (recovered this run).** **Note that it bites hardest on old European industrials with defined-benefit schemes — which is most of the screen's band-two candidates.** **Requires `commit model`. Not bundled with anything in this memo.**

**Evidence for MODEL question 8, and it runs the other way for once.** **GEA is the first name where the basis moves the answer 22 points and changes nothing.** **Question 8 should be resolved because it decides marginal names; this memo is evidence that it does NOT need to be resolved before a clearly-passing or clearly-failing name can be written.** **That materially reduces the cost of leaving question 8 open, and it argues against the restock's instruction to hold four of six priority names behind it.** `[inference]`
