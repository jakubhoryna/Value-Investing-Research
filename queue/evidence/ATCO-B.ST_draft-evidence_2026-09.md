### 5. The bear case — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**H1 2026: orders +11.1%, revenues +1.9%, operating profit +2.4%** `[computed]`. **Book-to-bill 1.13x in the quarter.** **Meanwhile ROCE fell two points and ROE three** `[sourced]`. **A business adding orders while its return on capital falls is either buying growth with capital or booking it at a lower margin, and the disclosure read here does not say which** `[sourced absence]`.
**The specific mechanism is visible: Vacuum Technique orders rose 60% on what the company's own coverage describes as a semiconductor surge** `[sourced for the +60%; the semiconductor attribution is from the release headline]`. **Vacuum Technique carries a 26% service share and a 21.0% margin against Compressor Technique's 45% and 24.1%.** **A backlog shifting toward vacuum converts, over the next several quarters, into revenue at a lower blended margin and a lower annuity content — the Alfa Laval mechanism in a second company on the same day.** `[inference]`
**The honest counter.** **Compressor Technique is 43.5% of revenue at a 24.1% margin with a 45% service share and orders still +19%** `[sourced and computed]`. **That business alone, at a sane multiple, is what the framework is built to own.** **The problem is entirely that it comes attached to a 28x price and to a vacuum business at a semiconductor peak.**

**The correction:** the queue records prior-year ROCE at 26% and ROE at 27%. The company reports **28% and 29%**. **The decline is twice as large as the corpus believes it is on ROCE and 2.5× as large on ROE.** `[primary]`

**One qualification, offered so the correction is not itself over-claimed.** The figures above are **full-year 2025 versus full-year 2024**. The 2026-09-07 project-knowledge memo that generated the queue item may have been working on an **LTM basis through H1 2026**, in which case its 26%/27% could be a different measurement rather than a wrong one. **Either way the queue item as written cites no period, which is the defect** — and the standing rule the corpus already adopted covers it: *do not place a period figure in a series with periods of a different length.* **Route: correct the item to name its period explicitly, and carry the full-year pair as sourced.**

**The company's explanation, in its own terms** `[primary]`: a *"strong negative currency effect"* on margins — currency was **−6% on revenues** and **≈−0.6pp on the operating margin** — alongside *"increased costs related to trade tariffs"* across business units.

**What that explanation does and does not cover** `[computed]`: a −0.6pp currency hit on the operating margin accounts for **under half** of the −1.3pp margin decline, and margin alone does not produce a −4pp ROCE fall. The rest has to come from the denominator and from volume: **net debt rose 14% while equity fell 3%**, so capital employed grew while profit fell 11%. **A REVIEW of this row should ask what the 14% net-debt increase was spent on in a year of −3% order intake** — that is the unanswered half, and it is an acquisition-and-buyback question, not a currency one.

**Two clean tie-outs worth recording, because they tell a PC session which basis each disclosed ratio uses** `[computed]`:

- **ROE ties out on year-end equity:** 26,425 / 110,383 = **23.9%** ≈ the disclosed 24%. So ROE is a year-end-equity measure, not an average-equity one.
- **ROCE does not tie out on equity plus net debt:** 110,383 + 20,665 = MSEK 131,048, and operating profit over that base is **26.0%** — 2pp above the disclosed 24%. So the ROCE denominator is larger (average capital employed, and/or provisions included) or the numerator is after-tax. **Same shape as Palfinger in §3.1, and the same instruction: carry the basis flag, do not pick a reconciliation.**
