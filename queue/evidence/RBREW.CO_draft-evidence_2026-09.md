### Verdict — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
1. **2026-04-21 — PepsiCo ends the licence in Denmark (incl. German border trade), Finland and the Baltics at end-2028, and hands it to Carlsberg.** The affected business is **"approximately 13% of Royal Unibrew's net revenue"** `[sourced, company announcement]`. Transition costs of **~DKK 300m**, and profitability only *"exceeding 2028 levels"* **from 2030** `[claim]`. The shares fell roughly 20% on the day `[sourced, press]`.
2. **2026-08-17 — the H1 print disclosed that the 2027 input-cost hedges roll off.** CFO Lars Vestergaard, verbatim: *"For 2027, we do not have hedges from the past. Here you will see a step up in costs, in particular on packaging material."* `[sourced, call transcript]` The shares fell **7.8%** on results that were, on the face of them, good.
So the H1 margin expansion the queue cited — EBIT margin 13.3% from 12.5%, EBIT +7.0% `[sourced]` — is **partly a hedge that is about to disappear**, and the revenue base it sits on **contracts by ~13% at end-2028**. The market did not misprice good news; it priced two pieces of bad news the queue had not seen. `[inference]`
**That does not make the name uninteresting — it makes it assessable, which the queue's version was not.** At DKK 419.20 the shares trade on **11.2x EV/FY2026E guided EBIT**, **12.5x LTM earnings** and a **7.0% free-cash-flow yield** `[computed]`, for a business earning **13% ROIC including goodwill / 21% excluding it** `[sourced]` with a twenty-year record of local-scale beverage operations. That is a fair price for a good business with a known hole in it, not a bargain.

### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Yes. This is the clearest gate-1 pass the project has produced.**

Royal Unibrew brews and bottles beer, soft drinks, malt drinks, cider and ready-to-drink products, and distributes them in a handful of small markets where it holds local scale. It buys ingredients and packaging, fills containers, and sells them through off-trade retail and on-trade hospitality. There is no technology risk, no order book, no percentage-of-completion accounting, and no segment reorganisation obscuring the history. Volumes are reported in hectolitres; price/mix is reported as net revenue per hectolitre. The unit economics can be written on one line.

**FY2025 segment structure** `[sourced, AR2025]` — three segments, and they sum:

| Segment | Net revenue (mDKK) | EBIT (mDKK) | EBIT margin | Volume (mhl) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Northern Europe | 10,440 | 1,518 | 14.5% | 11.0 |
| Western Europe | 3,741 | 480 | 12.8% | 5.5 |
| International | 1,542 | 239 | 15.5% | 1.6 |
| **Sum of segments** | **15,723** | **2,237** | | **18.1** |
| **Group reported** | **15,723** | **2,202** | 14.0% | 18.1 |

Revenue and volume reconcile exactly. **EBIT does not: the segments sum to 2,237 against a group 2,202, a difference of −35mDKK** `[computed]`, unquantified in the extract and presumably unallocated corporate cost or eliminations. Small, but it is the one line in this file that does not tie, and it should be confirmed against the note before any segment-level multiple is used.

**H1 2026 by segment** `[sourced]`: Northern Europe 4,975 revenue / 646 EBIT (vs 4,972 / 632); Western Europe 1,906 / 260 (vs 1,884 / 218); International 856 / 138 (vs 788 / 122). Segment EBIT sums to 1,044 against a group 1,026 — again a −18 gap `[computed]`. Consistent with the FY2025 pattern, which is mildly reassuring about the cause.

**The geography that matters for the thesis:** Northern Europe is 66% of revenue and 68% of segment EBIT, and it is the segment the PepsiCo licence sits in (Denmark, Finland, the Baltics). `[computed / sourced]` Western Europe is Italy and the Netherlands; International is export, principally Faxe beer, Crodo and malt beverages into Africa `[sourced, call transcript]`.

The step change is FY2022–FY2023 (margin 18.9% → 13.2% → 12.7%) and the volume step is FY2024 (14.1 → 17.4 mhl), consistent with the **Vrumona** acquisition in the Netherlands from Heineken, finalised **2023-09-29** `[sourced]`. **I did not verify a complete acquisition list, and the FY2022 margin collapse predates Vrumona** — energy and aluminium cost inflation is the obvious candidate and I have not confirmed it. Recorded as an open item rather than asserted.

**The `!!` basis flag applies here too.** Royal Unibrew publishes ROIC on both bases and the spread is wide — **13% vs 21%** `[sourced]`. This is the third file in the register (after Krones and KION) where gate 2 is being applied to a company-defined return number without the framework specifying the basis. **On the goodwill-inclusive basis Royal Unibrew earns 13%, which clears a 10% bar but not by much; on the exclusive basis it earns 21% and looks like a different company.** MODEL question 8 is not academic.

Trailing ROIC at H1 2026 was **12.8%, up from 12.0%** `[sourced]` — the recovery is real and it is being driven by margin, not by capital release.

#### Durability — and the specific thing that ends it — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**What is durable:** local brand positions in small markets, off-trade-weighted, with distribution scale that a national competitor can match and a global one generally will not bother to. CEO Lars Jensen's framing on the H1 call is the right one and is worth keeping: *"Consumers are not drinking more, they are drinking differently"* — toward off-trade and non-alcoholic `[sourced]`. Royal Unibrew gained share in Northern Europe on flat volumes in H1 2026 `[claim, unverified against a third-party panel]`, and International grew **11% organically** `[sourced]`.
- Markets affected: **Denmark (including German border trade), Finland, and the Baltic states.** BeNeLux continues beyond 2028 `[sourced]`.
- Expiry: **end-2028** `[sourced]`.
- Size: **"approximately 13% of Royal Unibrew's net revenue," expected to decline toward 2028** `[sourced]`.
- Counterparty: **Carlsberg takes the licence** `[sourced, trade press]`.
- Compensation: **the announcement contains no reference to any** `[sourced absence]`.
**The EBIT contribution of the Pepsi business is not disclosed** `[sourced absence]`, and it is the single most valuable missing number in this file. Licensed bottling of a third-party cola brand normally earns **below** group margin — the licensor takes the brand rent — so 13% of revenue probably represents **less than 13% of EBIT**. I cannot show that. Sizing it at group margin (14.0%) gives **~DKK 286m of EBIT, ~13% of FY2025 group EBIT** `[assumption, explicitly not data]`, and that is the number the stress case below uses, deliberately as the pessimistic end.

#### Financial strength
**Adequate, levered, and carrying no tangible equity.** At 30-06-2026 `[sourced]`: NIBD **6,610** (from 6,374 a year earlier), **NIBD/EBITDA 2.2x** (2.3x), equity **6,211**, total assets **19,263**, goodwill **5,217**, other intangible assets **4,483**.

**Goodwill plus intangibles = 9,700, which is 50.4% of total assets and 1.56x equity. Tangible equity is approximately −3,489 mDKK** `[computed]`. That is the same structural feature the framework flagged on Alimak, and it deserves the same weight: the returns shareholders actually earn on the capital they actually committed are the 13% line, not the 21% line.

**Caveat on that computation:** some presentations report "intangible assets" as a line *inclusive* of goodwill. The extract lists the two separately, and 5,217 + 4,483 = 9,700 against 19,263 of assets is plausible for this company, but **confirm against the balance-sheet note before relying on the −3,489 figure** `[open item]`.

Cash generation is good and stable: FY2025 FCF **1,413** on net profit **1,560** = **91% conversion** `[computed]`. H1 2026 FCF **458**, flat year-on-year, on operating cash flow **908** (from 931) and capex **450** (from 504) `[sourced]`. Guided capex is **~7% of revenue** `[claim]`; FY2025 actual was **6.4%** `[computed]`.

#### Capital allocation
Mixed, and the mix is the gate-2 reservation. **Dividends have been raised slowly and never cut** across the five years (14.5 → 16.0) `[sourced]`. **Buybacks are being used at scale and at depressed prices**, which is the right time: the programme increased to DKK 700m on 2026-04-30 completed on 2026-08-14 at an average of **DKK 457.7** for 1,529,200 shares, and a further **DKK 300m** programme runs to 2026-12-18 `[sourced]`. DKK 1.0bn of repurchases inside one year is **~5.0% of the current market capitalisation** `[computed]`.

Against that: the acquisition record above. Revenue bought, margin and ROIC diluted. **The current buyback is the better use of the same money, and management appears to agree**, which is a point in their favour rather than against.
