**In 2019 — an ordinary pre-pandemic year, not a trough — Krones earned €41.7m of pre-tax profit on €3.96bn of revenue.** Six years later it earns €424.1m on €5.66bn. Revenue grew 43%; pre-tax profit grew more than tenfold `[computed]`. **The entire investment case rests on whether that step-change is structural.** It is far too large to be explained by operating leverage on 43% more revenue, and it coincides with a restructuring programme (roughly €72m of personnel charges in 2020 alone `[sourced]`), an unusually strong beverage-capex cycle, and the industrial pricing window of 2022–2024. Those are three different explanations with three different futures and **the disclosure reviewed here does not separate them.**
**The second finding is a measurement one and it matters beyond this name.** Krones defines ROCE as EBIT over net capital employed where **"net capital employed is defined as non-current assets (excluding goodwill and financial assets) plus working capital"** `[sourced]`. KION defines capital employed to **include** goodwill and other intangibles, over adjusted EBIT `[sourced]`. **The register was one step away from recording "Krones 19.1%, KION 7.7%" as a like-for-like quality ranking. It is not one.** Krones' 19.1% is an *operating* return; KION's 7.7% is a *committed-capital* return. They differ in three dimensions at once — goodwill in or out, adjusted or reported numerator, four-quarter or five-quarter averaging.

### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Pass, comfortably.**

Krones sells the physical line that turns empty containers into palletised finished goods: stretch blow-moulders that make PET bottles from preforms, fillers, cappers, labellers, pasteurisers, inspectors, packers and palletisers, plus the software that runs them and the service organisation that keeps them running. The customer is a brewer, soft-drink bottler, dairy or water producer. The purchase is a multi-year capital decision measured in throughput per hour and in downtime avoided.

Three segments `[sourced]`:

1. **Filling and Packaging Technology** — the core.
2. **Process Technology** — the upstream side: brewing, syrup and liquid processing.
3. **Intralogistics** — System Logistics, warehouse and internal materials handling. *This is the segment that puts Krones in the same bucket as KION.*

Innovation spend runs at a stated **4–5% of revenue annually** `[sourced, claim]`.

**The service annuity is the part of the business that would matter most and is the part disclosed least.** Krones states a medium-to-long-term aim that "three out of every four new machines and lines will be supported by our own service team" and that roughly **1,200 Modular Service Agreements** are active `[sourced, claim]`. **No revenue or margin split for lifecycle service was obtained.** For a business whose durability case rests on an installed base, that is the single most important missing disclosure, and it is flagged in gate 2 rather than here — the *business* is easy to describe.

#### The measurement finding, first, because it governs everything below — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
> **Krones:** "Return on capital employed…the ratio of **EBIT** to average net capital employed in the past **four** quarters. Net capital employed is defined as non-current assets (**excluding goodwill and financial assets**) plus working capital." `[sourced]`
> **KION:** capital employed "comprises net working capital and the following line items: **goodwill**, other intangible assets, leased assets, rental assets, other property/plant/equipment, and equity-accounted investments less other provisions and other liabilities"; ROCE is **adjusted EBIT** over the average of the past **five** quarters. `[sourced]`

#### The record, weighed honestly
**For:**

- **ROCE 18.2% (FY2024) → 19.1% (FY2025) → 19–20% guided (FY2026)** `[sourced]`, on a definition that excludes goodwill but also excludes financial assets — so the €404.5m of net cash is not flattering the denominator either. On its own terms this is a genuinely high return.
- **Net cash of €548.2m at FY2025 and €404.5m at 30 June 2026** `[sourced]`, equity ratio **42.2%** `[sourced]`. There is no solvency question and no leverage flattering the return.
- **Order intake grew 4.5% in H1 2026 to €2,852.5m and the backlog grew 3.3% to €4,328.0m** `[sourced]` — about **0.76x FY2025 revenue, roughly nine months** `[computed]`. **Set against KION's 5.2 months and −19.7% Q2 order intake, this is the sharpest contrast in the bucket.**
- **51.9% held by the Kronseder Family Consortium GbR**, descendants of the founder `[sourced]`. Aligned, patient, and — on the evidence of a restructuring carried through a loss-making 2020 — willing to take short-term pain.
- Free cash flow **€247.7m in FY2025 against €113.2m in FY2024** `[sourced]`, and **€221.3m in the loss-making year 2020** `[sourced]`. **A capital-goods business that generated its best free cash flow of the period in the year it lost money is telling you its working capital unwinds when volumes fall.** That is a real and underrated quality, and it is the strongest single item in the bull case.

**Against, and it is heavier than the list above suggests:**

- **The pre-2021 record is poor.** 5.7% EBITDA margin and a 1.1% EBT margin in FY2019 — a year with no pandemic, no war and no supply shock. On a Marshall reading, **a business that converted 1% of revenue to pre-tax profit in a normal year had no durable competitive advantage in 2019.** Something changed. Until it is established *what*, gate 2 cannot pass on three good years.
- **The step-change coincides with three simultaneous tailwinds** — a post-Covid beverage-capex catch-up, industry-wide pricing power through 2022–2024 inflation, and the company's own restructuring. Only the third is repeatable.
- **H1 2026 shows the first deceleration.** Revenue −0.4% reported (+1.8% currency-adjusted), net income **−4.7%** to €138.9m, EPS €4.39 against €4.60, and **ROCE 17.7% against 19.0%, down 130 basis points** `[sourced]`. EBITDA margin still improved, to 10.8% from 10.6%. **The margin is holding and the return on capital is not** — which is what you would expect if capital employed is growing faster than profit, and is worth watching precisely because the 2028 target requires the opposite.
- **H1 free cash flow was −€30.8m against +€46.7m** `[sourced]`. Q2 alone was −€21.3m against −€118.5m, a large improvement, so the swing sits in Q1 (−€9.5m against +€165.2m `[computed]`). **Lumpy, and the FY2025 full-year figure was strongly positive, so this is not yet a deterioration — but it removes the ability to lean on cash conversion in the current year.**
- **The 2028 targets are ambition, not evidence:** revenue ~€7bn, EBITDA margin 11–13%, **ROCE above 20%** `[sourced, claim]`. They should not carry weight at any gate. Recorded because a company that misses them will have said something about durability.
- **Structural risk sits in the product, not the market.** A large share of the installed base blows and fills PET. European packaging regulation, deposit-return schemes, refill mandates and any material shift in container mix act directly on Krones' addressable spend. The company positions PET recycling as an opportunity `[sourced, claim]`; that may be right, and it is unexamined here.
- **No lifecycle-service revenue or margin split was obtained** — the disclosure that would separate an installed-base annuity from a capital-equipment order book.

### Open items and data gaps
- **Goodwill and financial assets were not obtained**, so the committed-capital return — the figure directly comparable to KION's 7.7% — **cannot be computed.** This is the largest single gap in the memo and it is the one that would make the bucket's central comparison rigorous.
- **EBIT is not disclosed** in the results releases obtained; **EBT is used as a proxy** throughout, which understates EV/EBIT slightly given net cash.
- **Depreciation and amortisation is not disclosed separately**; the ~€178.2m used in the normalisation is the FY2025 EBITDA-to-EBT gap, which also contains the net financial result and any associates. **An inference, not a disclosure.**
- **No lifecycle-service revenue, margin or installed-base figure was obtained** — only the aspirational "three in four machines" statement and the count of ~1,200 Modular Service Agreements.
- **No segment-level revenue or profit split** between Filling and Packaging Technology, Process Technology and Intralogistics was obtained. **Intralogistics is the segment that places Krones in this bucket and it is entirely unquantified here.**
- **ROCE is available only for FY2024, FY2025, H1 2026 and FY2026 guidance.** The durability judgement rests on a three-year window. This is stated in the verdict rather than buried.
- **FY2019 dividend not obtained**; the FY2020 payout of €0.06 is described as the statutory minimum.
- **No balance sheet total** was obtained, so price/book could not be computed. Only the equity ratio (42.2% at FY2025, 39.4% at FY2020) is available.
- **MarketScreener prints a 52-week range of €112.2–€122.6** against Google Finance's €105.80–€144.20. These cannot both be right; the Google range is used and **the discrepancy is a sixth instance of the third-party-price-data defect** already logged for Moncler, Mips, CHAPTERS, Acast and RTL.
- **PET regulation, deposit-return schemes and container-mix risk are named but not analysed.** They are the structural risk and they deserve their own work.
- **No named third-party bear thesis was obtained.** The bear case is built from the company's own historic disclosures.

#### 3. The record is five years, not three — and the shape of it is the new bear signal

**FY2026 guidance of 19–20% therefore requires H2 2026 to run at roughly 20–22% to hit the mid-point** `[computed, approximate — the company's ROCE is a four-quarter average, so this is indicative only]`. **That is the single most testable claim the company has made, and the Q3 statement in late October tests it.**

#### 1.3 The lifecycle-service split: CONFIRMED NOT DISCLOSED
Checked in three primary documents — the Corporate IR Presentation 2025, the Annual Report 2025 strategy section, and the H1 2026 release. **None discloses LCS revenue in euros, LCS as a share of group revenue, or LCS margin against new-machine margin.**

**Read carefully, the second of those is an admission.** A company targeting three-in-four service attachment *in the medium to long term* is telling you it is not there now, and it declines to say where it is. **The growth rate is disclosed and the base is not.** That is a deliberate disclosure choice, and it means the LCS question cannot be resolved from public filings by any amount of further searching.
