**RHI Magnesita discloses a Group return on invested capital of 8.4% for H1 2026, up from 5.8%, on the stated basis of annualised NOPAT over average invested capital** `[sourced]` — **which is the register's own method.** **On the register's own arithmetic the same period gives 8.9% on the company's fully adjusted numerator, 6.4% on a numerator this memo can defend, and 5.3% on reported operating profit** `[all computed]`.
**First — a FOURTH correction to the restock's "not disclosed" claims, and the pattern is now conclusive.** The restock recorded *"No ROIC disclosed `[sourced absence]`"* for this name. **It is disclosed, at Group, Refractory and Vertical Integration level, on the register's own definition.** **With Daifuku (ROE 18.4% and ROA 14.5% disclosed), Konecranes (ROCE formula published in full) and Hexagon (ROCE 10.2% disclosed), that is four of four such claims wrong in one run.** **The queue-hygiene rule proposed in the register addendum should be adopted: an absence noted in a results PRESENTATION or a press summary is not an absence in the company's disclosure.**
**Third — the company discloses that its own vertical integration earns 4.0%.** **Refractory ROIC 9.3%, Vertical Integration ROIC 4.0% and FALLING from 4.2%** `[sourced]`. **RHI Magnesita mines its own magnesia, and it publishes the fact that the mining half earns less than half what the refractory half earns.** **That is an unusually candid disclosure and it is a capital-allocation finding: the integration is diluting the group return, by the company's own measure.**

### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Pass.**

RHI Magnesita makes refractories: the heat-resistant brick, monolithic linings and precast shapes that go inside anything that has to hold molten material — a blast furnace, a basic-oxygen or electric-arc steel converter, a cement kiln, a glass tank, a copper smelter. **The product is consumed in use.** A steel ladle lining is destroyed over a few hundred heats and replaced. **So this is a consumable sold against a customer's production volume, not a capital good sold against a customer's capex cycle** — which is a structurally better place to be, and it is why the name was worth analysing.

**Vertical integration is the company's distinguishing feature and it is reported separately.** RHI Magnesita mines and processes its own magnesia and dolomite. **The company discloses a Vertical Integration ROIC of 4.0% against a Refractory ROIC of 9.3%** `[both sourced]`.

**Segments, H1 2026** `[revenue and adjusted EBITA sourced; margins and shares computed]`:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Steel** | **EUR 1,130m** | **EUR 126m** | **11.2%** | revenue −1.4%, margin from **8.3%** |
| **Industrial** | EUR 423m | EUR 37m | **8.8%** | revenue **−13.1%**, margin from **9.3%** |

**Steel is 70.8% of revenue and 76.4% of adjusted EBITA** `[computed]`. **That is the whole business, and it is the single most cyclical, most politically exposed heavy industry in the world — currently absorbing Chinese overcapacity and European decarbonisation costs simultaneously.**

**And the diversification is shrinking while the concentration improves.** **Industrial revenue fell 13.1% and its margin fell 50 basis points, while Steel's margin rose 290 basis points** `[both computed]`. **So the segment that was supposed to reduce the steel dependence got smaller and worse, and the group is MORE steel-levered after this half than before it.**

**Listing and currency, and this row is the register's most complicated case.** **RHI Magnesita N.V. is Netherlands-incorporated, reports in EUR, is listed on both the London Stock Exchange (RHIM, quoted in GBX) and the Vienna Stock Exchange, and pays its dividend in EUR** — the H1 2026 interim is **EUR 0.60 per share**, payable 24 September 2026 to holders on the register at 28 August 2026 `[all sourced]`. **This is the fourth EUR-reporting, non-EUR-quoted row (Diageo GBX/USD, Kardex CHF/EUR, Hexagon SEK/EUR, RHI Magnesita GBX/EUR) — and the FIRST dual-listed one, which adds a VENUE question on top of the currency question.** **MODEL question 4b needs a clause for dual listings: which venue's last completed session is the row's price basis, and it must be stated in the cell.** **Shares outstanding approximately 47.0m** `[sourced/computed — see open items]`.

**On the register's arithmetic** — invested capital of equity EUR 1,251m plus net debt EUR 1,528m = **EUR 2,779m**, at a 25% tax rate `[the H1 effective rate was 23.4%]`, annualising the half `[all computed]`:

| Numerator | Annualised | NOPAT | **ROIC incl. goodwill** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adjusted EBITA (company's basis)** | EUR 330m | EUR 247.5m | **8.9%** |

| **Reported EBIT** | EUR 196m | EUR 147m | **5.3%** |

**The 8.9% brackets the company's disclosed 8.4% closely — the residual is average versus period-end capital and the tax rate — so the register can confirm that the disclosed figure is struck on the FULLY ADJUSTED numerator.**

**The middle row is the honest one and it follows note (cc)'s own taxonomy applied mechanically.** Note (cc) holds that the amortisation or impairment of what was paid for acquisitions **belongs in the numerator** — RHI Magnesita's EUR 527m of other intangibles are overwhelmingly the purchase accounting of the 2017 RHI/Magnesita merger, so their amortisation is that first kind and should not be added back. **A strike or a restructuring programme is the second kind and may be.** **And "digitalisation investment" is neither: it is ordinary operating expenditure, it appeared in both halves, and a company that spends EUR 21m a half on digitalisation is a company whose cost base includes EUR 21m a half of digitalisation.**

**Goodwill and other intangibles of EUR 935m are 33.6% of invested capital — a wedge in the same band as Kalmar's 35.0%** `[both computed]`. **Excluding them, invested capital is EUR 1,844m and the defensible numerator gives 9.7%** `[computed]` — **so even granting the goodwill-exclusive basis this row does not clear the register's break.** **MODEL question 8 is inert here in the sense that matters: the verdict does not move on either half of it.**

#### The margin improvement is cost, not volume — the third instance in one run
**Revenue fell 4.9% and adjusted EBITA rose 17.0%** `[computed]`. The company's own framing is *"consistent self-help delivery"* and it names the mechanisms: *"administrative cost reductions…EUR 15 million earnings increase"*, *"network optimisation…EUR 15 million"*, and capital expenditure **cut from EUR 130m to EUR 115m** `[all sourced]`.

**That is the same pattern this run found at Cognex (opex down 3.4% on revenue up 17.0%) and at Hexagon (EBIT1 margin held while gross margin fell 170 basis points).** **Three names in one run whose reported improvement came from the cost line while revenue fell or was flat.** **A cost lever is pulled once and it is symmetric.**

**And the guidance requires the next pull to be much larger.** **Full-year adjusted EBITA is confirmed at EUR 400m against H1's EUR 165m — an H2 of EUR 235m, a 42.4% step-up half on half** `[computed]` — **while the company simultaneously guides a full-year foreign-exchange headwind of approximately EUR 35m** `[sourced]`. **Note the shape: a demanding second half, on falling revenue, with the FX going the wrong way, from a company that has already cut capital expenditure to help the cash flow.** **Achievable, and it is the single thing to watch.**
