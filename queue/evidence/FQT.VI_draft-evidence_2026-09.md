### ⟪ `claude_2026-09-08_ERRATA-and-supplement_candidate-dossiers_v1.md` → 2.1 The two questions the dossier flagged, both answered ⟫
**Question 1 — "use the adjusted 6.7% EBIT margin, never the reported 8.1%." Confirmed, and now datable.** The report ties the ~EUR 8m one-off to the **Special Committee on Commerzialbank Mattersburg**, established 2020, *"essentially concluded in early 2025"*, dissolved **16 June 2025** `[primary]`. **So it is a legacy legal recovery, non-recurring by construction, and management's ≈7.0% FY2026 guidance is the run-rate to use.** ⚠ **The report's extract does not quantify the settlement precisely; the EUR 8m remains the reported approximation.**

**Question 2 — the equity ratio fall from 44.3% to 38.6% — is GROWTH, not leverage.** `[computed]` Total assets grew **EUR 114.5m (+29.0%)** while equity grew **EUR 22.0m (+12.6%)**, so total liabilities grew **EUR 92.5m** (220.0 → 312.5). Over the same period cash grew EUR 30.2m and orders on hand grew EUR 70.9m. **On a project business, an asset base and a liability base both expanding with the order book is the signature of contract assets and customer advances, not of borrowing** `[assumption — the contract-liability line was not obtained, and it should be before this is written into a memo]`. **The ratio fell because the denominator grew, not because debt did.**

### ⟪ `claude_2026-09-08_ERRATA-and-supplement_candidate-dossiers_v1.md` → 2.2 ⚠ The finding that changes how this name should be read ⟫
`[computed, all inputs primary except where noted]`

- **Goodwill plus other intangibles = EUR 21.2m against EUR 196.8m of equity — 10.8%.** **This is NOT a serial-acquirer balance sheet.** Against Interpump's 44.5% and Hexagon's 90.4%, Frequentis is at the opposite end of the distribution, and **MODEL question 8's basis problem barely bites here**: the goodwill-inclusive and goodwill-exclusive capital bases differ by about 11%, not by a factor.
- **Capital turnover is high.** Revenue 580.1 against equity 196.8 is **2.9× on equity**, and against a net-of-cash capital base it is far higher.
- **So the thin margin and the return on capital point in opposite directions.** Adjusted EBIT of **EUR 38.8m** `[computed: 46.8 − 8.0]` on equity of 196.8 is **19.7%**; on a capital base net of the cash pile it is materially higher again. **A 6.7% margin business that turns its capital several times is not the same animal as a 6.7% margin business that does not, and the framework measures return on capital, not margin.**

**⚠ Explicitly NOT computed here: a capital-employed figure or a worst-of-four ROCE.** The total interest-bearing debt and the current portion of lease liabilities were not obtained, the only debt figure available is secondary, and **the corpus's own rule is that a large net cash position makes the choice of whether to net it the single biggest driver of the answer.** **Assemble the debt lines at the PC and compute it there.** **The point of §2.2 is that the margin-based first impression recorded in the dossier understates this business on the metric the framework actually uses — not that the return is any particular number.**
