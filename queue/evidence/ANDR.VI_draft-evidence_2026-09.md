### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Pass on understandability. Partial on thesis fit, and the caveat should be stated rather than buried.**

Andritz supplies plant, equipment and automation to process industries, plus the service business on the installed base. **Four business areas** `[all sourced — H1 2026 financial report]`:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pulp & Paper** | EUR 1,470.0m | **38.3%** | 148.0 | **10.1%** | 151.1 | 3.1 |
| Hydropower | EUR 872.7m | 22.7% | 61.6 | **7.1%** | 61.7 | 0.1 |
| **Metals** | EUR 809.4m | 21.1% | 36.1 | **4.5%** | 48.3 | **12.2** |

| **Group** | **EUR 3,841.3m** | | **312.2** | **8.1%** | **329.7 (8.6%)** | **17.5** |

**Pulp & Paper is 38% of revenue at a 10.1% margin and is the best of the four. Metals is 21% at 4.5% and consumes 70% of the group's non-operating items.**

**Thesis fit, honestly.** The file's thesis is industrial automation and robotics for European manufacturing — brownfield productivity in discrete manufacturing, DACH and CEE. **Andritz is Austrian, industrial, and sells systems with a service annuity, which is three of the boxes.** **But its end markets are PROCESS industries and energy: pulp mills, hydro turbines, steel and aluminium processing lines, waste-to-energy and flue-gas plant.** **Hydropower — 22.7% of revenue — is infrastructure, not manufacturing.** **So this row belongs in the register on its economics and its geography, and it is not a direct expression of the thesis.** **Record that in the row rather than letting a good return imply a thesis fit it does not have.**

**Order book** `[all sourced]`: order intake H1 2026 **EUR 5,919.7m, up 25.2%**; **order backlog EUR 12,602.1m, up 20.5%** on year-end 2025 — a record. **Backlog is 1.64x annualised revenue** `[computed]`, the longest in the register.

**Where the order growth came from, and it is not evenly distributed** `[all sourced]`: **Hydropower order intake +81.8%**, Pulp & Paper +8.8%, Metals +4.3%, **Environment & Energy −12.7%.** **So a quarter of the business drove almost the whole increase, and it is the long-cycle, lumpy, execution-risk-heavy one.** **A hydropower order booked in 2026 is revenue in 2029–2031 at a margin fixed today.**

**Capital structure, and it has a share-count trap** `[sourced/computed]`. **Issued shares are 104,000,000** — the provider's page reconciles to exactly that (see gate 3). **The company's own per-share figures use the NET count: earnings per share of EUR 2.04 on net income to shareholders of EUR 200.7m implies 98.4m shares, and the 2025 dividend of EUR 2.70 totalling EUR 265.2m implies 98.2m** `[both computed]`. **So approximately 98.3m shares net, with roughly 5.7m — 5.5% — in treasury.** **Verification item 22's rule confirmed for a sixth name: reconcile on issued, value on net.** Treasury activity in H1 2026: **61,495 shares resold at EUR 31.20 and 208,495 at EUR 38.80, with 42,062 transferred to employees** `[sourced]` — **and note that those resale prices are a small fraction of the current EUR 84.50, so they are historic plan prices and not market transactions.**

**Service share of revenue was not disclosed in the H1 report** `[sourced absence]`. **For a business whose entire quality argument rests on an installed-base annuity, that is the memo's most important gap.**
