### 5. The bear case, stated at its strongest — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**Q2 order intake of MSEK 22,235 is +29% organic and gives a book-to-bill of 1.23x against 0.98x a year earlier** `[sourced and computed]`. **And in the same half: operating income MSEK 5,608 against 5,681, DOWN 1.3%; net income MSEK 3,956 against 4,028, DOWN 1.8%; EPS SEK 9.50 against 9.69, DOWN 2.0%; adjusted EBITA margin 17.5% against 17.8%** `[all computed from sourced]`.
**The honest counter, and it is not weak.** **Net debt to EBITDA of 1.11x, a 21.7% disclosed ROCE, a 19.99% computed pre-tax return, and a record order book are not the profile of a business in trouble — they are the profile of a business absorbing a large, fast, lower-margin growth opportunity.** **If Energy's orders are a structural demand shift (process electrification, data-centre thermal management, and marine decarbonisation are the obvious candidates, none of them disclosed here** `[sourced absence]`**), then a temporarily diluted margin on a much larger revenue base is a good outcome and the market is pricing it correctly at 20.9x.** **The framework's answer is that it cannot tell which, and 20.9x is not a price at which it needs to guess.**
