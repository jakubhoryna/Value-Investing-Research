### Verdict — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
Acast is a genuinely improving business and the memo should say so before it says anything else. Adjusted EBITDA has gone **−SEK 150m → −272m → −112m → +26m → +116m across 2021–2025** on net sales rising from SEK 1,026m to SEK 2,517m `[sourced, Annual Report 2025]`, with the gross margin steady at **39%** `[sourced]`. Q2 2026 was the highest-revenue quarter in its history: **SEK 775.6m, +28% reported and +29% organic, adjusted EBIT of SEK 31.6m against a SEK 7m loss a year earlier** `[sourced]`. Operating cash flow was **+SEK 50.1m against −SEK 53m** `[sourced]`. The company holds **SEK 630m of cash and no meaningful debt** `[sourced]`.
**First, gate 3 is not close.** At **SEK 35.05** the enterprise value is roughly **SEK 5.63bn against trailing-twelve-month EBIT of SEK 95m — about 59x** `[computed from sourced]`. Consensus puts it at **39.6x 2026 earnings and 24.1x 2027** `[sourced]`. Even granting the company's own **2028 target of a 10% EBIT margin** `[sourced]` and its **>15% organic growth target**, the shares trade at roughly **15x an EBIT figure three years out that has not yet been earned** `[computed from assumption]`. There is no reading of this as cheap.
**Second — and this is the finding that should be carried into any future review — the growth is almost entirely price, on a flat audience.** In Q2 2026, **listens and views grew 2% while average revenue per listen or view grew 26% to SEK 0.69** `[sourced]`. Those two compound to 28.5%, which is the reported growth `[computed]`. **Roughly 93% of Acast's revenue growth came from monetising the same audience harder, and about 7% from a larger one.** `[computed]`

### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Marginal pass on the mechanics; the competitive position is the part I cannot resolve.**

Acast is a two-sided marketplace: it hosts and distributes independent podcasts, sells advertising against them, and shares the revenue with creators. **140,000+ connected creators, 4,000+ advertisers, roughly 1bn listens and views a quarter** `[sourced]`. The unit economics are simple and the company discloses the right two variables — volume (listens and views) and price (ARPLV) — which is more than many advertising businesses do.

The economics are those of a distributor, not an owner. Gross margin is **39%** `[sourced]`, meaning roughly 61% of revenue is paid away, principally to creators. Cumulative creator payments are disclosed as **SEK 5,900m through 2025** in the annual report and as **"$690 million since launch"** in the Q2 2026 slides `[both sourced — these are not reconcilable and one is wrong; see gaps]`.

Geography, Q2 2026 `[all sourced]`:

| | Net sales | Growth (organic) | Contribution margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Europe | SEK 398m | 25% (26%) | **25%** (from 24%) |
| North America | SEK 319m | 34% (37%) | **13%** (from 10%) |
| Other markets | SEK 58m | 17% (13%) | 11% |

**The fastest-growing region earns roughly half the contribution margin of the mature one.** `[computed]` That is normal for a scaling business and it is also the reason group margin expansion will lag revenue growth for as long as mix shifts toward North America.

**What I do not understand well enough:** why an advertiser or a creator stays. Management's answer is that Acast is *"the world's largest independent pure-play podcast company"* and a *"category of one"*, and that its model *"has grown further and further from Spotify, which has made us stronger partners"* `[commentary]`. That is a positioning statement, not evidence. The independent-aggregator position is real — but it sits between creators who can leave and two vertically integrated platforms that own both audience and ad stack at far greater scale. **No churn figure, no creator-retention figure, and no contract-length disclosure was found** `[sourced absence]`. For a marketplace, those are the three numbers that establish whether there is a moat at all.

**Two years of profitability, one of them marginal.** The framework's durability gate asks for evidence of returns held through a cycle. Acast has not been profitable through a cycle; it has been profitable through an upswing. **The 2022 advertising recession is in this table, and Acast lost SEK 272m in it** — which is the honest answer to what happens to this business when advertisers cut budgets.

Reported results below adjusted EBITDA are volatile in both directions and should be treated with care: **net profit of +SEK 78.2m in 2024 against an operating loss of SEK 57.5m, and a net result of −SEK 112m in 2025 against positive adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EBIT of SEK 25m** `[sourced]`. Trailing-twelve-month net income to mid-2026 is **+SEK 104m** `[sourced, third party]`. **Three consecutive years in which the sign of the bottom line does not follow the sign of the operating result** means below-the-line items — tax assets, impairments, financial items — are doing more work than operations. None of it is explained in what was reviewed.

Until that is resolved, an invested-capital denominator cannot be constructed honestly, and **a return on capital is exactly the kind of number that should not be estimated.**

#### Financial strength
**Good, and better than the income statement implies.** Cash of SEK 630m at Q2 2026 `[sourced]`, no meaningful debt, and a consensus net cash position of about **SEK 690m** `[sourced]`. Trailing-twelve-month operating cash flow of **SEK 176m** `[sourced]` against TTM EBIT of SEK 95m — cash conversion above accounting profit, which is what a low-capital-intensity marketplace should produce and is a point in the bull case's favour.

Note the equity ratio fell from 63% to 56% in 2025 `[sourced]` and cash fell from SEK 713.7m to SEK 589.3m `[sourced]` while the business was profitable at the EBITDA line — so something consumed roughly SEK 124m of cash in a year of reported improvement. **Not explained in what was reviewed.**

#### The monetisation lever, quantified — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
The company's targets are **organic net sales CAGR above 15% from 2025 to 2028, and a 10% EBIT margin by 2028** `[sourced, financial targets announced 2025-04-07 and restated in the Annual Report 2025]`. Both are being beaten on growth — 33% organic in 2025, 29% in Q2 2026 `[sourced]`. **But if audience volume stays near flat, the 15% growth target is a commitment to raise effective advertising prices by roughly 15% a year for three more years.** That may well be achievable from a low base — podcast advertising has historically monetised below its audience share — but it is a convergence trade with a natural end, and **the memo should not treat a convergence as a growth franchise.**
