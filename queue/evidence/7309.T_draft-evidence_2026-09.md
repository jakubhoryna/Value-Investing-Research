### Verdict — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
Shimano is the dominant supplier of bicycle drivetrains and braking worldwide, with a balance sheet that is 92.5% equity and holds **¥482.8bn of net cash and investments — roughly 30% of the market capitalisation** `[sourced, computed]`. It is buying back **2.95m shares (3.4%) for up to ¥50bn** through to 31 January 2027 `[sourced]`. On the reform theme's own terms it is a textbook case: idle capital being returned, reported ROE of 3.9% that says almost nothing about the operating business `[sourced, FY2025]`.
| Bicycle Components segment | FY2019 | FY2021 (peak) | FY2025 | FY2026 guided |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales `[sourced]` | ¥290.0bn | ¥443.7bn | ¥355.0bn | ~¥355bn |
| Operating income `[sourced]` | ¥57.9bn | ¥125.2bn | ¥42.8bn | ¥39.0bn |
| Operating margin `[computed]` | **19.9%** | **28.2%** | **12.1%** | **~11.0%** |
**FY2025 bicycle sales are 22.4% higher than FY2019's, and bicycle operating income is 26.0% lower** `[computed]`. That is not operating deleverage — volume is *above* the pre-pandemic base. Something in price, mix, cost or competitive position has taken roughly eight margin points out of the business and has not given them back six years later. **Management guides that margin down again in FY2026** `[sourced]`, which is the company's own statement that it does not expect recovery next year either.

### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Pass, comfortably. This is the most explainable business analysed in the current cycle of work.**

Two segments, both simple `[sourced, FY2025]`:

| Segment | Sales | Operating income | Margin `[computed]` | Share of sales `[computed]` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bicycle Components | ¥354,972m | ¥42,841m | 12.1% | **76.1%** |
| Fishing Tackle | ¥110,832m | ¥8,865m | 8.0% | 23.8% |
| **Group** | **¥466,243m** | **¥51,677m** | **11.1%** | |

Segment operating income sums to ¥51,706m against a group figure of ¥51,677m `[computed]` — so there is **no material unallocated corporate cost**, and segment economics can be read directly as group economics. That is unusually clean and it makes normalisation arithmetic honest.

Shimano makes gears, shifters, brakes, cranks and hubs and sells them to bicycle manufacturers, who specify them on complete bikes, and to the aftermarket. The consumer sees the brand on a component they did not choose directly — the same structural position Mips occupies in helmets, at forty times the scale and with manufacturing rather than a royalty behind it. The fishing business is a genuine second franchise, not a diversification for its own sake: it shares precision metal-forming and materials know-how.

**One thing I do not understand well enough and should say so:** the extent to which Shimano's position is protected by manufacturing capability and integration with frame designers versus by patents. The moat is usually described in terms of the former, which if true is durable and hard to attack, but nothing reviewed in this run quantifies it. That gap is not fatal to gate 1 — the *business* is easy to describe — but it is central to gate 2 and is flagged there.

#### The long record is exceptional and should not be lost in the current numbers
At the FY2021 peak Shimano earned a **27.1% group operating margin** on ¥546.5bn of sales `[computed from sourced]`, with the bicycle segment at 28.2%. In the pre-pandemic year FY2019 — an ordinary year, not a peak — the bicycle segment earned **19.9%** `[computed]`. A component supplier sustaining a high-teens-to-twenty operating margin across a cycle is prima facie evidence of pricing power and scale advantage, and Shimano did that for many years.

#### Returns as reported are meaningless, and this is the analytically important point
**FY2025 ROE was 3.9%** `[sourced]`. Taken at face value that is a failing business. It is nothing of the kind. Total assets were ¥938.3bn against net assets of ¥869.5bn — an **equity ratio of 92.5%** `[sourced]` — and within that sat ¥477.3bn of cash and deposits plus ¥29.3bn of investment securities `[sourced, 31.12.2025]`. **More than half the balance sheet is not employed in the business.**

Return on the capital actually used in operations `[computed, and label it as mine]`: on FY2025 operating income of ¥51.7bn against roughly ¥431bn of assets excluding cash and investment securities, the pre-tax return on operating assets is about **12%** — against a consolidated ROE of 3.9%. On FY2019-level margins the same denominator would give roughly 16%.

**This is the goodwill fork from the previous run's memos, running in the opposite direction.** There, a good operating business sat inside an entity whose capitalisation (goodwill, brands) made the consolidated return ordinary — Alimak 22.7% ex-goodwill against 10.0% including it; LVMH 34.7% against 15.7%. Here the wedge is idle cash rather than purchase accounting, and it depresses the return by an even larger factor. **In both cases the register needs to show both numbers or the name is scored wrongly.** This is the second independent argument for the ex-goodwill / as-reported returns columns already queued as a MODEL-mode change, and it broadens it: the column pair should be *operating* return and *committed-capital* return, whichever direction the wedge runs.

#### The margin defect — stated at its strongest, because it decides the file
Set the group series out `[all sourced, margins computed]`:

| | FY2019 | FY2021 | FY2025 | FY2026 guided |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | ¥363.2bn | ¥546.5bn | ¥466.2bn | ¥467.0bn |
| Operating income | not sourced | ¥148.3bn | ¥51.7bn | ¥47.0bn |
| Operating margin | — | 27.1% | 11.1% | 10.1% |

Group sales in FY2025 were **28.4% above FY2019** `[computed]` and group operating income was **65% below the FY2021 peak** `[computed]`. The bicycle table in the verdict is the cleaner comparison because both years are sourced: **+22.4% sales, −26.0% operating income, versus 2019.**

Candidate explanations, none of which the disclosure settles `[inference]`:

- **Mix.** Growth has come at lower price points and in e-bike componentry where Shimano competes against motor specialists rather than in its traditional stronghold. Plausible and would be structural.
- **Cost base built for the boom.** Shimano added capacity through 2021–22 for demand that did not persist; the fixed cost of that capacity now sits against normal volumes. Would be cyclical and would unwind, slowly.
- **Competition.** SRAM's wireless electronic groupsets took share at the high margin end of the market during exactly this window. Would be structural and the most damaging reading.
- **Inventory and price realisation.** The company's own FY2025 commentary describes European inventories "at a somewhat high level" and Chinese retail as "lackluster" with high inventories `[sourced]`, which implies discounting into the channel.

**Management's FY2026 guidance of a further 9.1% fall in operating income on flat sales** `[sourced]` is the single most informative disclosure in the file. A company guiding margin down in year six of a "trough" is telling you it does not regard the current level as temporary. **Until that reverses, gate 2 rests on history rather than on evidence.**

#### Financial strength — unimpeachable, with one caveat that is not trivial
92.5% equity ratio, minimal interest-bearing debt, ¥506.6bn of gross cash and investment securities `[sourced]`. There is no solvency question here at all.

**The caveat: the cash is not inert.** FY2025 carried **¥19.6bn of currency losses against a ¥12.2bn gain in FY2024 — a ¥31.8bn swing** `[sourced]`, which is why ordinary income fell 52.3% while operating income fell 20.6% `[sourced]`. The same mechanism ran in reverse in Q1 FY2026: operating income **−35.6%** while net income was **+30.9%** `[sourced]`. **A shareholder in Shimano owns a bicycle-component business and a large unhedged currency position, and in recent periods the currency position has moved earnings more than the business has.** Any P/E computed on reported net income is therefore close to noise, and this memo uses operating income throughout for that reason.

#### Capital allocation — improving, and the improvement is the reform theme working
- **Buyback:** 2,950,000 shares, 3.4% of issued, up to ¥50bn, running 12 February 2026 to 31 January 2027, via ToSTNeT-3 and market purchases, for "enhancing shareholder returns, improving capital efficiency, and executing flexible capital policies" `[sourced, company announcement 2026-02-10]`. Roughly half the authorisation had been used by mid-2026 `[sourced]`.
- **Dividend:** ¥339 per share for FY2025, up ¥30, with ¥363 forecast for FY2026 `[sourced]` — **raised into a year of falling profit**, which is a deliberate signal.
- **Share count:** 86,530,000 issued with 64,521 treasury at 31 January 2026 `[sourced]`; roughly 84.9m outstanding by September 2026 on the market-cap arithmetic below `[computed]`.

This is real and it is new. It is also **modest against the size of the problem**: ¥50bn is about 10% of the net cash and investments, and one year of it. `[computed]` A company holding 30% of its market value in cash while earning 3.9% on equity has room to do considerably more, and the fact that it is now doing something is the reason the name appears in this sweep at all.

#### The moat, weighed honestly — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
**Against:** eight points of bicycle margin lost since 2019 and not recovered; a well-funded competitor that has led on the highest-margin technology transition of the past decade; **no patent, share, or price-realisation disclosure of any kind in the material reviewed** `[sourced absence]`, so the moat cannot be audited in the dimension where it is being tested.

### Open items and data gaps
- **FY2019 and FY2018 group operating income not sourced.** Only bicycle segment FY2019 (¥57,850m on ¥290,038m) and group sales (¥363,230m) were obtained. The group margin series therefore has a hole in exactly the year used as the normalisation anchor — **the segment anchor is sound, the group one is inferred from it.**
- **Direct conflict on FY2024 net income.** The FY2025 results filing implies roughly ¥76bn (net income −55.5%, EPS ¥388.17 against ¥853.36); a trade-press report of the same release states ¥98.6bn and a 65% decline. **Unresolved — the filing is the primary source and is used here; do not use the FY2024 comparative until reconciled.**
- **Market capitalisation differs by ~2% between sources** at the same price: ¥1,635.3bn (MarketScreener, ex-treasury) against ¥1.67tn (Google Finance, apparently on issued shares). The ex-treasury figure is used. Share count should be confirmed from the Q2 FY2026 filing.
- **No gross margin disclosed** in any material reviewed; the margin analysis is at operating level only, which cannot separate price from cost.
- **No market share, unit volume, or price realisation disclosure.** The competitive question at the heart of gate 2 is therefore not directly testable from company sources.
- **No patent or IP term disclosure**, the same gap flagged at Mips.
- **Interest-bearing debt described as "minimal" rather than quantified** in the extract obtained; net cash rests on the Q1 FY2026 company figure of ¥482.8bn, which is a secondary rendering of a company statement rather than a balance sheet line read directly.
- **The FY2026 half-year result (June 2026) was not obtained** — only Q1. The most recent full segment margin datapoint is therefore Q1, and a fresher one exists.
- **No Japanese-language sources were used.** Shimano's fuller disclosure, including the quarterly supplementary materials, may answer several of the questions above.
- No named third-party bear thesis was obtained. The bear case is built from the company's own segment history and guidance.

The first two are one-yen rounding. **The third arose from computing that row on ¥79.5bn while the rest of the table used ¥79.6bn** — the normalised operating income figure was rounded inconsistently within a single table. The 12x and 15x rows of that table (¥16,932 and ¥19,743) are correct as printed.

**The register row in `claude/2026-09-05_register-update-and-commit-block_v2.md` cites the 12x guided trigger as ¥12,326; read ¥12,325.** Apply both edits at the PC session.

### Why this is recorded rather than fixed by reissue
Same reasoning as the Mips errata: a full memo reissue creates a second copy to keep in sync, and here the largest error is **0.08% on a row the memo explicitly labels "shown for completeness, not as a trigger"** because it rests on an assumed margin recovery. The row that actually enters the register — the 12x guided line — is wrong by one yen.

### The point worth keeping
**The error was found because every trigger figure written today was re-derived programmatically before the run closed, and one of eighteen did not reconcile.** Trigger arithmetic is the part of a memo most likely to be read as authoritative and least likely to be re-checked by a reader, since it looks like a simple identity. **A verification pass over the trigger tables should be a standing step before any memo is committed** — it is cheap, it is mechanical, and it caught something a careful reader would not have.

That is a process recommendation for MODEL mode rather than a framework change, and it should not be bundled with a content commit.
