### Gate 1 — Do I understand it?
**Pass.**

Moncler sells luxury outerwear under two brands. FY2025 revenue €3,132.1m, of which the Moncler brand €2,720.9m (**86.9%** `[computed]`) and Stone Island €411.2m `[sourced]`. Channel mix is overwhelmingly direct: in H1 2026, Moncler DTC €933.2m against wholesale €156.4m `[sourced]`. The network is 298 Moncler and 95 Stone Island directly operated stores at 30 June 2026 `[sourced]`.

Two features must be carried through everything below.

**Seasonality is extreme and it is the market's live doubt.** H1 2026 revenue of €1,289.9m against FY2025 of €3,132.1m implies roughly **59% of the year in H2** `[computed]`, and the margin skew is worse: H1 2026 EBIT margin 19.0% against 29.2% for FY2025. On the H1 2026 call, analysts from BNP Paribas, Barclays, CLSA, Oddo BHF, Jefferies and RBC all pressed on the same theme — spring/summer availability, the economics of the S/S offer, and S/S inventory risk `[sourced]`. Six brokers converging on one question is the market telling you where it doubts the story.

**Moncler does not disclose a category split** `[sourced absence]`, so the "how dependent is this on down jackets" question — the single most obvious question about the business — **cannot be answered from disclosure at all.** That is a real criticism, not a nitpick.

#### Returns
Moncler reports **no ROIC, no ROCE and no ROE** in any document reviewed `[sourced absence]`. The following are mine, on the company's own invested-capital definition and a 29.4% FY2025 effective tax rate `[computed from sourced]`:

| Return on year-end invested capital | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Incl. goodwill and brands, incl. leases | **19.9%** | **18.2%** |
| Excl. goodwill and brands, incl. leases | 39.4% | 33.3% |
| Incl. goodwill and brands, excl. leases | 27.0% | 25.6% |
| ROE on year-end equity | 17.8% | **16.3%** |

**Methodological caveat, stated plainly:** the "excl. leases" rows remove right-of-use assets from the capital base *without* restating EBIT for the rent that IFRS 16 reclassified into depreciation and interest. They overstate the lease-free return and should be read as an upper bound. The rent figure needed to do this properly is not disclosed.

**The 18.2% including goodwill and brands is the number the framework should use, and it is genuinely good.** The decay from 19.9% is the concern, not the level.

#### Pricing power — the strongest evidence in the file
| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin `[sourced]` | 77.1% | 78.1% | 78.1% | **77.2%** (H1 2025: 76.9%) |
| EBIT margin `[sourced]` | 30.0% | 29.5% | 29.2% | 19.0% (seasonal) |

Held, and improving at the gross line in the trough. Santel on 2027 pricing: *"low- to mid-single-digit"* `[commentary]`. Stone Island's +11% constant-currency growth in H1 2026 was achieved with price increases *"about low single digit"* `[commentary]` — i.e. the second brand grows on volume, not price, so the group pricing evidence belongs to the Moncler brand.

#### Financial strength — read this carefully, the headline is misleading
Moncler's headline "net cash" **excludes lease liabilities** `[sourced]`. On an all-in basis:

| €m | 31.12.2023 | 31.12.2024 | 31.12.2025 | 30.06.2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net cash excl. leases `[sourced]` | 1,033.7 | 1,308.8 | 1,458.0 | 1,112.4 |
| Lease liabilities `[sourced]` | (805.2) | (924.1) | (1,109.1) | (1,198.6) |
| **All-in net position** `[computed]` | +228.5 | +384.7 | +348.9 | **−86.2** |

**The group swung to an all-in net debt position of €86.2m at 30 June 2026 — the first negative reading in the period.** Part of that is seasonal (the €374.1m FY2025 dividend was paid in H1), and the comparable figure at 30 June 2025 was +€40.0m. But the trend is clear: **lease liabilities grew 48.9% between end-2023 and mid-2026** `[computed]` while revenue grew 1% in FY2025. The "fortress balance sheet with over a billion of net cash" framing is a definitional artefact — it is true only if a €1.2bn contractual obligation to landlords is set aside.

This is not a solvency issue. It is a reminder that the store-expansion programme is being financed off balance sheet and that the capital base is growing faster than the profit.

#### Capital allocation
Simple, and the simplicity is both strength and problem. Free cash flow on the company's definition was €549.4m, €587.5m and €529.0m in FY2023–25 `[sourced]`. It goes to capex (6–7% of revenue), a rising dividend (€303.4m → €311.0m → €353.2m paid, and €374.1m in H1 2026), and cash accumulation.

**No buyback has been executed** `[sourced absence]` — only a standing 10% AGM authorisation with no cancellation contemplated. Treasury shares actually *fell* from 1.2% to ~1.0% between February and July 2026, consistent with shares being delivered out under share plans. **No M&A since the Stone Island completion in 2021** `[sourced]`.

Two things a reader should not miss. First, roughly €424m of cash accumulated on the balance sheet between end-2023 and end-2025 `[computed]` earning a return that was **negative at the EBIT line in FY2025** — net financial expenses of €26.2m against €6.5m in FY2024 `[sourced]`. Second, **the headlined free cash flow is struck before lease principal repayments**, which ran at €120.2m in H1 2026 alone `[sourced]` — call it ~€240m annualised `[computed]`, a real cash claim that never appears in the reclassified statement.

#### The Stone Island problem, quantified from disclosure — *sourced lines and tables only; verdict prose dropped*
Moncler paid €805m for 70% of Sportswear Company in December 2020 and €345m for the remaining 30% in February 2021 — an implied 100% enterprise value of **€1,150m on stated pre-actual 2020 EBITDA of €68m, i.e. 16.9x** `[sourced, computed]`.
**And the impairment disclosure is the number to carry forward.** The group tests Moncler and Stone Island as separate CGUs at WACCs of 8.5% and 9.0% respectively, with g = 2.5%. The sensitivity states that the Moncler brand and goodwill *"are confirmed in all scenarios of reasonable changes"*, while **Stone Island's brand and goodwill are supported only up to WACC of 9.6% and 9.8%** `[sourced]`. That is **60–80bps of headroom** on a business whose network growth has halted. Goodwill and brands together are €1,602.8m, or **43.9% of shareholders' equity** `[computed]`, and the split between the two CGUs **is not disclosed** `[sourced absence]`.

### Governance — an item the queue did not flag and should have
This is not a standard shareholder register and a memo must say so.

Remo Ruffini controls Moncler through Double R S.r.l., which holds **18.227%** of Moncler. Double R is **78.055% owned by Ruffini Partecipazioni Holding and 21.945% by White Investissement SAS, a wholly-owned LVMH vehicle** `[sourced, Consob shareholders'-agreement disclosure, data at 31.12.2025]`. Ruffini's look-through economic interest in Moncler is therefore roughly **14.3%**; LVMH's is roughly **4.0%** `[computed]`.

The transaction was announced in September 2024 and **completed in September 2025** `[sourced]` — note this corrects a common framing of it as a 2026 event. **LVMH did not buy Moncler shares. It bought into the founder's control vehicle and funded that vehicle's purchases of Moncler stock from ~15.8% to 18.2%**, so the control block grew without Ruffini putting up more capital.

The governance terms are not those of a passive minority `[all sourced from the Consob disclosure]`: LVMH designates one director on the Moncler board and two on Double R's; holds vetoes over Double R distributing less than its maximum distributable amount, over related-party transactions above €100,000 a year, and over any "Reserved Business Combination" with a Direct Competitor — a veto that reaches through to how Double R's 18.2% is voted at Moncler's own AGM; holds a right of first refusal if Double R sells Moncler shares outside the LVMH group and over RPH's own Double R interest; and holds a special withdrawal right triggered if **Remo Ruffini ceases to be CEO or executive chairman**.

**In substance, LVMH has bought a call on the succession, for a price that has never been publicly disclosed** `[sourced absence — the LVMH release says only that terms would be disclosed as required by law, and no euro figure appears in the Consob document or in Il Sole 24 Ore, MarketScreener or FashionNetwork coverage]`.

Two dates belong in the calendar: the **initial three-year term and lock-up expire 26 September 2027**, with renewal discussions required to open by late March 2027, and non-renewal is itself a withdrawal trigger. And the **standstill caps the concert party at 20% of voting capital against a current 18.2%** — very little room to buy, so any move above that line would be a deliberate negotiated act.

One live question: Moncler announced on 20 January 2026 that **Bartolomeo Rongone would become CEO**, effective April 2026 `[sourced]`. Whether Ruffini's post-2026 title satisfies the agreement's "executive chairman" condition **could not be sourced**. That is the first question to put to the company.

Italy's loyalty/multiple-voting regime **does not apply** — the bylaws in force from 20 March 2025 state at Article 5.4 that *"Each share gives the right to one vote"* `[sourced]`. Ruffini's control is economic and contractual, with no dual-class insulation.
