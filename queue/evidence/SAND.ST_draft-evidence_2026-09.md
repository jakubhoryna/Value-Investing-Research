**The test the screen set, and the answer.**

**RHI Magnesita's memo established the mechanism: refractories are consumed as steel is made, so the supplier is paid out of the customer's PRODUCTION budget, which is far more stable than the customer's CAPEX budget.** **The memo then failed RHI on a 10.3% adjusted EBITA margin, 2.9x net debt to adjusted EBITDA, and a customer base 71% steel** `[all carried]`. **The open question was whether the mechanism failed or the name did.**

**Sandvik answers it: the mechanism works.** **The one segment that is purely consumable — Machining — earns 28.7%, eight percentage points above the equipment segment and fourteen above rock processing, and produces nearly half the group's segment profit off two fifths of its revenue** `[computed]`. **And the equipment segments are themselves majority-aftermarket: Mining at 66% and Rock Processing at 59% of revenues** `[sourced]`. **On revenue mix, Sandvik is not principally a capital-goods company at all; it is a consumables and aftermarket company with an equipment business attached.** `[inference]`

**Two things follow, and the second is a correction to the register.**

**First, RHI's verdict stands and its reasoning should be amended.** **RHI failed on leverage and on the returns its own customer mix permits, not because consumables are a weak structure.** **The register note on that row should say so, or a future reader will draw the wrong general lesson from a correct specific verdict.**

**Second, the Bucket K sweep's sub-sector map needs a row it does not have.** The map ranked intralogistics systems and capital-goods-machinery-with-service above component and truck layers `[carried]`. **It has no CONSUMABLES layer, and on this evidence a consumables layer would sit at or above the top of it.** **That is a real gap in the map and it was invisible until a name with both layers inside it was measured.** `[inference]`

**Gate 1 verdict: PASS, with the caveat stated.** **Sandvik is understandable — three areas, all metallurgical, all disclosed with revenue and margin.** **But mining capex and industrial metal-cutting are two unrelated cycles, and every group-level figure in this memo averages them.** **A 19.6% group return could be a 25% machining business and a 15% mining business, or the reverse, and the disclosure given here does not separate invested capital by segment** `[sourced absence]`. **That is the single largest analytical gap on this row.**

**Gate 2 verdict: PASS, with three honest qualifications and none of them fatal.**

**One — the numerator is adjusted, and Sandvik's adjustment is large.** **Adjusted EBITA excludes both amortisation of acquired intangibles and items management designates as adjusting.** **Q2 reported EBIT was SEK 7,234m against adjusted EBITA of SEK 8,306m — a 12.9% wedge in a single quarter** `[computed]`. **No LTM reported-EBIT series was obtainable from the interim report extract, so the strict-numerator return that this corpus computed for GEA could not be computed here.** **That is a genuine measurement gap and it means Sandvik's 19.6% is NOT on the same basis as GEA's 27.5%.** **The two must not be ranked against each other in a register table until both are on a reported numerator.** **This is MODEL question 8's numerator half, and it is the reason the register needs the two-column schema rather than the resolution of a single basis.**

**Two — intangibles are 50.4% of invested capital.** That places Sandvik near the middle of the eight-name goodwill-intensity spectrum this corpus built today: **Hexagon 90.4%, Konecranes 75.8%, Andritz 74.5%, Sandvik 50.4%, Cognex 43.6%, Kalmar 35.0%, RHI Magnesita 33.6%, Daifuku 0.6%, Harmonic Drive nil** `[carried, and this row added]`. **Consistent with the corrected note (cc): the wedge tracks acquisition history, and Sandvik has been an acquirer for a century.**

**Three — the disclosed 17.5% could not be replicated because the definition was not extracted.** **This is the third name in a row where a disclosed return's footnote decides its comparability, and the second today where the footnote was the single most valuable line in the document** (GEA's ROCE excludes only a 1999 goodwill block, not all goodwill — a fact that changes how that number reads). **Record as an unfinished item, not as a pass.**
