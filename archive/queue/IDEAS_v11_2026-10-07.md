**R5 in plain words (for Jakub).** When a listed company controls another listed company (Roche and Chugai, Unilever and Hindustan Unilever, Siemens and Healthineers, Christian Dior and LVMH), each business is looked at on its own: the parent's own businesses in the parent's memo, the subsidiary in the subsidiary's memo. Returns come from the group accounts, which already add the two together, so they are the average of the two weighted by the capital each uses. Competitive position is graded for each business and the grades are averaged, weighted by the operating income each earns. Price is judged on the whole group: the group accounts count all of the subsidiary's profit, but outside shareholders own part of it, so the market value of their slice (or its book value, if higher) is added to enterprise value. The stake is never credited at its share price, and the group is never valued on NAV.
