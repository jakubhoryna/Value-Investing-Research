# 2330.TW — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | STRONG | ADEQUATE | PASS | EXPENSIVE | **WATCH** |

TSMC is the best-positioned manufacturing business in the world: the dominant leading-edge foundry, earning 21–36% returns on capital every year for a decade while compounding per-share operating income ~20%/yr, run by managers who reinvest at high rates and return the rest through a growing dividend. The only thing between here and a BUY is price — at ~NT$2,490 it trades at roughly 4× the level at which this framework would call it cheap. Nothing about the business needs to change; the price does. The verdict flips to BUY only on a severe, broad market dislocation (or a Taiwan-risk repricing) that drags the stock toward the trigger band — which is precisely why TSMC belongs on the downturn shopping list, not in the AVOID bin.

## 2. Understanding

TSMC is a Taiwan-incorporated, Taiwan-listed (TWSE:2330; US ADRs on NYSE) contract semiconductor **foundry** — it manufactures integrated circuits to order for fabless chip designers and IDMs (Apple, Nvidia, AMD, Qualcomm, MediaTek, Broadcom and hundreds of others), does **not** sell its own branded chips, operates advanced and specialty wafer fabrication plus advanced packaging, earns revenue globally (mostly from North-America-headquartered customers) with manufacturing concentrated in Taiwan and expanding in the US, Japan and Germany, and is the clear market leader in leading-edge logic, growing. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

A decade of exceptional, consistent returns. ROCE (with cash, the conservative denominator) ran 21.5%–36.3% every year 2016–2025; on an ex-cash basis (where the breakout exists, 2022–2025) it was 38–64%. FCFROCE was 7–19%, dipping only in heavy-capex years (2019, 2023). Per share, operating income compounded ~19.9%/yr, free cash flow ~18.9%/yr and tangible book ~16.5%/yr, all on a share count essentially flat at ~25.93bn for ten years. Leverage never exceeded ~73% liabilities/equity (peak reflects bond issuance + IFRS-16 leases) — far below the 200% flag. **STRONG.**

## 4. Quality — future

The moat is among the deepest anywhere, sourced from **cost/scale** (largest foundry, ~90%+ of leading-edge, funding the industry's highest R&D and capex, compounding a yield/learning advantage), **switching costs** (designs are co-optimised to TSMC's process design kits; re-porting is slow and risky) and **ingrainedness** (embedded in customer roadmaps and IP ecosystems) — and it is **stable-to-strengthening** as TSMC's 3nm/2nm lead widens over Samsung and Intel. Market growth is YES (AI/leading-edge demand), treated as support, not substitute, for the moat.

The breadth test mechanically **fails**: the customer base is concentrated and consolidating (ten largest = 78% of 2025 revenue, up from 70% in 2023; largest 19%, second 17%), and a critical supplier (ASML for EUV) is a monopoly. Forces therefore tilt unfavourable on bargaining power, though substitutes and new entrants are LOW (capital intensity >NT$1T/yr and decades of know-how are near-insurmountable). Applying the **moat-decides rule** (as for ASML/TROW/BKE): concentration does not translate into realised pricing power — TSMC has been raising prices into 50–62% gross margins — so the dominant, durable moat floors Future at **ADEQUATE** rather than WEAK. *(Key judgment call — see Appendix C.)*

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

All four tests pass. **Compensation:** the executive-officer group received ~NT$9.1bn (~US$289m) in 2025 — 0.47% of operating income — performance-linked and committee-reviewed. **Ownership/control:** single share class, one-share-one-vote, no dual-class; no controlling holder (founder retired 2018); largest holder is Taiwan's National Development Fund at 6.38% (flagged — potential state influence, but identical voting rights). **Buybacks:** effectively none (a token 3.2m-share repurchase in 2024, ~0.01%, then cancelled); no option-dilution sterilisation. **Capital allocation:** rational — retain to reinvest at 20%+ ROCE, distribute the rest via a growing dividend (DPS NT$11→24 over 2021–2025, ~25–33% payout); no empire-building M&A. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- **Geopolitical/single-jurisdiction:** manufacturing concentrated in Taiwan; a Taiwan-Strait conflict or blockade is an existential tail risk that no multiple can price away.
- **Customer concentration:** top-10 at 78% and rising; loss of, or in-sourcing by, the largest one or two customers (e.g. Apple/Nvidia) would dent revenue and pricing.
- **Process-leadership loss:** the entire moat rests on staying one-to-two nodes ahead; a sustained Intel or Samsung catch-up (or a TSMC stumble at 2nm/A16) would erode it.
- **Capex/cyclicality:** structurally enormous capex makes FCF lumpy and the stock cyclical; a demand air-pocket compresses returns.
- **Valuation/sentiment:** priced for sustained AI-driven growth; any disappointment de-rates a 32× EV/OI stock sharply.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Flips to **BUY** only if price falls toward ≈ NT$600 (EV/OI) on a broad market dislocation or Taiwan-risk repricing — then a full REVIEW before any action, since the trigger ages as fundamentals move. Flips toward **AVOID** only on a durable loss of process leadership (moat erosion). Next review: **FY2026 results (Feb 2027)**, or price < NT$700 (NEAR band), or any of the named thesis-breakers.
