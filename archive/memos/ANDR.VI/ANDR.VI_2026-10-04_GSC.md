# ANDR.VI — ANDRITZ AG: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past ADEQUATE, Future ADEQUATE, shareholder-friendliness PASS, cheapness EXPENSIVE: **WATCH**.

ANDRITZ is worth owning for a customer-funded capital-goods franchise: customers prepay about €2.2bn of a €12.6bn backlog, so the group runs on net cash, and 44% of FY2025 revenue was service on its installed base of mills, turbines and processing lines. Its returns are middling. Worst-of-four ROCE is 10.9% and FCFROCE 8.0%, because operating margins sit around 7%. The price is far away: 18.0× FCF and 13.7–15.4× EV/OI against a band of €33.5–36.9. A BUY needs the price to fall by more than half at unchanged earnings, or a lasting step-up in free cash flow and margins.

## 2. Understanding
ANDRITZ designs, builds and services process equipment: complete pulp mills, paper and tissue machines and their spare parts, machine clothing and rebuilds (Pulp & Paper, 38% of FY2025 revenue); hydro turbines, generators and pumped-storage equipment and their modernisation (Hydropower, 22%); metal-forming presses and strip-processing lines (Metals, 21%); and solid/liquid separation, boilers, flue-gas cleaning and pumps (Environment & Energy, 19%). The customers who pay are pulp and paper producers, utilities and state power companies, steel, aluminium and automotive manufacturers, and municipal and industrial plant owners. It is an Austrian public company headquartered in Graz that sells worldwide, and it is a long-established global leader of large size. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**ADEQUATE.** Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **10.9%** (end of period, with cash); the cash-stripped reads are 17.1–18.2%. Worst FCFROCE is **8.0%**. Returns troughed in FY2019, at 5.6%, the year after the Xerium deal, and have since recovered to 13–15%. The halves are 8.3% (FY2016–20) and 13.2% (FY2021–25), so there is no sustained decline. OI/share compounded 5.2% and BV/share 8.0% over the decade. FCF is lumpy because it follows the advance-payment cycle. L/E is 251%, but the excess over 200% is customer advances and accruals: interest-bearing debt and leases are 32% of equity, and the group holds net cash. That is ruled within P6 (Appendix C).

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth **PASSES**. The filing states that no single customer reaches 10% of revenue, and simple components come from qualified suppliers with procurement alternatives. Customer power is **STRONG** in every project business: a few large buyers tender a few large orders, and pulp and steel are consolidating. Metals also faces **STRONG** new entrants, from low-cost local competitors, which shows in its 4.5% margin. Supplier power and substitutes are WEAK. Pulp & Paper, Hydropower and Environment & Energy therefore carry one strong force each. Metals carries two but earned only 12% of FY2025 segment EBITA. The moat is **switching costs**: OEM spares, rebuilds and service on proprietary installed equipment. It is stable. Market growth is MIXED: pulp capital spending is soft and hydro and pumped storage are growing. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
Pay is modest. The FY2025 top total was €4.20m, paid to a former board member, against a €22.7m fiscal-year threshold and €18.6m normalized; the CEO received €2.61m. The supervisory-board chair's fee is €60k plus meeting fees. Custos (a foundation co-founded by Wolfgang Leitner, the chairman) and his Cerberus hold 31.5%. The only related-party dealings are de minimis (corporate-jet hire €414k). Capital allocation is **SOUND**. The 2024 buyback ran at 7.4–9.5× EV/OI. Dividends take about 50–58% of earnings and are funded from FCF. The FY2025 bolt-ons cost €500m, are INDETERMINATE on price, and are the named re-test. **PASS.**

- The H1 2026 order surge (+25%) came mostly from Hydropower: long-cycle, fixed-price work with execution risk years out.
- Metals runs a 4.5% reported margin, with non-operating charges in FY2024, FY2025 and H1 2026; its weakness is one of the reasons ROCE sits near the floor.
- The 2025 acquisitions contributed negative EBIT in their first months; if that persists, the €500m becomes value-destructive M&A.
- Pulp-capex consolidation strengthens buyers further and could compress the capital business's margins.
