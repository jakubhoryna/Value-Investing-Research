# ESSITY-B.ST — Essity Aktiebolag (publ): GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | ADEQUATE | ADEQUATE | **FAIL (P19)** | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** |

Essity owns two global number-one franchises — TENA in incontinence, Tork in away-from-home hygiene — plus a real wound-care business. Returns are unremarkable rather than bad: worst-of-four ten-year ROCE of 10.3% clears the ADEQUATE floor by three-tenths of a point and is nowhere near STRONG. Weak free-cash conversion is what convicts it: the shares screen at 22× owner earnings against 12× on the sage metric, and both completed repurchase programmes bought stock at the first number, the FY2024 tranche breaching 20× on both its reads. One hard strike is one FAIL. Price is a second, independent problem — the band sits roughly 55% below the market — but the deciding gate is capital allocation, and a flip needs a repurchase year executed at or below the flag lines, not a lower share price.

## 2. Understanding

Essity is a Stockholm-listed, dual-class, vertically integrated manufacturer and marketer of branded hygiene and health products — incontinence, feminine and baby care, medical and wound-care solutions, consumer tissue, and professional hygiene systems — sold under owned brands (TENA, Tork, Libresse, Leukoplast, JOBST, Zewa) to retailers, pharmacies, distributors and healthcare buyers across some 150 countries from roughly 70 plants, as the hygiene half of the 2017 SCA split under AB Industrivärden's voting control. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ten-year ROCE is **10.3%** (end-of-period, with cash) against 11.4% on the most flattering read — clearing P3's 10% floor, failing P2's 15% badly. Worst-of-four FCFROCE is **6.5%** all-capex, 8.1% on the disclosed maintenance basis, so the 8% STRONG condition fails on the conservative read. No sustained decline: 2022 was the pulp-inflation trough at 6.1%, 2024–25 back to 12.6–14.9%. Per-share growth is positive (ΔOI/sh +8.6%, ΔFCF/sh +3.2% geometric); L/E has fallen from 225% in 2016 to **94.7%**. **ADEQUATE** — STRONG is unavailable on returns, not record length.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both sides, no waiver: no customer above 10% of sales in any of the last three years, the largest at 4.6% and the top ten at 24.9%; a broad commodity supplier base with no disclosed dependency. Forces: **customer power STRONG** — 54% of sales through a retail channel the company itself flags as consolidating, private label a live backward-integration route, near-zero switching costs; supplier power WEAK; substitutes WEAK; new entrants WEAK at group level, with a logged Consumer Tissue exception (32% of sales, commodity economics). One strong force, contained. Moat **Brand**, stable — strengthening in Health & Medical, eroding in tissue. Market growth MIXED. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation is clean and not close: the CEO's annualised SEK 42.7m is **0.51%** of the lower leg (FCF SEK 8,356m) against a 5% strike, and ~US$4.4m against a ~US$40m trigger; the chairman's SEK 3.40m sits at the Swedish norm. Related-party dealings are ordinary-course JV purchases at 2.5% of COGS. Dividends have risen every year since listing at a 48% payout. The buybacks strike: FY2024 at SEK 300.62 and FY2025 at SEK 265.64 imply year-read MCap/FCF of 21.6× and 21.9×, and FY2024 reads **23.8×** normalized — above 20× on both reads of one basis. **FAIL — P19 hard strike.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Consumer Tissue, 32% of sales, is under strategic review from 6 May 2026 — separation reshapes returns, capital base and force tally either way.
- Free-cash conversion is the structural flaw: FY2025 operating income of SEK 18.5bn became SEK 8.4bn of all-capex FCF, and that ratio drives every P19 breach.
- Retail consolidation and private label press directly on tissue and personal-care pricing; Q2 2026 already showed tissue price and volume decline.
- Pulp, recovered paper and energy costs are volatile and passed through with a lag; management flagged renewed input inflation in Q2 2026.
- Dual-class control (Industrivärden, 29.5% of votes on 11.4% of capital) is a documented caveat, a strike only if paired with value-destructive allocation.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

A lower price clears cheapness, not the strike. It sinks deeper into AVOID if worst-of-four ROCE slips under 10% at the next window roll, where the margin is three-tenths of a point. **Maintenance-capex basis — the one live judgment call.** Essity discloses an annual split of capital expenditure between "Measures to raise the capacity level of operations (Strategic)" and "Measures to uphold capacity level (Current)". Section 2·1A directs that a company-disclosed maintenance-capex breakout be used where one exists; P20's constructed basis is the fallback where none does, and P20 is not active here because there is no capex super-cycle. P20's *effects* clause nonetheless supplies the project's settled handling of a maintenance basis — valuation gate on the maintenance figure, quality gates on all-capex — and that split treatment is what this memo applies, with the strict all-capex band stated alongside. FY2025: all-capex FCF = CFO 15,459 − capex 7,103 = SEK 8,356m; maintenance-basis FCF = 15,459 − 4,937 = SEK 10,522m, a 26% difference. Corroboration that the disclosed "current" figure is a genuine maintenance level: at SEK 4,937m it sits close to plausible property, plant and equipment depreciation, given that total depreciation, amortisation and impairment of SEK 7,157m includes SEK 972m of acquisition-related intangible amortisation and SEK 453m of capitalised selling-expense depreciation. **The basis choice is not verdict-bearing.** Past is ADEQUATE either way (the ROCE test, which decides it, is unaffected); cheapness is EXPENSIVE either way; and P19 strikes on both bases by different routes (E below). It does move three numbers, which is why both are printed throughout: worst-of-four FCFROCE (6.54% vs 8.07%), the band (SEK 99 vs SEK 124), and the FY2024 normalized P19 read (23.84× vs 19.94×).

**Vinda basis sensitivity.** Removing SEK 32,327m — the FY2023 assets-held-for-sale figure, used as a proxy for Vinda's asset base — from the FY2021 and FY2022 capital employed raises the worst-of-four ten-year ROCE from 10.32% to **10.86%**. The distortion therefore runs against the company, and the ADEQUATE call is robust in the conservative direction. No adjustment is made in the tables; the reported basis stands.

**Bias check.** None live. Two were watched and cleared. *Peculiarity*: the disclosed maintenance-capex split is unusual for a European staples filer and could invite over-reliance because it flatters the numbers — handled by printing both bases at every point of use and confirming the verdict is invariant. *Miscontrast*: at 12× EV/OI Essity looks cheap against consumer-staples peers on 15–20×, which is a comparison to the wrong yardstick; the framework's thresholds are 7× and 8×, and peer relativity was excluded from every gate.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customer.** PASS. Note B2 of the FY2025 annual report states that Essity had no customer in 2025, 2024 or 2023 generating more than 10% of net sales; the single largest customer accounted for 4.6% (4.4; 4.4) and the ten largest for 24.9% (24.3; 25.8). The disclosure sentence P15 looks for is present and explicit. The company separately discloses a consolidation trend among retailers and distributors through purchasing alliances and mergers, which "could increase dependence on individual, large customers" — that concern is carried into the customer-power force call rather than treated as a present breadth failure. No waiver used.

**Breadth — supplier.** PASS. Principal inputs are pulp, recovered paper, superabsorbent polymers, nonwovens and energy — traded commodity markets with many vendors, in which Essity is a scale buyer and forward integration into branded hygiene is implausible. The company describes itself as dependent on "a large number of suppliers"; no single supplier is disclosed at or near 10% of COGS plus operating expenses. The largest identified related-party purchase relationships (Uni-Charm Mölnlycke SEK 1,246m, ProNARO SEK 670m, Bunzl & Biach SEK 338m) are immaterial against COGS of SEK 92,351m. Looking through to the effective supplier does not change the answer: pulp is fungible and Essity is partly integrated into recovered fibre.

**Forces — four binary calls.**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | 54% of sales run through a retail channel the company itself flags as consolidating via purchasing alliances and mergers; private label is a direct backward-integration route in tissue and increasingly in personal care; retailer switching costs are near zero; public tendering adds a second powerful buyer bloc in the 20% healthcare channel. |
| Supplier bargaining power | WEAK | Pulp, recovered paper, superabsorbents, nonwovens and energy are commodity markets with many vendors; Essity buys at scale; forward integration into branded hygiene is implausible. |
| Threat of substitutes | WEAK | No wholly-different-function substitute exists for incontinence care, wound care or away-from-home hygiene, and doing without is not viable in the core categories; the reusable threat in feminine care is marginal and partly internalised through Knix and Modibodi. |
| Threat of new entrants | WEAK (group) | Regulatory and clinical barriers in Health & Medical (20% of sales), an installed dispenser base and distributor network in Professional Hygiene (26%), and brand plus scale in branded Personal Care (~22%) cover 68% of sales. **Logged segment exception:** Consumer Tissue (32% of sales) has low barriers — capital is the only real one, regional converters and private label compete effectively, and the segment's chronic margin shortfall is precisely why it is now under strategic review. Called WEAK at group level because the exposed segment is a minority of sales and a smaller minority of EBITA. |

**Tally: 1 STRONG, contained** → judgment zone (P16), consistent with ADEQUATE given a plausible moat.

**Moat.** Single identified source: **Brand**. TENA holds the global number-one position in incontinence products, sold into both retail and institutional healthcare where clinical familiarity and procurement relationships compound the brand; Tork is the global number one in away-from-home hygiene, where an installed base of proprietary dispensers converts brand into recurring consumable pull; Leukoplast, JOBST, Cutimed and Actimove carry the same effect into wound care and compression. Direction: **stable** — strengthening in Health & Medical and Personal Care, where the FY2025 and Q2 2026 disclosures show growth in Incontinence Retail, Feminine Care and Medical Solutions, and eroding in Consumer Tissue, where Q2 2026 saw both price and volume decline. The moat is real but not dominant; it does not extend across a third of the revenue base.
