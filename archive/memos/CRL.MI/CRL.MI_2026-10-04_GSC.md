## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past ADEQUATE; Future ADEQUATE; SF PASS (one flag — capital-allocation CAUTION); price EXPENSIVE — verdict **WATCH** on price.

CAREL sells the controllers, sensors and humidifiers that HVAC and refrigeration equipment makers design into their units. No customer reaches 5% of revenue, the controlling families pay themselves modestly, and the decade compounded operating income per share at 9.0% a year with leverage now low. Price is the only obstacle, and it is a large one: the stock trades at 33× free cash flow and about 40× operating income, so the bargain line sits near €4.66 against €28.55. Two things would weaken the case even at a lower price: acquisitions have pulled returns down from the mid-20s to the low teens, and the 2023 Kiona deal cost at least 49× the target's adjusted EBITDA, financed by a €200m rights issue. A BUY needs the price to fall about 84%, or operating income to grow about sixfold at today's price, with acquisition discipline holding through the Kiona put settlement.

## 2. Understanding

CAREL Industries S.p.A. — founded 1973 near Padua, listed in Milan since June 2018 and controlled through a voting syndicate of the Rossi Luciani and Nalini family holding companies, with €629m of revenue in 2025 — designs, makes and sells electronic controllers, sensors, valves, humidifiers and related software (including Kiona's building-energy SaaS) that original-equipment makers of air-conditioning, heat-pump, data-centre-cooling and commercial-refrigeration units, plus dealers, contractors and food retailers, build into their systems; HVAC is 72% of revenue and Europe, the Middle East and Africa 64%, with production in Italy, China, the United States, Croatia, Germany, Poland and Brazil. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 13.9% and FCFROCE 9.6% (both end-of-period, cash-inclusive); cash-exclusive reads run 18–21% and 12–15%. The level clears 10% but not 15%. Returns stepped down twice on acquisitions: from 22–24% (2016–17) to 14–16% after the 2018 deals, and to 9–11% after Kiona in 2023, during the 2024 heat-pump destocking. FCFROCE does not trend down, and H1 2026 operating income rose 68%; the step-downs are logged as acquisition dilution plus one cyclical trough, not a sustained decline (App. C). Per-share growth beats the floor: ΔOI/sh +9.0%, ΔFCF/sh +16.9% geometric. L/E is 80.7%. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: no customer reaches 5% of revenue; electronic and mechanical inputs come from a broad base of global distributors and manufacturers. Forces: customer power WEAK — fragmented OEMs that co-develop designed-in controls; supplier power WEAK — standard components, multi-sourced; substitutes WEAK — efficiency rules and the shift to natural refrigerants raise electronic-control content; new entrants WEAK — the stated pressure from large groups has been the same wording since 2017 while operating margins held at 13–17%. Zero strong. Moat: switching costs in designed-in controls, stable but re-contested at each OEM platform redesign against larger international groups. Market growth YES. **ADEQUATE.**
