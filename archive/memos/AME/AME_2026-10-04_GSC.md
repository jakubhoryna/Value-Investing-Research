## 2. Understanding
AMETEK designs and makes differentiated electronic instruments (66% of FY2025 sales: process, analytical, aerospace, power and test instruments) and electromechanical products (34%: precision motion, medical components, specialty metals, interconnects, aerospace MRO). Its customers are industrial OEMs, plant operators, laboratories, utilities, medical-device and aerospace-defence firms in many niche industries. It is a decentralised, acquisition-built holding of operating businesses headquartered in Pennsylvania, with roughly half its sales outside the US. It is a 95-year-old NYSE-listed company with $7.4bn of FY2025 sales, about $9bn annualised after the August 2026 Indicor Instrumentation purchase. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**ADEQUATE.** The worst-of-four average ROCE is 12.6% (end-of-period, cash in) and the best is 14.5%. Worst FCFROCE is 11.0%. ROCE clears the adequate line but misses the strong line. The reason is the purchase price of a decade of acquisitions: goodwill and intangibles exceed equity in every year, so tangible book is negative throughout. Returns were flat across the window (≈12–13% in both halves), with no decline. Per-share growth is well above the floor: geometric OI/share +8.2%, FCF/share +11.2%, BV/share +13.1%. Liabilities to equity were 51% at FY2025 and 47% at 30 June 2026, about 92% pro-forma for the $5.0bn debt-funded Indicor close. Both readings are well inside the flag level.

## 4. Quality — future
**STRONG.** Breadth passes on both sides. EIG's five largest customers take about 4% of its sales and no single customer more than 2%. EMG's five largest take about 15%, with none above 5%. Suppliers are multiple, with no concentration disclosed. All four forces are weak in both segments. Instruments are a small, specified and qualified line item for the buyer, and aerospace and medical approvals block both substitutes and entrants. The moat is switching costs from spec-in, certification and installed-base aftermarket, and it is stable: group operating margin stayed between 20.6% and 25.9% in every year of the window, including 22.6% in 2020. Markets are growing: aerospace, medtech, power grid and semiconductor test.
