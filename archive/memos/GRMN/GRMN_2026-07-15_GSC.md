Garmin is a genuinely excellent operating business — debt-free, founder-aligned, earning 17%+ on capital even with a $4.1B securities hoard weighing down the denominator, a decade of double-digit per-share compounding, and a defensible brand across outdoor, aviation, and marine. It fails on two independent counts. Decisively: across FY2024–25 it repurchased stock at ≈ $199 average, i.e. ≈ 28× MCap/FCF and 18–22× EV/OI — a P19 hard strike (framework flags > 16×/14×, strikes > 20×). One hard strike fails Gate 1C regardless of the rest. Separately, at $241.64 the stock trades at 34× FCF / 23–25× EV/OI — deeply EXPENSIVE. Absent the buyback strike this would be a WATCH, but a WATCH sitting ~330% above its ≈ $56 trigger. To flip: Garmin ceasing above-worth repurchases (SF → PASS) and price collapsing toward the mid-$50s–$70s. Neither is close.

Materiality caveat, plainly: the offending buyback is small — ≈ $181M in FY2025 against $1.36B FCF and $664M dividends. A holistic read could call it a P19 *flag* (heightened scrutiny) rather than a standalone strike, which without a second negative would leave SF PASS → WATCH. But the framework strikes at > 20× on any basis, and MCap/FCF at 28× is not borderline; the mechanical verdict is FAIL. Flagged for your judgment.

## 2. Understanding

Garmin designs, vertically manufactures (Taiwan, US, EU, China), and sells GPS-enabled hardware plus tethered software/services — fitness wearables, outdoor/adventure watches and satellite comms, marine electronics, certified aviation avionics, and auto-OEM embedded systems — to consumers through dealers/distributors/retail/DTC and to aviation and automotive OEMs worldwide; a Swiss-incorporated, Kansas-run, ~$46B founder-influenced company. PASS.

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized averages (FY2016–25, both cash variants × beg/end CE): ROCE 17.4% (≥15% STRONG) even keeping the whole liquid securities book in capital employed; strip it and returns run 33–37%. Worst-of-four FCFROCE 13.5% (≥8%). Per-share compounding clears the 3% floor comfortably — OI/sh +12.6%, FCF/sh +9.0%, BV/sh +10.9%, TBV/sh +10.7% CAGR. Debt-free; L/E 22.5% (leases only); $4.1B net cash + STI ($21.36/sh). One soft patch (FY2022 OI −16%) fully recovered by 2024–25; no sustained decline. **STRONG.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: no customer ≥10% of net sales (top-ten ~20–25%); supplier side vertically integrated and diversified, with contained sole-source component pockets (no single vendor >10% of COGS+opex disclosed). Four forces — customer power WEAK (fragmented, brand pull; Auto-OEM the exception), supplier power WEAK (large integrated buyer), **substitutes STRONG** (Apple Watch / smartphone convergence bites the largest segment, Fitness 33%; doing-without via phone apps), new entrants WEAK (brand + vertical mfg + aviation certification + Garmin Connect switching costs). Tally 1 strong → judgment zone. Moat: brand/ingrainedness with serious athletes, pilots, and mariners, reinforced by ecosystem switching costs and aviation regulatory certification; stable. Market YES (wearables/outdoor growth; aviation/marine steady; auto-OEM growing but loss-making). **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

But **P19 buyback strike:** the 2024 Program repurchased 1.23M shares at ≈ $199 avg → implied 28.2× MCap/FCF and 18.4–21.7× EV/OI (lower-EV variant), above the 20× hard-strike line. **FAIL** — single hard strike.
