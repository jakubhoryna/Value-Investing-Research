## 2. Understanding

Novo Nordisk makes and sells prescription peptide medicines — mainly one molecule, semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy injectable and pill, 49% of 2025 sales), plus insulins (17%) and a small Rare Disease unit (6%) — bought by three US wholesalers on terms set by PBMs, insurers and government (US 56% of sales) and by health systems elsewhere; a 103-year-old, Foundation-controlled (77% of votes) Danish group, integrated from API to fill-finish, selling in ~170 countries. PASS.

## 3. Quality — past record

Restated — no new fiscal year since v9. Worst-of-four normalized ROCE 56.0% and FCFROCE 34.0% clear the STRONG bars; operating income per share compounded 11.6% a year, book value 17.0%. Free cash flow per share compounded 0.5% over FY2015–25, below the 3% floor, after DKK 60bn of expansion capex and the DKK 30bn Akero outlay booked as intangibles. Leverage 180%. The three-year FCFROCE fall is a documented build-out — the CMD confirms capex peaked in 2025 — so P4 waits for FY2026. **ADEQUATE**.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: customers FAIL as written — three US wholesalers took 23%, 17% and 17% of 2024 sales; the waiver needs a stable moat and is refused. Suppliers PASS. Forces: customer power STRONG — payers and the US government set net price, as management itself concedes; substitutes STRONG — tirzepatide leads, and Novo's latest answer is an in-licensed Chinese dual agonist; supplier and entrant power WEAK. Tally 2. Moat: exclusivity on one molecule, **eroding** — the company plans around its loss, its sales and margin bridges book lower prices, and the US share gain rides the pill launch. Market: volume growing, value deflating. **WEAK** on three grounds.
