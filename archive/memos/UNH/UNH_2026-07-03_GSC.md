# UNH — UnitedHealth Group: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

|Understanding|Past|Future|Shareholder-friendliness|Cheapness|FINAL|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|PASS|WEAK|WEAK|FAIL (comp strike; acquisition audit adverse)|EXPENSIVE|**AVOID** (Past + Future + SF)|

A decade of STRONG-caliber returns broke in 2024–25: FY2025 operating income fell 41% as Medicare risk-model changes (V28), rate pressure and a medical-cost surge hit simultaneously, and returns declined for a second straight year with leverage above flag level — mechanically Past WEAK. The deeper problem is structural: one customer, the US government via CMS, is 44% of revenue and sets its own prices — a breadth failure no moat can waive. Add a $60.9m front-loaded CEO award (2× the P17 stop) and a serial-M&A record capped by Amedisys at >20× operating income under a DOJ consent decree, and the gates fail three ways. At ~$425 the shares also sit at 23–25× trough OI. A flip to WATCH would require FY2026 returns re-inflecting, the comp strike lapsing (plausible by the 2027 proxy), and a defensible answer to CMS concentration — the last is the hard one, and it is growing, not shrinking.

## 2. Understanding

UnitedHealth Group is a Minnetonka (US)-based, $447.6bn-revenue vertically integrated payer-provider — America's largest health insurer — selling (i) medical benefit plans through UnitedHealthcare to employers, individuals, states and above all the federal government (CMS premiums = 44% of consolidated revenue, chiefly Medicare Advantage and Part D), and (ii) through Optum: pharmacy-benefit management (OptumRx), physician-led care delivery (OptumHealth), and data/analytics/services (OptumInsight) sold to employers, other insurers, providers and, heavily, to itself; a ~50-year-old, prominent, US-centric conglomerate now under intense regulatory scrutiny. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four 10y normalized ROCE 14.0% and FCFROCE 11.0% — comfortably ADEQUATE-zone levels — with OI/sh +6.2% and FCF/sh +7.6% geometric (both above the 3% floor). But ROCE_wc fell 17.4% (2023) → 15.7% (2024) → 8.4% (2025) and FCFROCE_wc 13.8% → 10.1% → 7.1% — a sustained multi-year decline that forces a downgrade regardless of level — and FY2025 L/E is 221%, above the 200% flag without P6 cover (equity grew; 2025 metrics are not great). **WEAK** — two independent P4 conditions bind; noting honestly that liabilities are dominated by non-interest-bearing insurance float (debt/equity alone is 83%) and that through 2023 this was a STRONG-caliber record.

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth **FAIL**: CMS alone is 44% of revenue (40% in each of FY2023–24) — 4× the 10% threshold and rising. The captive-customer waiver is unavailable: the government demonstrably can "backward-integrate" (traditional fee-for-service Medicare is the default program) and resets rates and risk models unilaterally. Supplier breadth passes (fragmented providers, much supply owned via Optum). Forces: customer power STRONG (CMS price-setting); supplier power WEAK; substitutes WEAK; new entrants WEAK — tally 1. Moat: real (scale, data, payer-provider ingrainedness) but eroding at the margin under DOJ criminal/civil investigations, FTC suit and congressional probes. Market growth YES. Breadth FAIL decides: **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Strike 1 — compensation: CEO Stephen Hemsley's 2025 Summary Compensation Table total is **$60.94m** (a $60m front-loaded option grant), breaching the $30m absolute stop; 2025 say-on-pay drew heavy opposition and the board renounced future front-loading — prospective, not curative. Strike 2 — capital allocation: Amedisys closed Aug 2025 at $3.3bn, >20× its pre-deal operating income, requiring divestiture of 164 sites (~$528m revenue) and a court-appointed monitor; pattern-consistent with Change Healthcare (2022) and LHC (2023). Buybacks of ~$24bn in FY2022–24 at ~3–4× the framework limit price. Offsets noted (Hemsley's ~1.34m shares, $1m salary, no bonus; director fees ≈ $423k < stop; RPT clean; dividend raised through the crisis) — but one strike disqualifies. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- CMS rate-setting and the V28 risk-model phase-in directly compress the largest revenue pool — single-customer policy risk is the business model.
- DOJ criminal and civil investigations into Medicare billing remain open; adverse findings could bring fines, exclusion risk and forced practice changes.
- Medical-cost trend running above pricing for a second year would break the FY2026 recovery the market is paying for.
- Optum's payer-provider integration is itself an antitrust target (FTC insulin suit; Amedisys monitor).
- Reputational/political tail risk after 2024–25 events keeps the regulatory temperature elevated.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To WATCH: FY2026 returns re-inflect (breaking the decline) with L/E back under 200%; comp strike lapses at the April 2027 proxy if the SCT normalizes (Hemsley takes no annual equity before 2028, so likely); and a documented resolution of the breadth failure — which, with CMS share rising, this framework does not expect.
