Epiroc is the better half of the 2018 Atlas Copco demerger: a focused underground hard-rock franchise taking two-thirds of revenue from aftermarket work bolted to an installed fleet, at 18.4% worst-of-four ROCE and 19–23% adjusted margins through a cycle. Quality is not the problem. Price is far off at 25.7× EV/OI, but the deciding gate is shareholder-friendliness. Epiroc's only repurchase authority, renewed at every AGM and run by a standing Repurchase Committee, exists to hedge and deliver option-plan shares — note 25 says so. That is dilution sterilisation, a P19 hard strike at any price, unaveraged against three clean tests. The verdict flips on a worth-referenced repurchase policy, not a cheaper price.

## 2. Understanding

Epiroc AB is a Swedish-listed, dual-class, Investor AB-influenced (≈23% of votes) vertically integrated capital-goods manufacturer of underground and surface rock-drilling rigs, loaders, haulers, blasthole drills, hydraulic breakers and attachments and consumable drilling tools, which takes two-thirds of revenue from the attached aftermarket of parts, field service, rebuilds and automation/electrification systems (plus a small captive finance and rental arm), sold ~80% direct from a service network in ~150 countries to mining companies (79% of orders — BHP, Glencore, Rio Tinto, Vale and thousands of smaller miners) and infrastructure, demolition and scrap-recycling contractors (21%), run from Nacka with plants in Sweden, the USA, Canada, India, China and France. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

On the framework's conservative capital base the worst of four normalized averages is ROCE 18.4% and FCFROCE 10.9% (FY2021–25), both clear of P2. OI per share compounded 11.3% since 2016, book value per share 11.9%, FCF per share 6.7% over four years. Leverage is comfortable at 90% liabilities-to-equity, leases included. Epiroc's own ten-year series never shows ROCE below 18.9% — but on a more generous capital base. A framework-consistent series runs five years only: pre-2021 reports were unreachable, 2016–17 are pre-listing carve-out. **ADEQUATE** — thresholds cleared with room, but STRONG needs a ten-year record this name cannot yet show.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both sides: ten largest customers are 18% of revenue with none dominant; >3,000 significant suppliers, deliberately spread by region. Forces, binary — customer power **WEAK** (thousands of buyers, backward integration implausible, mid-life switching costs); supplier power **WEAK**; substitutes **WEAK** (rock must still be broken; in-situ recovery suits few ore bodies); new entrants **WEAK** (the aftermarket network is the barrier, as Epiroc says, though specialty attachments face many small rivals). Tally 0 strong. Moat: **switching costs**, fingerprinted by the 66% aftermarket mix, **strengthening** as automation deepens integration. Market growth **MIXED**. **STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Three tests are clean: the CEO's SEK 24.0m is 0.28% of the lower of FCF and NI against the 5% strike and ~USD 2.5m against the ~USD 40m trigger; the chair's SEK 3.4m is below even the unscaled norm; related-party dealings are trivial; the ≈50% payout is rational. The strike is the buybacks. Every AGM mandate authorises repurchase **only** "to fulfill the obligations under Epiroc's performance based employee stock option plans," and note 25 names buy-backs as the hedging instrument. Treasury stock of 5.36m A shares, all bought post-listing, is being handed to option holders; diluted count is up 0.25% across seven vintages. **Hard strike at any price.** Stanley audits to ≈10.6–15.4× pre-deal OI on estimated inputs: **INDETERMINATE**. **FAIL.**

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: PASS.** "Our ten largest customers accounted for 18% (20) of Epiroc's revenues in 2025. All are mining customers, but none is dominant in size" (AR2025). Thousands of smaller customers besides. No single customer approaches the 10% P15 threshold; the disclosure sentence exists and is affirmative. Customer base is unlikely to consolidate to a degree that matters — mining consolidation is slow and Epiroc's revenue is spread across ~150 countries.

**Breadth — suppliers: PASS, with a logged caveat.** "We leverage a global network of more than 3 000 significant suppliers"; "our broad supplier base across multiple countries reduces dependency on any single region"; the risk section describes "regional networks of sub-suppliers, to prevent supplier dependency." No single supplier is disclosed above 10% of COGS plus operating expenses. Looking through the nominal count as the framework requires: the inputs are steel, hydraulics, engines, electronics and, increasingly, battery cells. Battery-cell supply for the electrification range is the plausible effective concentration, and it is not disclosed. Caveat logged; not treated as a FAIL on the evidence available. Named re-test.

**Forces (binary; one line each):**

| Force | Call | Reason |
|-------|------|--------|
| Customer bargaining power | **WEAK** | Top ten are 18% of revenue and thousands of smaller buyers besides; miners cannot credibly build their own drill rigs; mid-life fleet, operator training and automation integration make switching costly. |
| Supplier bargaining power | **WEAK** | Over 3,000 significant suppliers deliberately spread across regions; forward integration into rock-excavation equipment is implausible; components are largely re-sourceable. |
| Threat of substitutes | **WEAK** | Doing without is impossible — ore must be broken and moved; the only wholly-different-function substitute, in-situ recovery, is confined to particular deposit types, and the secular trend to deeper, harder ore increases mechanised equipment intensity. |
| Threat of new entrants | **WEAK** | Epiroc's own words: performance-critical products "require a strong aftermarket network, which makes entry barriers high." Scale, a service presence in ~150 countries, safety certification and installed base are the barriers to *success*, not merely entry. |

**Tally: 0 strong forces** — the favourable case under P16.

*Dissent recorded.* The Tools & Attachments business area (24% of revenue, 15% of segment operating profit) faces "a wide range of smaller local and regional competitors" and lower entry barriers than Equipment & Service. At segment level the entrant threat there is closer to STRONG. Equipment & Service is 76% of revenue and 88% of segment operating profit, so the group call follows it; the T&A exposure is logged and appears in Risks (Chinese OEMs).

**Moat: switching costs. Strengthening.** Mechanism: Epiroc equipment has a multi-decade service life; the customer's economics are dominated by uptime, so parts availability, field service at remote sites and rebuild capability bind the customer to the original manufacturer for the life of the machine. Aftermarket is 66% of revenue against 34% equipment — the ratio is the moat's fingerprint. Automation platforms, mixed-fleet control systems and battery-electric fleet management deepen the integration, which is why the direction is strengthening rather than merely stable. Honest limit: Sandvik, Caterpillar and Komatsu enjoy the same structure on their own installed bases, so this protects Epiroc from entrants more than from the incumbent three — which is precisely what the forces table says.

**Market growth: MIXED.** Mining demand is firm, driven by copper and gold; orders grew 7% organically in 2025. Infrastructure and the attachments market were weak, and group revenue fell 3% (up 2% organic). Directionally supportive over a cycle, soft today.

**Verdict: STRONG** — breadth PASS both sides, zero strong forces, an identified single-source moat rated stable-to-strengthening. Market growth is not a STRONG condition under the §2·1B tiering and does not block it.

### 3. Buybacks — **P19 HARD STRIKE (dilution sterilisation)**

The evidence, all from primary sources:

- **AR2025 note 25:** "In order to limit the exposure of the performance based employee stock option plan, hedging measures have been adopted in the form of share buy-backs (see note 21), which can be transferred to the participants of the plan."
- **AR2025 note 21:** the AGM mandates of 8 May 2025 and 14 May 2024 both authorise the Board "to repurchase, transfer and sell own shares **in order to fulfill the obligations under Epiroc's performance based employee stock option plans**" — acquisition of up to 1,900,000 (2025) and 1,950,000 (2024) series A shares, of which up to 1,800,000 / 1,900,000 transferable to option holders, plus up to 20,000 A shares for board synthetic-share settlement. **There is no general repurchase authority.**
- **Corporate governance report:** a standing Board **Repurchase Committee** (Ulla Litzén, chair; Ronnie Leten) exists solely to prepare and execute these repurchases.
- **Epiroc IR, "Share repurchases":** "Repurchases of shares is made in order to cover the share-based long-term incentive programs."
- **Treasury record:** 5,362,105 A shares held at end-2024, 4,695,191 at end-2025. Epiroc listed in June 2018 with no treasury stock, so the entire holding was repurchased post-listing, inside the window. Divestments of 1,405,910 A shares (2024) and 666,914 A shares (2025) are deliveries to option holders.
- **Effect:** diluted share count 1,206m (2018) → 1,209m (2025), **+0.25%** across seven option-plan vintages (2018–2024 plans plus 2025), with a stated maximum of 3,264,256 deliverable shares ≈ 0.3% of the register.

That is the mechanism P19 names: shares are bought with company cash and handed to option holders so that the dilution never appears in the share count. **Hard strike at any price.** The multiple-based tests (14×/16× flag lines, 20×-both-reads strike) are not reached — the sterilisation limb is independent of price and of the dual-read.

*Stated fairly:* the programme is small. Roughly 0.16% of shares a year, and in both FY2024 and FY2025 Epiroc was a **net seller** of treasury shares (cash inflows of SEK 290m and SEK 142m). Epiroc has never run a general buyback. The strike is nonetheless mechanical and is not softened — but the reader should not confuse this with Atlas Copco's SEK 9,955m over the same window.

*Redemption clause (v3.5.0) — fails all three limbs.* (a) No published worth-referenced repurchase price discipline; the published criterion is expressly option-delivery-referenced, which is the opposite. (b) No bargain-line execution year and no refusal year — the mandate is used as designed. (c) No allocator turnover: Ronnie Leten has chaired since listing and Investor AB's position is unchanged. Partial satisfaction changes nothing.

*Not a P19 issue:* the SEK 3.00 per share **redemption** in 2020 was a Swedish pro-rata share-redemption programme — economically a distribution to all holders, not a market repurchase — and share count was unchanged through it. Excluded from the buyback test.
