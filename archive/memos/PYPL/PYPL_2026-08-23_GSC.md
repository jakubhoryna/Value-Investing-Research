# PYPL — PayPal Holdings, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS, Past ADEQUATE, Future WEAK, shareholder-friendliness PASS on one flag (capital allocation CAUTION), price FAIR — **AVOID**, now on one gate rather than v2's two.

The gate move is mechanical, not charitable: v2's FAIL rested on the P19 strike ladder retired at v4.0.0, and the same evidence now reads CAUTION with nothing to pair it. The one genuinely new item points the same way — $3.05bn repurchased in H1'26 at $45.18, about 7.3× free cash flow and 7.1× operating income, the first buying in the record at a price this framework would itself pay. The business has not moved: branded checkout still loses share, transaction expense still consumes half of revenue, supplier breadth still fails. Press reports since mid-July have Stripe and Advent bidding $60.50 with the board holding out for more; nothing is filed, and a bid from the rival that spent a decade eroding the franchise is not evidence the franchise is good. At $61.54 the price sits 31% above the top of the band.
## 2. Understanding

Unchanged and still **PASS**: PayPal Holdings is a US-listed Delaware financial-technology company earning volume-based transaction fees from hundreds of millions of consumer accounts and tens of millions of merchants for digital payment processing — branded checkout (PayPal, Venmo), unbranded processing (Braintree), P2P, BNPL, credit and value-added services — across roughly 200 markets with the US its largest revenue geography, as the incumbent leader in online branded checkout, growing revenue mid-single-digits while losing checkout share to platform wallets.

## 3. Quality — past record

**ADEQUATE, unchanged.** No new fiscal year: the four normalized ROCE reads stand at 14.2 / 13.2 / 22.2 / 20.6%, worst 13.2% against the 15% line; worst-of-four FCFROCE 16.0%. Per-share growth is the strong leg — operating income per share compounding 19.1% and free cash flow per share 12.2%, flattered by a 21% share-count reduction since 2021. The new observation is the interim direction, and it is negative: H1'26 operating income of $2,915m is 3.9% below H1'25, so FY2026 tracks down. Leverage 317% headline, ~100% ex-custodial funds.

## 4. Quality — future

**WEAK, unchanged, and not close.** No results since Q2'26. Supplier breadth still fails: transaction expense — $8,550m in H1'26, 50.2% of revenue and the dominant cost line — flows to the card duopoly and two concentrated processors. Customer breadth passes. Force calls stand at customer STRONG, supplier STRONG, substitutes STRONG, new entrants WEAK: three against a stop signal of two. The moat — two-sided network, brand trust, vaulted credentials — remains **eroding**, and H1'26 corroborates it: revenue +5.9% while operating margin fell 18.9% → 17.1%. Market growth YES — the problem, not the consolation.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
