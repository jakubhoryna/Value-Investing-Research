# PYPL — PayPal Holdings, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS, Past ADEQUATE, Future WEAK, shareholder-friendliness PASS on one flag, price FAIR — **AVOID**, on Future alone.

Rebuilt from primaries, not rolled forward, and the answer holds. The past record is better than its headline — the last three years run 14.9%, 16.0% and 17.2% on end-period capital against a ten-year average of 13.2% — but quality of the past is not what fails here. Half of revenue leaves as transaction expense to a card duopoly PayPal cannot bargain with, three of four forces read strong, and the erosion sits in the company's own numbers: active accounts flat at 439 million across five quarters, transaction margin down 150 basis points. The takeover ending removes a price distortion and leaves that untouched. At $53.72 the free-cash-flow leg is 3.2% from the bargain line, and binds nothing while Future fails.

## 2. Understanding

PayPal Holdings is a US-listed Delaware financial-technology company earning volume-based transaction fees from hundreds of millions of consumer accounts and tens of millions of merchants for digital payment processing — branded checkout (PayPal, Venmo), unbranded processing (Braintree), P2P, buy-now-pay-later, credit and value-added services — across approximately 200 markets with the US its largest revenue geography, as the incumbent leader in online branded checkout, growing revenue mid-single-digits while losing checkout share to platform wallets. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

**ADEQUATE.** Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 13.2% and worst-of-four FCFROCE 16.0% — clearing the adequate floor, missing strong on ROCE alone. The shape matters: the yearly series troughed at 10.3% in FY2020 and has risen four years running to 17.2%, the opposite of a decline, but the average carries the 2016–22 period when capital employed doubled and operating income did not. Per-share growth is the strong leg, operating income per share compounding 19.1% and free cash flow 12.2%, both flattered by a share count down a fifth since FY2021. Headline leverage of 317% breaches the flag; the carve-out at C.1 is load-bearing.

## 4. Quality — future

**WEAK.** Supplier breadth fails: transaction expense was $15,987m in FY2025, 48.2% of revenue, and 50.2% in H1'26, routed to the card duopoly plus two concentrated processors, with no ready alternatives. Customer breadth passes. Forces read customer, supplier and substitutes STRONG, new entrants WEAK — three against a stop signal of two. The moat, two-sided network plus brand plus vaulted credentials, is **eroding**, and Q2'26 quantifies it: active accounts 439m, up 0.3% and down 0.2m sequentially; transaction margin 44.9% against 46.4%; international revenue up 2% but down 3% currency-neutral. Market growth is real and is the problem — it drew Apple, Google, Shopify and Stripe to the checkout layer.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**PASS on one flag.** Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: FY2016–22 repurchases were plainly overpriced under allocators who have all left, FY2023–25 ran 10.1–17.7× on free cash flow, and the H1'26 tranche printed $3,027m at $45.18, or 7.1–7.8× — at the bargain lines, but across two quarters, not a completed year, and against no published price criterion. P17 is clean on the FY2025 test year: Chriss $25.24m against 5% lines of $261.7m and $227.1m, and below the ≈$40m indexed absolute trigger. Highest outside-director total $530,101 against a ≈$1.02m stop. Related-party transactions immaterial. No second negative, so no pair.
