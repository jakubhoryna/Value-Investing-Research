## 2. Understanding

Kaspi.kz is a Kazakhstan-headquartered, Nasdaq- and KASE-listed group operating a two-sided mobile Super App that sells payment processing and merchant acquiring, marketplace listing, fulfilment and travel, and consumer credit and deposits through its 98.95%-owned licensed bank — paid for by ~900k merchants via take-rates and ~26m consumers via interest and fees, in Kazakhstan and, since 2025, Türkiye through 86.7%-owned Hepsiburada and Hepsi Bank. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Two independent failures, neither about the level. Total liabilities are 340% of book at FY2025 and 353% at 30 June — above the 200% flag, and the 700% allowance does not reach: equity is compounding, not shrinking, and the borrowing funds a loan book rather than a repurchase. Second, returns fall in every period measured: 29.3% → 27.8% → 25.8% → 20.9% on opening capital, 22.7% → 19.5% → 19.2% on closing. The level is genuinely high — worst-of-four normalized ROCE 21.1%, FCFROCE 7.7%–15.1% depending on cash-flow basis — and the three-year series is itself record-limited. Level cannot rescue either failure. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both sides: tens of millions of consumers, ~900k merchants, self-owned rails removing card-network dependence. Forces: customer power WEAK (atomised, 77 transactions a month is switching cost as habit); supplier power WEAK (owns the rails, funded by granular retail deposits); new entrants WEAK (scale, ingrainedness, banking licence); substitutes **STRONG** in Türkiye, where Hepsiburada trails Trendyol with no ecosystem lock. One strong force, contained. Moat is network effect plus ingrainedness, strengthening in Kazakhstan, absent in Türkiye. Kazakhstan is 75% of 1H26 segment revenue. Market growth YES. **ADEQUATE.**

## C. Calculation notes

**C.1 — Lens: §2·1A substitution deliberately NOT applied [decisive, user-directed; reverses v2].** v2 invoked the financial-institution substitution on the balance-sheet condition — customer accounts 87% of liabilities funding a loan book at 62% of assets. That condition still holds and nothing here disputes it. This memo runs the industrial machinery instead, on the counter-argument v2 itself logged: the deposit-funded segment produced **33% of FY2025 net income** (Payments ₸433,001m, Marketplace ₸279,773m, Fintech ₸354,933m; 20-F Item 3.D states 33% and 31% for FY2025 and FY2024). Both readings are defensible and they reach opposite verdicts. The governing framework question is IDEAS **A2**, whose parked recommendation would set the boundary on the finance segment's share of *assets* — on which test Kaspi substitutes and v2 stands. This memo records the operating-lens answer as instructed; it does not settle A2.

**C.2 — Cash flow: acquisitions excluded, and the definitional problem named [user-directed check].** Capex is the investing line "purchase of property, equipment and intangible assets" — ₸50,257m / 95,726m / 182,513m for FY2023–25 and ₸193,583m TTM. "Acquisitions of subsidiaries, net of cash acquired" (₸552,834m in FY2025, ₸265,716m in 6M25, nil in 6M26) is **not** deducted anywhere. v2's FY2025 FCF of ₸491,098m is confirmed arithmetically correct on the same definition — 673,611 − 182,513 — and did not net the Hepsiburada consideration. The real problem is upstream: reported CFO embeds the change in loans to customers (−₸1,662,914m in FY2025) against the change in customer accounts (+₸974,460m), so FCF moves ₸1,056bn → ₸486bn → ₸491bn → ₸953bn on a business whose trading did not move that way. Both bases are therefore carried throughout and labelled at every use: the **reported basis** (CFO − capex, the §2·1A default, binding for the gate) and the **pre-balance-sheet basis** (CFO before changes in operating assets and liabilities, less income tax paid, less capex — reference only). No P20 maintenance-capex construction is invoked; none of its three conditions holds.

**C.3 — OI, and the numerator/denominator consistency fix.** Kaspi publishes no operating-income subtotal; revenue less costs and operating expenses equals PBT exactly, and "costs and operating expenses" includes interest expenses and fees. PBT is therefore *after* funding cost. Because capital employed and enterprise value both contain the interest-bearing liabilities that generate that cost, the consistent operating-lens numerator is **OI = PBT + total interest expenses** (§2·1A, captive-finance consistency; §2 Gate 2 calls EV/OI financing-blind). Fees for collection of credit card receivables (₸82,849m FY2025) are an operating cost and are **not** added back. The as-reported-PBT reading is logged as the alternate in C.4 and is the one v2's C.2 used, which is why v2's literal ROCE read 12.9%–16.1% where the consistent build reads 20.9%–24.4%.

**C.4 — Logged alternate: as-reported PBT over capital employed.** ROCE_wc beginning 20.0% (2022), 20.0% (2023), 18.8% (2024), 15.9% (2025), 12.2% (TTM); ending 14.1%, 15.0%, 15.3%, 12.0%, 11.2%. This pairing charges the business for funding cost in the numerator while leaving the funding in the denominator; it is directionally identical (declining) and materially lower in level. The gate verdict is unchanged either way — WEAK on leverage regardless, and declining on both.

**C.5 — Capital employed.** `CE_wc = total assets`. NIBCL is set to zero: the only candidates are trade liabilities (₸304,384m) and other liabilities (₸205,077m) at 30 June, both of which contain derivative and accrued-interest components, and §2·1A puts the burden of proof on the item and leaves ambiguous items in CE. Sensitivity: deducting the full non-interest-bearing residual of ₸582,670m (total liabilities less due to banks, customer accounts and debt securities) raises TTM ROCE_wc ending from 19.2% to 20.2% — 100bp, not verdict-moving. `CE_xc = CE_wc − cash and equivalents − investment securities and derivatives`. Mandatory NBK balances (₸469,802m) are restricted and stay in CE; due from banks (₸55,520m) stays in CE. Both retentions are conservative.

**C.6 — Leverage and the P6 test.** `L/E = total liabilities ÷ equity attributable to shareholders` = 8,480,171 ÷ 2,491,843 = **340.3%** at FY2025, 9,326,988 ÷ 2,639,116 = **353.4%** at 30 June. Above the 200% flag. P6's 700% allowance requires all other metrics great **and** a creditworthy company borrowing cheaply to repurchase clearly under-priced stock, or equity shrunk by rational distributions. Kaspi is creditworthy and its repurchases are under-priced, but the borrowing is retail deposits funding a loan book — not repurchase funding — and equity compounded 49.9% a year. Neither limb holds; the carve-out is for a company that levered up to shrink equity, which this is not. Breach stands. Note the direction of travel is favourable: 621% → 524% → 530% → 449% → 340%.

**C.7 — Symmetric-exclusion alternative (not computable).** §2·1A offers symmetric exclusion of the finance segment's result and assets as the logged alternative. Kaspi discloses segment revenue and (to FY2025) segment net income but **no segment balance sheet**, and from 2026 replaced segment net income with adjusted EBITDA, so no post-FY2025 segment earnings exist either. The alternative cannot be computed and is recorded as unavailable, not as a passed test. Directional context only, and not a framework metric: Payments and Marketplace earned ₸712,774m of FY2025 net income, 67% of the group.

**C.8 — Currency.** Reporting currency KZT throughout. All ratios, bands and distances computed in tenge; USD shown at the ADS-implied cross of 454.89 (see A). Company period-end rates: 505.53 (31-Dec-25), 480.72 (30-Jun-26). The tenge appreciated ~5% over the half, which raises USD-denominated bands mechanically and is not an improvement in the business.

**C.9 — Bias check.** Live: **cleverness**, **anchoring**, **miscontrast**. The consistency argument in C.3 is elegant, and elegance is a reason for suspicion, not confidence — it is therefore stated as a construction with the alternate fully computed in C.4, and the gate verdict is shown to be invariant to it. Consistency hazard not applicable — not held. **Peculiarity** noted and not treated as live: Kazakhstan is unfamiliar, which is a reason for care in the evidence, not for a worse verdict.
