# KSPI — Joint Stock Company Kaspi.kz: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

**WATCH** — Understanding PASS, Past ADEQUATE, Future ADEQUATE, Shareholder-friendliness PASS (one flag), Cheapness FAIR.

Kaspi is worth owning for a closed-loop consumer ecosystem that no one in Kazakhstan can replicate: 26m consumers transacting 77 times a month on rails the company owns outright, monetised across payments, marketplace and a deposit-funded loan book. What stands between here and a BUY is eight percent of price. The band moved from roughly $42–52 to roughly $98 **on mechanics, not on the business** — v1 was written pre-v3 and priced a bank on capital-employed and tangible-book machinery that is structurally inapplicable to it; under the substitution rule the anchor is earnings, and on earnings the stock is close. The verdict does not flip on price alone. Net income has now been flat for five consecutive quarters while equity compounds, returns are falling in a straight line, and the Türkiye build has gone from one acquisition to two. A year from now this is either a business that absorbed a funding-cost shock and a second market and resumed compounding, or one whose returns are converging toward ordinary. Cheapness is the near constraint; quality is the real one.

## 2. Understanding

Kaspi.kz is a Kazakhstan-headquartered, Nasdaq- and KASE-listed holding company operating a single mobile Super App that sells payment processing and merchant acquiring, marketplace and e-commerce listing and fulfilment, and consumer credit and deposits through its licensed bank — paid for by ~900k merchants via take-rates and by ~26m retail consumers via interest and fees, in Kazakhstan and, since 2025, in Türkiye through 86.7%-owned Hepsiburada and newly acquired Hepsi Bank. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Substitution rule applied (§2·1A): 87% of liabilities are customer deposits funding a loan book that is 62% of assets, so ROE replaces ROCE and FCFROCE is n.m. Worst-of-two normalized ROE over FY2021–25 is 60.5% (end-of-period; 90.8% beginning) — four times the STRONG threshold. Two things cap it. The audited record runs five years, not ten, so STRONG is unavailable. And returns decline without interruption: 116.7% (2022) to 67.9% (2025) to 41.0% TTM on beginning equity. EPS compounded 32.9% a year to FY2024 and then stopped — +3.0% in 2025, flat in 1H26. Non-deposit debt is 22.4% of equity. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASSES on both sides — tens of millions of consumers, ~900k merchants, and self-owned rails that remove the card-network supplier dependence. Forces: customer power WEAK (atomised, deeply ingrained); supplier power WEAK (owns the rails); new entrants WEAK (scale, switching costs, banking licence); substitutes **STRONG** in Türkiye, where Hepsiburada is second to Trendyol and alternatives are one tap away. One strong force, contained — the judgment zone. Moat is network effects plus ingrainedness, strengthening in Kazakhstan (3P take rate +160bp to 16.1%, VAS revenue +49%), unproven in Türkiye. Market growth YES. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation clears both P17 reads by a wide margin: FY2025 aggregate executive and board cash pay of ₸654m is 2.7% of the fiscal-year limit and far below the normalized one. Insiders hold ~46%; single share class; related-party dealings are ordinary-course deposits. Capital allocation is **CAUTION** — one flag. Repurchases are disciplined on every bound of the 1H26 price range, dividends were restored and raised 18%, but ~$1.2bn into a loss-making Hepsiburada plus a Turkish bank and ~$300m to capitalise it is reinvestment whose consistency with the returns record is asserted, not yet demonstrated. **PASS**, heightened scrutiny.
