# ABB — ABB Ltd: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | ADEQUATE | ADEQUATE | PASS | EXPENSIVE | **WATCH** |

ABB is worth owning as a self-improving electrification franchise: the decentralised "ABB Way" model installed in 2020 lifted ROCE_xc from 6.6% (2020) to 29.5% (2025) while management pruned every low-return unit (Power Grids, Dodge, Accelleron, now Robotics to SoftBank for EV $5.375B). What stands between here and a BUY is price, and the gap is enormous: at ~$106 the stock trades at 43× FCF and 32.3× EV/OI — the latter above even this framework's *sell* signal of 25. The market has fully priced the data-centre electrification boom; the stock sits near its all-time high after a near-doubling off the 52-week low. Verdict flips to BUY only on a dislocation of a severity ABB last saw in 2020; it flips to AVOID if post-2020 returns prove cyclical rather than structural (a return of ROCE_xc below ~10% for consecutive years).

## 2. Understanding

ABB Ltd is a Zurich-headquartered Swiss stock corporation (US-GAAP reporter in USD, listed in Zurich and Stockholm) that manufactures and services electrification hardware (switchgear, breakers, UPS, EV charging), motors, drives and industrial process-automation systems, sold worldwide (revenues roughly balanced across Americas, Europe and Asia/Middle East/Africa) to utilities, data centres, industry, buildings and transport; it holds #1–#2 global positions in most product lines, is growing (FY2025 revenue $33.2B, record orders), and is exiting Robotics via sale to SoftBank in 2026. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Two different companies share the decade. The conglomerate era (FY2016–2020) earned ROCE_xc of 6.6–11.3% and FCFROCE_xc of 4.8–9.6%, with L/E drifting to 230% in 2019 — above the 200% flag at unexceptional returns. Post-restructuring (FY2021–2025) ROCE_xc runs 16–29.5% (24%+ in each of the last three years), FCFROCE_xc reached 21.8% in 2025, L/E settled at ~170%, and OI/share compounded from $0.90 (2016) to $3.30 (2025). Strict P2 fails (ROCE_xc ≥15% in only 5 of 10 years); the trajectory is the opposite of decline. **ADEQUATE** — decade average misses STRONG; current run-rate would earn it.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: no customer ≥10% of revenue; supplier base broad (semiconductor tightness in 2021–22 was cyclical, not structural). Forces MIXED: customer and supplier power LOW–MEDIUM, substitutes LOW (electrification is the substitute), entrants MEDIUM (Chinese low-voltage competition), rivalry MEDIUM–HIGH (Siemens, Schneider, Eaton at comparable scale). Moat: switching costs and ingrainedness — spec'd-in, certified electrical gear with a vast installed base, service attach and distributor lock — plus brand premium in drives/motors; **stable-to-strengthening** as grid and data-centre spend deepens the installed base. Market growth clearly YES (Q1-26 record orders; guidance raised), with Marshall's caveat that this growth is attracting capital industry-wide. **ADEQUATE** (STRONG blocked only by the rivalry tilt).

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Comp: CEO total ≈$7.7M, 75% variable — ~0.13% of OI, proportionate and performance-linked; single share class, one vote per share; Investor AB (Wallenberg sphere) is the engaged anchor shareholder. RPTs: none material disclosed. Buybacks: ~324M shares (~$10.3B, avg ≈$31.8) repurchased and cancelled since July 2020 — genuine capital reduction, FCF-funded, though executed programmatically without price discipline (caveat, not a strike). Dividend CHF 0.94, ~47% payout, grown ~5%/yr for a decade; portfolio divestments at full prices are the opposite of empire-building. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Data-centre/AI electrification capex cycle reverses, exposing how much of the 2023–25 margin surge was demand-cyclical rather than structural.
- Chinese low-voltage and drives competitors compress pricing in Asia and eventually Europe.
- SoftBank Robotics sale ($5.375B EV, ~$5.3B net cash, ~$2.4B book gain) fails to close or proceeds are deployed into overpriced M&A/buybacks at 30×+ EV/OI.
- FY2024–25 figures are ex-Robotics while FY2023 and earlier include it — trend comparisons overstate nothing material (~7% of revenue) but the perimeter shifts twice in the decade (Power Grids, Robotics).
- Valuation itself: at >25× EV/OI the framework's own sell discipline would apply to a holding.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To BUY: price into the $10–12 band (implausible short of systemic crisis) — realistically ABB re-enters consideration below ≈$21 (EV/OI ≤7 with TBV excused via MODEL-mode ruling). To AVOID: two consecutive years of ROCE_xc <10% or a leverage breach above 200% without exceptional returns. **Next review:** SoftBank closing (mid-to-late 2026) and FY2026 results (early Feb 2027).

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth.** Customers: PASS — sales span utilities, data centres, process industry, buildings, transport across three balanced regions; 20-F-era disclosures report no single customer ≥10% of revenue; consolidation risk low. Suppliers: PASS — broad component/electronics base; 2021–22 semiconductor shortage was industry-wide and cyclical; no critical single-source dependency disclosed.

**Five forces:**

| Force | Threat | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer power | LOW–MED | Fragmented buyers; products mission-critical, small share of project cost |
| Supplier power | LOW–MED | Commodity inputs + broad electronics base; brief chip-era leverage |
| Substitutes | LOW | Electrification *is* the substitution wave; no alternative to switchgear/drives |
| New entrants | MED | Chinese LV/drives entrants credible in Asia; certification, installed base, distribution are real barriers elsewhere |
| Rivalry | MED–HIGH | Siemens, Schneider, Eaton, Rockwell, Emerson at equal scale and ambition |

Overall: **MIXED** (favourable on four, rivalry prevents FAVOURABLE).

**Moat.** Switching costs + ingrainedness: ABB gear is specified into electrical infrastructure with 20–40 year lives; certified, code-embedded products; service and spares attach; entrenched distributor/panel-builder channel. Secondary brand moat (price premium in drives, motors, switchgear where ABB holds #1–#2 share). Direction: **stable-to-strengthening** — every data-centre and grid project enlarges the installed base that feeds service and replacement demand.

**Market growth.** YES — electrification of transport/buildings/industry, grid reinforcement, and data-centre power (Q1-26: record orders >$10B in Q4-25, guidance raised to high-single/low-double-digit growth). Caveat applied: this growth is drawing capital from every rival and from China; the moat, not the growth, carries the verdict.
