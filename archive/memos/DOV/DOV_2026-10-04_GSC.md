# DOV — Dover Corporation: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past ADEQUATE, Future ADEQUATE, shareholder-friendliness PASS (one capital-allocation flag), cheapness EXPENSIVE — **WATCH**.

Dover is a diversified owner of niche industrial equipment and component businesses whose returns have improved across the decade while the portfolio was pruned. It is worth owning for a broad customer base, installed-base aftermarket and consumables, and a record of selling or spinning weaker units at sensible prices. What stands between here and a BUY is price: the band sits at about $47–60 against $189.69, a distance of +214%. The verdict flips to BUY only on a price fall of about two-thirds with returns intact. It flips to AVOID if repurchases at about 25× earnings continue into a fresh authorisation with no price discipline, or if a large deal adds a second negative.

## 2. Understanding
Dover makes differentiated equipment, components and consumables in five roughly equal segments (FY2025 revenue): pumps, bearings, compressor parts, biopharma connectors and process measurement (27%); fuel dispensers, fluid-transfer and cryogenic components and vehicle-wash systems (26%); food-retail refrigeration, brazed heat exchangers and can-making machines (19%); product-marking printers and their inks (14%); and vehicle-service lifts and defence electronics (13%). Its customers are industrial OEMs, process plants, fuel retailers, grocers, packagers and garages. It is a decentralised operating holding headquartered in Illinois, with 54% of sales in the US. It is a 70-year-old NYSE company with $8.1bn of sales, recently reshaped by the 2018 Apergy spin-off and the 2024 sale of its waste-truck business. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**ADEQUATE.** The worst-of-four average ROCE is 12.0% (end-of-period, cash in) and the best 13.8%. Worst FCFROCE is 9.1%. ROCE clears the adequate line but not the strong one, so STRONG is unavailable. Returns rose, not fell: 10.8% in FY2016–20, 12.9% in FY2021–25, with operating margin up from 10.5% (2016) to 17.0% (2025). Per-share growth is above the floor: chained same-basis OI/share +8.1% a year and FCF/share +7.0%, with endpoints at +5.6% and +5.0%. Liabilities to equity fell from 202% after the 2018 spin-off to 81% at FY2025 and 78% at June 2026.

## 4. Quality — future
**ADEQUATE.** Breadth passes. No customer exceeds 10% of revenue, and raw materials come from many sources. Force calls are made per segment. Pumps and marking show no strong force: they are spec-in components and razor-and-blade consumables. Three segments carry one strong force each. In refrigeration and can-making, a few large grocers and can-makers buy, so customer power is strong. In fuel equipment, electrification is a long-run substitute. In vehicle lifts, entry barriers are low. The moat is switching costs from the installed base and aftermarket. It is stable, with margins up five points over the window. Market growth is mixed: biopharma, cryogenics and data-centre cooling are growing; fuel retail and refrigeration are mature and cyclical.
