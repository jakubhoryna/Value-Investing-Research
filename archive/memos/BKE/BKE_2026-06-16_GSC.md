## Takeaway (read this)

**What it is.** The Buckle runs 440 denim‑led casual‑apparel stores across 42 central‑U.S. states, selling to fashion‑conscious 15‑to‑30‑year‑olds. It is founder‑controlled (the Hirschfeld family owns ~38% of a single share class), carries no financial debt, and pays out essentially all of its free cash flow as regular‑plus‑special dividends. This is a high‑quality, exceptionally shareholder‑friendly business — but at $44 it is reasonably, not cheaply, priced. Hence **WATCH**, not BUY.

**Why the quality is real (Past = STRONG).** Buckle's return on capital is the headline. On a beginning‑of‑period, cash‑excluded basis, ROCE cleared 15% in **all ten** years studied, ranging 30.6% to 92.7% (FY2025: 53.6%); cash‑flow ROCE cleared 8% in **all ten** years (FY2025: 42.2%). Per‑share owner economics compounded over the decade — operating income per share +63% and FCF per share +67% (FY2016→FY2025) — despite a shrinking store base and a decade of mall decline. Book value per share fell ~6% over the same span, but **by design**: the company returns capital rather than retaining it, so book is a poor yardstick here. Earnings are cyclical (operating income per share swung from a $2.49 trough in FY2018 to a $6.79 COVID‑era peak in FY2021, settling at $5.16 in FY2025), yet the *return on capital* never wavered. Leverage is lease‑only; the firm holds net cash, zero borrowings, and effectively infinite interest coverage.

**Why it isn't STRONG on the future (Future = ADEQUATE).** The franchise is durable but the moat is narrow. Buckle competes in a brutally crowded field (American Eagle, Abercrombie/Hollister, PacSun, Urban Outfitters, Zara, H&M, department stores, and online/DTC), against rivals it concedes are "considerably larger" with greater resources. Its edges — high‑touch commissioned selling, a loyal niche, and ~47% exclusive private‑label merchandise (higher‑margin, differentiated) — are genuine and have demonstrably sustained elite returns straight through the mall‑traffic decline and e‑commerce shift (online is a still‑modest 16.7% of sales). That durability is why this floors at ADEQUATE under the moat‑decides rule rather than WEAK. But it is not wide: no scale advantage, fashion risk is permanent, the format is secularly pressured, and there is a **single‑vendor concentration** — Axis Denim, which makes its private‑label denim, is 20.6% of net sales (denim overall is 42.5%). No long‑term supply contracts exist, which cuts both ways.

**Why it earns trust (SF = PASS, with caveats).** Capital allocation is exemplary: ~15 years of near‑100% FCF payout, no empire‑building M&A, and modest high‑return capex (~$45M/yr). Founder Daniel Hirschfeld owns 31.4% directly and takes no extra board pay; there is one share class and no dual‑class mischief; bonuses are formula‑linked to pre‑bonus profit; there are no buybacks at high prices (only ~0.5%/yr dilution from stock comp). **Caveats, not strikes:** CEO Dennis Nelson's FY2025 total comp of ~$11.7M is high in absolute terms (~4.5% of operating income), though profit‑linked; and this is a family business — the CFO is the CEO's son‑in‑law, with two other relatives employed (aggregate relative comp ~$2.8M) and a tiny legacy related‑party note. These are governance/succession watch‑items.

**Why it's a WATCH (Cheapness = FAIR).** At $44.01 against FY2025 flows: MCap/FCF 10.9 (threshold <8), EV/OI 9.0 leases‑as‑debt / 7.5 ex‑lease (threshold <7), MCap/BV 5.3 (threshold <5, nearly passing), MCap/TBV 5.3 (threshold <3). The two cash‑flow metrics that matter most are only modestly rich; the book‑value metrics overstate richness because ~100% payout keeps book structurally low for an asset‑light retailer. Net read: **FAIR, not EXPENSIVE — and not CHEAP.**

**Bottom line.** A wonderful, owner‑operated cash machine at a fair price. Put it on the shelf. It flips to CHEAP near **$31** (≈ −29% from here, with the book allowance) and is unambiguously cheap on every metric near **$25**. Buy the quality when the cycle — not the business — puts it on sale.

**What to watch:** (1) the Axis Denim / private‑label denim relationship and any single‑vendor disruption; (2) comparable‑sales and online mix as mall traffic evolves; (3) CEO‑succession signals in a family‑controlled firm; (4) any drift away from the full‑payout dividend policy.

## A. Company & gate facts (FY2025 10‑K, filed for FY ended Jan 31 2026)

- **Footprint:** 440 stores, 42 states; most in regional malls/lifestyle centers, with ongoing relocation to power centers. ~8,000 teammates (~2,900 full‑time) — heavy part‑time mix (relevant to the pay‑ratio caveat).
- **Merchandise mix (FY2025 net sales):** Denims 42.5%, Tops 28.9%, Accessories 10.9%, Footwear 4.9%, Sportswear 4.8%, Outerwear 2.2%, Casual bottoms 1.9%, Kids 3.9%. Brand‑name ≈ 53% of sales; **private‑label ≈ 47%** across exclusive brands (BKE, Buckle Black, Daytrip, Departwest, etc.).
- **Supplier concentration:** **Axis Denim = 20.6% of net sales** (private‑label denim); no other vendor >10%; **no long‑term or exclusive supply contracts**. Centralized distribution from Kearney, NE (also corporate HQ + online fulfillment — facility concentration risk).
- **E‑commerce:** Online sales $217.1M in FY2025 (+9.8% YoY from $197.7M) = **16.7% of net sales** (16.2% FY2024, 16.4% FY2023). Online included in comparable‑store sales.
- **Competition (10‑K):** "highly competitive"; competes mainly on customer service and merchandise selection; "many of the Company's competitors are considerably larger and have substantially greater financial, marketing, and other resources."
- **Balance‑sheet character:** no goodwill, no intangibles → **TBV = total equity**. No financial borrowings; the only "debt" is operating‑lease liabilities (ASC 842).

### Lease‑accounting convention (applied consistently)
Operating‑lease liabilities are treated as **debt‑like**: current lease liability is **excluded** from NIBCL (not netted against assets); ROU assets remain inside capital employed; EV total debt = total operating‑lease liabilities. This is internally consistent with the EV definition used in the cheapness gate. **Flag:** ASC 842 adoption in FY2019 mechanically lifted capital employed by ~$310M and lowered reported ROCE from FY2019 onward — an accounting artifact, not a deterioration. Ex‑lease EV/OI (7.49) is reported above as the cross‑check.

## D. Shareholder‑friendliness evidence (FY2026 DEF 14A + 10‑K)

- **Ownership / control:** Founder Daniel Hirschfeld 31.4% (16.2M sh); insiders as a group 38.4% (19.79M sh). **Single share class** (no dual‑class). BlackRock 9.2%, Vanguard 7.8%. Founder takes no incremental board compensation.
- **Compensation design:** Bonus pool formula‑linked to pre‑bonus net income (2.5% base pool + excess sharing); store/field comp is base‑plus‑commission. **Caveat:** CEO Dennis Nelson FY2025 total comp ≈ $11.7M (≈4.5% of operating income); statutory pay ratio 1,536:1 is distorted by the part‑time workforce (median teammate $7,622) — elevated absolute level, profit‑linked, not a governance strike.
- **Related‑party:** Hirschfeld Family Trust owes the Company $1.515M (legacy 1994–96 notes, 5%, secured). CFO Thomas Heacock is the CEO's son‑in‑law; the CEO's daughter is a VP; one further relative employed; aggregate relative comp ≈ $2.84M FY2025. Family‑business concentration = succession/governance watch‑item.
- **Buybacks:** no material repurchases; ~0.5%/yr share creep from stock comp (not repurchasing at highs).
- **Capital allocation:** ~100% FCF payout via regular + special dividends for ~15 years; no empire‑building M&A; modest high‑ROCE capex (~$45M/yr). **Exemplary.**
