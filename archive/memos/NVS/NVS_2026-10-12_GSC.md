# NVS — Novartis AG: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK (9.0% ROCE); Future WEAK (2+ forces); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (director pay; acquisitions); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** (prior: WATCH).

Novartis is now a focused innovative-medicines company with rising returns: ROCE has climbed from about 6% to 17% as Alcon (2019) and Sandoz (2023) left the balance sheet. The ten-year consolidated record, though, still averages 9.0% on capital employed, below the 10% floor, and the recent improvement is three years old. Two strong forces bear on the future: the three largest customers take 38% of sales and US payers are tightening, while generics now erode Entresto. Governance fails twice over: the non-executive Chair is paid about five times the outside-director cap, and acquired assets were written down by 30% or more twice within three years under the same CEO. At $143.75 the stock trades at 19.3× EV/OI against a $34.87 trigger. A flip needs the window to roll past the conglomerate years, a Chair fee inside the cap, and a clean deal record.

## 2. Understanding

Novartis discovers, develops, manufactures and sells patented prescription medicines (cardiovascular, immunology, neuroscience, oncology, including radioligand and gene therapies) that drug wholesalers, retail chains, hospitals and government health systems buy and payers reimburse, in over 100 countries with about 43% of 2025 net sales in the US. It is a Basel-headquartered Swiss AG with one reportable segment since the 2023 Sandoz spin-off, among the largest drug companies in the world. **PASS.** Lens STD.

## 3. Quality — past record

## 4. Quality — future

The three largest customers take 18%, 13% and 7% of net sales: a customer concentration. No strong moat carries it, because the position is not stable: US generics entered Entresto in Q3 2025 and the portfolio moat renews only through new patents. Customer power is therefore STRONG, reinforced by Medicare price negotiation under the IRA. Substitutes are STRONG as well, with generic and biosimilar entry at each loss of exclusivity and in-class rivals in every major franchise. Supplier power and entrants are WEAK. The moat is government-granted exclusivity, renewed by R&D, stable at portfolio level; market growth YES. Two STRONG forces: **WEAK.**
