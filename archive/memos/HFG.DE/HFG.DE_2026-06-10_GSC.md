# HFG.DE — HelloFresh SE: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | WEAK | WEAK | FAIL | FAIR | **AVOID** |

A triple gate failure. HelloFresh is the consolidated winner of the meal-kit category — and that category turned out not to be worth winning at scale. Returns on capital collapsed from COVID-era highs to roughly zero on reported EBIT; the moat is a cost/scale advantage inside a shrinking niche surrounded by cheap substitutes; and shareholders have rejected the remuneration report three years running while the company bought back stock near peak multiples. The stock now trades below book value with a genuine FCF yield, which is why it screens FAIR rather than EXPENSIVE — but under this framework price cannot rescue a business that fails all three quality gates. To flip the verdict, HelloFresh would need a multi-year record of double-digit ROCE on reported (not adjusted) operating income plus a governance reset endorsed by its own shareholders. Neither exists today.

## 2. Understanding

HelloFresh SE is a Berlin-headquartered, Frankfurt-listed European company (Societas Europaea) selling subscription meal kits and ready-to-eat meals direct-to-consumer to households under eight brands (HelloFresh, EveryPlate, Green Chef, Chefs Plate, Factor, Youfoodz, Good Chop, The Pets Table) across 18 countries — ~62% of FY2025 revenue from North America — where it is the global meal-kit category leader but a shrinking business overall (revenue −12% in 2025, mid-single-digit decline guided for 2026), restructuring its declining meal-kit core to fund a contested ready-to-eat expansion. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Only ~9 listed years exist (Nov-2017 IPO); 2017–2019 were loss-making (net income −€92m, −€83m, −€10m), so the full window cannot have cleared any return floor. On the fully documented 2021–2025 stretch, ROCE_xc fell 62% → 36% → 11% → 0.2% → 7.4% — a sustained collapse that forces a downgrade under P4 irrespective of level — and reported EBIT (including €106–181m restructuring/impairment) was negative in 2024 and ~zero in 2025. OI/share fell ~69% from 2021 to 2025; TBV/share fell ~43%. L/E reached 211% in 2025, above the 200% flag without exceptional returns (mostly IFRS-16 leases; net debt/FCF 3.0×, interest cover 2.7×). FCF turned genuinely positive only in 2023–25, largely by cutting capex. **WEAK** — at most two of ten years clear 15% ROCE_xc, with a forced-downgrade decline on top.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: PASS — millions of household customers, commodity food suppliers, no concentration on either side. Forces: UNFAVOURABLE — substitutes HIGH (groceries, restaurants, delivery apps, retail meal kits), rivalry HIGH (CookUnity, Marley Spoon, retail RTE), entrants MEDIUM, customer power MEDIUM (zero switching cost, chronic churn), supplier power LOW. Moat: Cost (scale procurement, vertically integrated fulfilment, ~75% share of its niche) — real within meal kits, but economically eroding: the niche is shrinking, churn forces perpetual marketing spend (~25% of revenue), and the cost advantage confers no pricing power against the substitute set, as the ROCE collapse demonstrates. Market growth: NO for meal kits; RTE grows but is contested and lost money in 2025. **WEAK** — eroding moat amid unfavourable forces.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation: STRIKE — shareholders rejected the remuneration reports for FY2021, FY2022 and FY2023 (support 42% → 37%, among the lowest ~0.8% of say-on-pay outcomes globally), with the largest holder (Active Ownership Capital, 7.7%) and Norges Bank voting against. CEO Richter's ~5–6% stake is genuine skin in the game, but pay structure was repudiated by owners while the equity fell ~90%. Buybacks: supporting strike — €141m repurchased in 2022 at multiples far above conservative value (EV/OI then exceeded the P9 sell signal) against SBC of €55–94m/yr. RPTs: nothing material disclosed. Dividends: none; retention defensible only while returns were high, which they no longer are. **FAIL** (one strike disqualifies; here there are two).

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- The €300m efficiency reset could stick: meal-kit AEBITDA margin hit 13.5% in 2025 and management guides RTE back to profitability in 2026 — sustained reported-EBIT recovery would force a Past re-grade over time.
- Activist pressure (AOC 7.7%) could deliver the governance reset that clears the SF strike.
- Cash fell €276m in 2025 on buybacks and debt repayment; continued repurchases amid declining revenue could stress the balance sheet if FCF disappoints.
- Revenue is still shrinking (−13% in Q1 2026); cost cuts cannot outrun a melting top line indefinitely.
- US RTE retention problems recurring would remove the only structural growth leg.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

AVOID → WATCH requires all three: (1) two consecutive years of ROCE_xc ≥ 10% on reported EBIT; (2) an AGM-approved remuneration report; (3) revenue stabilisation. Price alone cannot flip the verdict. ## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth.** Customers: millions of subscribing households worldwide; no customer concentration disclosure — broad, will not consolidate. PASS. Suppliers: food producers, packaging, logistics across 18 countries; commodity inputs, no single-supplier dependence disclosed. PASS. → Breadth PASS.

**Five forces.**

| Force | Threat | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer power | MEDIUM | Individually powerless but zero switching cost, promo-trained, chronic churn — retention is management's own #1 stated problem |
| Supplier power | LOW | Commodity food inputs, fragmented supplier base, HFG is a scale buyer |
| Substitutes | HIGH | Supermarkets, restaurants, delivery apps, retail meal kits and frozen RTE all solve "dinner" cheaper or easier |
| New entrants | MEDIUM | Easy to launch, brutal to scale (Blue Apron's collapse); capital no longer chasing the category |
| Rivalry | HIGH | CookUnity, Marley Spoon, retail private-label kits; RTE segment posted an AEBITDA loss in 2025 amid competition |

Overall: **UNFAVOURABLE**.

**Moat.** Cost (one of Marshall's six): largest global meal-kit player with vertically integrated fulfilment, scale procurement and ~75% share of its US/EU niche; meal-kit AEBITDA margin of 13.5% (2025) evidences a real relative cost advantage. Direction: **eroding in economic effect** — the niche itself is shrinking, the advantage confers no pricing power against the substitute set, and the decade's ROCE trajectory (62% → 0% → 7%) is the moat's own testimony. No brand premium (discount sub-brands EveryPlate/Chefs Plate compete on price), no network effects, no switching costs, no ingrainedness (churn), no government protection.

**Market growth.** NO for meal kits (group revenue −12% in 2025, −3–6% cc guided 2026); RTE structurally growing but contested and loss-making for HFG in 2025. Marshall's caveat cuts the other way here: this is a flat-to-declining market *without* a durable moat — the worst quadrant.

- MCap/TBV = 605.7 / 281.3 = **2.2** (< 3 ✓).

Three of four pass; the laggard exceeds tolerance → not CHEAP; miss is 51% above threshold → **FAIR**.

**Threshold-implied prices (diluted 148m):** P(MCap/FCF = 8) = 8 × 167.3 / 148 = €9.04. P(EV/OI = 7) = (7 × 104.4 + 212.0 − 708.5 + 2.5) / 148 = €1.60. P(MCap/BV = 5) = €22.64. P(MCap/TBV = 3) = €5.70.

**Binding-constraint logic:** EV/OI is the sole failing metric and the other three pass with room, so the one-average allowance applies to it: CHEAP when EV/OI ≤ 8.75 → P ≈ €2.83. **Trigger band: CHEAP below ≈ €2.85.** Caveats: (i) band assumes FY2025 ex-restructuring OI of €104.4m persists — on reported EBIT (≈ 0) the metric clears at no positive price; (ii) the FCF leg of the band is flattered by IFRS-16 as noted. Mechanical threshold arithmetic, not a price target — and moot while three quality gates fail.
