# ZIP.AX — Zip Co Limited: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; Shareholder-friendliness FAIL; EXPENSIVE — **AVOID on three gates**.

Zip is a consumer lender that has just turned profitable after losing A$2.14bn over the ten years to FY2025 while diluting shareholders 6.2-fold. The recent improvement is real and large — trailing net profit A$109.2m, 1H26 cash EBTDA up 85.6%, US volumes compounding above 40% — and still nowhere near any bar here. What stands between this and a BUY is not one gate but three, and the binding one is behavioural: A$100m repurchased at an average A$2.87, the second tranche near A$3.50 or roughly 42× trailing earnings, seventeen months after raising A$267m at about A$1.56, with a further A$50m programme running through 2026 under a framework that publishes no price discipline. Two profitable years cannot lift the past record; four or five would begin to.

## 2. Understanding

Zip Co Limited lends small unsecured sums to retail consumers at point of sale in the United States (75% of volume) and Australia/New Zealand, earning interest and account fees from those consumers and merchant fees from about 90,600 retailers, funding its A$3.0bn receivables book through securitisation warehouses and asset-backed bonds rather than deposits, as a single-brand, vertically integrated originator-servicer listed in Sydney since 2015. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

The financial-institution substitution rule applies (§2·1A, extended to a non-deposit-funded lender). Worst-of-two normalized ROE for FY2016–FY2025 is **−83.8%** (−65.6% end-of-period), against a 10% adequacy floor; cumulative attributable result −A$2,138m. The literal all-metric reading agrees: worst-of-four ROCE −14.1%, FCFROCE −5.9%. Earnings per share were negative in eight of ten years, so the growth test has no computable base; book value per share rose from A$0.056 to A$0.549 but entirely through A$2.59bn of issuance against A$2.18bn of accumulated losses, which is dilution, not compounding. Leverage breaches P5 on both readings — total liabilities 393.9% of equity at FY2025 and 499.0% at December 2025; interest-bearing debt 341.6% and 382.4% — with none of the P6 relief conditions present. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth passes on the consumer leg (6.59m actives, 54.9m transactions); the merchant leg is undisclosed and logged INDETERMINATE. Supplier breadth **fails** — ten named facilities resolve to one effective supplier, the wholesale securitisation market, and FY2022 proved the dependency when funding repriced and exiting the corporate facility cost A$88.9m. Forces: customer power STRONG (switching between wallets is free); supplier power STRONG (no deposits, seven facilities maturing before 2028); substitutes STRONG (cards, debit, three scaled rivals, doing without); new entrants STRONG (Klarna, Affirm, PayPal, banks and platform wallets already in the checkout). Four of four. No moat: US distribution runs through Stripe and similar platforms Zip does not own. Market growth YES. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Capital allocation is **ADVERSE**. The A$100m programme closed December 2025 executed at 34.2–46.8× FY25 earnings and 24.0–41.7× trailing, against yardsticks of 8× and ~28.6×; A$24.6m more sterilised employee-equity dilution at the same prices. The decisive forward evidence: a fresh A$50m programme running since February 2026, no published valuation criterion, stock still four to six times the band. Compensation adds one flag — CEO A$2.795m clears 5% of FY25 net income but not the normalized read, where both legs are negative. Fees and related-party disclosure are clean. Serial M&A write-offs are **not** relied on. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Credit: the receivables book has never been tested through a full US consumer downturn, and net bad debts already drifted to 1.73% of volume from 1.56%.
- Funding: every facility is dated, seven mature before 2028, and warehouse markets close quickly when they close.
- Regulation: a US State Attorneys General inquiry into BNPL is open, and Australian credit rules now capture the product.
- Concentration of distribution in platforms Zip does not control (Stripe, wallets, Amazon Australia warrants at A$4.70).
- Upside risk to this verdict: earnings are inflecting fast, and a US dual listing could re-rate the stock far above any framework band.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Past cannot reach ADEQUATE until the window sheds the FY2021–FY2023 losses, which runs to FY2031; nothing management does accelerates that. Future flips only if funding diversifies structurally — a licence, deposits, a genuinely multi-sourced stack — and two force calls turn. Shareholder-friendliness is the fast route and the one to watch: it flips if repurchases stop, restart with published price discipline, or execute at or under conservative worth. A credit cycle or a failed warehouse renewal would confirm the verdict, not change it. Price would need to fall about 77% to reach the band. ## C. Calculation notes

**Latest-year builds (31 December 2025 balance sheet, A$'000).**
- Capital employed: total assets 3,977,579 − NIBCL (trade and other payables 757,044 + employee provisions 16,947 = 773,991) = **CE_wc 3,203,588**; less cash 169,624 + restricted cash 367,340 + term deposit 4,600 = 541,564 → **CE_xc 2,662,024**.
- Tangible book: equity 664,103 − goodwill 207,186 − identifiable intangibles 41,891 = **TBV 415,026**.
- Enterprise value: market capitalisation 3,622.6m + borrowings 2,528.4m + lease liabilities 11.1m = **EV_no-cash A$6,162m**; less cash and equivalents 541.6m = **EV_all-cash A$5,621m**. Both are reported for completeness and both are **n.m. as valuation denominators** — Zip's borrowings fund the earning asset, so adding them to enterprise value without adding customer receivables double-counts the balance sheet. This is the mechanical reason the substitution rule is needed.

**Substitution-rule treatment (§2·1A) — the session's most consequential judgment.** The rule names deposit-funded lenders, insurers, securities-heavy holding companies and captive-finance-dominated groups. Zip is none of these literally: it is warehouse- and ABS-funded and holds no banking licence. It is admitted as an extension of the class on the rule's stated rationale, because the structural facts the rule exists for are all present: customer receivables are 75.7% of total assets; interest expense of A$248.9m sits above the profit line as cost of goods, leaving no separable operating-income subtotal; and cash from operations is dominated by movements in the receivables book, swinging from +A$272.5m in FY2024 to −A$49.8m in FY2025 purely because the book grew A$525.4m. Cheapness therefore anchors on a net-income proxy rather than MCap/FCF, since CFO is not economically meaningful. **The extension does not decide anything** — the literal reading in B2 reaches WEAK independently, and a second session rejecting the extension would arrive at the same verdict by a different route.

**Other treatments.** Goodwill retained in capital employed throughout — Zip was acquisitive across FY2017–FY2022, so the non-acquisitive deduction is unavailable. Leases: IFRS 16 adopted from FY2020; lease liabilities treated as debt-equivalent in capital employed, enterprise value and leverage. P18 pre-adoption capitalisation was tested and **not triggered** — undisclosed operating-lease commitments in FY2016–FY2019 were immaterial against total assets for a business whose assets are a loan book. No IFRS interest reclassification is required: interest paid already sits inside operating cash flow in every year. P20 is **not active** — no capex ramp exists to normalise; capex has run at A$17m–30m a year against A$1bn of revenue. No captive-finance consistency issue arises; the finance arm is the whole company.

**Bias check.** One live bias identified and handled: **hotness**. Zip's operating momentum is genuinely spectacular — cash EBTDA up 85.6%, US volumes up 44%, the stock up 363% in calendar 2024 — and the pull toward reading two profitable years as a new regime is strong. It was handled by holding the P1 window at its full ten years rather than reaching for a shortened one, and by testing whether the improvement could carry any single gate on its own: it cannot, since Past fails on returns, growth and leverage independently, and Future fails on breadth before any earnings figure is consulted. **Consistency** was checked and is not live — this is a first analysis with no prior verdict to defend. **Miscontrast** was checked: the register acquired an unrelated `ZIP` (ZipRecruiter) row the same day, and the two names were kept strictly separate; neither verdict informed the other. **Peculiarity** noted and discounted — the ticker collision is an administrative fact, not information.
