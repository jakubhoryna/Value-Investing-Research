# APTV — Aptiv PLC: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK (worst-of-four normalized ROCE 8.1% against a 10% floor); Future WEAK (customer breadth FAIL — one global OEM at 11% of H1-2026 sales, waiver denied — plus three strong forces); shareholder-friendliness FAIL (flag pair: fiscal-year compensation read breached 2.33×, capital allocation CAUTION); cheapness EXPENSIVE at 12.5×/11.9× EV/OI — **AVOID**.

Aptiv sells the sensing, compute and interconnect content that lets vehicles perceive and act, with real engineering depth and design-in lasting a model cycle. What it does not do is earn a return on the capital that content requires: a base that doubled to $18.5bn produced 8.1% on the least flattering reading and 10.0% on the most, while operating income per share compounded 1.3% a year. The Versigent spin-off removed 44% of revenue and halved leverage, but the retained half is the half whose goodwill was cut $648m when customers postponed software-defined-vehicle programmes. Not a pricing problem: at the band, two quality gates still fail.

## 2. Understanding

Aptiv PLC is a Jersey-incorporated, Swiss-tax-resident, NYSE-listed Tier-1 manufacturer of vehicle electrical and electronic hardware — connectors, high-voltage interconnects, cable management, radar and vision sensors, centralised compute — plus embedded software and lifecycle services (Wind River), sold on requirements contracts to the world's largest automotive OEMs (one at ~11% of sales) and increasingly to commercial-vehicle, aerospace, telecom and industrial customers, built from a best-cost footprint in Mexico, Eastern Europe, North Africa and China, and the ~$12.7bn-revenue successor to Delphi Automotive after spinning out its wire-harness arm on 1 April 2026. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE 8.1% (best 10.0%) and FCFROCE 6.2% both miss the ADEQUATE floor, so the verdict rests on level, not on P4: returns are erratic — 13.0% in 2019, 5.0% in COVID-hit 2020, 6.4% in 2025 — rather than in sustained decline. Capital employed doubled from $9.1bn to $18.5bn, chiefly through Wind River and Intercable; operating income did not follow. Operating income per share compounded 1.3% a year against the 3% floor. Book value per share grew 18.8% a year, but that is the 2020 equity-and-preferred raise plus a $2.1bn 2023 deferred-tax credit, not operating accretion. Leverage never breached P5. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

One global OEM took 11% of H1-2026 sales (12% a year earlier), the top ten 41%: customer breadth **FAILS**, waiver denied — these customers backward-integrate into vehicle software, annual price step-downs and termination for convenience are the opposite of prohibitive switching costs, and some twenty-five buyers exist. Supplier breadth passes, semiconductors logged as the look-through concern. Forces: customer power STRONG; suppliers WEAK; substitutes STRONG (deferred software-defined architectures; silicon vendors selling full-stack platforms); new entrants STRONG in Intelligent Systems, WEAK in Engineered Components. Three strong. The moat is design-in switching costs on cost scale, eroding because it does not hold price. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation: the top total, $19.25m, is 2.33× 5% of the lower FY2025 leg (net income $165m, carrying a $648m impairment) but 0.28× on the trailing-three-year read and under half the ~$40m indexed trigger — single-read breach, one flag, not a strike. Capital allocation **CAUTION**: $4.1bn of FY2024 repurchases at 14.4×/17.1× EV/OI, part-funded by $2.9bn of new notes, then the Wind River write-down; $1.8bn of authorisation still executes near $65 with no published price discipline, though Versigent proceeds repaid $2.1bn of debt. Related-party and director fees not retrieved — INDETERMINATE. **FAIL** (flag pair).

- Software-defined-vehicle timing keeps slipping; a second Intelligent Systems impairment would follow the first.
- FY2026 revenue guidance was cut $300m on China weakness and delayed launches — the post-spin growth case is unproven.
- Annual contractual price step-downs demand perpetual cost deflation simply to hold margin.
- Separation and semiconductor-resilience spending turned H1 free cash flow negative; the $750m full-year figure is guidance.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Shareholder-friendliness can clear on one leg: a normal FY2026 net income restores the compensation read. Next review: the FY2026 10-K (~February 2027), the first clean post-spin record; earlier if price approaches $20.

## C. Calculation notes

**Latest-year (FY2025) capital employed.** NIBCL = accounts payable $3,157m + accrued liabilities $1,799m = $4,956m. CE_wc = $23,413m − $4,956m = **$18,457m**. CE_xc = $18,457m − (cash $1,851m + restricted $3m + STI nil) = **$16,603m**.

**NIBCL judgment call.** Aptiv's condensed balance sheets disclose only accounts payable and a single "accrued liabilities" line. Income taxes payable, deferred income and the current portion of operating lease liabilities are all inside that line and cannot be split. Applying §2·1A's default (payables + accrued expenses) therefore deducts an undisclosed current lease liability that the lease rule says should stay in CE. Estimated magnitude: well under 1% of CE. Direction of bias: CE understated, ROCE overstated — i.e. against the analyst and in the company's favour, so the WEAK verdict is not an artefact of the call. Goodwill retained in CE throughout: Aptiv is acquisitive.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: FAIL.** Q2-2026 10-Q: the ten largest customers were ~44% of net sales for the quarter and ~41% for the half, "which included approximately 10% and 11%, respectively, to an individual Global OEM" (prior-year comparatives 12% and 12%). The disclosure sentence exists and puts one customer above a tenth of revenue. For context, the pre-spin FY2022 10-K table showed no customer above 10% (General Motors 9%, Stellantis 9%, Ford 8%, Volkswagen 8%, Tesla 5%, Geely 5%): removing the wire-harness business concentrated what remained.

**Captive-customer waiver: DENIED.** All three conditions must hold and none does cleanly. (i) The concentrated customers demonstrably can and do backward-integrate — in-house software stacks and electronics organisations at the largest OEMs are the sector's defining trend, and Aptiv's own goodwill impairment traces to OEM decisions about their own architectures. (ii) Switching costs are real but not prohibitive: supply agreements "generally provide for annual reductions in pricing … over the period of production" of one to three percent, customers reserve termination for convenience, and the FY2022 10-K states plainly that OEMs "have possessed significant leverage over their suppliers, including us". (iii) The concentration is not structural in the book's sense — Aptiv serves the twenty-five largest OEMs; buyers are many, not few. Precedent alignment: ASML/TSMC/QCOM were granted waivers on genuinely un-integratable, few-buyer positions; Visteon was denied on facts closely resembling these.

**Breadth — suppliers: PASS.** Inputs are copper and petroleum-based resins procured regionally from many vendors, with contractual copper pass-through in the power-distribution lines. The look-through concern is semiconductors — the Q2-2026 call flags continued "investments in supply chain resiliency for semiconductors" — but multiple qualified vendors serve each socket and no single supplier is disclosed above a tenth of cost of sales plus operating expenses. Logged, not failed.

**Four forces (binary; segment-level where they differ).**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|-------|------|-----------------|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | One OEM at 11% of sales, top ten at 41%; contractual annual price step-downs of 1–3%, requirements contracts with no minimum volumes, termination for convenience, and re-sourcing rights at model changeover. |
| Supplier bargaining power | WEAK | Many regional vendors for copper and resins with pass-through clauses; semiconductor sockets have multiple qualified sources. |
| Threat of substitutes | **STRONG** | Doing-without is live — OEMs deferred software-defined-vehicle architectures, producing the FY2025 $648m Wind River goodwill impairment — and silicon vendors sell competing full-stack sense-and-compute platforms. |
| Threat of new entrants | **STRONG** (Intelligent Systems) / WEAK (Engineered Components) | The FY2022 10-K itself records that these markets "have attracted, and may continue to attract, new and disruptive entrants … particularly in countries such as China or in areas of evolving vehicle technologies such as automated driving technologies and advanced software"; connectors retain scale, tooling and qualification barriers. |

**Tally: three strong** — twice the book's stop signal.

**Moat.** Single identified source: **switching costs**, arising from per-platform design-in validated over a three-to-seven-year model life, with cost scale from the best-cost-country footprint as support. Direction: **eroding**. A moat that cannot hold price is not holding much, and Aptiv's own contracts concede annual price reductions; the spin-off also removed the 44%-of-revenue volume base that underwrote much of the cost advantage, and the Wind River write-down marks the software leg of the moat down rather than up.

**Market growth: MIXED.** Content per vehicle rises with electrification and active safety, and non-automotive revenue grew 12% in Q2-2026 with $10bn of year-to-date bookings; against that, global light-vehicle production is broadly flat, and FY2026 revenue guidance was cut $300m at the midpoint (to $12.6–12.8bn) on China weakness and delayed programme launches.

**Verdict: WEAK** — breadth FAIL alone is sufficient; three strong forces and an eroding moat are independently sufficient.
