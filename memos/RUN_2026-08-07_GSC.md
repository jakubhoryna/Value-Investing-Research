# RUN — Sunrun Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; SF FAIL (flag pair — P17 breach + capital-allocation CAUTION); cheapness EXPENSIVE despite a 0.70× price-to-book — **AVOID (four gates)**.

Sunrun is the register's sharpest illustration of why the primaries decide and the qualifiers only investigate. The stock trades at **0.70× book and 0.70× tangible book** — a bargain on the qualifier table — while both primaries are unmeasurable, because no year of the window carries positive operating income or positive free cash flow. Cumulative OI FY2021–25 is **−$7.13bn** and cumulative FCF **−$15.18bn**, against $14.75bn of debt built to fund it. That is the design: Sunrun raises non-recourse debt and tax-equity, installs a fleet it owns, and collects a twenty-five-year annuity — an infrastructure fund whose income statement is dominated by depreciation and by losses allocated to partners. The framework has no substitution rule for that structure, so it is read literally, and fails every quality test.

## 2. Understanding

Sunrun designs, installs, finances and maintains residential solar and battery systems across the United States, mostly not by selling equipment but by signing homeowners to 20–25-year leases and power-purchase agreements, so the payer is the household over decades and the economics are a project-finance vehicle funding an owned fleet with non-recourse debt and tax-equity partnerships; a Nasdaq-listed San Francisco corporation, founded 2007, listed 2015, the largest US residential solar company. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Every year shows negative operating income and negative free cash flow. Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **−9.43%**, worst FCFROCE **−18.51%** (bracket across both NIBCL bases: −7.33% to −9.43%). Σ OI **−$7.13bn**; Σ FCF **−$15.18bn**. ΔOI/sh and ΔFCF/sh n.m.; **ΔBV/sh −21.1%/yr**; ΔTBV/sh +5.7%/yr only because $4.28bn of goodwill was written to zero. **L/E 566%** (616% in FY2024) against a P5 flag of 200%, with no P6 condition available. **WEAK** on every limb.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: customers **PASS** — atomised households, none material. Suppliers **FAIL** on look-through: the effective input is not panels (several vendors stand ready) but **tax-equity and securitisation capital**, supplied by a handful of institutions and re-priced by federal policy. Forces: customer power **WEAK** (25-year contracts, high switching costs), supplier power **STRONG**, substitutes **STRONG** (grid electricity at a regulated price, cash-purchase solar, doing without), new entrants **STRONG**. Tally **3 STRONG**. Moat: **Government** — the tax-credit and net-metering regime — and **eroding**: NEM 3.0 cut California export credits in 2023 and the federal §25D credit has wound down, pushing the industry into third-party ownership under §48E. A moat that can be legislated away belongs to someone else. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 (FY2025): CEO Powell **$8,331,132** against a **$22.50m** ceiling (5% × net income attributable to common $449.95m, the only positive leg) — passes the year read; the FY2023–25 normalized read has **no positive leg**: **breach = one flag**. The judgment keeping this a flag rather than a strike is named — FY2025 income is positive only *attributable to common* (group net loss −$1,009m), and on the group figure neither read has a positive leg. Absolute trigger untripped; director retainer $70,000; RPT not retrieved. P19 **CAUTION** — no repurchases, no dividends (both findings), but ~$2.5bn a year of capex funded by ~$4bn of gross debt issuance into an asset base that has never earned a positive operating income, with the entire $4.28bn Vivint goodwill written off. **FAIL — flag pair.**

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth.** Customers: **PASS** — the payer is the individual household under a 20–25-year lease or PPA; no single customer approaches 1% of revenue, let alone 10%, and the base cannot consolidate. Suppliers: **FAIL**. Nominal supplier counts are irrelevant here and the framework says so; looking through to the effective supplier gives two candidates, and the binding one is capital. Panels, inverters and batteries have several ready vendors, so that limb would pass under the bulk-terms exception. **Tax-equity and securitisation capital does not:** it is supplied by a small number of financial institutions, its availability is set by federal tax policy rather than by negotiation, and its withdrawal halts installation regardless of homeowner demand. This is precisely the "securitisation market" example §2·1B gives for looking past nominal counts. Breadth therefore **FAILS**, and the captive-customer waiver is not applicable (it addresses customer-side concentration, not supplier-side).
