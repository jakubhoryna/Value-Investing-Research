# 4324.KL — Hengyuan Refining Company Berhad: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK (losses); Future WEAK (customer conc.); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (RPT); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past + Future + SF)**.

Hengyuan runs one refinery at Port Dickson, Malaysia, turning imported crude into diesel, petrol, jet fuel and LPG, and sold 89% of its 2025 revenue to one customer, Shell. Over FY2016–FY2025 its operating income summed to a loss on about MYR 3bn of capital, with four loss years running from 2022, and liabilities reached 3.8 times equity in 2022 before a 2025 rights issue. The controlling shareholder's group charged it MYR 35.9m in 2025 for management and advisory services. A 2026 margin surge (first-half pre-tax profit MYR 1.13bn) changes none of this; only an end to the charges, a positive decade record and less reliance on Shell would.

## 2. Understanding

Hengyuan Refining Company Berhad — formerly Shell Refining Company (Federation of Malaya) Berhad, incorporated in 1960, listed on Bursa Malaysia's Main Market, and since December 2016 controlled (55.7% at March 2026) by Malaysia Hengyuan International Limited of China's Shandong Hengyuan Petrochemical group — is a single-site, 156,000-barrel-a-day crude oil refinery at Port Dickson, Negeri Sembilan, that buys imported crude from global suppliers and refines it into commodity fuels and feedstocks (gasoil 40.0% of FY2025 yield, petrol 38.5%, jet fuel 8.6%, LPG 6.9%, plus propylene, naphtha, fuel-oil components and sulphur), paid for mainly by Shell's Malaysian marketing companies under a ten-year product offtake agreement priced off Singapore Platts quotes (one customer took 89% of FY2025 revenue of MYR 13.17bn), with the rest sold to other Malaysian fuel marketers (Petronas, Petron, Five) and exporters; over 80% of products are sold in Malaysia. One reportable segment. PASS.

## 3. Quality — past record

STD lens; no G6 classes. Operating income (J1, with net currency and derivative gains taken out in the years they were positive) was negative in six of ten years, so every worst-of-four average is negative: ROCE −1.9% over FY2016–FY2025 and −8.7% over five years; FCFROCE on maintenance-capex FCF (constructed tier) −3.4% and −4.8%, all-capex −4.3%. Even the operating line as reported, hedging and currency gains included, gives 3.1% and −7.9%. Per-share OI and FCF growth reads are n.m.; a J6 limb (b) decline fires; L/E is 275% at FY2025. **WEAK** — losses (leverage, decline and growth also fail).

## 4. Quality — future

One customer, Shell, took 89% of FY2025 revenue (85% in 2024, 59–71% in 2018–2023): a customer-concentration flag. No strong moat carries it. The refinery prices off Singapore quotes, holds no evidenced dominant share, and lost money in 2022–2025 while selling mainly to that customer, so the flag is a STRONG customer-power call with no containment. Supplier power is WEAK (crude from many global sellers); substitutes WEAK within the window (domestic demand exceeds domestic supply); entrants WEAK (scale and permits). No moat; market growth MIXED. **WEAK**.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

The controller's group charged MYR 12.6m for central management, business-support and administrative services and MYR 23.3m for technical and research advisory in FY2025 (MYR 20.0m in 2024, MYR 31.2m in 2023). That is half of administrative expenses and 25 times the CEO's pay, for functions management is already paid to perform: a glaring RPT and a hard strike. Hairiest pay number: MYR 1.42m (CEO Yin LuJiang, FY2025), below the loss-year token line; other executives' pay is printed only in aggregate. Management holds no shares: NOT ALIGNED. No repurchases or acquisitions. **FAIL** at step 1 (`RPT`).
