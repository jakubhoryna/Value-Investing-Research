# LISN.SW — Chocoladefabriken Lindt & Sprüngli AG: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | STRONG | ADEQUATE | **FAIL** (P19 hard strike) | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** |

A world-class business that fails the model on capital allocation, not economics. Lindt is the dominant global premium-chocolate brand with a genuinely strong operating record — mid-to-high-teens operating ROCE, ~6.5% per-share OI growth, 30 consecutive years of dividend increases, and low leverage. But it repurchases its own shares at ~30–40× earnings: the 2025 programme bought registered shares at avg CHF 112,643 and PCs at CHF 11,340, an implied **EV/OI ≈ 28× and MCap/FCF ≈ 54×** — well past the P19 20× hard-strike line, and at a premium to worth rather than a discount, so no mitigation applies. That is an automatic SF FAIL → AVOID, the same failure that just placed Nestlé on the register. Nothing but a durable change in buyback pricing discipline (or a price collapse of ~80% that made repurchases below-worth) flips this; neither is in prospect. The stock is also plainly EXPENSIVE (~24× EV/OI vs the 7× line), so even a hypothetical capital-allocation reform leaves it a WATCH far from range.

## 2. Understanding

Lindt & Sprüngli manufactures and sells premium branded chocolate (Lindt, Lindor, Excellence, Ghirardelli, Russell Stover, Caffarel) bean-to-bar, to grocery/mass retailers, ~560 own boutiques and >100 distributors across ~120 countries, as a vertically integrated, foundation-controlled Swiss dual-class listed group (registered shares vote; participation certificates do not), 180 years old and the clear global leader in the premium segment. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

STRONG on the operating record. On operating capital employed (excluding the CHF 2.5bn non-operating overfunded-pension surplus — a numerator/denominator consistency correction), worst-of-four normalized ROCE runs ~19% and FCFROCE ~13%; on the strict mechanical CE (pension surplus left in) the worst-of-four compresses to ROCE ~11–13% / FCFROCE ~8% (ADEQUATE) — the entire gap is the ring-fenced pension asset (logged, App. C). Per-share OI grew ~6.5%/yr (2016→2025) and FCF/share ~15%/yr ex the cocoa-distorted 2025; BV/share only ~3.6% but suppressed by heavy payout + buybacks (read in context). No sustained decline (2020 Covid dip fully recovered to record 2025 OI). L/E 83.6%, far inside the 200% flag. **STRONG.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS both sides: no single customer >10% of revenue (fragmented retail + consumers; the report notes limited client concentration); suppliers many (cocoa, sugar, dairy, packaging bought across origins/traders, none >10% of COGS), though record cocoa costs are a live margin risk. Forces: customer power WEAK, supplier/commodity power WEAK-but-caveated (Lindt passed 19% price in 2025 and held margin, evidencing pricing power over the cocoa spike, at the cost of −6.6% volume), substitutes contained (premium indulgence/gifting, but discretionary "doing-without" bit in the 2025 downturn), new entrants WEAK (brand + scale + heritage barriers). Moat: **BRAND**, dominant and stable-to-strengthening on premiumisation. Rated ADEQUATE rather than STRONG only because the cocoa-cost cycle and downturn volume pressure keep one force live. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**FAIL — P19 buyback hard strike.** 2025 repurchases (468 reg @ CHF 112,643; 23,840 PC @ CHF 11,340) imply EV/OI ≈ 28× and MCap/FCF ≈ 54× — above the 20× hard-strike line on both bases, and bought at a premium to conservative worth (not a Berkshire-style discount), so the below-worth exemption does not apply. Comp is clean: CEO A. Lechner total CHF 4.8m (2025) is ~2.5% of the lower of FCF/NI and far under the ~CHF 40m absolute flag; Executive Chair E. Tanner now takes only CHF 1.2m fixed. RPT: none material surfaced. Foundation-controlled dual-class is a logged caveat, not an independent strike. Dividends rational (30-yr growth). The buyback price is the strike.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- **Cocoa cost super-cycle:** record bean prices pressure gross margin industry-wide; Lindt offsets via price, but volume (−6.6% in 2025) shows the ceiling on pass-through.
- **Discretionary demand:** premium chocolate is a treat; deeper consumer weakness accelerates the volume-for-price trade the model already shows.
- **FX translation:** ~90%+ of sales outside CHF; a strong franc is a persistent reported-growth drag (−3.9% in 2025).
- **Valuation air:** at ~24× EV/OI the multiple is the risk — any growth disappointment de-rates a long way before reaching value.
- **Governance:** foundation/dual-class entrenchment means the expensive-buyback policy is unlikely to change from outside.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To AVOID → WATCH: a **REVIEW showing buybacks executed below 20× (or suspended)** clears the P19 strike — but even then the name is EXPENSIVE and would sit as a distant WATCH; to reach BUY additionally requires price into the ≈ CHF 16,700/reg band (~−80%), i.e. a severe drawdown. Not held, so no sell-flag line. **Next review:** FY2026 results (~Mar 2027) — re-test P19 against the new CHF 1bn 2026–29 programme launched 1 Jun 2026 (expected to strike again at prevailing multiples), refresh the band, and extend the record. Earlier only on a price collapse toward the band or a formal change to buyback pricing policy.
