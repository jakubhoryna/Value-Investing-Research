# CELH — Celsius Holdings, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS · Past **WEAK** · Future **WEAK** · SF **FAIL** · Cheapness **EXPENSIVE** → **AVOID**, on three gates independently.

Celsius sells a differentiated product into a category consumers keep wanting more of, and revenue compounded from $22.8m to $2,515.3m across the window. The decade's economics do not support the claim: ten years of operating income sum to $386.2m against a capital base now at $4.0bn, and six of the ten years were operating losses. The 2025 rebuild — $2,055.6m for Alani Nu, $307.6m for Rockstar and $598.8m to PepsiCo for enhanced distribution effort, funded by a $900m term loan and $935.8m of 5% preferred — bought scale and left the returns record unproven. Two customers pay for 54% of revenue, one of them a competitor holding board seats. Nothing flips on price; the quality gates must be re-earned over several clean years.

## 2. Understanding

Celsius Holdings is a Nevada-incorporated, Boca Raton-based owner and marketer of canned functional energy drinks and wellness beverages under the CELSIUS, Alani Nu and Rockstar brands, formulated in-house but manufactured predominantly by third-party co-packers alongside one owned plant, and sold not to consumers but to a handful of paying organisations — above all PepsiCo, simultaneously distributor, 5%-plus preferred holder and board-represented partner, which bought 43.2% of FY2025 revenue, and Costco at 10.8% — in a business 96.3% North American that converted in 2025 from a single-brand organic grower into a debt- and preferred-funded three-brand consolidator. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalised average ROCE is **5.03%** against P3's 10% floor; the spread is 5.03/7.87/10.36/28.79%, and only the flattering beginning-of-period ex-cash read looks respectable. Worst-of-four FCFROCE is 8.70%, clearing P2 — cash conversion is the one strong metric. Per-share growth is unreadable for most of the window because the base is negative; on the two computable years OI/share fell 41.6% then 15.6%. L/E is 184.4%, inside P5, but excludes $1,760.0m of mezzanine preferred; counting it, 333.3%. Beginning-CE ROCE_wc has fallen twice running (30.0 → 14.1 → 11.2%). **WEAK** — the level fails outright, before the trend.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **FAILS** on the customer side, and not narrowly: PepsiCo 43.2/54.7/59.4% of FY2025/24/23 revenue and 46.2% of receivables, Costco 10.8/11.6/12.0%. The captive-customer waiver is refused — Pepsi sells competing energy brands, and switching costs run the wrong way. Supplier breadth passes on the company's disclosure sentence, against a risk-factor admission that some materials come from few sources. Forces: customer **STRONG**, supplier WEAK, substitutes **STRONG** (coffee, RTD, pre-workout, doing without — zero switching cost), new entrants **STRONG** (Alani Nu went nought to a $2.1bn price in six years). Three strong. Moat: brand, **eroding** — FY2024 revenue grew 2.9% before the acquisitions arrived. Market growth YES. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

CEO total was $9.61m in FY2025 against 5% of the lower of FCF and net income — $5.40m on the fiscal-year read, $8.00m on the trailing-3y normalised read. Breach on **both** is a P17 hard strike. Independently, capital allocation is **ADVERSE**: a $300m authorisation opened 10 Nov 2025 with no expiry and no published price discipline has drawn $39.8m at ~$41.53 and $32.9m at ~$38.70 in Q1-2026, executing at 31–33× MCap/FCF and 85–89× EV/OI, 44–46× and 64–67× normalised. Directors are paid $205–235k, well inside the norm; insiders hold 2.33%. **FAIL**, twice over.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- PepsiCo holds distribution, two board designees and $1,760.0m of convertible preferred — a renegotiable relationship whose interests are not Celsius's.
- FY2025 acquisition consideration of $2,363.2m rests on $1,280.3m of indefinite-lived brand intangibles; an impairment would hit a balance sheet already at negative tangible book.

- FY2025 operating income is depressed by $327.5m of distributor termination fees, so FY2026 is the first observable post-Rockstar margin.
- A securities class action and a consolidated derivative action are outstanding.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Any one alone leaves AVOID standing. Downward: an intangible impairment, or a third consecutive beginning-CE ROCE decline firing P4. ## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: FAIL.** The 10-K's own concentration table discloses revenue from customers over 10%: Pepsi 43.2% (FY2025), 54.7% (FY2024), 59.4% (FY2023); Costco 10.8%, 11.6%, 12.0%. Receivables concentration is worse — Pepsi 46.2% and Costco 10.5% at FY2025. Q1-2026 related-party revenue was $461.7m of $782.6m (59.0%). Two customers over the threshold, one of them four times over.

*Captive-customer waiver: refused.* All three conditions must hold and none does. Pepsi is not prevented from backward-integrating — it owns and sells competing energy brands (it sold Rockstar to Celsius rather than exiting the category, and retains Mtn Dew and Gatorade energy lines); switching costs run against Celsius, not against Pepsi, since Pepsi's route-to-market serves hundreds of brands while Celsius has one primary route; and the concentration is contractual rather than structural — several national distribution networks exist. The $598.8m Celsius paid Pepsi in August 2025 for enhanced selling effort is direct evidence of which party holds the power.

**Breadth — suppliers: PASS (qualified).** The 10-K states packaging materials and most ingredients are sourced from multiple suppliers and that the company is not currently dependent on any single supplier — the disclosure sentence the test looks for. Against it: risk factors concede some raw materials come from a limited number of suppliers, the industry has experienced aluminium-can shortages, and manufacture is predominantly outsourced to co-packers. Look-through: the effective bottleneck is the can supply chain, which is concentrated industry-wide but has ready alternatives at the co-packer level. Pass, with the observation that the acquisition of Big Beverages in 2024 was itself a partial answer to this exposure.

**Four forces (binary).**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | One buyer takes 43.2% of revenue, holds two board designees and $1,760.0m of preferred, and was paid $598.8m to sell harder. |

**Tally: 3 strong.** The book's stop signal at ≥2. Future is WEAK on this alone, and separately on breadth.

**Moat.** Source: **brand** — the CELSIUS name carries a specific functional-beverage positioning that supports shelf presence and price. Mechanism: consumer pull created by sustained marketing spend (FY2025 selling and marketing $548.3m, 21.8% of revenue) converts into retailer shelf allocation, which in turn sustains trial. Direction: **eroding** at the core. FY2024 revenue grew 2.9% on a single-brand base; the response was to buy Alani Nu and Rockstar rather than to defend organically. A brand moat that must be replenished by acquisition of the brands taking your share is not stable.

**Market growth: YES.** Functional energy is expanding — pro-forma FY2025 revenue of $3,003.7m against pro-forma FY2024 $2,318.0m is +29.6% on a comparable three-brand basis, and functional energy was 92.9% of revenue. The category tailwind is genuine; guarded against the glow, it does not rescue breadth or the forces tally.

**Verdict: WEAK** — breadth FAIL, three strong forces, eroding moat. Any one would suffice.
