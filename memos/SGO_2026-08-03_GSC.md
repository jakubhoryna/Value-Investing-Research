# SGO — Compagnie de Saint-Gobain: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past **WEAK**; Future **ADEQUATE**; SF **PASS** (one flag); Cheapness **EXPENSIVE** — **AVOID** on Past.

Two things decide this memo, and neither is the price. First, Saint-Gobain's income statement carries a line labelled "Operating income" (€5,293m in FY2025) sitting *above* other business income and expense, and "Business income" (€4,551m) below it. The €742m between them is restructuring, impairment, litigation and disposal results. §2·1A forbids adding those back, so the operative figure is Business income — on which ROCE reads 9.4% and 8.9% in the two measurable years against the 10% floor, where the labelled line reads 11.0% and 10.8%. The gate turns on which line you take. Second, Saint-Gobain's website blocks this session, so FY2016–FY2020 could not be retrieved and the window is five years — the best stretch in the group's recent history.

## 2. Understanding

Compagnie de Saint-Gobain is a Courbevoie-headquartered French société anonyme, single share class, founded 1665, making light construction and high-performance materials — plasterboard, mineral-wool insulation, ceilings, building and vehicle glazing, construction chemicals and mortars, roofing and siding, ductile-iron pipe, abrasives, ceramics and polymers — sold to builders' merchants, contractors and industrial OEMs in ~75 countries by ~160,000 employees on €46.5bn of sales, organised by country rather than product line, about half exposed to renovation and infrastructure. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

On the framework basis FY2025 worst-of-four ROCE is **9.4%** with FCFROCE **7.2%**; FY2024 reads **8.9%** and **7.1%**. Both clear 10% only on the ex-cash variant (11.2%, 10.8%), so the operative worst-of-four fails in both measurable years. Leverage is comfortable — €35,727m of liabilities on €24,541m of book equity is **L/E 146%**, well inside the flag — so nothing rests on gearing. Cash generation is real: €3,464m of free cash flow on €5,638m CFO. The problem is the denominator — €60.8bn of assets, €19.7bn of it goodwill and intangibles from five years of acquisition, against an operating result flat since FY2022. **WEAK**, subject to the §8 caveat.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS** both sides structurally — sales run through thousands of merchants and contractors in 75 countries, and a cost base of commodity inputs and energy admits no 10% supplier. Forces are benign: customer power **moderate** (merchants consolidate, but Placo, Isover, CertainTeed and Weber are specified brands), supplier power **weak**, substitutes **weak** (competing products are rivalry, not substitution — the building still needs board and insulation), new entrants **weak** (capital-heavy lines, and freight economics make these local-density businesses). Zero strong forces. Moat: **scale and local density** reinforced by brand specification, direction **stable**, with a real tailwind from European energy-renovation regulation. **ADEQUATE** — a decent business earning a mediocre return on the capital it has assembled.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

CEO Benoit Bazin's **maximum** 2025 direct remuneration is €7,160,189 against a 5%-of-lower(FCF, NI) cap of **€144.2m** — 5.0% of the cap on the fattest possible number, and a fifth of the ~US$40m indexed trigger. Related-party dealings are immaterial. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: buybacks genuinely retire stock — €402m net, 4,243,098 shares cancelled in December, count down 497m to 493m — funded from free cash flow alongside a €1,085m dividend, but the authorisation's **€150 maximum price** sits against a €26–42 band, a ceiling tracking the market rather than worth. ## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- €19.7bn of goodwill and intangibles inflates the denominator; a further large deal without margin accretion pushes ROCE down again.
- European new-residential construction is weak and the renovation tailwind is policy-dependent.
- Energy and raw-material shocks hit gypsum, glass and mortar economics directly, as 2022 showed.
- Restructuring and impairment have run €0.7–0.9bn a year — the gap between the two income lines recurs, which is why the framework basis is the right one.
- North America is now the profit engine; a US housing turn would bite harder than the geographic mix suggests.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

The decisive open item is the record, not the business. **Re-test the moment FY2016–FY2020 can be retrieved**: if Business income across a full cycle clears 10% on the worst-of-four basis, Past moves to ADEQUATE and SGO becomes a WATCH candidate on price alone. The available evidence points the other way — both measurable years fail, and the five-year window covers the group's strongest stretch, so a longer record is likelier to lower the average than raise it. That asymmetry is why AVOID is honest despite the thin record. Reaching the band would take a fall to roughly €42, a 48% decline. Next review: FY2026 results (~late Feb 2027) and URD 2026, or earlier on a successful rebuild.

## C. Calculation notes

**OI definition (verdict-determinative).** Framework OI = **Business income**, the line struck after other business income and expense. Saint-Gobain's "Operating income" is a pre-exceptionals measure; adopting it would be an add-back of impairments and litigation, which §2·1A prohibits. Logged as the single most contestable call in this memo, alongside the record limitation.

**Goodwill stays in CE.** Saint-Gobain is demonstrably acquisitive — goodwill rose from €11,181m (FY2021) to €14,401m (FY2025), with €1,770m of share acquisitions in FY2025 alone and €3,331m in FY2024 — so the non-acquisitive deduction is unavailable.

**Provisions stay in CE.** Current portion of other liabilities and provisions (€829m at FY2025) is left inside capital employed on the same burden-of-proof reasoning applied to DG in this session's earlier memo: provisions are none of accounts payable, accrued expenses, taxes payable or deferred revenue.

**FCF = CFO − capex**, capex being "acquisitions of property, plant and equipment and intangible assets, and changes in amounts due to suppliers of fixed assets" (gross, not net of the €391m FY2025 disposal proceeds Saint-Gobain nets into its own free-cash-flow measure). P20 maintenance basis not active: no documented super-cycle, capex has run a steady 3.1–4.7% of sales.

**Interest.** The cash flow statement begins at net income with no add-back of financial expense, so interest paid (€240m net in FY2025) sits inside CFO as required. Lease interest (€102m) likewise. No IFRS reclassification. Lease liabilities (€3,164m) are treated as debt in CE, EV and L/E.

**TBV** = 24,541 − 14,401 − 5,296 = **€4,844m**, positive and meaningful, unlike the concession-heavy comparables — so MCap/TBV is a live qualifier here and it fails at 8.36×.

**Bias check.** Hope bias is the live one and it runs *against* leniency: the temptation is to accept the company's flattering operating-income line because it is labelled "operating income" and would produce a cleaner, more interesting verdict. Cleverness bias noted on the same point in the opposite direction — the definitional catch is satisfying enough to over-weight, which is why Appendix B3 states the alternative grading explicitly rather than burying it. Consistency hazard: no position held, no prior memo, no register row.
