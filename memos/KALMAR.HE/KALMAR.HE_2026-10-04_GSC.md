## 2. Understanding

Kalmar Corporation is a Helsinki-headquartered Finnish company, listed since July 2024 after its demerger from Cargotec and controlled through unlisted class A shares by three Herlin-family companies, that designs, assembles (Poland, Sweden, China and elsewhere) and services differentiated heavy material-handling machines — reachstackers, empty-container handlers, terminal tractors, straddle and shuttle carriers, heavy forklifts, increasingly electric and partly automated — sold directly and through dealers to port and terminal operators, distribution centres and heavy manufacturers in over 120 countries, with Services (spare parts, maintenance contracts, refurbishment; 35% of FY2025 sales) running on the installed fleet. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 14.3% (opening capital including cash, FY2022–25); the best read is 21.6% and worst FCFROCE 9.2%. FY2021 operating income includes a €241m Navis disposal gain and FY2022 a €42m restructuring charge; the gain sits outside the worst read. Reported operating income per share fell 8.9% a year from the gain-inflated 2021 base but compounded 23.1% from 2022; FCF per share rose 34.3% a year. Book value per share drifted −1.9% a year under €752m of dividends and equity transfers to Cargotec in 2021–23. Liabilities-to-equity is 144%. **ADEQUATE** — returns clear the floor and the cap applies to five carve-out and standalone years.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes: no customer reaches the IFRS 8 disclosure threshold and Kalmar describes a diverse, geographically dispersed base; inputs (engines, batteries, steel, drivetrains) come from a wide network with single-sourcing avoided. Customer power is WEAK — thousands of buyers, no backward integration, fleet standardisation and service contracts. Supplier power is WEAK. Substitutes are WEAK — boxes must be lifted, and automation is Kalmar's own product. New entrants are STRONG: Chinese makers compete on price in reachstackers and terminal equipment, and tariffs already weigh on margins. One strong force, contained by service and dealer reach. The moat is switching costs — fleet commonality, operator training, parts and service — stable. Markets: MIXED. **ADEQUATE.**
