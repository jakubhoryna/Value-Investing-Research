# BAS.DE — BASF SE: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | SF | Cheapness | FINAL |

| PASS | WEAK | WEAK | FAIL (P19 hard strike) | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** |

Triple-gate AVOID. The world's largest chemical company earns commodity returns on an enormous capital base: worst-of-four 10-year normalized ROCE is 5.8% — barely half the ADEQUATE floor — with OI/share shrinking 13.6% a year over the decade and the decline sustained, not cyclical noise. The industry's forces are hostile (price-taking customers, state-backed feedstock-advantaged entrants), the Verbund cost moat is eroding in its home region, and management spent €355m repurchasing shares in late 2025 at ~36× EV/OI — a P19 hard strike on both reads of both bases. Nothing here is price-fixable: at FY2025 operating income no positive share price satisfies EV/OI ≤ 7, and even backvaluing against the 2021 cycle peak puts the band ~31% below today's price.

## 2. Understanding

BASF is a 160-year-old Ludwigshafen-headquartered German SE, the world's largest chemicals producer, selling mostly commoditized upstream and intermediate chemicals plus more differentiated agricultural seeds/crop protection, catalysts and battery materials to hundreds of thousands of paying industrial customers (autos, agriculture, construction, consumer-goods manufacturers) worldwide, produced in vertically integrated Verbund complexes concentrated in Europe and, with the new Zhanjiang site, increasingly China; it is mid-transformation — Wintershall oil & gas exited 2019/2024, Coatings sold to Carlyle (closing 2026), Agricultural Solutions being readied for separation. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four 10y normalized averages (FY2016–2025, 11 balance years): **ROCE 5.8%** (end/wc; best variant 6.1%) and **FCFROCE 4.0%** — both far below the P3 10% floor. The shape is worse than the level: ROCE on beginning CE ran 9.5–11.0% in 2016–17, then slid to 2.2% by 2025 with only the 2021 reopening spike interrupting — a sustained multi-year decline (P4 trigger in its own right). ΔOI/sh −13.6%/yr and ΔFCF/sh −10.0%/yr geometric; BV/sh roughly flat (+0.8%/yr) despite ~€30bn of dividends. L/E 126%, within P5 — leverage is the one clean line. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: customer PASS (no customer near 10% of revenue); supplier PASS via the ready-alternatives exception for commodity feedstocks — noting the 2022 Russian-gas episode showed how concentrated the German core's effective energy supply had become. Forces: customer bargaining power **STRONG** (commodity chemicals are bought on price; switching costs minimal); supplier power WEAK; substitutes WEAK; threat of new entrants **STRONG** — feedstock-advantaged, state-backed Chinese and Middle-Eastern capacity is the direct cause of the current margin depression, and scale has not been a barrier to their success. Tally 2 strong = the book's stop signal. Moat: Verbund integration is a genuine cost moat, but **eroding** where most of the capital sits — structurally higher post-2022 European energy prices invert the advantage; Zhanjiang replicates the moat inside the competitor's cost base. Market growth MIXED (global chemicals ≈ GDP; European production shrinking). **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

The v3.5.0 redemption clause fails at limb (a): the €1.5bn programme is volume-and-schedule-referenced, not worth-referenced. FY2023 residual tranche flags (28.0× year-read); FY2022 tranche clean (10.2×). Dividends of €3.0–3.3bn/yr were paid through the downturn partly in excess of FCF (2024: €3,284m paid vs €748m FCF) — distribution funded by borrowing and divestments. **FAIL — P19 hard strike.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Chinese capacity additions in base chemicals continue through 2027+ — the margin trough may be structural, not cyclical.
- European energy costs remain structurally above pre-2022 levels, permanently impairing the Ludwigshafen Verbund's economics.
- Zhanjiang (~€10bn) concentrates incremental capital in China precisely as geopolitical risk to that exposure rises.
- The €2.25 dividend plus buybacks exceed through-cycle FCF; continued payout defense means rising leverage.
- Portfolio surgery (Coatings sale closed, Ag Solutions separation) can flatter optics while shrinking the earnings base under the same debt stack.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Past cannot flip before ~FY2029 at the earliest arithmetic (worst-of-four must reach 10%). Future flips only if the Chinese supply cycle clears and two force calls soften — structural, not observable soon. SF flips only through the strict v3.5.0 redemption path: a published worth-referenced repurchase policy plus a bargain-line or refusal year plus allocator turnover — none present. **Next review: FY2026 report (~late Feb 2027)** — re-test P19 on the €1.5bn programme's Jan–Jun 2026 tranches, re-anchor the (currently non-computable) band on FY2026 OI, and check Agricultural Solutions separation terms; a completed Ag spin-off/IPO would warrant a fresh ANALYSE of the remainder.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

- **Breadth.** Customers: PASS — sales dispersed across hundreds of thousands of industrial buyers; no 10% customer disclosed or plausible. Suppliers: PASS on the ready-alternatives exception — feedstocks (naphtha, gas, LPG) and energy are exchange-traded commodities with multiple standing vendors; the 2022 episode (loss of pipeline Russian gas) showed the German core's *effective* supply was narrower than the nominal count, since remedied via LNG at structurally higher cost — the cost consequence is scored in the moat/forces, not as a breadth FAIL.
- **Forces (binary).** Customer power **STRONG** — most volume is spec-equivalent commodity chemistry bought on price; switching is a purchase order. Supplier power **WEAK** — commodity inputs, many vendors, hedgeable. Substitutes **WEAK** — chemistry is non-optional for the customer industries; doing-without is rare. New entrants **STRONG** — Chinese state-backed and Middle-East feedstock-advantaged capacity has entered at scale and succeeded (the present downcycle is their supply); scale, permits and know-how have not barred them. **Tally: 2 strong → stop signal.**
- **Moat.** Single source: **Cost** (Verbund integration — shared feedstock streams, energy recovery, logistics). Direction: **eroding** in Europe (structurally higher energy costs invert the advantage at the largest sites) while Zhanjiang rebuilds it inside China's cost curve — capital migrating toward the threat.
- **Market growth.** MIXED — global chemical demand tracks GDP; European production is in structural decline.
- **Verdict: WEAK** (≥2 strong forces; eroding moat).
