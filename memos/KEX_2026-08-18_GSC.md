# KEX — Kirby Corporation: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; past quality WEAK (worst-of-four window ROCE 4.6%); future quality WEAK (three strong forces); shareholder-friendliness PASS with one capital-allocation flag; cheapness EXPENSIVE at 17.4× EV/OI — **AVOID** on two gates.

Kirby is the best-run operator in a structurally poor business. It owns ~27% of the US inland tank-barge fleet behind a Jones Act wall and has retired 12% of its shares since 2021, yet earned 4.6% on capital employed across the retrieved five years against a cost of capital near 8% — and the two preceding years were worse, at operating losses of $258m and $421m on $902m of impairments. Barges are a price-taking service sold to concentrated buyers who can and do self-supply; the capital intensity is permanent and the pricing power is rented from the cycle. Only a durable step-up in the level of returns, not this cycle's recovery, flips the verdict.

## 2. Understanding

Kirby Corporation is a Houston-headquartered, Nevada-incorporated Jones Act marine transportation company that carries third-party petrochemicals, black oil, refined products and agricultural chemicals by tank barge for refiners, petrochemical producers and traders across the Mississippi River System, the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway and all three US coasts (57% of 2025 revenue, 74% of segment operating income — the dominant unit), and that separately runs a franchised diesel-engine and power-systems distribution, service and manufacturing business selling OEM-authorised parts, generators, e-frac and pressure-pumping equipment to oilfield, data-centre, utility, marine and on-highway customers from 61 US branches and Colombia; it is the largest domestic tank barge operator (1,134 inland barges, ~27% of the national fleet) and traces to 1921. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Returns are low and rising, not low and falling. Worst-of-four normalized average ROCE is 4.6% (end-of-period, cash included); the most flattering variant reaches 7.2%; FCFROCE worst-of-four is 5.1%. The path — −5.3%, 3.9%, 6.6%, 7.7%, 9.3% — is a recovery from an impairment trough; even the 2025 peak sits below the 10% floor. Per-share growth looks excellent (FCF/share +18.1% geometric, TBV/share +6.9%) but is measured off a loss year, not compounding; operating income per share is not computable across the sign change. Leverage is comfortable at 78%. The window is five years, record-limited — and the five retrieved are the favourable half of the decade. **WEAK** on the level of returns.

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth passes on explicit disclosure — no single customer reached 10% of revenue in either segment across 2022–2024. Supplier breadth passes for marine but is unproven in distribution, whose economics rest on authorised-dealer rights from Caterpillar, Cummins, Allison, MTU and EMD. Customer power is STRONG in both segments — ~25 competing operators plus captive refiner fleets, a third of inland revenue on spot; distribution work won on competitive bid — and distribution supplier power STRONG; marine supplier power, substitutes and new entrants WEAK, though pipelines cut the crude-carrying inland fleet from ~550 barges in 2015 to 170–180 in 2024. Three strong forces. The moat is cost, from fleet scale and the densest fleeting network — stable, but non-economic: the Jones Act protects the industry, not Kirby. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

The CEO's $6.69m 2025 total sits far under 5% of the lower of free cash flow and net income on both the fiscal-year ($17.7m) and three-year ($14.4m) reads; the top outside-director total is the chairman's $451k against a $1.02m stop. Related-party dealings total ~$6.9m of ordinary-course classification, insurance and legal spend — 0.27% of costs. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: repurchases scaled from $2.9m in 2021 to $354.2m in 2025 at $96.27 average (13.0× EV/OI, 15.7× normalized) and continue through 2026 above $139 with no published price discipline — spending more as the stock dears, though funded from free cash flow, with no empire-building M&A and no dividend since 1989. One flag. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Pricing is set by industry utilisation; 45–50 newbuilds a year against ~600 barges over 30 years old makes supply discipline an assumption, not a contract.
- Fuel is a live margin risk — Q2 2026 cost per gallon rose 80% year-on-year and escalators lag 30–120 days.
- Capital employed keeps outgrowing operating income at each trough, which is how a 4.6% five-year return repeats.
- Power generation, the one fast-growing unit, depends on OEM engine allocation Kirby does not control.
- A marine spill or wider Jones Act waivers would hit the protected core directly.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Upward: worst-of-four ROCE crossing 10% on two consecutive prints with capital employed contained, plus one strong force removed. Downward: a fresh impairment cycle, or repurchase pricing rising toward 25× while the programme runs, pairing with the existing flag. Next review: FY2026 10-K (~February 2027) — the ROCE level test on a fourth recovery year, the 2026 tranche re-read, and the ten-year rebuild that lifts the window cap.
