# ABCA.PA — ABC arbitrage: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK (losses); Future WEAK (no moat); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (flag pair); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past, Future, SF)**; v1 failed on Future alone.

ABC arbitrage makes its money by trading its own capital through Quartys, an Irish subsidiary it carries at fair value, and by billing Quartys for strategies and staff. The framework treats the trading gains as financial income. What remains is a consolidated operating business that lost money in nine of the last ten years (FY2025 operating income −€12.1m), with negative free cash flow once Quartys's dividends are taken out. The company's own figures look better: about 15% average ROE, €25m of profit and a 6% yield. All of that comes from the fair-valued trading book, which counts neither in operating returns nor at Gate 2. No price is CHEAP while operating income and FCF are negative. A flip needs years of positive operating income and FCF from outside clients, a stronger Future, and clean pay and repurchase records.

## 2. Understanding

ABC arbitrage SA is a Paris holding company, founded in 1995 by its chairman-CEO Dominique Ceolin and listed on Euronext Paris. Its AMF-licensed (Paris) and MAS-registered (Singapore) asset-management subsidiaries design and run systematic arbitrage strategies (mean reversion, event-driven, volatility, commodities) in listed equities, derivatives and futures across Europe, North America and Asia. They trade mainly the group's own €174m of capital, held through Quartys, its 100%-owned Irish trading company, and the Irish AIF ABCA Funds Ireland; those two pay 93% of the group's €22.9m of fees. Professional investors in the funds paid the other €1.65m in 2025.

**PASS.** The filings show what is sold and who pays for all revenue: trading gains on own capital make up 61% of FY2025 net revenues, fees from the group's own vehicles 35% and outside investors 3%. Lens STD. There are no reportable segments and no listed subsidiary or holding, so this is not a group.

## C. Calculation notes

**Lens test.** ABC arbitrage SA is not a licensed bank or insurer, nor the parent of a banking or insurance group. Its regulated subsidiaries are ABC arbitrage Asset Management, an AMF portfolio-management company (AIFM since 2014), and ABC arbitrage Asset Management Asia, an MAS-registered fund manager. No capital ratio covers the group's assets. **STD.** Total assets are 72–88% securities that are neither cash-like nor listed holdings: STD rule (c) applies, and those securities are S3, read on the G6 readings in Gate 1A with no credit at Gate 2.

**Gate 0, groups and the class.** No IFRS 8 segment note exists. The filer speaks of two "areas of expertise" in its APMs but reports no segments, so there is one unit, and this is not a group. ABC arbitrage SA is itself an IFRS 10 investment entity (note 1.9.1). It consolidates only the two management companies, which provide investment services. It measures Quartys Ltd (100%) and its stakes in the ABCA Funds Ireland sub-funds (Opportunities 77.1%, Reversion 0.8% at 31 Dec 2025) at fair value through profit or loss. Neither is listed, and "the vast majority" is pledged or mortgaged to counterparties (note 3.4). They are not a unit (not a reportable segment) and not H (not listed). Quartys is controlled but not consolidated, and its statements are not published, so it cannot be re-consolidated. Its income would be J3 financial income either way: fair-value changes, dividends, interest and carry.
