# SAF.PA — Safran SA: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK (leverage); Future ADEQUATE (unit customer conc.); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (buybacks); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** (v2: AVOID on SF alone).

Safran owns half of the engine that powers most of the world's single-aisle jets and earns most of its profit servicing that fleet. The franchise is real, but the ten-year record is not a quality record: on the worst reading capital returns 10.2%, per-share operating income grew 4.2% a year, and liabilities stand at 312% of equity — mostly customer advances, which still count. Management keeps buying stock at prices clearly above worth: about €1.25bn in 2026 at roughly €312 a share, under a €5bn programme with no worth-referenced limit. At €271.80 the stock trades at 29.1× EV/OI against a €53.77 trigger. A flip needs leverage under 200% with stronger growth, repurchases tied to worth, and a price near the trigger.

## 2. Understanding

Safran designs, makes and services aircraft engines (with GE, through CFM International, the LEAP and CFM56 single-aisle engines; military and helicopter engines on its own), aircraft equipment (landing gear, wheels and brakes, nacelles, electrical systems, actuation and flight controls, avionics, optronics and defence electronics) and cabin interiors (seats, galleys, lavatories), sold to airframers (Airbus and Boeing chief among them), airlines, lessors, maintenance shops and governments; a French-headquartered, global group of €31.2bn revenue formed in 2005, enlarged by Zodiac Aerospace in 2018. **PASS** on the group sentence. Interiors; FY2025 −€479m). The FY2016 €367m ArianeGroup revaluation gain leaves (a disposal gain). Realised hedge results sit in the financial result, below OI; consolidated revenue is at spot.
