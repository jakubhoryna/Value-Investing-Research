# MIPS.ST — Mips AB: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS · Past ADEQUATE · Future WEAK · SF FAIL · Cheapness EXPENSIVE → **AVOID** (Future and SF).

Mips earns very high returns on a tiny asset base by licensing a patented low-friction helmet layer that brands pay for per unit. Two gates stop it. One customer took 17% of FY2025 sales, and the top five took 43%, while several brands have built their own rotational systems; that fails customer breadth and leaves two strong forces. CEO pay of SEK 7.06m exceeds 5% of the lower of FCF and net income on both the fiscal-year and the three-year reads, which is a hard strike. The price is also far from the band: 70.9× EV/OI on FY2025. The Future verdict flips only if the largest customer falls below a tenth of sales and own-brand alternatives stop gaining. SF flips only if owner earnings outgrow pay on both reads.

## 2. Understanding

Mips is a Täby-based Swedish Large Cap, listed since 2017, that designs and licenses a patented rotational-impact layer (Mips safety system), manufactured by about 90 contract partners mainly in China. Since December 2025 it also owns the Koroyd impact-structure brand. More than 150 helmet brands pay for it, mostly in North America and Europe, across bike, snow, motorcycle and workplace-safety helmets. It operates as an outsourced ingredient brand. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 26.0% and FCFROCE 18.3%, both far above the STRONG levels. OI per share compounded 24.2% a year over FY2016–25 and FCF per share 27.0%. The FY2015 base is near zero, so these rates flatter the record; FY2018–25 still shows about 11% for both. L/E was 15% until FY2024 and is 133% after the Koroyd purchase, which was funded with bank debt and an earn-out. The path is volatile. End-basis ROCE peaked at 53.9% in the FY2021 bike boom, fell to 10.1% in the FY2023 destock, recovered to 25.9%, and stepped down to 12.9% when SEK 682m of Koroyd capital entered the base. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth FAIL. Customer 1 took 17% of FY2025 sales (16% in FY2024), and the top five took 43%. No waiver applies, because the annual report names brands that have developed their own technology, so customers can backward-integrate. Supply is broad, with about 90 contract manufacturers. Forces, binary: customer power STRONG (concentrated brands that can self-supply); supplier power WEAK; substitutes STRONG (own-brand rotational systems, rival ingredient technologies); new entrants WEAK (about 60 patent families, a consumer-facing brand, test-lab data). That is two strong forces. Moat: brand, as an ingredient brand helmet buyers recognise. It is stable but contested: SEK 43m of FY2025 legal costs, a USD 3.25m settlement in July 2026, and a rival technology bought outright. Market growth YES. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

The FY2023 destock depresses the three-year read; the fiscal-year read breaches regardless. The chair's fee is SEK 0.86m. The CEO holds 78,025 shares and 69,000 warrants. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: Koroyd cost ≈ 19× pro-forma OI, was funded with SEK 300m of bank debt, and was followed by a dividend cut from SEK 6.50 to 2.50. **FAIL — P17 hard strike.**
