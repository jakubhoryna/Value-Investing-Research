# NKE — NIKE, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past **WEAK**; Future **WEAK**; shareholder-friendliness **PASS** (capital allocation CAUTION, one flag); cheapness **EXPENSIVE** — final verdict **AVOID**, on two failed quality gates.

Nike's ten-year averages remain good — 19.8% worst-of-four ROCE across a decade including COVID — but the verdict turns on trajectory, not level. Returns on capital have fallen two consecutive years and free-cash returns three, to the worst non-COVID readings in the window, while a decade of operating income delivered none per share. The model treats that as disqualifying regardless of the average, and the qualitative side corroborates: the brand moat is intact but measurably narrower, with three-year relative shareholder return at the 1st percentile of the S&P 500. Price is not the obstacle — at 25.0× free cash flow the stock would be expensive even if both quality gates passed.

## 2. Understanding

NIKE, Inc. is a mature, NYSE-listed, dual-class Oregon company that designs and markets differentiated brand-premium athletic footwear, apparel and equipment under the NIKE, Jordan and Converse marks plus licensing royalties, selling both to wholesale accounts (distributors, sporting-goods, athletic-specialty and department-store chains, licensees) and directly to consumers through 988 owned stores and digital platforms in 40+ countries, operating as an asset-light outsourced brand owner that owns no manufacturing — 95 contract footwear factories across 11 countries, 321 apparel factories across 34 — and instead owns design, athlete endorsement, marketing and distribution, with 44% of revenue in the United States and 56% abroad, currently in a self-declared turnaround. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized averages over FY2017–FY2026: ROCE **19.8%** (end-of-period, with-cash) and FCFROCE **16.5%** — both comfortably above the P2 levels. The trend defeats them. ROCE_wc ran 20.6–25.5% through FY2024, then 14.0% and 13.4%; FCFROCE_wc fell 22.8% → 12.3% → 7.7% across three consecutive years, the terminal reading below even the STRONG floor on a single-year basis. Per-share growth is negative over the full window: ΔOI/sh **−0.2%/yr**, ΔFCF/sh **−1.0%/yr**, both failing the 3% inflation floor. Leverage is fine at 158.4%. **WEAK** — a sustained multi-year decline in both return series, which downgrades regardless of level.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS** both sides: no customer reached 10% of revenue in FY2026, and no vendor approaches 10% of COGS plus operating expenses, though 52% of footwear is made in Vietnam. Forces: customer power WEAK, supplier power WEAK, substitutes WEAK, **new entrants STRONG** — Nike's own filing concedes reduced barriers to starting footwear companies, and On and Hoka scaled to billions inside this window. Tally 1 strong, the judgment zone. Moat: **brand**, single-source, dominant but **eroding** — FY2024–26 relative TSR at the 1st percentile, two years of falling revenue, removal from the S&P 100. Market growth YES. **WEAK** on the eroding moat.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation clears on all three legs: Hill's FY2026 total of $36.34m is 33% of 5% of the lower of free cash flow and net income on the fiscal-year read and 18% on the trailing-three-year read, and sits at 91% of the ≈$40m indexed absolute trigger — close, not breached. Top outside-director total $458,319 against a ≈$672k ordinary-seat stop. Related-party dealings remain minor; the FY2026 section was not retrieved, logged INDETERMINATE. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: $12.1bn repurchased at $97.57 average, 25–47.5× EV/OI, against a live evergreen authorisation with no published price criterion — but paused for five straight quarters. **PASS**, one flag.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth (P15).**
- *Customer — PASS.* The FY2026 10-K states plainly that no customer accounted for 10% or more of consolidated revenues. Three largest US customers ≈29% of US sales, and the US is 44% of revenue, so those three together are ≈12.8% of group revenue — roughly 4% each. Three largest non-US customers ≈16% of non-US sales. The "unlikely to consolidate" limb is the weaker one: Nike's own risk factors record that retail-market-share concentration "has increased and may continue to increase". Noted, not disqualifying, and offset by the DTC mix.
- *Supplier — PASS.* Four footwear contract manufacturers each exceed 10% of footwear *production*; footwear is ≈64% of revenue, so >10% of footwear production is ≈4% of COGS plus operating expenses — under the threshold on the metric the rule actually specifies. Looking through to the effective supplier, the real concentration is geographic (Vietnam 52% of footwear, ≈21% of COGS+opex), which would breach if country were the supplier unit. It is not: 15 footwear and 64 apparel CMs across 11 and 34 countries respectively, 205 strategic Tier-2 suppliers, and alternative capacity exists in Indonesia, India and Bangladesh. Nike's claim that it could develop adequate alternative sources "over a period of time" is treated as a company claim, not verified fact. **No waiver used**; the geographic concentration is carried into Risks instead.

**Forces (P16) — binary.**

|Force|Call|One-line reason|
|---|---|---|
|Customer bargaining power|**WEAK**|No customer at 10%; wholesale accounts atomised at ~4% each and cannot backward-integrate into a global brand; Nike unilaterally exited wholesale accounts in 2020–22, which is not the behaviour of a price-taker.|
|Supplier bargaining power|**WEAK**|Contract manufacturers are numerous, brandless, cannot forward-integrate, and Nike is typically their dominant customer — the switching cost runs their way, not Nike's.|
|Threat of substitutes|**WEAK**|Direct substitution is rivalry, not substitution; doing-without and wholly-different-function substitution are both limited for a category people replace on a wear cycle.|
|Threat of new entrants|**STRONG**|Barriers to *success* have demonstrably fallen — the 10-K concedes "a reduction in barriers to starting new footwear and apparel companies"; On and Hoka scaled from negligible to multi-billion inside this window, taking share directly.|

**Tally: 1 strong — the judgment zone**, which by itself supports ADEQUATE if contained. It is not well contained: revenue fell from $51.4bn (FY2024) to $46.3bn and operating income from $6,311m to $3,702m over the two years the entrants gained.

**Moat.** Single identified source: **brand** (with ingrainedness as a secondary channel effect). Mechanism — decades of athlete endorsement and sport authenticity sustain premium pricing, shelf priority and consumer mindshare that a new entrant cannot buy quickly. Direction: **eroding**. The evidence is not narrative: FY2024–26 relative TSR at the **1st percentile of the S&P 500** (proxy-disclosed, PSUs paid 0%), two consecutive years of falling revenue, gross margin down from 44.6% to 42.7%, removal from the S&P 100, and a share price at 12-year lows. The moat is still wide in absolute terms — Nike remains the largest athletic footwear and apparel seller in the world, with trademarks registered in 190+ jurisdictions — but "wide and narrowing" is what the eroding call means.

**Market growth.** **YES** — global athletic footwear and apparel continues to grow; Nike is losing share within a growing market, which is the relevant distinction.

**Verdict WEAK** — on the eroding moat, which is an independent sufficient condition under the §2·1B tiering. Had the moat been called stable, the 1-strong-force tally would have supported ADEQUATE.
