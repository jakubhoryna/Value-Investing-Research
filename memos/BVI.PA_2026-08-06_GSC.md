## 2. Understanding

Bureau Veritas sells differentiated, accreditation-gated conformity assessment — testing, inspection, certification and technical verification, priced per assignment — to the regulated party who must produce an independent attestation (shipowners, building owners and contractors, commodity traders, industrial operators, brand owners and retailers), not to the regulator who demands it, in the testing-inspection-certification services industry; it is a French *société anonyme* listed on Euronext Paris running offices and laboratories in 140 countries with ~82,000 staff, revenue spread across Asia-Pacific, the Americas, Europe and the Middle East with no material single client; and it is a mature, cash-generative, dividend-paying serial acquirer whose reference shareholder is selling down. **PASS.**

## C. Calculation notes

**Latest-year capital employed (FY2025, from the filed 31 Dec 2025 balance sheet).**
Total assets 7,063.4 − NIBCL 1,394.4 = CE_wc 5,669.0; − (cash 1,366.1 + short-term investments 9.8) = CE_xc 4,293.1.

**NIBCL — the burden-of-proof call, and the consistency problem it creates.** The framework's NIBCL is accounts payable + accrued expenses + income taxes payable + deferred income. At 31 Dec 2025 the filed balance sheet supports the full construction: trade and other payables 1,394.4 + contract liabilities 247.7 + current tax liabilities 96.9 = **1,739.0**, which reconciles exactly to total current liabilities 2,163.4 less current borrowings 229.9, current lease liabilities 118.0, derivatives 2.8 and other current financial liabilities 73.7. **But contract liabilities and current tax liabilities are not separately retrievable for FY2021–FY2024**, and the framework forbids tweaking individual years. The window is therefore run on a single consistent basis — **trade and other payables only** — for all five years. The direction of the resulting bias is stated rather than hidden: it leaves deferred revenue and tax payable inside capital employed, which **overstates CE and understates ROCE** in every year. On the full FY2025 NIBCL of 1,739.0 the FY2025 reads would be CE_wc 5,324.4 / CE_xc 3,948.5, ROCE_wc 18.64% and ROCE_xc 25.13%, and the worst-of-four would rise from 15.15% to roughly 15.9%. The conservative basis is the one carried into the verdict. Short-term debt and the current portion of long-term debt stay in CE throughout, as required. **Liabilities directly associated with assets held for sale** (40.6 at FY2025, 147.7 at 30 Jun 2026) have undisclosed composition; burden of proof rests with the item, so they stay in CE.

**Goodwill stays in capital employed — logged as an explicit call.** The framework permits deducting goodwill only for a *non-acquisitive* company. Bureau Veritas is the opposite: an explicitly acquisition-led strategy (LEAP | 28), €634.0m of acquisition cash across the five years, deals closing continuously, goodwill at 32.2% of total assets at FY2025 and 31.2% at 30 Jun 2026. Goodwill therefore stays in. The size of the concession being withheld is worth stating so that no future session re-opens it casually: deducting FY2025 goodwill of 2,273.7 would cut CE_xc to 2,019.4 and lift ROCE_xc to **49.1%**. The rule exists precisely to stop a serial acquirer from being credited with returns on capital it spent.

**⚠ The French operating-income-line convention — fourth instance, and the first where it is not verdict-determinative.**
Bureau Veritas presents both lines. The statutory **operating profit** (FY2025 €992.4m) is struck after amortisation of intangibles arising on acquisitions, impairment, restructuring and disposal results. The **adjusted operating profit / *résultat opérationnel ajusté*** (FY2025 €1,052.9m) adds those items back. Per the SGO.PA / DG.PA / ML.PA / SW.PA label precedent, **ROCE is computed on the as-reported statutory operating profit line**, and that is what B1/B2 carry.

*The alternative reading, and exactly what it does.* On the adjusted line the four normalized reads become: end-CE with cash **16.62%**, end-CE ex cash 22.02%, beginning-CE with cash **17.25%**, beginning-CE ex cash 22.86% (Σ adjusted OP FY2021–25 = 4,683.2; FY2022–25 = 3,881.4). The worst-of-four moves 15.15% → 16.62%, a gap of **147 basis points**. Both readings clear the 15% STRONG threshold and both clear the 10% ADEQUATE floor, so **the verdict is ADEQUATE on either line and the convention decides nothing here.** Its only live effects are on the band — the EV/OI legs move from €8.67/€10.80 to €9.62/€11.75, about 8–11% — and on what a future ten-year rebuild would conclude about the STRONG threshold, where 147bp of headroom versus a 15% line is not comfortable.

*Why this instance is the one the open MODEL item needs.* The three prior cases turned on charges that are arguably operating in substance and recurring in fact — Michelin's restructuring, Saint-Gobain's "business income" absorbing restructuring, impairment, litigation and disposals, Sodexo's "other operating income and expenses" (which management has guided to ≈€300m for FY2026, i.e. anything but non-recurring). **Bureau Veritas is a different animal: the dominant component of its adjustment is amortisation of intangibles recognised on acquisitions** — H1 2026 shows €23.4m of it against €9.6m of restructuring and €9.8m of disposal losses. That is not a disguised operating cost; it is the accounting unwind of a purchase price already paid, and the asset it amortises is *already sitting inside capital employed*. Charging it against operating profit while the corresponding intangible remains in the denominator taxes a serial acquirer twice for the same transaction — once in the capital base and once in the return. The counter-argument is equally sharp and must be recorded: for a company that buys businesses every single year, that amortisation *is* a recurring cost of the business model, and adding it back credits management with earnings on capital it will have to keep spending to stand still. **A serial acquirer is the cleanest test case the open item will get, because here the two rules diverge for a structural reason rather than an accounting-policy reason, and — uniquely so far — nothing is riding on the answer.** Decide it here, where it is free, per the SGO.PA item's own request.
