## 2. Understanding

Ericsson designs and sells mobile-network equipment and software — 5G/4G radio access, transport, core and cloud software, managed services, a communications-API platform, and a large standard-essential-patent licensing business (**products**) — to communications service providers principally, plus enterprises and handset licensees paying royalties (**customers**), in telecom network infrastructure and IPR licensing (**industry**), as a vertically integrated Swedish listed company (publ) whose dual-class A/B structure leaves practical control with Investor AB and Industrivärden (**form**), headquartered in Kista, Stockholm and selling worldwide (**geography**), a ~150-year-old incumbent and one of three Western-scale RAN vendors retrenching after a failed enterprise diversification (**status**). **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized 10y ROCE is **4.74%** (range 4.74–6.69% across the four bases) — a clear miss of P3's 10% floor. Worst-of-four FCFROCE is 10.10%, above the 8% line; the gap is impairments — SEK 17.2bn (2017), 31.9bn (2023), 15.3bn (2024) — which crush OI without touching cash. No add-backs are permitted. Growth is unusable as a trend: OI/sh flips sign twice (geometric mean n.m.); BV/sh compounds at −2.4%, TBV/sh −1.9%. L/E is 154% latest, inside P5, but breached 200% in five years; Moody's rates it Ba1. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **FAILs** on customers: largest ~14% of net sales, top ten 46%, against P15's one-tenth test. The captive-customer waiver is denied — switching costs are real but not prohibitive (Open RAN displacement is live), and the moat is contested, not dominant. Supplier breadth also fails, on dependence on a few advanced-node foundries. Forces: customer power **STRONG** (concentrated, consolidating buyers; the company concedes reduced bargaining power); supplier power **STRONG**; substitutes WEAK; new entrants WEAK (scale, SEPs, vetting). Tally **2 strong** — the book's stop signal. Moat: switching costs plus SEP-based advantage, **eroding**. Market MIXED — RAN capex is post-peak. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17's executive test clears easily: CEO total SEK 75.6m against a 5%-of-lower(FCF, NI) line of SEK 1,436m. Two hard strikes elsewhere. **(1)** The chair's SEK 5.0m base fee (SEK 6.38m total) is 3.8–4.9× the sourced Swedish Large Cap median chair fee of SEK 1.305m, versus a 2× stop. **(2)** Dual-class control paired with value-destructive allocation: Vonage lifted goodwill SEK 46.4bn in 2022, all since impaired. Buybacks clean in-window (Item 16E: none). Flags: the DOJ Iraq matter; dividends through loss years. **FAIL.**

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: FAIL.** FY2025 risk factors disclose the largest customer at ~14% of net sales and the ten largest at 46%; note F1 adds that the ten largest customers represent 47% of trade receivables and contract assets. The P15 test is a single customer above one-tenth of revenue — breached on the company's own disclosure sentence.

**Captive-customer waiver — DENIED.** (i) Prohibitive switching costs: not demonstrated — RAN vendor swaps occur, and Open RAN architectures are an explicit carrier programme to reduce lock-in. (ii) Dominant, stable moat: not present — a contested three-vendor Western market with an eroding position. (iii) Structural concentration: satisfied (the global carrier buyer set is genuinely small). One of three limbs holds; the waiver requires all three.

**Breadth — suppliers: marginal FAIL.** Ericsson lists "limited number of third-party suppliers" among its principal risks; the effective supplier is a handful of advanced-node foundries and a small set of RF/ASIC vendors, and the §1B look-through rule ("many suppliers" proves nothing) applies. Ready-alternatives exception does not clear at leading-edge nodes.

**Four forces (binary):**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | Concentrated, consolidating carriers; company concedes concentration "reduces Ericsson's bargaining power". |
| Supplier bargaining power | **STRONG** | Effective dependence on a few advanced-node foundries; no ready alternative at the leading edge. |
| Threat of substitutes | WEAK | No substitute for mobile network infrastructure within the function; satellite/fixed wireless are adjacent, not replacing. |
| Threat of new entrants | WEAK | Scale, SEP portfolio, decade-long carrier qualification and national-security vetting dull entry. |

**Tally: 2 strong** → the book's stop signal; Future is WEAK on the tally alone, and again on breadth.

**Moat:** switching costs (multi-year integrated network contracts) reinforced by an SEP-based cost/licensing advantage. Direction: **eroding** — Open RAN disaggregation, carrier vendor-diversification policy, and a decade of flat-to-declining revenue at a shrinking share of a post-peak RAN market.

**Market growth: MIXED.** 5G RAN capex is past its peak in the mature markets that dominate Ericsson's revenue; enterprise and network-API growth is real but small and, so far, value-destroying.
