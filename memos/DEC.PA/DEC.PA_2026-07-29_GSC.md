# DEC.PA — JCDecaux SE: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future WEAK, Shareholder-friendliness PASS (one standing flag), Cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** (Past and Future both fail independently).

The world's largest out-of-home advertiser is a genuinely durable franchise trapped in a structurally low-return contract architecture: the cities, transport authorities and airports that grant its exclusive concessions auction them competitively and extract most of the economics through fixed minimum guarantees, leaving a decade's worst-of-four average ROCE of 3.0% against €8–9bn of lease-inflated capital employed — a third of the ADEQUATE bar, and no better than ~4–5% even excluding the COVID year. Nothing here is mispriced governance or a fixable cycle; the return deficit is the business model. A flip would require the concession model itself to change — grantors ceding guarantee risk, or a structural shift to asset-light, revenue-share contracts that lifts through-cycle ROCE toward double digits — none of which is in evidence. Not a candidate for the downturn shopping list at any price the register can compute.

## 2. Understanding
JCDecaux SE sells out-of-home advertising space — a differentiated media service on street furniture, transport hubs and billboards, increasingly digital and programmatic — to a broad base of advertisers and their media agencies (organisations), operating under multi-year exclusive concession and lease contracts won in competitive tenders from cities, transport authorities and airport operators across ~80 countries; a French SE headquartered in Neuilly-sur-Seine, founded 1964, listed 2001, the industry's global #1, 65.4%-controlled by the founding family via JCDecaux Holding with two sons of the founder as rotating co-CEOs. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
Worst-of-four normalized average ROCE 3.0% (range 3.0–3.7%) against the 10% ADEQUATE bar; ex-FY2020 it is 4.1%, and the pre-COVID FY2016–19 stretch averaged 4.2% — the weakness is structural, not pandemic-made. Worst-of-four FCFROCE prints 8.9%, but only because IFRS 16 routes ~€550–950m of annual lease principal into financing while the matching €2.0bn lease debt sits in CE — an artifact, not cash economics. ΔOI/sh +6.8% and ΔFCF/sh +16.4% geometric clear the 3% floor but are inflated by the 2018 IFRS 16 basis break; ΔBV/sh is −0.9% a year against a thin payout. L/E 258% breaches P5 with no P6 conditions. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth PASS both sides: top-10 advertisers under 13% of revenue (sourced); no single concession grantor approaches 10% of opex. Forces: customer power **STRONG** (agency-intermediated buying, fluid discretionary budgets); supplier power **STRONG** (grantors auction unique locations and extract minimum guarantees — FY2020's −€476m EBIT shows who carries the risk); substitutes **WEAK** (OOH's ad-spend share stable, DOOH growing); new entrants **WEAK** (incumbency, installed base, tender machinery). Tally 2 strong — the book's stop signal. Moat: Government (exclusive term concessions) — real contract-by-contract, re-auctioned away at each renewal; stable-to-eroding. Market growth YES. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
P17 clean: top FY2025 total €2.75m (co-CEO Jean-Charles Decaux) vs €13.1m/€12.2m year/normalized caps; board fees far under French norms; 65.4% family ownership aligns. RPT: leases and services with JCDecaux Holding, ~€21m expense, disclosed, small — caveat only. P19 buybacks: de-minimis withdrawn on the cumulative-OI bar; the window aggregate (€258m, liquidity-contract churn plus two 2025 plan-coverage tranches at €14.75–14.80) tests at ~4–5× MCap/FCF — rescued; sterilisation purpose logged and worth-check-cleared (App. E). ## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers
- Minimum-guarantee concession economics transmit any advertising downturn straight to EBIT, as FY2020 demonstrated at −€476m.
- Re-tender risk on flagship city and airport contracts hands renewal economics to grantors at each cycle.
- €3.9bn of financial-plus-lease debt against €458m EBIT leaves little equity cushion at 258% L/E.
- Agency-holding-company consolidation concentrates effective pricing power on the demand side.
- China/Hong Kong transport exposure adds concentrated macro and FX risk to the highest-margin segment.
