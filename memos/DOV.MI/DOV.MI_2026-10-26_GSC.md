# DOV.MI — doValue S.p.A.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS (two material units); Past WEAK (6.8% ROCE); Future WEAK (unit customer conc.); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (compensation); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past + Future + SF)**.

doValue collects bad loans and manages repossessed property for banks and securitisation vehicles in Italy and Greece. It earned more than 15% on capital every year until 2019; debt-funded acquisitions then filled the balance sheet with goodwill, returns fell to 6–8%, liabilities reached almost seven times equity and 2024 share issues multiplied the share count about twelvefold. CEO pay rose while the parent lost money, and Gardant was bought far above its earnings. WATCH needs returns above 10%, liabilities near twice equity and pay that follows profit; at today's debt no buy price exists.

## 2. Understanding

Group sentence: doValue S.p.A. is a Verona-based credit servicer, listed on Euronext Milan since 2017 (formerly doBank, which gave up its banking licence in June 2019), that manages, works out and collects non-performing and unlikely-to-pay loans and repossessed real estate for banks, securitisation vehicles and the investors behind them, earning base fees on the gross book value it manages and success fees on what it collects, and adds master servicing, structuring, data, due-diligence and alternative-fund management (Gardant Investor SGR), in Italy (51% of FY2025 gross revenue), Greece and Cyprus (41%) and Spain (8%); since April 2026 it also collects small consumer and commercial receivables for e-commerce, payment, mobility and utility companies in the UK, Ireland, the Nordics and Central Europe through coeo, which also buys some receivable portfolios (FY2025 revenue EUR 582.0m). PASS.

- **Italy** (50.8% of FY2025 segment revenue): servicing of bad and unlikely-to-pay loans and real estate for Italian banks and securitisation vehicles, plus credit funds for institutional investors.
- **Hellenic Region** (40.8%): servicing of bad loans and repossessed property for Greek and Cypriot banks and state-guaranteed (HAPS) securitisation vehicles.
- Spain (8.4% of revenue, 3.8% of segment assets) is immaterial; coeo joined after the latest fiscal year-end.

## 3. Quality — past record

STD lens (no licence since 2019). Investments above the P31 line, so two readings. The worst-of-eight ROCE is 8.0% over ten years and 6.8% over five; FCFROCE on constructed maintenance capex (P20; all capex in deal years) is 8.1% and 4.6% (all-capex 7.8% and 4.3%). Returns were above 15% in every year to 2019, then 3–14% once Altamira, FPS and Gardant put EUR 634m of goodwill and intangibles into capital. Operating income per share fell 22.0% a year and FCF per share 17.1% (harsher reads), with heavy dilution in 2024. FCFROCE over the last three years is 36–37% below the window's: a sustained decline. L/E is 685%. **WEAK**.

## 4. Quality — future

Both units sell to organisations. Italy has one customer worth EUR 55.3m, 19% of the unit's revenue. In Greece the group-level statement does not rule out a client above 10% of the unit, so silence counts as concentration. Neither unit is the dominant servicer in its market, so each flag becomes a STRONG customer-power force. It is uncontained: Greek EBITDA fell from EUR 144.4m to 121.4m (FY2023–FY2025) and Spanish contracts were written down. No moat with a single source exists: licences and multi-year mandates are widely held. Bad-loan stocks are shrinking (market MIXED). Weighted score 1.0. **WEAK**.

The parent lost money in FY2025 and over the three years to FY2025, yet her pay rose from EUR 2.60m. That is a hard strike. Gardant (2024) cost EUR 244m against EUR 2.8m of operating income in its last year before the deal (85.7 times): a second strike. Management holds 0.15% of shares, worth EUR 0.47m: NOT ALIGNED. **FAIL** at step 1 (`compensation`, with `acquisitions`).
