**The verdict flips, and this is the second name in the queue whose strike was never about buybacks.** v1's FAIL rested on a P19 acquisition hard strike — the September 2021 purchase of CNC Software (Mastercam) at **28.7–38.1× the target's disclosed pre-deal EBIT** — under a rule that would have kept the strike alive until the deal aged out of the window in FY2031. v4.0.0 abolished that mechanical persistence: old actions are yardsticks, never convictions, and the M&A route to ADVERSE requires **serial** value-destructive acquisition.

That is not made out. CNC Software is **one** deal, five years old, and its severity is not in question — clearing 20× would have required a 48% pre-deal EBIT margin against the 25–30% Sandvik published. DSI Underground, the same year, is **INDETERMINATE** (14.1–20.0× across plausible margins) and under §2·1C cannot complete a pair. Nothing since approaches either in scale: the 2026 programme is sales-network bolt-ons into Mastercam (Mastercam India, CCCS), **Diemme Filtration** (announced 22 June 2026, ~SEK 1.1bn of FY2026 revenue, becoming a new Filtration division), and a **divestment** — Additive Manufacturing sold to Mimir on 29 May 2026. Q2 states plainly that "acquisitions were neutral to the margin."

The rest of the gate is clean by wide margins. The CEO's SEK 37.5m is **0.26%** of the 5% line; related-party dealing is **0.13%** of the cost base; there is one share class and no dual-class caveat; and there is **no general repurchase programme at all** — the SEK 579m of four-year "repurchases" are equity-swap settlements of performance-share plans against an unchanged share count of 1,254,385,923 since 2012, amounting to **0.13% of market capitalisation**.

One flag — an expensive acquisition in the last five years, with the absorbing segment showing recurring impairments and no published acquisition-price discipline — and no second. **SF PASSES.** At SEK 356.00 the shares sit **+307%** above the SEK 87.51 band.
