# OPFI — OppFi Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK (leverage; growth also fails); Future WEAK (funding conc.); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (compensation); cheapness CHEAP — **AVOID (Past, Future and SF)**.

OppFi services high-rate instalment loans that three Utah banks originate for non-prime US consumers, buys most of each loan and funds the book with private-credit facilities. At $5.42 the shares trade at 3.7 times free cash flow and an EV of 4.7 times financing-blind operating income, well under the $9.86 trigger. The gates fail first. The FY2022 operating loss leaves per-share growth unreadable while that year stays in the window. On OppFi Inc.'s own equity, liabilities were 761% of book at FY2025. Two lender groups provide over three quarters of liabilities. On attributable earnings, FY2024 executive pay was a strike. WATCH needs deposit funding after the pending bank merger, a record without the FY2022 loss, and pay inside the lines.

## 2. Understanding

OppFi Inc. is a Chicago-based, Schwartz-family-controlled specialty-finance company (founded 2012; NYSE-listed through a 2021 SPAC merger; an Up-C until April 2026) that markets online, underwrites with its own models and services unsecured instalment loans (OppLoans, APRs of about 59–195%) that three Utah-chartered, FDIC-insured banks — Capital Community Bank, FinWise Bank and First Electronic Bank — originate for US consumers with thin or impaired credit in 40 states; it buys participation interests in most of each loan within days and earns the interest the borrowers pay (99% of FY2025 revenue of $597.1m), funds that book with secured revolving facilities from private-credit lenders, owns 35% of Bitty (revenue-based financing for small businesses) and has agreed to buy BNCCORP and its national bank. **PASS.** One reportable segment; Bitty is an immaterial equity-method stake, so OppFi is not a group.

## 3. Quality — past record

STD lens, graded on operating income after funding cost, FY2019–FY2025 (history-limited: no balance sheet before December 2019 was published). Worst-of-four ROCE is 13.1% over the record and 11.8% over five years; FCFROCE on maintenance-capex FCF (P20 constructed, book growth beyond 3% taken out) is 12.4% and 10.5% (all-capex 12.2% and 10.3%). FY2022 lost $7.8m at the operating line, so both per-share growth series cross zero and their geometric reads are n.m.; P3 fails. L/E at FY2025 is 761% on OppFi Inc.'s equity (144% counting the members' interest), beyond the 700% cap. No sustained decline. **WEAK** (leverage; growth also fails).

## 4. Quality — future

Borrowers are millions of consumers: no customer concentration. Each partner bank originates 26–42% of loans and of revenue, a licence concentration that, without a dominant position, becomes a STRONG supplier-power force. Funding is strict: secured facilities are 78% of liabilities at 30 June 2026, and the Midtown-agented facility alone 55% (`funding conc.`, WEAK by itself). Substitutes are STRONG (credit cards, payday, earned-wage and pay-later products); customer power and entrants are WEAK. Two STRONG forces, no identified moat (80.6% of new loans arrive through paid lead partners), market MIXED. **WEAK** (funding conc.; also 2+ forces, no moat).

## C. Calculation notes

**Lens test.** OppFi Inc. is not a licensed bank or insurer and publishes no regulatory capital ratio; the loans are originated by three unaffiliated partner banks, and the BNCCORP / BNC National Bank acquisition had not closed at memo date. FI fails → **STD**, as a non-bank lender (§2 Lens). STD rules applied: the lending activity is graded on OI after its funding cost; its funding (secured revolving facilities and the term loan) is a debt-equivalent with the funding pairing; `funding conc.` is tested strictly; restricted cash pledged to the facilities is never cash. No captive finance arm (the lending is the business).

**J1 OI build.** Candidates: "Income from operations" (face statement) is the lowest operating subtotal; it carries interest expense and amortised debt issuance costs, salaries (share-based pay inside), marketing, professional fees, D&A, exit costs (FY2024 2.983; FY2025 −1.449 reversal), impairment of assets held for sale (FY2022 3.571), the lower-of-cost-or-market reversal (FY2023 −2.983) and management fees to a related party (FY2020 0.700; FY2021 0.350). Below it: change in fair value of warrant liabilities, the FY2021 PPP-loan forgiveness gain (6.444), income from the Bitty equity-method stake, and other income/expense. Adjustments:
- FY2025: other expense (−4.173) = a $4.5m legal contingency net of expected insurance recoveries + $0.327m sublease income. The legal charge is an operating charge booked below the operating subtotal and is charged to OI (J1); the sublease gain is not added.
- Other revenue (interest earned on interest-bearing deposits, bank-partner servicing fees and referral fees, never split; referral fees were 0.3% of revenue in FY2023 and FY2024) is removed in every year as income of ambiguous character, positive (J3; harsher, logged): FY2019 0.924, FY2020 0.789, FY2021 1.539, FY2022 1.411, FY2023 3.519, FY2024 4.736, FY2025 5.281, TTM 5.631. The servicing and referral part is operating, so OI is understated by at most that line.
- Gains booked below the line (sublease income, FY2023 debt-forgiveness gain 0.113, the PPP gain, warrant gains) are never added; warrant losses (FY2023–FY2025) are financing items on OppFi's own warrants and stay below OI.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers.** The payers are individual borrowers (non-prime US consumers in 40 states): interest on finance receivables is 99.1% of FY2025 revenue. Bank-partner servicing fees sit inside other revenue (< 1% of revenue). No customer above 10%; a consumer business needs no customer statement. No customer concentration.

**Breadth — suppliers (concentration worksheet).**
- *Bank-partner licences.* FY2025 net originations and "similar percentages of our net revenues": Capital Community Bank 42.3%, FinWise 31.3%, First Electronic Bank 26.3% (FY2024: 37.9%, 31.8%, 30.3%); 100% of originations came through them in FY2024–FY2025, and OppFi's "business model currently depends entirely" on them (10-K Item 1A). Each bank's lending licence (federal rate exportation from Utah) underpins more than 10% of revenue → **`supplier conc.` flagged**. Strong-moat carry: (1) dominance — not evidenced (consumer lending is "vast and competitive", 10-K Competition) → not carried. The flag becomes a **STRONG supplier-power force**. Containment evidence: three banks, nonexclusive 60-month agreements to 2029, random allocation between them and volume moved without lost revenue (FEB 30.3% → 26.3%, CCB 37.9% → 42.3%). But the banks "retain approval rights on all aspects of the program", all three rest on the same contested rate-exportation basis, and returns did not hold across the window (FY2022 operating loss) → not contained.
- *Lead-generation partners*: 80.6% of FY2025 new loans, paid per funded loan; not individually sized (direct marketing 50.890 = 23.7% of total expenses). Not determinable; logged.
- *Funding (strict).* At 30 Jun 2026 secured facilities are 276.453 of 356.474 liabilities (77.6%; FY2025 321.353 of 445.214, 72.2%) — the capital-market channel above 50%. Counterparties: the SPE V facility (Midtown Madison as agent) 197.453 = 55.4% (FY2025, with the Midtown-arranged Gray Rock facility, 242.353 = 54.4%); SPE IX 79.000 = 22.2% (17.7%). One counterparty above 10% and one channel above 50% → **`funding conc.` → Future WEAK**; no moat carries it. The $100m term loan of August 2026 adds a third capital-market lender.

**Forces.**

| Force | Call | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer power | WEAK | Millions of small individual borrowers paying 59–195% APR; no backward integration. |
| Supplier power | STRONG | Three bank licences each above 10% of revenue with approval rights over the program; capital from two lender groups (funding test above); paid lead partners supply 80.6% of new loans. |
| Substitutes | STRONG | Credit cards ("the convenience and ubiquity that credit cards represent", 10-K), payday and title loans, earned-wage access and pay-later products serve the same short-term need; doing without is the other substitute (analyst judgment on the newer products). |
| New entrants | WEAK | Barriers to success: years of loss data for models facing ~50% annual net charge-offs, bank-partner approval and committed funding; the bank-partner route itself is open to others. |

Tally: two STRONG → the book's stop signal; Future WEAK (`2+ forces`). Supplier power has no containment (above).

**Moat.** None identified. Government: the licence is the partner banks', not OppFi's. Cost: funding at SOFR plus 6.0–7.3% shows no cost edge. Brand: Trustpilot 4.4 and NPS 78, but 80.6% of new loans arrive through paid lead partners, so the brand does not bring the customer. Switching costs and network effects: none for a borrower taking a new loan. → `no moat`.

**Market.** MIXED: demand for non-prime credit is large and grew with origination volumes through FY2025, but state rate caps, "true lender" challenges and a book that shrank 9% in H1 2026 cut the other way. Caps Future at ADEQUATE in any case.

**Decline.** No sustained decline to explain; the FY2022 loss (net charge-offs 61.9% of average receivables, against 37.5% in FY2021) is a credit-cycle and underwriting event, and FY2023–FY2025 recovered.

**Grade.** WEAK on three counts — `funding conc.` (first in the vocabulary order), `2+ forces`, `no moat`.
