# NOVT — Novanta Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future WEAK, shareholder-friendliness FAIL, cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** (Past + Future + SF).

Novanta sells precision components that OEMs design into medical and industrial equipment, and design-in creates real switching costs. The record does not convert that into returns: the worst-of-four average ROCE is 7.9%, and returns fell in the second half of the decade as acquisitions grew. Two medical OEMs now take 23% of revenue, so breadth fails. Pay fails on both P17 reads, and the July 2026 Riverpoint deal cost about 43× the target's audited operating income. Price is moot, but the band is not computable: post-close debt-equivalents exceed seven times pro-forma operating income. The verdict would flip only with a decade of returns above 10%, customer concentration back under a tenth, and pay inside the owner-earnings limit — none is in sight.

## 2. Understanding
Novanta designs and makes precision subsystems and components — laser beam steering and laser sources, precision motion and robotic end-of-arm tooling, RFID, surgical insufflators and pumps with disposables, surgical video, and since July 2026 private-label minimally invasive surgical consumables — which medical, life-science and advanced-industrial OEMs design into their own equipment. It is a Canadian-incorporated, Massachusetts-headquartered company with manufacturing in the US, Europe and China and most staff in Europe. It is a 1968-founded former semiconductor supplier, renamed from GSI Group in 2016, with about $1bn of FY2025 revenue and a stated shift toward medical (53% of revenue, about 60% after Riverpoint). **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**WEAK.** The worst-of-four average ROCE is 7.9% (end-of-period, cash in), below the adequate line; the best reading is 10.4%. Worst FCFROCE is 8.3%. Returns declined across the window: ROCE fell from 8.4% to 7.7% and FCFROCE from 10.8% to 6.9% between the two halves. Each acquisition wave added capital faster than profit. Per-share growth clears the floor: operating income/share +11.9% a year and FCF/share +5.1%, but FY2025 FCF fell to $48m on working capital and taxes. Book value per share jumped 74% in FY2025 from the equity-unit issue, not from earnings. Liabilities to equity were 37% at FY2025 and about 98% pro-forma for Riverpoint.

## 4. Quality — future
**WEAK.** Breadth fails on the customer side: two medical OEMs took 12% and 11% of FY2025 revenue, up from one at about 10% in 2023–24 and none before. Riverpoint adds two customers at 30% of its own sales. The waiver fails: these OEMs can and do dual-source or make in-house, and concentration is not structural to the industry. Customer power is strong; supplier power, substitutes and new entrants are weak — one strong force. The moat is switching costs from design-in on long-life OEM platforms; it is stable but thin, with operating margins of 8.6–12.5%. Market growth is mixed: surgery and robotics grow while precision manufacturing shrinks.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
**FAIL — P17 hard strike.** The Chair and CEO's FY2025 total of $10.04m is 4.1× the year-read ceiling ($2.42m) and 3.2× the three-year ceiling ($3.18m); both reads breach. Capital allocation is **ADVERSE**. Repurchases ran at 38–83× across the four reads, to offset award dilution, and continue under a live $200m plan with no price criterion. Riverpoint cost $1.45bn, about 43× audited operating income, and was funded by debt and a 19% enlargement of the share count; management itself guides returns below its hurdle for five years. Directors' fees (top $317k) and related-party disclosure pass.
