Watsco is North America's largest HVAC/R distributor, debt-free, with returns on capital above 17% in every year of the decade and per-share operating income compounding at about 6% a year. Pay is what stands in the way: the proxy prints the founder-CEO's compensation actually paid at $265.8m for 2023 and $186.4m for 2024, the value movement of restricted stock that vests only at retirement age or later, against $497m–$536m of net income. ## 2. Understanding

Watsco is a Miami-based, NYSE-listed distributor (incorporated 1956, in HVAC/R distribution since 1989, revenue $7.24bn in 2025: 91% United States, 5% Canada, 5% Latin America and the Caribbean) that buys central air conditioners, furnaces, heat pumps and ductless systems mostly from Carrier (62% of purchases) and Rheem (8%) under exclusive territorial rights, plus parts, supplies and commercial refrigeration equipment from other makers, and resells them from 695 locations, about 53% of revenue through 80%-owned joint ventures with Carrier, to more than 130,000 licensed contractors who replace and install heating and cooling systems in homes and businesses (HVAC equipment 67% of sales, other HVAC products 29%, commercial refrigeration 4%).

One reportable segment; no listed subsidiary or holding. **PASS.** Lens STD.

## 3. Quality — past record

STD lens, ten fiscal years. Worst-of-four ROCE is 20.6% over ten years and 22.1% over five; FCFROCE on maintenance capex is 16.3% and 16.1%. Capex sits at or near depreciation, so maintenance equals all capex in seven years and the all-capex readings match to a tenth. The 38.4% Russell Sigler stake is below the G6 line: exclude reading alone. ΔOI/sh compounds at +5.7% and ΔFCF/sh at +3.8% on the harsher reads. L/E is 42.9%. ROCE fell three years running after the 2022 peak (31.0% → 18.1% on beginning capital with cash), a J6 decline whose cause is the post-2022 normalisation of unit volumes and pricing, plus cash accumulation. Both windows clear the STRONG levels and growth holds, so the decline caps the grade. **ADEQUATE** (`20.6% ROCE`).

## 4. Quality — future

No customer exceeds 2% of revenue. Carrier supplies 62% of purchases: `supplier conc.`. A strong moat is not evidenced (Watsco calls itself the largest distributor but prints no share, and manufacturer-owned networks compete), so the concentration is a STRONG supplier-power force. Its containment: Carrier holds only a 20% minority in the joint ventures Watsco controls, the exclusive rights carry no stated term, and returns held above 17% every year with Carrier at 62–65% of purchases. The other forces are weak. The moat is cost from scale and local density, stable (gross margin 26.8% to 28.0%). Market growth MIXED (revenue −5% in 2025, +1% in the first half of 2026). **ADEQUATE** (`supplier conc.`).
