Aumovio is Continental's automotive electronics business, spun off in September 2025 — and the four-year reported record explains why Continental wanted it gone: cumulative operating income of −€741m and cumulative derived FCF of −€1,093m across FY2022–25, net losses in all four years. The adjusted-EBIT recovery narrative (2.5% → 3.9% margin) is real operational progress, but the framework runs on reported figures, and reported FY2025 OI was −€70m after €787m of restructuring and spin-off charges. Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is −1.6% against a 10% ADEQUATE floor — not a near-miss, a different universe. Future fails independently: the largest customer group takes 22.6% of revenue, OEM buyers hold structural pricing power, and Chinese tier-1s are entering every product line. Nothing here flips without a multi-year demonstration that the 6–8% margin ambition converts into reported operating income; the FY2026 annual report is the first clean standalone read.

## 2. Understanding

Aumovio SE is a Frankfurt-headquartered tier-one automotive electronics supplier — brake systems, sensors, ADAS/radar, displays, and E/E architecture, hardware-skewed and top-3 positioned in ~80% of sales — selling to global vehicle OEMs (Europe 51%, North America 21%, APAC 25% of revenue), operating as an SE spun off from Continental's Automotive sector in September 2025 with the Schaeffler family's IHO Group holding 46%, a €18.5bn-revenue, 82,000-employee business that is new as a listed entity but decades old operationally. **PASS.**
