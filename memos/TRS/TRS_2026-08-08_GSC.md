# TRS — TriMas Corporation: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS · Past **WEAK** · Future **WEAK** · Shareholder-friendliness **FAIL** (flag pair) · Cheapness **EXPENSIVE** → **AVOID**.

TriMas today is not the company its record describes. On 16 March 2026 it sold its Aerospace arm for roughly $1.5bn gross / $1.2bn net, leaving a packaging-components business (83% of continuing sales), a steel-cylinder unit, and $1.24bn of cash against a $1.44bn market capitalisation. What remains is the low-return half: continuing operating income ran $51.0m → $13.1m → $31.9m across FY2023–25 on flat revenue near $650m, worst-of-four returns near 4%. What stands between here and better is proof, not price: management must show the packaging business can earn a real return, and deploy $1.24bn without repeating an acquisition programme that consumed $213m over five years while returns fell.

## 2. Understanding

TriMas designs and manufactures closure and dispensing systems — caps, pumps, sprayers, jars, specialty closures — for consumer-products, life-science and industrial companies that fill and sell packaged goods, plus a smaller unit making high-pressure steel cylinders. Mid-cap US-listed industrial manufacturer, Michigan-headquartered, plants across North America, Europe and Asia, holding cash equal to most of its market value after the aerospace sale. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four ROCE ≈ **4.1%** (wc, end-period) against the 10% floor; FCFROCE ≈ **3.9%**. Operating income fell three consecutive years — $95.4m (FY21) → $55.2m → $51.0m → $13.1m (FY24) — before a partial FY25 recovery to $31.9m, so the returns-decline flag fires alone. ΔOI/sh ≈ **−22.7%/yr**, ΔFCF/sh ≈ −4.6%/yr, both far under the 3% floor; ΔBV/sh +4.5%/yr, ΔTBV/sh +31.1%/yr — the latter an artifact of the held-for-sale reclassification. Leverage is the one comfortable line: L/E 110.5% at FY2025, 55.7% at 30 June 2026. **WEAK** — bound-proof: no defensible treatment lifts a 4% return to 10%.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS** consolidated — the 10-K states no individual customer reached 10% of net sales in 2025, 2024 or 2023 — but each segment carries 10%-plus customers at segment level, the concentration that bites a two-segment company. Supplier breadth passes. Customer power **STRONG** (consolidating buyers, segment-level concentration, five years of margin evidence that price cannot be held); supplier power **WEAK**; substitutes **WEAK** for dispensing systems, though composite cylinders substitute directly in Specialty Products; new entrants **WEAK**. Tally 1 strong — the judgment zone. The moat is switching costs from tooling and requalification, **eroding**: a business with a real one does not compress from 7.8% to 2.1% operating margin in a year. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

The June 2025 CEO inducement to Thomas Snyder — 152,439 RSUs plus 900,000 options struck from $30 to $50 — breaches the trailing-three-year ceiling of **$1.93m** on the RSU leg alone at any price above $12.68, below which the stock has not traded in the window; the fiscal-year ceiling of $3.46m is very probably breached too, but the Summary Compensation Table was not retrieved, so severity is INDETERMINATE and this stands as a **flag**, not a strike. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: roughly $213m of bolt-ons across FY2021–25 while operating income fell 87%, against a live programme buying stock at $36.82 average (≈16–17× continuing EV/OI), $76.5m authorised, no published deployment criterion for the $1.24bn. RPT clean; insiders and directors hold 17.5%. Two independent flags → **FAIL**.
