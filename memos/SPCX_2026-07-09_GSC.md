# SPCX — Space Exploration Technologies Corp.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | SF | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS (complexity noted) | WEAK | WEAK | FAIL | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** |

SpaceX listed on Nasdaq on 12 June 2026 — 556m shares at $135, ~$75bn raised, the largest IPO in history — so it enters the register as an ordinary ANALYSE, not an out-of-scope name. The framework's answer is unambiguous: AVOID on all three quality/governance gates. Three recast fiscal years of data (thin-history cap, P1), negative returns on capital, L/E ~1,973% at FY2025, breadth failures on both sides, and a governance file containing some of the largest hard strikes this register has recorded: a CEO performance grant of up to 1 billion shares, pre-IPO tender buybacks at triple-digit implied EV/OI, and the related-party xAI merger. At ~$2.0tn against negative operating income and −$14bn FCF, no cheapness question arises. What would flip anything here is structural — a Starlink spin-out above all — not price.

## 2. Understanding

One sentence: SpaceX is a founder-controlled, vertically integrated US space-and-AI conglomerate that sells (i) launch services on reusable Falcon and Starship rockets to NASA, the DoD and commercial satellite operators (~22% of FY2025 revenue, loss-making by choice during Starship development), (ii) Starlink satellite broadband subscriptions to ~10.3m consumers, enterprises, airlines and governments in 155+ countries (61% of revenue; the only profitable segment, $4.42bn OI), and (iii) AI products and compute via the February 2026 all-stock absorption of xAI (Grok, the X platform, data-center capacity rented to Anthropic) — HQ Starbase, Texas; newly public; Musk holds ~85% of votes on ~42% of equity via 10× super-voting stock. PASS — the businesses are describable, but the three-segment conglomerate with a just-closed related-party mega-merger stretches the form parameter; logged.

## 3. Quality — past record

WEAK, on the record that exists. The S-1 carries three recast fiscal years (FY2023–25, combined with xAI under common-control presentation) and two balance-sheet years — far short of P1, so STRONG is unavailable and every figure below carries the thin-history flag. Revenue grew 10.4 → 14.0 → $18.7bn, but operating income ran −$3.5bn / +$0.5bn / −$2.6bn and FCF +$0.1bn / −$5.4bn / −$14.0bn as capex tripled to $20.8bn. Normalized ROCE (Σ OI FY2024–25 ÷ Σ CE, end-of-period with-cash) is **−1.8%**; FY2025 four-way ROCE runs −3.6% to −7.7%; FCFROCE −16.5%. L/E at FY2025 is **1,973%** against the 200% P5 flag (the Mar-2026 interim reads 175% only because the xAI share exchange converted $38.8bn of NCI into common equity). Per-share growth is n.m. — the recast share base (2.76bn → 9.96bn diluted → 2.93bn) is a presentation artifact. No leg of P2/P3 is met.

## 4. Quality — future

WEAK. Breadth fails on both sides. Customers: the prospectus itself flags concentration in the DoD, NASA and Anthropic; the Anthropic compute contract carries a 90-day termination right, which by itself defeats the captive-customer waiver (switching costs demonstrably not prohibitive). Suppliers: look-through, Nvidia is the effective sole GPU supplier to the AI segment (GPU depreciation up $1.67bn in FY2025). Forces: at least two strong-adverse — rivalry (Amazon's ~7,700-satellite Leo constellation ramping, Eutelsat/OneWeb; hyperscalers in AI compute) and supplier power (Nvidia) — Future WEAK per P16. The launch moat (reusability cost advantage, near-monopoly flight share) is genuine but attaches to a deliberately loss-making 22% of revenue; Starlink's scale moat is real but neither dominant nor stable with Leo arriving. Moat floor not applied; logged in Appendix D.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

FAIL — multiple independent hard strikes. **P17:** the S-1 discloses a CEO performance grant of up to 1 billion additional shares tied to milestones (including a million-resident Mars colony) — ~$135bn of potential value at the IPO price against FY2025 net income and FCF that are both negative: the 5%-of-lower(FCF, NI) proportional cap is breached by construction (denominator ≤ 0, logged), and any fair-value reading sits orders of magnitude above the ~$40m CPI-indexed absolute trigger. **P19:** FY2024 tender repurchases of $1.02bn at the reported ~$350bn December-2024 private mark imply ~**750× EV/OI** on the company's only profitable year (OI $466m); FY2023/25 repurchases ran against negative OI (n.m.); SBC of $0.7–1.9bn/yr alongside gives the pattern a sterilisation character. **RPT:** the xAI merger itself — the CEO's own entity, all-stock at a reported ~$1.25tn combined mark, structured (per reporting) around X-debt provisions; board member Gracias's Valor Equity leasing >$20bn of equipment to xAI; the pending $60bn Cursor acquisition carrying a $1.5bn termination fee plus $8.5bn deferred-services fee if the deal breaks. The dual-class caveat (85% votes / 42% equity, controlled-company exemptions) pairs with this allocation record under §2·1C. No averaging rescues any of it.

P20 considered and not applied: the capex super-cycle is documented, but no defensible pre-cycle anchor survives the xAI recombination, and a maintenance basis cannot rescue negative OI (Appendix C).

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

The risk to this AVOID is compositional, not analytical: Starlink standalone ($11.4bn revenue, $4.4bn OI, 39% segment margin, genuine scale moat) would gate very differently and a spin-out — repeatedly hinted at — would warrant a fresh ANALYSE of that entity. A post-Starship, post-AI-buildout profitability inflection could eventually repair Past; nothing repairs the governance file short of restructuring the grant and the related-party web. Execution risk on Starship, FAA/FCC/CFIUS exposure, and the 180-day lockup expiry (December 2026) are noted for context; none bears on the verdict.
