# DSY.PA — Dassault Systèmes SE: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | **WEAK** | STRONG | **FAIL** | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** (Past + SF gates) |

A genuinely great franchise — the deepest switching-cost moat in enterprise software outside ERP — that fails the model twice. The Medidata acquisition (€5.2bn cash, 2019, >100× the target's operating income) permanently bloated capital employed: the worst-of-four normalized 10-year ROCE is 9.6%, below even the ADEQUATE floor. The same deal fails the acquisition audit, and buybacks are explicitly run to sterilise option/share-grant dilution at 17–45× EV/OI — the book's named strike. The −73% drawdown from the 2021 peak (worst trading day ever on 11 Feb 2026) still leaves both primaries at 2.1–2.9× their thresholds. The verdict would flip only if a decade of post-Medidata operating income rebuilds the ROCE record above 10% worst-of-four *and* capital-allocation behaviour changes under the new Daloz chairmanship — both observable, neither quick.

## 2. Understanding

Dassault Systèmes is a 45-year-old, family-controlled (GIMD/Dassault ≈40% of capital, majority of votes via French double-voting rights) French Societas Europaea headquartered in Vélizy-Villacoublay that develops differentiated 3D design, simulation, PLM and clinical-trial software (CATIA, SOLIDWORKS, ENOVIA, SIMULIA, DELMIA, MEDIDATA) sold as subscriptions, support contracts and licenses on its 3DEXPERIENCE platform — directly and through resellers — to ~370,000 paying manufacturing, life-sciences and infrastructure organisations spread roughly evenly across Europe, the Americas and Asia; it is the global #1 in high-end CAD/PLM. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized 10y average ROCE is **9.6%** (end-of-period, cash-in) — below the 10% ADEQUATE floor; the four readings span 9.6–14.4%. FCFROCE is the mirror image: worst-of-four **11.1%**, clearing even the STRONG 8% bar on every variant — deferred-revenue float plus ~2–3% capex intensity make this a cash machine on a goodwill-bloated balance sheet (CE doubled overnight in 2019 with Medidata; OI took five years to grow into it). Per-share growth is healthy: OI/sh +7.7%, FCF/sh +10.8% geometric, both above the 3% floor. L/E 71%, no leverage issue; no sustained return decline post-2020. **Verdict: WEAK** — the level test fails on the worst reading, and the model forbids excusing it.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS both sides: no customer near 10% of revenue (~370k customers); suppliers are labour and cloud infrastructure, partly self-owned (OUTSCALE). Forces: customer power WEAK (fragmented base, prohibitive switching costs); supplier power WEAK; substitutes WEAK (Siemens/PTC/Autodesk compete for new seats, but installed-base data gravity blocks displacement — AI-native CAD is the watch item); new entrants WEAK (decades of functionality plus certification barriers). Tally 0 strong. Moat: **switching costs** — aerospace and auto programmes run for decades on CATIA/ENOVIA data — stable. Market growth YES, though decelerating (2026 guide 3–5% cc). **Verdict: STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Comp passes: CEO Daloz ~€15.5m (2024, incl. 450k performance shares) vs strikes of €27.5m/$30m and €60m (5% of NI); director fees within French norms. RPT (Dassault Aviation under common GIMD control) modest; control block is a documented caveat, not a strike. **Two strikes elsewhere:** (1) buybacks (€283–640m p.a.) are explicitly framed as offsetting stock-option/share-grant programmes and executed at 17–45× EV/OI — sterilisation at premium prices, the book's "theft"; diluted shares still *rose* 1,287m→1,328m over the decade. (2) Acquisition audit: Medidata, €5.2bn net cash for a target earning ~$44m GAAP OI (>100× OI, ~9× sales) — indefensible under this framework, whatever the strategic merit. **Verdict: FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- AI-native generative design tools could, over a decade, erode the switching-cost moat that anchors the Future verdict.
- European automotive — a core vertical — is structurally contracting; Q4-25 revenue grew only 1%.
- Medidata/Life Sciences revenue declined 2% in 2025 as pharma cut trial starts.
- IFRS OI carries ~€330m/yr acquired-intangible amortization; as-reported discipline makes DSY screen worse than its cash economics — a known feature, not a bug, of the model.
- Family control plus loyalty voting removes any external check on capital allocation.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

SF flips only on observed behaviour change: buybacks priced against worth (or share cancellation exceeding grants) and no framework-hostile M&A under Daloz. Cheapness needs a further ~60–75% price decline. Next review: FY2026 results (~Feb 2027) or price < €10.

## C. Calculation notes

**IFRS interest:** interest paid sits within CFO (no financing-section interest line) — no D10 reclass needed. **Maintenance capex:** no disclosed breakout; all capex subtracted (overly-dark bias, minor here at 2–3% of revenue). **OI:** as reported, including ~€330m/yr amortization of acquired intangibles and the 2020 COVID-year compression — diluted by averaging only. **Shares:** 5:1 split of Jul-2021 applied retroactively to 2016–2020.
