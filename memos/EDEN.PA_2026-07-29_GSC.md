# EDEN.PA — Edenred SE: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; SF FAIL (P19); price FAIR — verdict **AVOID**, failing three gates independently.

Edenred is the thing the model is built to catch: a genuinely fine growth record (OI/share +12.2%/yr over nine years) sitting on top of a capital base that is mostly other people's money, a moat whose monetization layer governments have begun to legislate away, and a decade of buying back its own stock at 14–24× EV/OI to feed employee share plans — then finally buying cheaply only after the price collapsed. What separates it from a BUY is not price (the all-cash trigger bound sits 11% below spot) but quality on both time axes and a disqualified capital-allocation record. The verdict flips only if the float-in-CE treatment is amended in MODEL mode (Past would read 11.1–11.9% → ADEQUATE), the Italian/Brazilian fee-cap wave stops at two jurisdictions, and the 2025-vintage buyback discipline persists long enough to satisfy the redemption clause — all three, not any one.

## 2. Understanding

Edenred is a 60-year-old, French-headquartered, Euronext-listed global leader in specific-purpose prepaid payment solutions — meal and food vouchers, employee-benefit and engagement cards, fleet/mobility fuel-and-toll cards, and corporate virtual payments — whose paying customers are over one million employer and fleet-operator clients (fees on ~€49bn of loaded business volume) and two million affiliated merchants (redemption commissions), supplemented by float income on ~€6bn of prepaid funds, operating a two-sided digital network across 44 countries with revenue concentrated in Europe and Latin America. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

The worst-of-four normalized averages read ROCE 7.7% and FCFROCE 7.9% (both end-of-period, cash-inclusive), against 11.1–11.9% on the cash-exclusive variants — below the 10% floor, so **WEAK** on P3/P4 despite no return decline (ROCE_wc rose 6.7% → 10.0% across the window). The verdict is made by the §2·1A burden-of-proof call: "Funds to be redeemed" (€6.1bn) is the named gift-card-float class and stays in CE (Appendix C). Per-share growth is excellent — ΔOI/sh +12.2%, ΔFCF/sh +12.4% geometric — but ΔBV/ΔTBV are n.m.: equity has been negative every year since demerger. L/E n.m.; net debt/FCF 1.2×, OI covers net financial expense 5.0×. Verdict **WEAK** — the return floor, not the trajectory.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: the URD states no single customer exceeds 10% of operating revenue; the supplier base (personnel, IT, card schemes with Visa/Mastercard as ready alternatives) has no plausible >10% line. Forces: customer power WEAK (millions of SMEs, payroll-integration switching costs); supplier power WEAK; substitutes WEAK today — untaxed cash salary is kept inferior only by legislated tax wedges; new entrants WEAK (licenses, scale), though Swile/open-loop entry shows the barrier thinning. Tally 0 strong. Moat: Network (2m merchants × 1m clients × 60m users), single source — **eroding**: Italy's 5% merchant-fee cap in force since 1 Sep 2025, Brazil's PAT decree (Nov 2025, contested) capping fees and mandating open schemes, and open-loop issuance commoditizing the acceptance side. Market growth YES. Verdict **WEAK** on the eroding moat.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 clean: CEO Dumazy's FY2025 total €5.86m vs the €28.2m proportional cap on both reads; lead director €135k, inside the French-norm stop. RPT: none. P19 destroys it: seven flagged tranche-years (2017–2021, 2023, 2024), none rescued — repetition hard strike; FY2021 and FY2023 breach 20× EV/OI on both reads — egregious strikes; and 2019/2021/2023 purchases made explicitly to cover performance-share plans in years breaching the flag lines at every in-range price — sterilisation strikes. FY2025's €25.7-average tranche rescues at 7.0× EV/OI but redemption limbs (a) and (c) fail. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Regulatory take-rate compression spreads: Italy's private-sector 5% cap (Law 193/2024) and Brazil's contested PAT decree are templates other deficit-strapped states can copy; France has reviewed the sector.
- Float economics are rate- and regulation-dependent: other revenue (largely float income) already fell €247m → €229m in FY2025.
- Open-loop issuance keeps commoditizing merchant acceptance, converting the network moat into a compliance-and-distribution business.
- CFO is flattered by float growth (customer money): FY2025 CFO of €1,270m includes working-capital and restricted-cash swings; a volume stall reverses the tailwind.
- Negative equity plus €4.6bn gross debt leaves no book cushion if fee caps compress OI faster than deleveraging.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To WATCH: a MODEL-mode amendment of the float-in-CE call (Past → ADEQUATE on the 11.1–11.9% xc reads) **and** stabilisation of the regulatory regime (moat call back to stable) **and** SF redemption — worth-referenced board price discipline (limb a) plus allocator turnover (limb c) on top of the 2025 bargain-zone year (limb b, satisfied). Any one alone changes nothing. To deeper AVOID: France copies the Italian cap. Next review: FY2026 URD (~Mar 2027) — re-test the 2026 buyback year under P19 (the €300m programme extension runs to Nov 2027) and the Brazil court outcome.

## C. Calculation notes

**The decisive NIBCL call — float stays in CE.** "Funds to be redeemed" (€6,125m at Dec-2025) is client prepayments held pending merchant redemption — the gift-card-float class §2·1A names as ambiguous, and the burden of proof rests with the item: it is not a trade payable (no goods/services received by Edenred), not deferred income (face value never becomes revenue), not tax or accrued compensation. Retained in CE, symmetrically with the float-backing assets (restricted cash €1,661m, trade receivables). **Alternate reading logged:** deducting the float as NIBCL would put worst-of-four ROCE at ~17–18% and flip Past to STRONG-adjacent; adopting it is a MODEL-mode decision (float/insurance-adjacent substitution), not a per-name one. The verdict's sensitivity to this single call is flagged prominently.

**Cash & STI for CE_xc/EV:** cash & equivalents + other marketable securities only. Restricted cash and current financial assets stay in CE and in EV (they collateralize the float; treating them as excess would double-count the client money the wc-variant already charges). Conservative and consistent with the float call.

**Latest-year CE build (Dec-2025, €m):** Total assets 13,568 − NIBCL 3,124 (trade payables 1,911 + current tax 39 + other payables 1,174) = **CE_wc 10,444**; − cash 1,885 − OMS 1,689 = **CE_xc 6,870**.

**TBV build:** equity group −922 − goodwill 3,003 − intangibles 1,358 = **−5,283**.

**EV build (both variants, €m):** MCap 6,634 (235.399m sh × €28.18) + gross debt 4,507 (non-current 3,385 + current ex-OD 1,088 + overdrafts 34) + IFRS-16 lease liabilities 119 + NCI 104 = **EV no-cash 11,364**; − cash & STI 3,574 = **EV all-cash 7,790**. NCI put options (~€200m within other financial liabilities) excluded — contingent equity purchase, not debt.

**Leases:** window is IFRS-16 (2019+) on-balance-sheet; pre-2019 operating-lease commitments were far below the P18 10%-of-assets line (asset base is float-dominated) — no capitalisation. **Interest:** Edenred's CFS runs from net profit with net financial expense inside CFO — no IFRS financing-reclass needed. **OI composition:** as-reported "Operating profit incl. equity-accounted" (EBIT + associates + other income/expenses); FY2023's −€195m other income/expenses (impairments, hyperinflation, restructuring) left in per the no-add-backs rule and diluted by averaging. **No P20:** capex/sales ~6–7% and stable; no super-cycle. **Goodwill:** stays in CE — serially acquisitive (UTA, CSI, Reward Gateway, Spirii).
