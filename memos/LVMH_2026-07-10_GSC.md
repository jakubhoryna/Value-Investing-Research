# MC.PA — LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton SE: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | ADEQUATE | STRONG | **FAIL** | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID (SF gate)** |

LVMH is the closest thing the register holds to the archetypal moat portfolio — 75 heritage maisons, half a century of pricing power, a 49.8% owner-operator — and it fails the one gate that asks whether management treats shareholder cash the way the framework demands. Every material buyback year from 2021 through 2024 repurchased stock above 20× MCap/FCF at execution — 2023 at 37.7×, buying €1.5bn of stock at €837.5 within months of the all-time high — and the Tiffany acquisition paid ~22× the target's pre-deal normalized operating income. A recurring slice of every programme exists explicitly to cover bonus-share plans. The quality gates pass; the capital-allocation record strikes, repeatedly, and 49.8% family control means the policy is the owner's own. A BUY would additionally need the price to fall ~64% to the ≈€179 band; the SF flip needs a multi-year record of below-worth or halted repurchases first.

## 2. Understanding

LVMH designs, manufactures (substantially in-house, from vineyards to tanneries) and retails (≈5,900 own stores) differentiated luxury goods — leather goods and fashion (Louis Vuitton, Dior: roughly half of revenue and ~three quarters of profit), wines & spirits, perfumes & cosmetics, watches & jewelry (Tiffany, Bulgari) and selective retailing (Sephora) — sold to affluent individual consumers worldwide (Asia, US, Europe roughly balanced), organised as a French SE controlled 49.8%/65.9% by the Arnault family through Christian Dior SE, the world's largest and most prominent luxury group (roots to 1743, current form since 1987). **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE 14.1% (best variant 16.7%) — above the 10% ADEQUATE floor, below the 15% STRONG bar: the €40bn+ of acquired brands and goodwill (Tiffany, Dior Couture) sit in capital employed and price the record honestly. Worst-of-four FCFROCE 10.3% clears its 8% leg. Per-share growth is STRONG-tier: ΔOI/sh +10.3%/yr, ΔFCF/sh +10.8%/yr geometric over the decade. L/E 108%, comfortably inside P5. One warning logged: ROCE has fallen two consecutive years from the 2023 peak (19.1% → 14.9% → 12.9% on beginning CE) — cyclical luxury demand so far, but a third down year would force the sustained-decline re-read. FY2015–18 lease-capitalised per P18. **ADEQUATE** — returns level, not growth, is what denies STRONG.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: PASS both sides — millions of retail customers, no concentration disclosure because none exists; supply substantially internalised, no supplier near 10% of COGS+opex. Forces: customer power WEAK (fragmented, brand-captive); supplier power WEAK (vertical integration, LVMH is the dominant buyer of luxury inputs); substitutes WEAK for the dominant unit (Veblen dynamics keep substitute value low for the core customer — the 2024–26 aspirational lapse is demand elasticity, noted, and cognac faces genuine substitution in a 7%-of-revenue pocket); new entrants WEAK (heritage cannot be fabricated; barriers to success measured in decades). Tally 0 strong. Moat: **Brand**, portfolio-wide, stable — pricing power held through the downturn even as volumes softened. Market growth MIXED (personal luxury contracted 2024–25; long-run thesis intact). **STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 clean: Arnault's FY2025 total ≈€7.9m is 1.5% of the 5%-of-lower(FCF, NI) line (€544m); no disclosed pay near the ≈$40m indexed trigger; top outside director €129k < the ~€200k local stop; 49.8% insider ownership is the alignment positive. RPT small and disclosed (Agache billings €3m). **P19 fails the gate twice over:** repurchases breached 20× at execution in 2019 and every year 2021–2024 — MCap/FCF 20.9×/20.2×/24.0×/37.7×/24.5×, with 2021 and 2023 also over 20× EV/OI (21.1×/20.1×, lower-EV variant) — and the 2021 Tiffany acquisition audit reads **22.1×** pre-deal normalized OI ($17.2bn EV incl. assumed debt and leases ÷ $777m 3-yr average). Below-worth inapplicable throughout (executions ran 3–5× the framework worth line); a dedicated plan-coverage tranche each year (579,981 shares in 2025) adds the sterilisation limb. 2025's programme (17.2× EV/OI, 18.4× MCap/FCF) merely flags. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- A third consecutive year of ROCE decline (FY2026) would re-read Past as sustained-decline WEAK.
- Chinese luxury demand is the single largest earnings swing factor; a structural (not cyclical) aspirational-customer exit would attack the substitutes call.
- Succession: Arnault is 77; the five children hold operating roles — a transition mishandled would test both moat stewardship and the control caveat.
- US/EU tariff regimes on European luxury goods compress the price-mix engine.
- W&S (cognac destocking, structural volume decline) and DFS exit costs keep reported OI noisy.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

AVOID → WATCH requires the SF gate to clear: a sustained multi-year record of repurchases below the 14×/16× flag lines (or below demonstrated conservative worth), no plan-coverage tranches, and no >15× acquisitions — one clean programme year will not reverse it (NOVO precedent). Family control makes a policy reversal improbable; treat as structural. Past ADEQUATE→STRONG re-opens only if normalized ROCE crosses 15% worst-of-four. Next review: FY2026 results (~late Jan 2027) and the 2027 URD buyback tables (~Mar 2027); price alone triggers nothing while the SF gate fails.
