## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS (three material units); Past WEAK (6.9% ROCE); Future STRONG; Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (buybacks); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** (Past + SF).

Roper owns about thirty niche vertical-software and instrument businesses with segment margins of 27–44%. The businesses are good; the capital record is not. A decade of acquisitions paid at high multiples has built $31bn of goodwill and intangibles, so the whole company earns about 7% on the capital it employs, below every return bar. Repurchases began in late 2025 at prices clearly above worth, and the programme is still running with the stock above worth. At $436.23 the stock trades at 23.9× EV/OI against a $56.74 trigger. A flip needs returns on capital above 10% and repurchases held to worth.

## 2. Understanding

Group sentence: Roper is a 44-year-old, Sarasota-based US holding company of about thirty decentralised niche businesses, most selling vertical software by subscription, licence and maintenance to organisations — law firms, government contractors, school districts, insurance agencies, laboratories, utilities, childcare centres, universities, freight brokers and carriers, contractors and food distributors — and the rest selling instruments and devices (water meters to municipal utilities, airway and ultrasound devices to hospitals, RFID readers and measurement systems to equipment makers), 87% of revenue in the US; once an industrial group, it sold its industrial businesses (Indicor) in 2022.

- **Application Software** (56.7% of 2025 revenue): management, finance and compliance software for law firms (Aderant), government contractors (Deltek), K-12 districts (Frontline), P&C agencies (Vertafore), laboratories (Clinisys, Data Innovations), utilities (PowerPlan), childcare centres (Procare), campuses (Transact/CBORD), hospitals (Strata) and autism-therapy clinics (CentralReach), paid for by those organisations, with integrated payment processing at Procare and Transact.
- **Network Software** (20.3%): marketplaces and networked software — freight matching (DAT), pre-construction bidding (ConstructConnect), food supply chain (iTradeNetwork), life-insurance sales (iPipeline), visual effects (Foundry), care-pharmacy software and church giving (Subsplash), paid for by the participating businesses.
- **Technology Enabled Products** (23.0%): water meters and meter-reading networks (Neptune) bought by utilities, airway and bladder devices (Verathon) and ultrasound accessories (CIVCO) bought by hospitals, plus pumps, RFID readers and measurement systems sold to equipment makers.
