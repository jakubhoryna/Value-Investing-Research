# MC.PA — LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton SE: GSC Memo (v3 — fresh full ANALYSE)

## 1. Verdict

LVMH passes understanding, rates ADEQUATE on its past record and STRONG on its future, passes shareholder-friendliness with one flag (capital-allocation CAUTION), and is EXPENSIVE — **WATCH**.

LVMH is worth owning for the world's most valuable portfolio of luxury brands, led by Louis Vuitton and Dior, which still earned a 35% margin in Fashion & Leather Goods in 2025 after two years of falling demand. Price stands between here and a BUY: at €396.85 the shares trade at 13.4–14.1× EV/OI against a 7× bar, and the band of ≈€151–176 lies 56–62% lower. The nearer flip runs the other way: a third consecutive fall in ROCE at FY2026 forces Past WEAK and an AVOID, and H1-2026 operating profit was 3.2% lower year on year. A repurchase tranche reading ≈25× on every basis, or a second governance negative pairing with the buyback flag, would reach AVOID through shareholder-friendliness instead.

## 2. Understanding

LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton SE is a Paris-headquartered holding company, controlled by the Arnault family (≈50% of capital, ≈66% of votes), whose ~75 maisons design, make and sell differentiated luxury goods mainly through their own ~6,300 stores and websites to affluent individual consumers across Asia, the United States, Europe and Japan — Fashion & Leather Goods (Louis Vuitton, Christian Dior, Loro Piana; 47% of 2025 revenue, 74% of recurring profit), Selective Retailing (Sephora; 23%), Watches & Jewelry (Tiffany, Bvlgari; 13%), Perfumes & Cosmetics (10%) and Wines & Spirits (Moët Hennessy, 34% owned by Diageo; 7%) — and is the world's largest luxury group (€80.8bn revenue) two years into a post-boom downturn. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 14.1% (year-end capital including cash) and the best 16.7%; worst FCFROCE is 10.3%. From FY2015 to FY2025 operating income per share compounded 10.3% a year, free cash flow per share 10.8% and book value per share 10.9%; tangible book is not meaningful after acquired brands and goodwill. Liabilities-to-equity is 108%. The trend is the weakness: ROCE on opening capital fell from 19.1% (2023) to 14.9% and 12.9% — two consecutive declines, one short of the sustained-decline test. **ADEQUATE** — returns clear 10% on every reading, never 15% on the worst.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both sides: no customer reaches a tenth of revenue, and the largest suppliers are category-level (Louis Vuitton's top leather supplier ~22% of its leather) against a €63bn cost base. For the dominant unit, Fashion & Leather Goods, all four forces are weak: buyers are millions of individuals, suppliers are fragmented, substitutes lack the status value, and entrants face century-old brands and prime retail. Hennessy's cognac share loss (53.7% → 47.9%) is real but small. The moat is brand and **stable**: F&LG revenue fell 10% and its margin 4.9 points over 2023–25, yet the margin remains 35% and organic growth resumed in Q2 2026 — cyclical deleverage, not lost pricing power. Market growth is mixed. **STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation is clean: Bernard Arnault's ≈€7.9m is 1.5% of the €544m proportional line. Related-party flows with Agache are trivial. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: €1.7–1.9bn buyback tranches in 2023, 2025 and H1 2026 with no price criterion, executed at ≈15–17× EV/OI in 2025 and H1 2026 and at 37.7× free cash flow in 2023, funded from cash flow; Tiffany cost ~22× pre-deal operating income. No tranche reads ≈25× on all four bases, so not ADVERSE. The family's September plan to lock its stake into a partnership-limited holding strengthens a control caveat that stays unpaired. **PASS.**

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth.** *Customers — PASS:* the notes state that no single customer exceeded a tenth of consolidated revenue in any year presented (2019 statements, note 12; the retail model — 95% of F&LG revenue — makes the condition structural). *Suppliers — PASS:* URD 2025 — the largest grape supplier is under 10% of champagne supplies; Hennessy buys eaux-de-vie from ~1,600 growers; Louis Vuitton's leading leather supplier is ~22% of its leather and the leading metal-parts supplier ~25% of its metal parts, both deliberate concentrations with alternatives cultivated; subcontracting is "diversified". **Forces** (dominant unit: Fashion & Leather Goods — 47% of revenue, 74% of 2025 recurring profit):

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | WEAK | Millions of individual buyers; 95% sold in own stores; no backward integration |
| Supplier bargaining power | WEAK | Fragmented materials and ateliers; own workshops (Louis Vuitton: 29) and LVMH Métiers d'Art secure access |
| Threat of substitutes | WEAK | A non-luxury bag lacks the status value; doing without is cyclical, not a new function |
| Threat of new entrants | WEAK | Barriers to success: heritage, creative talent, prime retail and media scale |

Tally: **0 strong → favourable.** Segment notes: in Wines & Spirits substitution and doing-without are strong (Hennessy's share of Cognac shipments 53.7% → 47.9% in 2025; cognac region volumes −15.2%); in Perfumes & Cosmetics brand switching is easy; in Selective Retailing Sephora faces Ulta, Amazon and brand DTC. Together these three carried ~20% of 2025 recurring profit and do not move the dominant-unit tally.

**Moat.** Single source: **brand.** Heritage-and-scarcity desirability lets the maisons price far above cost while owning distribution — F&LG operating margin 39.9% (2023), 37.1% (2024), 35.0% (2025), 34.1% (H1 2026). Direction: **stable** (logged call). For erosion: F&LG revenue 42,169 → 41,060 → 37,770; margin −4.9 points; Hennessy share loss; H1-2026 F&LG organic −1%. Against: margins remain above a third of revenue through the downturn; F&LG returned to organic growth in Q2 2026 (+1%), Watches & Jewelry +11%, Sephora gaining share; creative renewals (Dior under Jonathan Anderson) are drawing demand. The evidence reads as cyclical deleverage after the 2021–23 boom, not a structural loss of pricing power. Re-test at FY2026: a third year of F&LG organic decline with further margin compression would support an eroding call and Future WEAK.

**Market growth: MIXED** — flat-to-down luxury demand in 2024–25; group organic +2% in H1 2026 with the US and Asia excluding Japan accelerating.
