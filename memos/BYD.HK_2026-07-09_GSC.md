# BYD.HK — BYD Company Limited: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | WEAK | WEAK | PASS | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** |

BYD is the world's largest new-energy-vehicle maker and, on cost and vertical integration, an unusually formidable industrial machine — it builds its own LFP "Blade" batteries, its own chips, and much of its own componentry, and it is the low-cost producer in the segment it dominates. But this is a GSC AVOID on two independent gates, and price cannot fix it. **Past is WEAK**: even across the favourable FY2021–25 boom window, the worst-of-four normalized ROCE is only ~7.4% on the model-default capital-employed treatment (below the 10% ADEQUATE floor), free-cash-flow return runs ~2%, leverage breaches P5 without P6 cover, and FY2025 free cash flow collapsed to **−RMB 97.7bn** as a supplier-payment-term unwind drained working capital while capex ran to RMB 157bn. **Future is WEAK**: three of the four competitive forces are strongly adverse (customer power, substitutes, new entrants), and BYD's real cost moat is not producing pricing power or stable returns — it is being competed away in China's price war, where BYD's own volumes fell for seven straight months into early 2026. Shareholder-friendliness passes cleanly (trivial pay against the cap, no dual-class, a net *issuer* not a buyer of stock). Cheapness is EXPENSIVE (EV/OI 22.7×/26.8×; MCap/FCF n.m. on negative FCF). What would change the verdict: a structural repair of segment economics — durable double-digit ROCE, positive sustained FCF, and forces easing as the China field consolidates — not a lower price.

## 2. Understanding

BYD Company Limited is a Shenzhen-headquartered, founder-controlled (Wang Chuanfu ~17%), dual-listed (Shenzhen A / Hong Kong H, one-share-one-vote) industrial conglomerate that, through deep vertical integration, designs and manufactures mass-market new-energy vehicles — BEVs and PHEVs, ~81% of revenue — sold mainly to Chinese retail consumers and increasingly exported to Europe, Latin America and Southeast Asia, alongside a 65.76%-owned Hong-Kong-listed handset-components-and-assembly arm (BYD Electronic, ~18%) serving global smartphone brands, plus smaller battery, energy-storage, photovoltaic and rail-transit lines. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Five fiscal years available (FY2021–25); the pre-2021 years were structurally weaker (2019 net profit RMB 1.6bn, the "darkest moment"), so this is the *favourable* window and STRONG is unavailable (<P1 history + the record itself). Worst-of-four normalized ROCE = **7.4%** on the conservative NIBCL treatment (leaving ambiguous "other current liabilities" in capital employed per the burden-of-proof rule); a fuller annual-report netting of trade notes payable could lift it to ~10.8% (borderline ADEQUATE) — logged. FCFROCE ~2–4% (all-capex). Growth is two-faced: OI/sh compounded ~49%/yr off a low base but *fell* in FY2025; FCF/sh ends deeply negative; BV/sh growth is partly placement-funded, not retained. **L/E 253.9%** breaches P5 with no P6 cover (the breach is trade-payable float — net cash RMB 14.8bn — but the mechanical test fails). Verdict **WEAK**: sub-floor returns on the default treatment, a leverage breach, and a −RMB 97.7bn FCF year.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS both sides (millions of retail buyers, none >10%; vertical integration makes BYD its own supplier). Forces — customer power **STRONG** adverse (huge choice, near-zero switching, active price war); supplier power **WEAK**/favourable (integrated batteries/chips); substitutes **STRONG** adverse (dozens of rival NEV models, free cross-shopping); new entrants **STRONG** adverse (Xiaomi's SU7, Huawei-backed marques, and a field of well-funded startups taking share). Tally **3 strong adverse** → the book's stop signal. Moat: a genuine **Cost** moat (scale + Blade LFP + integration), strengthening on volume — but it is *not* the dominant, stable, high-return moat that floors Future, because it yields only ~4–6% operating margins and is being eroded (BYD volumes fell seven straight months into 2026; margins compressed). Market growth MIXED (global exports rising; China saturating/contested). Verdict **WEAK**.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation clean: Wang Chuanfu's total ~RMB 6.6m (FY2023, ~industry median) sits far under the 5%-of-lower(FCF, NI) hard strike (≈ RMB 1.6bn on NI); all 34 directors/supervisors/senior managers combined ~RMB 1.02bn; independent-director fees modest (≪ $500k stop). Positive alignment: Wang holds ~17% of the company (~29% of A-shares) outright. Buybacks: **no strike** — BYD is a net *issuer* (a $5.6bn H-placement in March 2025); the small treasury increase (~RMB 3.3bn) is an employee/restricted-share plan (de-minimis, charitable pass per the ADS/NVS/AXFO precedent). No dual-class (one-share-one-vote); founder control is ordinary concentration, not a strike vector. Dividend cut (RMB 1.325→0.358) is rational retention in a capex/downturn phase. BYD Electronic and FinDreams are consolidated subsidiaries (RPT rule excludes them); a possible Lü-Xiangyang-linked lithium-supplier relationship is unquantified (data gap). Verdict **PASS**.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- China NEV price war persists — the single biggest driver of the thin, falling margins and the FCF reversal; a rational consolidation would be the bull case, its opposite the bear.
- Working-capital model under pressure: the regulator-driven ≤60-day supplier-payment shift structurally lowered the payables float that funded the build-out (the FY2025 CFO hit).
- Capex super-cycle (RMB 157bn, ~19% of sales) must convert to returns; if utilization/volumes disappoint, ROCE stays sub-floor.
- Geopolitics: Pentagon "military-tied" listing (June 2026) and tariff walls cap the addressable market (US effectively closed); Europe tariffs and localisation costs.
- Well-capitalised entrants (Xiaomi, Huawei ecosystem) keep compressing share and pricing at the mass-market core.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Flip toward WATCH requires a *structural* quality change, not a price move: worst-of-four ROCE durably ≥10% with positive sustained FCF (a normalised capex + stabilised payables cycle), and the forces easing as China's field consolidates (fewer credible entrants, moderating discounting). A moat re-rating to "dominant/stable" would need BYD's cost lead to translate into pricing power and stable high returns — the opposite of the current price war. Next review: FY2026 results (~late Mar 2027); H1 2026 (~late Aug 2026) optional to track the tentative mid-2026 sales recovery and margin trajectory.
