# FR.PA — Valeo SE: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past **WEAK**; Future **WEAK**; Shareholder-friendliness **PASS** under heightened scrutiny (one flag — capital-allocation CAUTION); Cheapness **EXPENSIVE**; final verdict **AVOID on the Past and Future gates**.

Valeo is a large, competent, globally scaled tier-one supplier that does not earn a return on the capital it employs: worst-of-four normalized ROCE 4.13% against a 10% floor, and 5.24% even on the flattering operating-margin line, so no accounting convention rescues it. The largest customer is arithmetically above the 10% breadth threshold, so Future fails independently of the force tally. Nothing here is a price question — 7× operating income is less than the debt and minority interest ahead of the equity, so no positive share price makes Valeo cheap on the conservative reading. What must change is the business, not the multiple.

## 2. Understanding

Valeo SE is a widely held French *société européenne* on Euronext Paris which designs and manufactures differentiated engineered vehicle systems — electrified powertrain and thermal (POWER), lighting (LIGHT), sensors, controllers and software (BRAIN) — supplied under multi-year platform nominations won in competitive tender and paid for overwhelmingly by a handful of global vehicle manufacturers (82% of sales original equipment, 11% aftermarket distributors and garages, 7% other), from a ~100,000-employee base weighted Europe & Africa 51%, Asia 28%, North America 19% of original-equipment sales, in a definitively cyclical, capital- and R&D-intensive, globally contested industry under simultaneous electrification, software transition and Chinese entry, financed independently on €6.9bn of gross debt. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **4.13%** (end-basis, with cash) and worst-of-four FCFROCE **1.61%**, over FY2021–FY2025. The reads cluster 4.13–5.52% and 1.61–2.04%, so no denominator choice rescues them; the flattering operating-margin line gives 5.24–6.66%, and the most aggressive defensible NIBCL treatment still gives 6.21–8.22%. Every one of those numbers is below the 10% ADEQUATE floor. Per-share growth clears the 3% floor (ΔOI/sh +17.6%, ΔFCF/sh +17.1% geometric), but off a COVID-depressed FY2021 base, and book value per share fell 3.0% a year. Liabilities are 488% of book equity — 2.4× the P5 flag level. **WEAK** — returns less than half the floor on every read.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **FAILS on the customer side**. The disclosure sentence is affirmative, not exculpatory: top two customers ≈25% of original-equipment sales, so the largest is ≥12.5% of OE sales, and OE is 82% of group sales — a floor of 10.25% of revenue, corroborated by 14% of receivables. The captive-customer waiver is tested and denied: OEMs can and do insource, switching is by re-tender each model cycle, and a 4% return on capital proves no dominant moat converts the concentration into economics. Two forces read STRONG — customer power and new entrants; supplier power and substitutes WEAK. Moat: **switching costs**, programme-scoped and **eroding**. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK** on both grounds.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation clears with headroom but not comfort: the CEO's €4.0m total (FY2024, tier-3) is 40% of the fiscal-year threshold (5% of the lower of FCF €234m and attributable net income €200m = €10.0m) and 41% of the €9.72m normalized threshold — a 2.4× margin, the tightest in the French block. The related-party note shows only equity-accounted trading. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: repurchases execute at 9.1–12.1× EV/OI, nowhere near the ≈25× calibration, and the dividend is FCF-funded and doctrinally right for a low-return business, but €8.8bn of five-year reinvestment has produced no returns record to sustain. One flag, no pair. **PASS**, under heightened scrutiny.

- The governance record is tier-3 because valeo.com is unreachable — an access failure, not a disclosure failure.

Future flips only if Valeo discloses that no single customer exceeds 10% of revenue **and** the entrants force reverses; the customer-power call is structural. **Leases.** IFRS 16 has been in force throughout the FY2021–FY2025 window, so no P18 pre-adoption capitalisation is required. Adding them again would be double-counting.

**FCF basis.** Valeo classifies **interest paid in the financing section** (FY2025 €294m, FY2024 €307m, FY2023 €261m), so per §2·1A it is moved into CFO before FCF is derived. The primary series is therefore **FCF = CFO − interest paid − capex** (FY2025: 2,289 − 294 − 1,761 = €234m). Three alternatives are stated rather than hidden, because the spread is wide enough to matter to Gate 2: **(a) unadjusted CFO − capex = €528m** — this is also MarketScreener's series and the arithmetic used to derive FY2021/FY2022 capex; **(b) net-interest basis** (also adding back interest received of €76m) **= €310m**; **(c) the company's own published free cash flow = €589m** post-restructuring / €756m pre-restructuring, which deducts net financial interest *and* lease-liability repayments and adds asset-disposal proceeds and investment subsidies. The framework's definition governs; the effect on the cheapness gate is set out in Appendix F. Note that because Valeo classifies lease principal repayments inside "repayment of long-term debt" (financing), CFO carries the standard post-IFRS-16 flattery in all four variants.

**Maintenance capex (P20) — NOT ACTIVE.** Condition (i) fails outright: capex has run 7.8%, 7.4%, 9.1%, 10.3% and 8.4% of sales across FY2021–FY2025 — no documented multi-year ramp far above a pre-cycle ratio, and the FY2025 figure is below the FY2023–FY2024 level. No company-disclosed maintenance figure exists; D&A is not used as a proxy. All capex is subtracted and the resulting dark bias is noted. **Capitalised development costs (€971m of the €1,761m FY2025 capex, and €443m in H1-2026 alone) are the single most consequential accounting judgment in this file** — they simultaneously depress FCF, inflate intangible assets inside capital employed, and drive tangible book negative. They are treated as capex without adjustment, which is the framework's position and also the conservative one.

**Captive-finance / substitution rule.** Neither applies. Valeo has no captive finance arm, is not a securities-heavy holdco, insurer or deposit-funded lender; EV/OI is meaningful and is the anchor.

**Currency.** EUR throughout, the reporting currency. The VLEEY ADR (OTC, unsponsored) is not priced here and no ratio is asserted.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

### Breadth (P15)

**Customer — FAIL.**

> *"In both 2024 and 2023, the Group's top two customers represented around 25% of original equipment sales."* — Valeo, 2024 Consolidated Financial Statements.
> *"At December 31, 2024, Valeo's largest automotive customer accounted for 14% of the Group's accounts and notes receivable."* — same source.

The framework's test is the annual-report disclosure sentence. Valeo's sentence is **affirmative of concentration**, not exculpatory: it does not state that no customer exceeds 10% of revenue, and no such statement appears anywhere in the retrievable record. The arithmetic then forecloses the point:

| Step | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Top two customers, share of original-equipment sales | ~25% | 2024 statements |
| Therefore the **larger** of the two is at least half of that | **≥ 12.5% of OE sales** | arithmetic bound |
| Original equipment as a share of group sales | 82% (aftermarket 11%, other 7%) | H1-2026 results |
| Therefore the largest customer is at least | **≥ 10.25% of group revenue** | arithmetic bound |
| Corroboration — largest customer's share of receivables | 14% | 2024 statements |

The bound is a floor, not an estimate: the largest customer cannot be smaller than half of the top-two figure, and the receivables disclosure points materially higher. **The 10% threshold is breached. No number has been invented — the exact single-customer percentage is not disclosed and is logged as INDETERMINATE, but INDETERMINATE on the *magnitude* does not rescue a threshold that the *floor* already crosses.** Named re-test at URD-2026, which may disclose the figure directly.

**Captive-customer waiver — TESTED AND DENIED.** The waiver requires all three limbs; Valeo fails all three.

| Limb | Requirement | Valeo | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customers demonstrably cannot backward-integrate **and** face prohibitive switching costs | OEMs are the canonical backward-integrators in this industry: Volkswagen took electronics and software in-house (CARIAD), Tesla vertically integrated powertrain and thermal from the start, BYD supplies itself through FinDreams, and several OEMs run captive lighting and seating operations. Switching is by **competitive re-tender at each model nomination** — a 3–7 year cycle, not a permanent lock — and dual-sourcing is standard practice. | **FAIL** |
| 2 | A dominant, stable moat | A 4.13% worst-of-four return on capital is direct evidence that whatever moat exists does not convert concentration into economics. A dominant moat that yields less than its cost of capital is not a dominant moat. | **FAIL** |
| 3 | Concentration structural to the industry | This limb **passes**: the world has roughly fifteen vehicle groups of scale, and any tier-one of Valeo's size will be concentrated on a handful of them. | Pass |
| | | **Waiver requires all three → DENIED on limbs 1 and 2.** | |

The precedents where the waiver succeeded — ASML, TSMC, QCOM — share the feature Valeo lacks: the customer cannot practically go elsewhere or make it itself, and the supplier's returns prove it. The precedent this name resembles is **V (Visa)**, where an 11%-client disclosure was a hard breach and the waiver was denied, and **NVDA / MU**, where concentration plus a contestable position gave breadth FAIL. Logged here in full because the brief asked for the reasoning to be preserved.

**Supplier — PASS, with a look-through caveat.** Raw materials consumed were €12,038m in 2024 (56% of sales) across metals, plastics, glass, electronics and purchased sub-assemblies; 10% of COGS + opex is ≈ €1,993m on the FY2025 base, and no supplier is disclosed at or near that. The *effective* supplier worth naming is the **semiconductor supply chain** — Valeo's BRAIN and POWER divisions are semiconductor-intensive, the 2021–22 shortage demonstrated real dependence, and the disclosure does not decompose it. Not resolvable from the reachable record; logged as an open look-through. **It is not gate-determinative** — customer breadth has already failed and the force tally already reaches two — but it would need resolving before any future session upgraded Future.

### Four forces (P16) — binary, never medium

| Force | Call | One-line reason |

**Moat — single identified source: Switching costs. Direction: ERODING.** Mechanism: a nomination win on a vehicle platform locks Valeo in as sole or lead supplier for that programme's production life, because re-validating a safety-relevant part mid-cycle costs the OEM homologation, tooling and time. That is a genuine source of incumbency — but it is **scoped to the programme, not the customer**, it is re-competed at every new model, and it does not travel across the electrification transition where the part itself is being redesigned. The returns record settles the direction: capital employed of €15.0–16.3bn has earned 2.9–5.3% throughout the window, which is what an eroding moat looks like from the outside.

**Market growth: MIXED.** Global light-vehicle production is broadly flat around 90m units and Europe — Valeo's largest region at 51% of OE sales — has not recovered its pre-2019 volume; content per vehicle is rising with electrification, ADAS and lighting sophistication, and Valeo's €24.6bn FY2025 order intake (+38%) is genuine evidence of that; but pricing runs the other way and the growth is concentrated in the region (China) where Valeo's share is most contested. Neither a clean YES nor a clean NO.
