Understanding PASS; Past WEAK (4.8% ROCE); Future WEAK (unit customer conc.); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (flag pair); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** (v1: AVOID on Past and Future).

MHI is worth owning for a sole-source defence franchise and a leading place in the three-firm market for large gas turbines, both now carried by unusual demand. On nine years of IFRS figures it still earns too little on its capital: the worst reading of return on capital is 4.8%, and per-share growth cannot be read through the FY3/2020–FY3/2021 losses. The Ministry of Defense pays 72% of the defence unit's revenue, and no unit evidences that it can hold its price against its buyers. The one recent deal was bought without demonstrated profit, and outside-director fees are not printed per person. At ¥3,527 the stock trades at 32.3× EV/OI against a ¥525.2 trigger. A flip needs years of returns above 10%, per-unit customer evidence, and a price far lower.
