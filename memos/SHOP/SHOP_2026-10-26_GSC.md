# SHOP — Shopify Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (hard strikes); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID**, unchanged, now on three gates.

Shopify is a fast-growing commerce platform whose last two years finally earn a real return, but the ten-year record is losses in six years, and read with the equity stakes it holds the decade's return on capital is negative. Merchants pay all the revenue and the filings never rule out one paying more than a tenth of it; payment processing runs through two providers and the card networks behind them. The chief executive's FY2024 award of $150.0m is a hard strike, and since February 2026 the company has bought back $1.93bn of stock at about 82 times free cash flow under an authorisation raised to $5bn. At $168.18 the stock trades at 120 times free cash flow; the trigger is $7.78. Nothing on the horizon flips the verdict: Past needs years of current returns, and pay and buybacks would both have to change.

## 2. Understanding

Shopify sells subscription commerce software and attached merchant services — payment processing above all (Shopify Payments, run through Stripe and PayPal), plus merchant loans and cash advances, installments, shipping labels and point-of-sale — to millions of mostly small and mid-sized merchant businesses (the merchants pay; their shoppers do not), in the e-commerce platform industry; a Canadian company headquartered in Ottawa, with 63% of FY2025 revenue from US merchants and 21% from EMEA; a prominent, twenty-year-old platform worth about $218bn, now three-quarters payments-led (merchant solutions 76% of revenue), listed on Nasdaq since March 2025 and the TSX. One reportable segment; no material listed holding; not a group. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

**WEAK.** Lens STD. The equity stakes in Affirm, Global-E, Klaviyo, Flexport and private companies are 29–39% of total assets since FY2021, so they are read both ways. Including them with their losses — the FY2022 mark-down of $2.87bn — the worst-of-eight ten-year ROCE is −12.7% (five-year −12.2%); excluding them it is 0.7%. FCFROCE (maintenance capex constructed, all-capex beside it) is 6.4% at worst. FY2024–25 ROCE runs 18–20% excluding the stakes, too young to carry the decade. Per-share OI and FCF growth reads are not meaningful (negative starting averages), which fails on its own. Leverage is negligible (L/E 12.7%).

## 4. Quality — future

**WEAK.** All revenue comes from merchants, and the 10-K says nothing that rules out a merchant paying 10% or more, so customer concentration is flagged; the dominance and pricing-power evidence a strong moat needs is not in the filings, so it is a strong customer-power force. Supply is concentrated too: Shopify Payments runs on two processors, behind them the card networks, and hosting on Google Cloud. Two strong forces; substitutes and entrants weak. Switching-cost moat, strengthening. Market growth YES.
