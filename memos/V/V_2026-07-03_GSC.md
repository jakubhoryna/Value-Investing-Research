# V — Visa Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

A great past record — worst-of-four 10-year ROCE 21.9%, FCFROCE 18.5%, double-digit per-share compounding — undone by two mechanical gates fired by Visa's own filings: **one client at 11% of total net revenue in fiscal 2025, 2024 and 2023** (a hard P15 breach the captive-customer waiver cannot rescue — the client has a ready rival network and the concentration is commercial, not structural), and CEO compensation crossing the $30m P17 ceiling ($31.56m), corroborated by a $642k board-chair fee and $18.2bn of buybacks at ~4.6× conservative worth. A flip needs the client back under 10% *and* comp reform; the price question is academic at ~32× FCF.

## 2. Understanding

Visa operates the world's largest open-loop payments network, selling differentiated transaction-processing services (authorisation, clearing, settlement) plus cross-border and value-added services, paid for by financial-institution issuers and acquirers — one unnamed client alone is 11% of net revenue — net of large incentive rebates back to those same clients, in the payments-network industry, as a Delaware corporation with an engineered multi-class structure (public Class A; bank-held B shares and litigation-escrow plumbing shielding Class A from US interchange liability; Class C and preferred from the 2016 Visa Europe purchase), headquartered in San Francisco with ~39% of net revenue from the US and operations in 200+ territories, a maximally prominent 68-year-old, ~$700bn franchise (BankAmericard 1958, IPO 2008). **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized 10y averages: ROCE 21.9% (end-of-period, with-cash), FCFROCE 18.5% — both well clear of P2, and rising: FY2023–25 beginning-CE ROCE runs 27–29% vs ~20% early in the window; no decline flag. Growth: ΔOI/sh geomean +15.8% (+11.6% off the cleaner FY2017 base — FY2016 was depressed by Visa Europe deal-year items), ΔFCF/sh +20.2%, far above the 3% floor. ΔBV/sh +3.9% read against ~$28bn dividends and >$120bn buybacks — unhelpful, itself a finding. L/E 163%, inside P5; debt dates from 2016. **STRONG.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: customer side **FAIL** — one client at 11% of net revenue in each of FY2023–25 [sourced]. Waiver inapplicable: the client can shift issuance to a ready rival network at renewal, large issuers part-own domestic alternatives, and thousands of issuers exist — the concentration is incentive-bought, not structural. Supplier side PASS. Forces: customer power **STRONG** (11% client; incentive give-back 28.3% of gross revenue and outpacing it); supplier power WEAK; substitutes **STRONG** (state A2A rails displaced cards at national scale in Brazil and India; stablecoin settlement legislated, Visa itself joining consortia); new entrants WEAK. Tally 2 strong — the book's stop signal. Moat: network effects — dominant, stable in the card core, eroding at the margin where state rails and wallets intermediate. Market growth YES. **WEAK.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- The 11% client cuts both ways: its renewal terms set the incentive curve for every mega-issuer; its loss would be a step-change.
- Client incentives (28.3% of gross revenue, rising) are transferring network economics to concentrated issuers — take-rate erosion already in the P&L.
- Interchange litigation and price-cap regulation are structural: FY2025's $2.6bn provision shows the machinery is not history.
- State A2A rails and stablecoin settlement are proven substitutes at national scale; the perimeter of the card moat is shrinking.
- Governance drift: comp crossed the ceiling as "compensation actually paid" hit $55.8m; 92% say-on-pay implies no pressure to reverse.
