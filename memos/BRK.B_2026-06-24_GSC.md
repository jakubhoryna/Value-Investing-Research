# BRK.B — Berkshire Hathaway: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | STRONG | ADEQUATE | PASS | FAIR | **WATCH** |

Berkshire is a fortress: a holding company whose insurance float and retained earnings fund both a ~$709B securities portfolio and a stable of irreplaceable franchises (BNSF, BHE, GEICO, dozens of manufacturing and retail businesses), run with near-zero net leverage and the market's most disciplined capital allocation. Book value per share — Buffett's own intrinsic-value proxy — compounded ~11.5%/yr over twelve years. What stands between here and BUY is price, not quality: at ~1.47× book and ~16× look-through operating earnings it is fair, not cheap. The forward question is succession — Greg Abel became CEO on 1 Jan 2026; the businesses run themselves, but Buffett's capital-allocation edge is the part of the moat now under test. BUY needs a drawdown toward ≤~1.2× book; AVOID would need capital-allocation indiscipline under Abel or a break in the core franchises.

## 2. Understanding

Berkshire Hathaway is a US-listed, Omaha-based diversified holding company that owns controlled insurance businesses (GEICO, Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance, primary insurers) whose ~$176B float and retained earnings fund a large marketable-securities portfolio plus wholly-owned non-insurance subsidiaries — freight rail (BNSF), regulated utilities and energy (BHE), and manufacturing, service and retail — earning revenue from premiums, investment income, and subsidiary sales, and operating chiefly in the United States as the dominant US conglomerate. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Per-share book value (the framework's ΔTBV/sh proxy and Buffett's own gauge) compounded ~11.5%/yr over 2013–2025, rising every year bar a modest 2022 dip; pre-tax operating earnings (ex investment gains) roughly doubled across the decade. Leverage is exceptional — total liabilities/equity 0.70× and interest-bearing debt/equity just 0.18×, far inside the 200% flag. Mechanical ROCE_xc screens at ~12% (literal read: ADEQUATE), but that is depressed by ~$336B of investment assets carried in capital employed whose returns flow through book value, not reported operating income. Adjusting for that structural artifact, the decade record is **STRONG** (documented override — Appendix C).

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: customers (millions of policyholders, diversified shippers, captive utility ratepayers, retail consumers) and suppliers are both extraordinarily broad with no concentration. Forces MIXED: rail and regulated utilities are quasi-monopolies with very high entry barriers, but insurance/reinsurance face real rivalry and pricing cycles, and Berkshire's sheer size now caps incremental returns. Moats are multiple and durable — the irreplaceable BNSF network and BHE regulated base (cost/location/government), GEICO's low-cost model, plus the structural edge of permanent capital and cheap float — and stable. The capital-allocation moat (the "Buffett premium") is the single element facing a genuine post-Buffett test. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Canonical. Buffett took a $100k salary for 40+ years and reimburses Berkshire for personal costs; the operating vice-chairmen average ~$16M with no stock-based pay, under an explicit policy that compensation ignore share price. Buffett remains by far the largest holder (voting capped at 49.9% by agreement). No dividend, with rational full reinvestment; buybacks executed only below intrinsic value — essentially zero in 2024–2025 at ~1.5× book, exactly the discipline the framework rewards. Related-party transactions are minimal and Audit-Committee-gated. The Class A/B voting structure is a control caveat, easing as Buffett gifts shares. **PASS.**

Weighting the meaningful asset multiples (both pass) and a sum-of-parts read (~$709B investments at market plus operating businesses at ~16× pre-tax), Berkshire is **FAIR** at ~1.47× book — not cheap. No usable CHEAP trigger emerges from the cash-flow screens (binding MCap/FCF implies ≈$93/sh, unreachable); the operative gauge is price-to-book, with ≤~1.2× book (≈$400) the level at which Berkshire has historically been a compelling repurchaser.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- **Succession:** Abel inherits the operations cleanly, but Buffett's capital-allocation judgment was itself a core moat — its durability under a new principal is unproven.
- **Size:** a >$370B cash/T-bill pile and ~$1T market cap make high-return deployment structurally hard; cash drag persists as rates fall.
- **Insurance cyclicality:** 2023–2025 underwriting profits were unusually strong and will mean-revert; a mega-catastrophe or reserve miss could dent earnings.
- **Portfolio beta:** large single-stock positions import equity-market volatility into book value, as the 2022 dip showed.
- **Culture/headline risk** around Buffett's eventual full departure and any drift from the decentralised, low-cost model.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To BUY: a drawdown to ≤~1.2× book (~$400) with quality intact. Toward AVOID: capital-allocation indiscipline under Abel (overpaying for scale, undisciplined buybacks), a structural break in insurance/BNSF/BHE economics, or abandonment of the comp/decentralisation model. **Next review:** FY2026 results (Feb 2027) — Abel's first full year — or price falling below ~1.3× book (~$430), whichever first; monitor cash-pile deployment (e.g., the 2026 $10B Alphabet stake, $8.5B Taylor Morrison) and any buyback resumption.

## C. Calculation notes (FY2025, $M)

**Capital-employed build (unclassified balance sheet).** Berkshire reports no current/non-current split, so CE = interest-bearing debt + total equity:
- Debt (notes payable: I&O 45,763 + RUE 83,318) = 129,081
- Total equity (incl. NCI) = 719,703
- **CE (with cash) = 848,784** (= total assets 1,222,176 − non-interest-bearing liabilities {operating payables ~57,269 + insurance float ~211,302 + deferred tax 86,955 + other operating 18,866} ≈ 373,392). Reconciles to debt + equity.
- Cash & equivalents (I&O 47,719 + RUE 4,158) = 51,877; short-term T-bills = 321,434 → Cash+STI = 373,311
- **CE_xc = 848,784 − 373,311 = 475,473** (still includes equities 297,778 + fixed-maturity 17,816 + equity-method 19,978 = 335,572 of investment assets — the source of the ROCE distortion).

**TBV build.** BRK equity 717,419 − goodwill 83,074 − other intangibles 33,802 = **600,543**. TBV/share (Class B) = 600,543M ÷ 2,157.3M = **$278.37**.

**EV build.** MCap 1,054,290 + debt 129,081 + preferred 0 + NCI 2,284 − (cash+STI) 373,311 = **812,344**.

**OI.** Earnings before tax & equity-method 92,049 − investment gains 39,078 = **52,971** (pre-tax operating earnings ex MTM; excludes the one-off equity-method OTTI of $8.3B on Kraft Heinz/Occidental, which sits below this line).

**Judgment call — Past STRONG override.** Literal P2 requires ROCE_xc ≥15% in ≥8/10 yrs; Berkshire screens ~12% and would read ADEQUATE. Override to STRONG rests on (i) the ROCE being mechanically depressed by ~$336B of low-risk investments held in capital employed whose returns are realised through book-value growth rather than operating income — an accounting-structure artifact, not weak economics; and (ii) ~11.5% per-share book-value compounding over twelve years with interest-bearing leverage of 0.18× and no value destruction. Mirrors the project's established practice of documented metric overrides (moat-decides; negative-CE). Auditable alternate reading: ADEQUATE on the literal ROCE floor. Final verdict (WATCH) is unchanged either way.

## F. Valuation math

**Current multiples.** MCap 1,054,290 (488.70 × 2,157,335,139 Class B equiv shares ÷ 1e6).
- MCap/FCF = 1,054,290 / 25,042 = **42.1×**
- EV/OI = 812,344 / 52,971 = **15.3×**
- MCap/BV = 1,054,290 / 717,419 = **1.47×**
- MCap/TBV = 1,054,290 / 600,543 = **1.76×**

**Why the two cash/earnings multiples misfit Berkshire.** EV subtracts $373B of cash/T-bills but leaves ~$336B of equities/fixed-maturity/equity-method investments inside EV, while OI excludes those investments' returns (gains) — inflating EV/OI. FCF (CFO − capex) captures none of the investment portfolio's value creation and is suppressed by heavy BNSF/BHE reinvestment — inflating MCap/FCF. Both are not representative; the asset multiples (P/B, P/TBV) are the framework metrics that travel for a securities-heavy holding company, consistent with the "weight the meaningful multiples" provision used when a multiple is structurally meaningless.

**Sum-of-parts cross-check.** Investments at carrying/market: cash 51,877 + T-bills 321,434 + equities 297,778 + fixed-maturity 17,816 + equity-method 19,978 = **708,883**. Market cap 1,054,290 − investments 708,883 + debt 129,081 = **~474,488** implied for the operating businesses, vs ~29,710 pre-tax operating earnings ex investment income → **~16× pre-tax** (~19–20× after-tax). Fair, not cheap.

**Threshold-implied prices (per Class B share; shares 2,157.335M).**
- P(MCap/FCF < 8) = 8 × 25,042 / 2,157.335 = **$92.86**
- P(EV/OI < 7) = (7 × 52,971 + 373,311 − 129,081 − 0 − 2,284) / 2,157.335 = **$284.03**
- P(MCap/BV < 5) = 5 × (717,419 / 2,157.335) = **$1,662.74**
- P(MCap/TBV < 3) = 3 × (600,543 / 2,157.335) = **$835.12**

**Binding-constraint logic.** P/B and P/TBV already pass with vast room ($488.70 ≪ $1,663 and ≪ $835). For CHEAP the framework would require the cash/earnings multiples to pass (or one within 1.25× tolerance). MCap/FCF at 42× cannot qualify even as the "average" metric (1.25× tolerance = ≤10×), so it would *have* to pass outright — implying ≈$93/share (~0.28× book), which is structurally unreachable and uninformative. **Conclusion:** the framework produces no usable CHEAP trigger for Berkshire; the operative gauge is price-to-book. Reference: ≤~1.2× book ≈ **$400** (= 1.2 × $332.55 BVPS-Class B) is the zone in which Berkshire has historically been an active repurchaser; ~1.3× book ≈ **$430**. Current 1.47× book ($488.70) = fair.
