Krones is the world's leading supplier of complete beverage bottling lines, and on the qualitative gates it is a better company than most of this register: a genuine switching-cost moat, no customer above a tenth of revenue, a family controlling block, zero buybacks, unremarkable pay, net cash. It fails the thing the model tests hardest — the level of returns on capital employed. The worst of the four normalized ROCE averages is 9.3%, below the 10% ADEQUATE floor and far from the 15% STRONG line. Two things stand between here and WATCH: the 2019–20 trough (restructuring plus COVID — operating income €44m, then −€41m) rolling out of the window, and price. Both are arithmetic, and the first arrives on schedule.

## 2. Understanding

Krones AG designs, manufactures, installs and services complete high-speed lines — process technology, filling, labelling, packaging, palletising, intralogistics and PET recycling equipment — for the world's breweries, soft-drink and water bottlers, dairies and liquid-food producers, who pay both for the capital equipment and for a large recurring spares and lifecycle-service stream; a 75-year-old, family-controlled (Familie Kronseder Konsortium GbR), vertically integrated German public company in Neutraubling, Bavaria, manufacturing largely in Germany and selling ~90% outside it. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Returns bind. On the conservative capital-employed basis the four normalized FY2019–FY2025 averages run 9.3% / 9.8% / 11.3% / 11.7% ROCE — worst 9.3%, below P3 — and 7.6% / 8.1% / 9.3% / 9.7% FCFROCE, worst 7.6%. The *trend* is the opposite of the usual failure: ROCE_wc rises from −2.1% (2020) to 14.6% (2025) — a depressed average, not a decaying franchise. Per-share growth clears the 3% floor (OI/sh +10.8%, FCF/sh +9.8%, BV/sh +5.8%, TBV/sh +4.7% CAGR). Leverage is a non-issue: L/E 137% against the 200% flag, €361m net cash. **WEAK** — on level of returns.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASSES both legs: no IFRS 8.34 major-customer disclosure in any year (the standard compels one at 10%) and an explicit diversification statement in the risk note; the supplier base is broad and Krones unusually vertically integrated. Forces: customer bargaining power **STRONG** — buyers are a handful of tender-disciplined beverage multinationals that dual-source against Sidel and KHS; supplier power **WEAK**; substitutes **WEAK** (no filling 80,000 bottles an hour without a line); new entrants **WEAK**, though Chinese suppliers are real at the low and mid end. Tally 1 strong, contained. Moat: **switching costs** — installed base, validated aseptic processes, spares and service — **stable**. Market growth MIXED. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Far below the ~$40m absolute trigger. Related-party dealing is immaterial — €1.5m of services received in FY2023, 0.05% of the relevant expense lines, at arm's length. Buybacks: **none ever** — no treasury shares, the 10% authorisation unused, share count constant at 31,593,072. Dividend €2.80, ~27% payout. Two items log INDETERMINATE and cannot pair: the Netstal price and the Supervisory Board chair's fee. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Customer power is the live force: a few global bottlers set price through competitive tenders.
- Chinese mid-market competitors compress the emerging-market growth Krones needs to lift returns.
- CEO succession: Christoph Klenk leaves 10 June 2026, Thomas Ricker succeeds — a decade of allocation discipline changes hands.

## C. Calculation notes

**FY2025 CE build (€ thousand).** Total assets 5,042,124 − NIBCL 2,183,931 = **CE_wc 2,858,193**; less cash 549,488 = **CE_xc 2,308,705**.
NIBCL = trade payables 868,256 (current) + 1,397 (non-current) + contract liabilities 884,129 + current tax liabilities 37,185 + other liabilities and accruals 392,964 = 2,183,931.

**The deciding judgment call — "Other provisions".** Krones reports current *other provisions* (FY2025: €223,617k) on a line separate from *other liabilities and accruals*. Because the accruals bucket is already captured elsewhere, "other provisions" is exactly the ambiguous "other" category the §2·1A burden-of-proof rule names, so it is **left in capital employed**. Consequence, stated plainly: on the alternate reading that treats current other provisions as NIBCL, the four-way ROCE averages become 10.6% / 10.0% / 12.8% / 12.4%, worst **10.03%** — which clears P3 and would make Past ADEQUATE and the final verdict WATCH. **The name sits on the P3 boundary and the verdict turns on this call.** The conservative reading is applied per the rule; the alternate is recorded here so the next REVIEW can see the knife-edge rather than rediscover it. Non-current provisions, pensions (€159,520k), deferred tax liabilities and non-current tax liabilities all stay in CE. Bank debt and lease liabilities stay in CE.

**FY2025 TBV build.** Equity (parent) 2,128,386 − intangible assets 600,564 = **1,527,822**; per share €48.36. Krones does not disclose a goodwill/other-intangible split on the face of the balance sheet; the whole intangible balance is deducted, which is the conservative direction.

**FY2025 EV build.** MCap 3,494,194 (€110.60 × 31,593.072k) + bank debt 1,266 + other financial and lease liabilities 187,093 (52,982 current + 134,111 non-current, treated whole as debt-equivalent) + NCI 309 = **EV 3,682,862 (no cash excess)**; less cash 549,488 = **EV 3,133,374 (all cash excess)**. Pension provisions are not added — the §2 formula does not name them; a pension-inclusive EV would be €3,842,382 (no cash excess), EV/OI 9.2×, which does not change any verdict.

**Goodwill** stays in CE: Krones is acquisitive (Netstal, Ampco, System Logistics, MHT, R+D Custom Automation, Javlyn, Perfinox).

**Leases.** IFRS 16 applies from FY2019; lease liabilities are treated as debt-equivalent in CE, EV and L/E. FY2018 is pre-adoption, but P18 is not triggered — lease-scale obligations are ~€140m against €3,321m of total assets, well under the 10% capitalisation threshold. No adjustment made; FY2018's adoption-year artifact is noted rather than corrected.

**P20 not active.** Capex/revenue is flat and modest across the window — 4.6%, 4.3%, 2.8%, 2.9%, 2.8%, 3.4%, 3.4%, 3.3% — so there is no documented super-cycle and no constructed maintenance basis. All-capex FCF is used throughout, in both the quality gates and the valuation gate.

**Captive finance / substitution rule:** neither applies.

**Bias check: none live.** The one to name is *cleverness* — the temptation to reach for the alternate NIBCL reading because a 9.26% result feels harsh on a company whose current-year ROCE is 14.6%. The rule decides, not the feeling; and the flip trigger in §8 is the disciplined way to hold the question open.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: PASS.** No IFRS 8.34 major-customer disclosure appears in the segment note in any year examined; because IFRS 8.34 compels disclosure once a single customer reaches 10% of revenue, its absence in an IFRS filing is affirmative evidence rather than mere silence. Corroborated by the FY2023 risk note: regional and customer-related diversification leaves no material concentration of risk.

**Breadth — suppliers: PASS.** No supplier above a tenth of goods and services purchased (€2,713,353k in FY2025) is disclosed; Krones manufactures the great majority of its machinery in-house. Effective-supplier caveat logged: the FY2023 risk report flags supply bottlenecks in electronic components, an ecosystem-level exposure shared across the industry, with multiple vendors available — it does not meet the "single effective supplier" test.
