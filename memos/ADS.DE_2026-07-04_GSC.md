# ADS.DE — adidas AG: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

|Understanding|Past|Future|Shareholder-friendliness|Cheapness|FINAL|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|PASS|**WEAK**|ADEQUATE|**FAIL**|EXPENSIVE|**AVOID (Past + SF)**|

A durable global brand in operational recovery, but the ten-year record is mid-pack and the balance sheet carries the scars of the worst capital-allocation sequence in the register: €3.5bn of buybacks in 2021–22 at roughly 2–4× this framework's conservative worth, followed within months by a 79% dividend cut, a collapse in equity, and L/E stuck above the 200% flag ever since. Past fails on the leverage test (P6 conditions absent), and the buybacks are an independent SF strike. Even a clean record would leave the shares at ~18–19× EV/OI. A verdict flip needs three things together: L/E back under 200%, a demonstrated disciplined-buyback record under the new €1bn programme, and several more years of restored returns.

## 2. Understanding

adidas designs, develops and markets branded athletic and sports-lifestyle footwear (majority of sales), apparel and accessories under the adidas brand, produced entirely by independent contract manufacturers (92% of volume in Asia; largest factory ~6%), sold to wholesale retailers and franchisees and directly to consumers through ~2,000 own stores and >€4bn of e-commerce, worldwide; a German AG (DAX, dual-board, HQ Herzogenaurach), founded 1949 with roots to 1924, the global #2 sportswear brand, recovering from the 2022–23 Yeezy/China crisis under CEO Gulden (since 2023). **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four 10y normalized averages: ROCE 11.1%, FCFROCE 9.6% — above the P3 floor, well short of P2's 15%. The shape is a boom (ROCE_wc ~17–20% in 2017–19), a double trough (2020 COVID; 2022–23 Yeezy/inventory: ROCE 4.0%/1.8%, FCF −€1.3bn in 2022), and a recovery to 14.1% in 2025. ΔOI/sh geo-mean +4.9% and ΔTBV/sh +4.5% clear the floor; ΔFCF/sh is n.m. on endpoints (FY2025 FCF €54m after a €1.7bn working-capital build). Decisive: **L/E 244.8%** breaches P5, and P6 does not hold — other metrics are not great, and equity shrank via buybacks at prices this framework cannot call rational (see §5), not via rational distributions. **WEAK** — ADEQUATE-level returns dragged down by the leverage test.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: PASS on both sides — the FY2025 report states no customer above 5% of sales, and the largest factory is ~6% of sourcing volume with fungible contract-manufacturing capacity behind it. Forces: customer power WEAK, supplier power WEAK, substitutes WEAK, threat of new entrants **STRONG** — the same outsourced factories and DTC channels that serve adidas are open to anyone, and On/Hoka proved barriers to success are surmountable within a decade. Tally 1 strong, contained. Moat: **Brand**, single source — a 75-year global mark whose consumer pull makes shelf presence near-mandatory; eroded 2015–22, restored 2023–25 (terrace franchises, football, World Cup 2026); judged stable. Market growth: YES, above GDP — which is precisely what keeps attracting entrants. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Comp: Executive Board total €19.4m FY2025; Gulden individually far under $30m; the 5%-of-lower(FCF, NI) leg fires only against WC-depressed FY2025 FCF (5% = €2.7m) — judgment call, no strike, MODEL flag logged (Appendix C/E). Supervisory Board €3.0m across 16+ members — within German norms. RPT: immaterial (€0.2m ambassador deal). **Strike — buybacks:** €3,501m repurchased 2021–22 at an implied ≈ €223/share (derived) against conservative worth of ≈ €62–99, at >20× EV/OI at purchase, followed by the FY2022 dividend cut €3.30 → €0.70. Overpriced acquisitions of its own stock at the cycle top. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Leverage math is the past-gate breaker: L/E must fall below 200% (equity rebuild plus lease/debt reduction) before the record can re-rate.
- US tariffs on >90% Asian sourcing compress gross margin and drove the March 2026 guidance disappointment (shares −8%).
- Entrant momentum (On, Hoka, New Balance) keeps taking premium-price share; the brand moat contains but does not stop it.
- FY2025's €1.7bn working-capital build either normalises in FY2026 or signals channel stuffing ahead of tariff pricing.
- The new €1bn 2026 buyback at ~15–19× EV/OI repeats the 2021 pattern at a smaller scale.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

AVOID → WATCH requires all of: L/E < 200%; FCF normalisation confirming FY2025 as a WC artifact; and an SF cure — either the 2026 buyback executed only into genuine price weakness or a multi-year record of restrained, dividend-led distributions. Next review: FY2026 results (~early March 2027), earlier if the price approaches ≈ €52 (within ~10% of the band).

## C. Calculation notes

**CE build FY2025:** Total assets 20,262 − NIBCL 5,650 (AP 2,910 + income taxes 357 + current accrued liabilities 2,383) = CE_wc **14,612**; − cash 1,617 (no STI) = CE_xc **12,995**. Burden-of-proof calls: other current provisions (1,208), other current liabilities (652, contains any deferred income undisclosed separately) and other current financial liabilities left **in** CE — ambiguous, conservative.

**TBV build FY2025:** BV 5,776 − goodwill 1,203 − other intangibles 443 = **4,130** (€23.13/sh). Goodwill retained in CE (acquisitive history: Reebok legacy).

**EV build (price €185.30):** MCap 33,107 + borrowings 2,641 (645 ST + 1,996 LT) + lease liabilities 2,913 (603 + 2,310) + NCI 349 + preferred 0 = 39,010; − cash&STI 1,617 → **EV_A 37,393** (all cash excess) / **EV_B 39,010** (none).

**Leases.** IFRS 16 from FY2019 (on balance sheet; liabilities debt-equivalent in CE/EV/L/E; adoption-year jump visible in 2019 CE and L/E — flagged). FY2015–18: commitments 2,199–2,984 = 16–19% of total assets > P18 → capitalised at the disclosed weighted-average interest rate on gross borrowings, 2.4% (AR2016); beyond-5y lump spread along the visible run-rate downtrend. Derived FCF excludes lease principal repayments throughout (pre-2019 by the §2 formula; post-2019 because IFRS puts principal, ~€600–660m/yr, in financing) — a flattering convention for a lease-heavy retailer, noted; the EV side compensates by carrying €2.9bn lease debt.

**IFRS interest reclassification.** adidas moved interest paid to financing from FY2019; deducted from CFO before deriving FCF (amounts in B1 note ³). FY2015–18 interest paid was already in operating.

**Maintenance capex:** no disclosure → full capex subtracted (overly-dark bias noted).

**P6 leverage call (denied).** L/E 244.8% > P5. P6 requires all other metrics great (they are not: worst-of-four ROCE 11.1%) **and** cheap borrowing to repurchase clearly under-priced stock or equity shrunk via rational distributions — the 2021–22 buybacks at ≈ €223 vs conservative worth €62–99 fail both readings. Contrast logged: AAPL passed P6 on exceptional metrics + rational-distribution branch; adidas qualifies on neither limb. → Past WEAK per P4.

**P17 5%-leg judgment call + MODEL flag.** 5% × lower(FCF €54m, NI €1,377m) = €2.7m — mechanically breached by any plausible CEO figure, solely because the FCF denominator is WC-depressed (5y-avg FCF €1,038m → 5% = €52m, passed by an order of magnitude; vs NI: <1.5%). Ruled no strike on proportionality, consistent with the MSFT precedent; adds a second data point to the standing MODEL item on the comp ceiling (proposal: normalise the 5% denominator or test against NI when FCF is single-year-distorted). SF verdict unaffected — the buyback strike stands alone.

**Bias check.** Live candidates: availability (vivid 2022 Yeezy narrative), hotness/consensus (turnaround press, 24 analyst Buys, CEO-extension coverage), miscontrast (Nike comparison). None drove the verdict — both failing gates are arithmetic (L/E 244.8%; buyback prices vs worth).
