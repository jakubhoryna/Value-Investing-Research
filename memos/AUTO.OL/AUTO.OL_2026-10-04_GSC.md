# AUTO.OL — AutoStore Holdings Ltd.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future WEAK, shareholder-friendliness FAIL, cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID on three gates (Past, Future, SF).**

AutoStore owns a genuinely good product economics layer — 72% gross margins and a grid that customers keep extending — wrapped in a capital base that is almost entirely a 2019 buyout price. On the capital shareholders actually carry, the six-year record returns about 4% pre-tax. Four distribution partners take 74% of revenue, the live buyback is executing at roughly 40× operating income, and the price sits about seventy times above the FCF leg of the band. Nothing short of a multi-year step-change in owner earnings, partner de-concentration, and a priced buyback discipline flips this.

## 2. Understanding
AutoStore designs and sells cube-storage automation — aluminium grids of stacked bins served by robots and picking ports, plus control software and subscriptions — paid for by a handful of warehouse-automation integrators (Swisslog, Element Logic, Dematic, Fortna and others) who resell to retailers, grocers and 3PLs; it sits in the warehouse-automation equipment industry, is a Bermuda holding company headquartered and engineered in Norway with global end-users, and is a sponsor-controlled (SoftBank, THL) Oslo listing since October 2021 with roughly 2,000 systems installed. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **4.2%** and FCFROCE **1.8%** over FY2020–FY2025, on capital employed of ≈USD 1.9–2.1bn that is ≈80% goodwill and buyout intangibles. Two of six years show operating losses (FY2021 IPO-era option costs, FY2023 Ocado settlement); the best year (FY2024) reaches 11.6%. Even deducting goodwill, the alternate reading is 9.6%. OI/share compounds from a FY2020 trough; FCF/share falls at −20.3% a year; TBV is negative at every year-end. L/E 30.5% — no leverage issue. **WEAK** — returns below the ADEQUATE floor on every reading.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth **FAIL**: four distribution partners each exceed 10% of revenue, together **74% in 2025** (65% in 2024). No captive waiver — integrators are free to carry rival storage systems. Forces: customers STRONG, suppliers WEAK, substitutes STRONG (shuttle AS/RS, AMR goods-to-person, other grid systems, conventional racking), new entrants STRONG (well-funded grid and robot rivals; the 2023 Ocado settlement ended a patent fight rather than a competitor) — **three strong**. Moat: switching costs on the installed grid base, stable, but they protect upgrades, not new-system wins. Market growth MIXED: revenue fell FY2023→FY2025, then rose 62.7% in H1 2026; the Amazon framework of 13 Aug 2026 carries no purchase commitment. **WEAK.**
