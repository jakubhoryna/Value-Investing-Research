# MGNI — Magnite, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; shareholder-friendliness FAIL (flag pair — P17 normalized-read breach + capital-allocation CAUTION); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (four gates)**.

Magnite turns other people's advertising money into a take rate, and does it well enough to throw off $165–202m of free cash a year. What it has never done is earn a return on the capital assembled to get there. Cumulative operating income across FY2021–25 is **−$200m**, so every normalized ROCE variant is negative (−2.0% to −3.6%) even as FCFROCE runs 11–15% — the gap is acquired-intangible amortisation from SpotX and Telaria: real money spent, recovered only in an add-back column. The second problem is who pays: **two buyers account for ~44% of revenue**, and they own competing supply paths. FY2024–25 are genuinely better, but two good years against three bad ones does not clear a ten-year test, and 38–41× EV/OI already assumes the recovery is permanent.

## 2. Understanding

Magnite runs the largest independent sell-side programmatic advertising marketplace: streaming/CTV broadcasters, publishers and app developers list ad inventory on its platform and pay a take rate when agencies and demand-side platforms buy it automatically; a Nasdaq-listed New York corporation (Rubicon Project 2014, renamed 2020 on the Telaria merger, enlarged by SpotX in 2021), mid-cap, US-centred with global operations. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE (FY2021–25) is **−3.61%** — all four variants negative on both NIBCL treatments (bracket −1.97% to −3.61%), because Σ OI over the window is **−$200.2m**. The trend is genuinely improving — ROCE −11.8% (FY2023) → +3.7% → +6.3% — but the level, the leverage and the cumulative sign each fail independently. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

Sellers are concentrated too, and the filing says so for CTV. Forces: customer power **STRONG**, supplier power **STRONG**, substitutes **STRONG** (Google's exchange, Amazon, direct and PG deals, retail media), new entrants **STRONG** (Amazon and the streamers in-housing are already through). Tally **4 STRONG**. Moat: CTV-integration switching costs — real but **eroding** under take-rate compression. Market growth YES. **WEAK** on three independent routes.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth.** Customers: **FAIL**, verbatim from the FY2025 10-K — *"In 2025, there were two buyers of advertising inventory that indirectly contributed to approximately 44% of revenue through their buying activity from sellers on our platform."* Two payers at 44% cannot satisfy the no-single-payer-above-10% test on any allocation. Note the routing: Magnite's contracting counterparties are sellers, but the cash originates with buyers, and the framework asks who *pays* — the company's own disclosure treats these buyers as the concentration risk, and its risk factor says the loss of "a buyer or group of buyers representing a significant portion of the demand in our marketplace… could cause an immediate and significant decline in our revenue". **Waiver tested and denied:** (i) these buyers can and do backward-integrate (the largest operate or are building their own supply paths) and switching between SSPs is a configuration change, not a rebuild; (ii) the moat is eroding, not dominant; (iii) concentration is a market outcome, not a structural necessity — there are many DSPs. Sellers: also concentrated by the company's own language — *"a relatively small number of them provide a large share of the unique user audiences… particularly true in CTV, where sellers tend to be larger"* — so the supplier limb does not rescue the test either.

**Forces (binary; one-line reasons):**

| Force | Call | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | Two buyers behind ~44% of revenue, each with an alternative route to the same inventory |
| Supplier bargaining power | **STRONG** | Large CTV sellers multi-home across SSPs and renegotiate the take rate at every renewal |
| Threat of substitutes | **STRONG** | Google's exchange, Amazon, direct and programmatic-guaranteed deals, and retail-media networks all buy the same attention |

**Tally: 4 STRONG.** **Moat:** switching costs, in the form of CTV publisher integrations and certified demand — real, and the reason Magnite still holds premium supply, but classed **eroding**: take rates compress at renewal, sellers multi-home by design, and the largest sellers have in-housing options that did not exist three years ago. **Market growth: YES** — CTV advertising is taking share from linear television, and Magnite's revenue has compounded through the loss years. **Future WEAK** — breadth FAIL, ≥2 strong forces, and an eroding moat, any one of which is sufficient.
