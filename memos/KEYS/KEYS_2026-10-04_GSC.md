# KEYS — Keysight Technologies, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past ADEQUATE, Future STRONG, shareholder-friendliness FAIL, cheapness EXPENSIVE: **AVOID** on shareholder-friendliness (capital allocation ADVERSE).

Keysight is the scale leader in electronic design and test, and it is a good business. No customer reaches a tenth of revenue, gross margin rose from 55.7% to 62.1% over the decade, and an in-house chip fab plus a measurement-software ecosystem keep customers tied to its instruments. The returns are respectable rather than exceptional: worst-of-four ROCE is 11.1%, weighed down by the Ixia deal and its $709m goodwill write-off in FY2018. The verdict fails on how capital is being deployed now. In the nine months to July 2026, $520m of stock was repurchased at an average of $282, which is 33–48× earnings and cash flow on every read, under a fresh $1.5bn authorisation with no price criterion. Spirent was bought for $1.4bn against $10m of reported operating profit. At $384.64 the stock trades at 76–79× EV/OI, far from a band of $19.6–34.6. The verdict flips only if repurchases become tied to worth and acquisitions are priced on earnings.

## 2. Understanding
Keysight designs, makes and sells electronic test and measurement instruments (oscilloscopes, network and signal analysers, network-emulation and protocol test systems), plus design and simulation software and calibration and support services. It sells them to organisations that pay to design, validate and manufacture electronics: communications chip, device and network-equipment makers, cloud and data-centre operators, aerospace and defence agencies and contractors, and automotive, energy and semiconductor firms. Revenue is 69% Communications Solutions (commercial communications 46%, aerospace/defence/government 23%) and 31% Electronic Industrial Solutions. Geographically it is 41% Americas, 18% Europe and 41% Asia-Pacific. Keysight is a vertically integrated manufacturer headquartered in Santa Rosa, California, with its main plant in Penang, Malaysia, about 16,800 employees and $5.4bn of revenue. It is the former Hewlett-Packard/Agilent test business, independent since November 2014. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**ADEQUATE.** Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **11.1%** (end, with cash); the cash-stripped reads are 14.9–16.8%. Worst FCFROCE is 13.0%. The window carries the Ixia acquisition's drag: FY2017–18 ROCE ran 4.6% and −7.6% because of the $709m impairment. FY2019–23 then ran 13–20%, and FY2024–25 fell back to about 11% as revenue cycled down and Spirent's capital arrived at year-end. OI/share compounded 7.2% a year and FCF/share 16.1%, both above the floor. FY2025 L/E is 92%. The FY2024–25 decline is read as cyclical, not sustained, because LTM operating income to July 2026 is $1,333m against $876m in FY2025.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth **PASSES**: the 10-K states "no one customer makes up more than 10 percent of our revenue". Materials come from "various suppliers globally" alongside an in-house fab, and no supplier is disclosed near a tenth of spend. Forces: customer power WEAK (thousands of customers; test is a small share of their R&D budgets; software and measurement correlation make switching costly), supplier power WEAK, substitutes WEAK (simulation complements physical test, and Keysight sells both), entrants WEAK (scale R&D of about $1bn, metrology accuracy, standards participation). Market growth is MIXED: AI networking, 6G and defence spending against cyclical capex, with revenue flat FY2022–25. **STRONG.**
