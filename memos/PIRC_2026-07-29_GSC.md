# PIRC — Pirelli & C. S.p.A.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future STRONG; SF PASS (one governance flag); Cheapness FAIR — **AVOID on Past**.

This is a good business sitting on a balance sheet that cannot earn its keep. Pirelli holds the number-one position in ≥18" High Value tyres, a genuine brand moat, fragmented replacement demand and no strong competitive force against it — Future reads STRONG. But the 2015 take-private pushed €5.0bn of brand, technology and goodwill onto the books, and the eight-year worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 6.2%, far under the 10% floor. Deducting goodwill entirely still only lifts it to 7.4%. Nothing management does operationally closes that gap; only a write-down or a decade of retained growth would, and neither is a thesis. At €6.50 the shares are FAIR, not cheap, and cheapness is moot while the quality gate fails.

## 2. Understanding

Pirelli is a Milan-headquartered listed Italian S.p.A., founded 1872 and relisted in 2017, that designs and manufactures differentiated premium car, motorcycle and bicycle tyres — plus the Cyber Tyre sensor system and licensed brand royalties — in around eighteen owned plants and two Asian joint ventures, selling roughly three-quarters into the replacement channel through independent distributors and dealers and the balance to prestige car OEMs that homologate its fitments, with 2025 revenue of €6.78bn split Europe 38.7%, North America 26.3%, APAC 17.7% and South America 10.1%, and with about 34% of its equity held by Sinochem under an Italian golden-power decree. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE 6.2%, worst-of-four FCFROCE 4.6% over FY2018–FY2025 — the four reads cluster tightly (6.2–7.3% and 4.6–5.4%), so this is not a denominator artifact. The trend is the redeeming feature: ROCE_wc climbs from 6.4% in 2018 through the 1.8% COVID trough to 8.3% in 2025, and FCFROCE from near zero to 7.2%, as capex normalised and debt fell. It is not enough. OI per share compounds at 3.4% and book value per share at 4.9%; FCF per share growth is arithmetically large but off a distorted 2018 base. Leverage has repaired convincingly, from 207% in 2020 to 110%. **WEAK** — below P3 on returns, on both the default and goodwill-deducted bases.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both sides: no customer near a tenth of revenue across a fragmented replacement channel and a spread of premium OEMs, and inputs are exchange-traded commodities with many vendors — the largest single related-party supply, €185m of JV tyre purchases, is roughly 3% of cost. All four forces read WEAK: customers are numerous and cannot backward-integrate, suppliers sell commodities, tyres have no substitute, and High Value entry needs homologation cycles, motorsport credibility and brand that Chinese volume entrants have not replicated. Tally zero. The moat is **Brand**, single-sourced and stable — F1 sole supply since 2011 pulls OE homologation, which pulls premium replacement pricing. Market growth MIXED: flat units, growing High Value mix. **STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation clears both P17 reads with room: Tronchetti Provera's €13.83m plus a €4.88m accrued severance totals €18.7m against 5% of the lower leg at €26.5m (year) and €25.5m (three-year normalized), net income binding both times. Directors take €75k plus committee fees, far under any norm. No repurchases were authorised anywhere in the window — a clean finding. Related-party dealing is large but ordinary-course JV supply and Prometeon brand licensing, 3.1% of the other-costs line ex-associates. The flag is control: six of fifteen directors voted against the 2024 accounts over a disputed IFRS 10 determination that Sinochem no longer controls Pirelli. One flag, no pair. **PASS**, under heightened scrutiny.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- US connected-vehicle rules could shut Cyber Tyre out of a market worth 26.3% of revenue while Sinochem's stake stands; management confirmed negotiations with shareholders failed in May 2025.
- The Camfin–Sinochem shareholder agreement lapsed on 28 February 2026, leaving control genuinely contested rather than merely awkward.
- €5.0bn of intangibles carries ~€104m of annual PPA amortisation through operating income and an impairment risk that would hit book value hard.
- Raw-material and freight swings pass through with a lag; natural rubber and butadiene are tracked as primary earnings drivers.
- Chinese entrants moving up-market would attack the one segment where the moat actually prices.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Past flips to ADEQUATE only if worst-of-four ROCE clears 10% — plausible late this decade if operating income keeps compounding against a shrinking capital base, and mechanically immediate if a large intangible write-down reset capital employed, which would be a book-value event rather than an economic one. Future would break on a US exclusion that stranded the technology programme or on breadth failing. A resolution of the Sinochem overhang is the single largest swing factor in either direction. Next review: FY2026 annual report, expected around March 2027; earlier on a connected-vehicle ruling or a change in the Sinochem holding.

## C. Calculation notes

**Window selection (judgment call).** FY2018–FY2025, eight fiscal years, nine balance-sheet years. Rejected alternatives: FY2016–FY2025 (FY2016's balance sheet consolidates the Industrial business while its income statement is continuing-operations only under IFRS 5 — the ratio is incoherent) and FY2017–FY2025 (FY2017's own statements are clean, but its beginning-of-period capital employed rests on the contaminated 12/31/2016 base, which would break the symmetry of the four-way test). Mixing windows across the four reads was not considered — §9 forbids one-off tweaking. Labelled **history-limited** per §2·1A: the pure-consumer perimeter is a permanent property of the name post-demerger, so no rebuild flag is warranted.

**NIBCL.** Defined reproducibly across all nine balance sheets as current trade payables + current other payables + current income-tax payables. Note 26 of the 2025 accounts confirms current other payables comprise accrued expenses and deferred income, indirect-tax payables, payables to employees, social-security payables and contract liabilities — all squarely within §2·1A's default definition. Burden-of-proof items left inside capital employed: current provisions for liabilities and charges, current provisions for employee benefits, current derivative liabilities, and all current borrowings. Non-current other payables also stay in.

**FY2025 CE build (EUR thousands).** Total assets 13,353,183 − NIBCL 2,590,324 (trade payables 2,082,442 + other payables 375,586 + tax payables 132,296) = **CE_wc 10,762,859**; less cash and equivalents 1,525,886 and other current financial assets at FVTPL 79,904 = **CE_xc 9,157,069**.

**FY2025 TBV build.** Equity attributable to owners 6,277,848 − intangible assets 5,047,766 = **1,230,082**. The intangibles are Pirelli Brand 2,270,000 (indefinite life), goodwill 1,880,250, technology 664,573, customer relationships 107,805 and sundry — all purchase-price-allocation balances pushed down from the 2015 ChemChina take-private, not the product of acquisitions Pirelli made. Roughly €104m of annual amortisation on technology and customer relationships runs through as-reported operating income; **no add-back was made**, per §2·1A.

**FY2025 EV builds.** Market capitalisation 7,046,308 (1,084,881,933 shares × €6.495) + borrowings 2,957,794 (non-current 2,747,192 + current 210,602) + non-controlling interests 178,889 + preferred nil = **EV no-cash 10,182,991**; less cash and STI 1,605,790 = **EV all-cash 8,577,201**.

**Goodwill treatment.** Goodwill stays in capital employed per the §2·1A default. The optional deduction for a non-acquisitive company is arguably available here — Pirelli's in-window acquisitions are trivial (€3.8m in 2025, €20.3m in 2024) and the goodwill arose from Pirelli being bought, not buying. **Sensitivity, logged not adopted** (goodwill held flat at the FY2025 sourced €1,880,250 across the window — an assumption, since FY2018–FY2022 goodwill was not retrieved): worst-of-four ROCE **7.4%**, worst-of-four FCFROCE **5.5%**. Still short of P3's 10%. The Past verdict is therefore robust to the goodwill call, which is why the default was left undisturbed.

**Leases.** IFRS 16 applies from FY2019; lease liabilities sit inside "borrowings from banks and other financial institutions" and are therefore already treated as debt in capital employed, enterprise value and leverage. FY2018 is pre-adoption, but undiscounted operating-lease commitments were nowhere near P18's 10%-of-total-assets trigger, so no capitalisation was performed. Lease principal and interest payments remain in financing, consistent with treating lease liabilities as debt; the amounts (€129.4m in 2025) are disclosed as a combined line, and the interest component was not separately reclassified — logged as immaterial to the verdict.

**IFRS interest reclassification.** Pirelli reverses financial income and expenses out of operating cash flow and shows the net cash amount in financing. That line was moved back into CFO for every year before deriving FCF, per §2·1A. The adjustment is large and matters: it cuts FY2023 FCF by €342m.

**Share count.** 1,000,000,000 shares from the 2017 relisting until December 2025, when €496.5m of the €500m 2025 equity-linked bond converted at €5.8493 into 84,881,933 new shares, taking the count to **1,084,881,933**. The FY2025 weighted average was 1,003,256k and is used for per-share history; the current count is used for market capitalisation, enterprise value and the trigger band. P20 was not activated — no capex super-cycle is in evidence; capex has run €193m–€463m and is trending down against rising revenue.

**Bias check.** None live. The candidates considered: *hotness* around the Cyber Tyre and F1 associations, which are genuinely attractive and were kept out of the returns arithmetic; *miscontrast* against the four Italian luxury names run this week, three of which failed on shareholder-friendliness rather than on returns — Pirelli fails somewhere else entirely, and the comparison was not allowed to soften the read; and *cleverness* around the goodwill-deduction argument, which was tested and disclosed rather than used to rescue the verdict.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers.** No customer disclosure sentence appears in the annual report, and the structure supports its absence: roughly three-quarters of revenue is replacement demand sold through independent distributors, dealers and Pirelli's own retail and franchise network across more than 160 countries, with the balance spread across premium and prestige car and motorcycle OEMs. No single counterparty plausibly approaches a tenth of €6.78bn. **PASS.** Note that Italian issuers face no US-style mandatory 10%-customer disclosure, so absence is weaker evidence here than it would be for an SEC filer; the structural argument is doing the work.

**Breadth — suppliers.** Natural rubber (about a quarter of tyre weight), synthetic rubber, carbon black, steel cord and chemicals are commodity inputs bought from many vendors; the annual report tracks TSR20 and butadiene as market prices, not negotiated ones. The largest identifiable single-counterparty supply is €185m of tyre purchases from the Jining Shenzhou joint venture, roughly 3% of combined cost of sales and operating expenses. **PASS.** No waiver required on either side.

**Forces (binary).**

| Force | Call | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | WEAK | Fragmented replacement channel dominates revenue; backward integration by carmakers is unheard-of; homologation locks fitments for a model cycle. OE is the contested quarter and is run thin deliberately, as a funnel. |
| Supplier bargaining power | WEAK | Exchange-traded commodity inputs, many vendors, low switching costs. Price volatility is a margin risk, not supplier power. |
| Threat of substitutes | WEAK | No direct substitute for a tyre; doing without is not available to a car owner; wholly-different-function substitution (fewer vehicle-kilometres) is slow and marginal. |
| Threat of new entrants | WEAK | Barriers to success exceed barriers to entry: multi-year OE homologation, motorsport credibility, brand and distribution. Chinese entrants have taken volume at the low end without breaching High Value. |

**Tally: 0 strong** — favourable.

**Moat.** Single source: **Brand**. Mechanism: sole Formula 1 tyre supply since 2011 plus 150 years of prestige positioning earns homologation on premium and prestige vehicles; homologated fitments then pull branded replacement demand at premium prices, which is where the margin lives. Direction: **stable**. In 2025 Pirelli grew replacement volumes 7% against a 6% market and held number one in ≥18". The threat to the moat is regulatory and ownership-driven rather than competitive.

**Market growth: MIXED.** 2025 global tyre demand was roughly flat — replacement +0.3%, original equipment +2.0% — while the High Value segment where Pirelli concentrates continues to take mix.

**Verdict: STRONG** — breadth passes both sides, zero strong forces, a durable and stable single-source moat.

**Related-party transactions.** Disclosed in note 43 with percentage incidence, which is itself a mark of disclosure quality. **P19 — buybacks.** **No repurchases in the window.** The annual reports for 2019, 2021, 2023 and 2025 each record that the shareholders' meeting did not authorise any purchase of own shares under Articles 2357 et seq. of the Italian Civil Code. **P19 — acquisitions.** No audit triggered. In-window acquisition spend is de minimis (€3.8m in 2025, €20.3m in 2024). The BV ≫ TBV condition is present but arises from the 2015 push-down of the ChemChina take-private price — Pirelli was the target, not the acquirer — so there is no target to analyse pre-acquisition and no multiple to test.

**Governance flag — control.** On 28 April 2025 the board approved the FY2024 accounts 9–15, with Chairman Jiao Jian and directors Chen Aihua, Zhang Haitao, Chen Qian and Fan Xiaohua against and Grace Tang abstaining, the dissent resting solely on management's determination under IFRS 10 that Sinochem, through Marco Polo International Italy, no longer controls Pirelli. Marco Polo publicly disputed the conclusion. The Board of Statutory Auditors raised the question, CONSOB referred it back to the board, and outside audit and law firms were engaged. The 14 May 2025 quarterly vote split 9–15 again. Management has stated the determination was a step toward aligning governance with US connected-vehicle regulation and that negotiations with the main shareholders failed. The Camfin–Marco Polo shareholder agreement lapsed on 28 February 2026 after Party B declined renewal. This is **one documented governance negative — one flag**, not a strike: it is a dispute about the characterisation of control, not evidence of value extraction, and no capital-allocation harm has been demonstrated.

**Strike tally.** Hard strikes: none. Flags: one (control). Independent second negative: none — compensation passes both reads, related parties are proportionate, and the buyback record is empty rather than adverse. **SF PASS**, under heightened scrutiny, with the control flag standing and named for re-test.

**Dividends and allocation.** €259.1m paid during 2025 against FY2024 profit attributable to owners of €468.0m, a payout of roughly 55%, up from €222.9m in 2023 and €185.4m in 2022. Retention has gone into deleveraging — gross borrowings fell from €5,854m in 2020 to €2,958m in 2025 — and into capex that has not run away. Rational for a mature business earning single-digit returns on employed capital.
