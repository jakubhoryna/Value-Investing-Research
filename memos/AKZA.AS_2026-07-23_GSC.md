# AKZA.AS — Akzo Nobel N.V.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | WEAK | ADEQUATE | FAIL (P19 hard strikes) | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** (Past WEAK + SF FAIL) |

AkzoNobel owns real consumer and industrial coatings brands, but the ten-year record shows a business that earns single-digit returns on its capital and converts them into cash erratically — worst-of-four normalized ROCE 7.6%, FCFROCE 3.0%. Management then spent ~€5.0bn repurchasing shares between 2017 and 2022 at 16–59× the framework's price tests: three tranches strike P19 outright on both dual-reads, and no tranche in the window was clean, which is itself a hard-strike pattern. The stock today trades at double its trigger band, and the price additionally embeds a pending ~€2.5bn special dividend tied to the Axalta all-share merger (EGM Aug 5, 2026; close expected late 2026 / early 2027), after which AKZA becomes a different company — "MergeCo" — and this memo's standalone basis expires. Nothing here flips before that: the AVOID rests on a decade of arithmetic, not a cycle.

## 2. Understanding

AkzoNobel is a 230-year-old Amsterdam-headquartered, Euronext-listed pure-play paints and coatings producer — differentiated branded products (Dulux, Sikkens, International, Interpon) sold to consumers and trade painters (Decorative Paints, ~43% of revenue, Europe/Asia/Latin America tilt) and to industrial, marine, aerospace and powder-coating customers (Performance Coatings, ~57%), the #3 global player behind Sherwin-Williams and PPG, currently in transformation: Specialty Chemicals divested 2018, and an all-share merger of equals with Axalta pending, with Nippon Paint separately bidding ~€7.5bn EV for the Decorative Paints business. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four 10y normalized ROCE (FY2016–25, continuing-ops OI over as-reported CE) is **7.6%** (beginning-of-period, with-cash); even the friendliest variant reaches only 9.4%, and the clean post-divestment subseries (2019–25) bottoms at 8.2%. Worst-of-four FCFROCE is **3.0%** — pension top-ups (2017–19) and working-capital swings produced FCF of €28m, €2m, −€181m and −€29m in four of ten years. ΔOI/sh ran +7.1%/yr geometric off the restated 2016 base, but ΔFCF/sh is n.m. (sign changes) and ΔTBV/sh −5.7%/yr; BV shrinkage reflects ~€9bn distributed 2017–22. L/E 196% sits just under the P5 flag. **WEAK** — the ROCE level fails P3 on every variant and the cash record is worse.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: PASS — no IFRS 8 major-customer disclosure (none >10% of revenue); raw materials (TiO2, resins, solvents) are multi-vendor. Forces: customer power WEAK (millions of fragmented buyers), supplier power WEAK (commodity inputs, several vendors), substitutes WEAK (surfaces must be coated; doing-without corrodes), new entrants WEAK (brand, trade-channel distribution, OEM specification). Tally 0 strong — favourable. Moat: brand plus channel ingrainedness (Dulux #1 positions; marine/aero spec lock-ins), stable — but a moat that produces 8% ROCE for a decade is protective, not dominant. Market growth: MIXED (GDP-like, European tilt). **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 clean: CEO Poux-Guillaume €6.29m total (2025) = 1.0% of lower(FCF €606m, NI €635m), under the ~$40m indexed trigger; Supervisory Board totals €1.0m across all members — no seat near 2× norm. RPTs immaterial (Note 24). Dividends steady (€1.98). **P19 fails the gate:** FY2017–22 buybacks of ~€5.04bn priced at 16–59× — 2017 (23.5×/22.2× EV/OI, both reads >20, and executed to sterilise stock-dividend issuance), 2019 (€2.52bn at 21.5×/23.9× EV/OI, both reads >20), 2021 (€1.14bn at 58.9×/51.0× MCap/FCF, both reads >20) are hard strikes; 2020 and 2022 flag; zero clean tranches = dominant-pattern hard strike besides. v3.5.0 redemption fails all three limbs. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- The Axalta merger closes late 2026 / early 2027: AKZA becomes MergeCo (55/45), NYSE-listed, and this standalone record and band expire on completion.
- A Nippon Paint acquisition of Decorative Paints (~€7.5bn EV proposed) would strip the consumer-brand moat and leave an industrial-coatings rump.
- Q2-2026 balance sheet already carries €6.6bn debt (special-dividend pre-funding) against €3.5bn cash — leverage rises structurally at close.
- Raw-material (TiO2, resin) cost spikes historically compressed OI faster than pricing recovered it (2018, 2022).
- Pension legacy: UK/NL schemes consumed >€1.5bn of operating cash 2017–19; further top-ups are a standing FCF hazard.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Past WEAK is structural — ten years of sub-10% worst-of-four ROCE does not flip on one good year; SF would additionally require the v3.5.0 redemption limbs (published worth-referenced repurchase policy + a bargain-line or refusal year + allocator turnover) — only turnover is partially met (CEO Nov 2022, chair 2022). Direction of travel is moot regardless: **next review at merger completion (late 2026 / early 2027)**, when MergeCo requires a fresh ANALYSE on the combined entity (new capital structure, Axalta's record, possible Deco divestment). If the merger breaks, review at FY2026 results (~Feb 2027) instead.

- **Verdict: ADEQUATE** (breadth PASS, 0 strong forces, plausible stable moat; STRONG denied on moat durability-into-returns).
