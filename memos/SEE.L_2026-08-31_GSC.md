# SEE.L — Seeing Machines Limited: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; shareholder-friendliness FAIL; cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID**, on three failed gates.

Seeing Machines sells the leading independent driver- and occupant-monitoring stack into a market Europe has just made compulsory, and volumes compound: 1.09m vehicles produced in H1 FY2026, up 62%, 4.8m cars on road. What is absent is evidence that the economics work. In twenty-six years the company has never reported an operating profit. Returns on capital are negative on all four normalized reads, tangible book is negative, and the US$47.5m convertible note falls due on 4 October 2026 against US$3.4m of cash. No price makes this a framework BUY: with no positive earnings base, the cheapness gate returns n.m. rather than a number. The verdict flips only on sustained audited operating profit — not adjusted EBITDA — a refinancing that does not hand the equity to the lender, and a customer base wider than two names.

## 2. Understanding

Seeing Machines is a 26-year-old, Australian-incorporated, AIM-listed, US-dollar-reporting technology group of ~365 staff that licenses embedded computer-vision driver- and occupant-monitoring software to automotive Tier 1 suppliers and OEMs for a per-vehicle royalty plus development fees (OEM segment, 60% of FY2025 revenue), and separately manufactures, installs and monitors the Guardian retrofit camera system sold to commercial fleet, mining and aviation operators on a hardware-plus-subscription basis (Aftermarket, 40%), with customers concentrated in Europe, North America and Australia and engineering in Canberra and Berlin. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized average ROCE is **−30.0%** (beginning-of-period, ex-cash; best read −18.3%); worst FCFROCE **−38.3%**. Every year and every variant is negative — this is not a depressed cycle but the absence of a returns engine. Per-share growth in operating income and free cash flow is undefined on negative bases; book value per share compounded at −25.9% p.a. across the window, and tangible book turned negative in FY2024 as capitalised development displaced equity. Leverage breached P5 at FY2025 (L/E 203.5%) and reached **416.8%** at 31 December 2025 as the convertible note went current. Verdict **WEAK** — no metric clears any threshold.

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth **FAILS**: two customers were 23.4% and 21.1% of FY2025 revenue. The captive-customer waiver is denied — Tier 1s and OEMs can and do switch vendors between programmes. Supplier concentration is not disclosed. Forces: customer power **STRONG** (44% in two hands; Magna exclusivity lapsed June 2025 and OEM licence revenue fell 99% in H1 FY2026); substitutes **STRONG** (Smart Eye, Cipia, Tier 1 and OEM in-house stacks; regulation specifies the function, not the supplier); new entrants **STRONG** (the mandate is itself an entry magnet for better-capitalised vision players); suppliers WEAK. Tally 3 — the stop signal. Moat: programme-level switching costs plus a large driving-behaviour dataset, real but narrow, and **eroding**. Market growth YES. Verdict **WEAK**.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 **hard strike** under the loss-leg rule: both owner-earnings legs were negative on the fiscal-year read (NI −US$25.3m, FCF −US$29.7m) and on the trailing-3-year read, yet FY2025 carried a US$1,162k short-term-incentive accrual and an 86.86m performance-rights grant — more than token pay against no owner earnings. Capital allocation **CAUTION**: no repurchases or dividends in the window (recorded), but development spend at 27–41% of revenue funded by 18.3% dilution at 4.09p and a note costing 11.8–13.1%, with nothing yet earned on it. Mitsubishi Electric at 19.9% is also a commercial counterparty — RPT amounts undisclosed, logged INDETERMINATE. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- The US$47.5m convertible note matures 4 October 2026 and was still unrefinanced at the H1 report date, against US$3.4m of cash and a US$7.8m receivables facility — dilution, distressed terms, or worse are live outcomes within weeks.
- US$67.5m of capitalised development is 3.2× total equity; any impairment would take book value close to nil.
- Royalty economics are set by Tier 1 intermediaries, so mandated volume growth need not convert into margin.
- Management reports on adjusted revenue and adjusted EBITDA that add back the very development spend the balance sheet capitalises; statutory operating loss widened in H1 FY2026 to US$18.8m.
- Loss of either of the two >20% customers would remove roughly a fifth of revenue at once.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Upward: two consecutive audited years of positive operating income with ROCE clearing 10% on the worst read, a refinancing without material dilution, and customer concentration below the disclosure threshold. Downward: little remains short of a going-concern qualification. FY2026 audited results land late September / early October 2026 with the first statutory read on the GSR ramp; the 4 October note maturity is the harder event. Either is a REVIEW trigger regardless of price.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customer: FAIL.** FY2025 Note 4(c): "Revenues of approximately $14,587,000 … and $13,182,000 are derived from two single external customers" — 23.4% and 21.1% of US$62,337k, both attributed to OEM. *Captive-customer waiver considered and denied:* the concentrated customers are Tier 1 suppliers and OEMs who can switch vendor at the next programme award and in several cases develop in-house; switching costs bind within a programme, not across the relationship; and the buyer set is wide, not structurally narrow.
