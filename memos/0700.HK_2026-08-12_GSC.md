## 2. Understanding
Tencent is a Shenzhen-based, Cayman-incorporated, HK-listed platform conglomerate — prominent, 28 years old, RMB 752bn revenue — that sells game virtual items and digital-content subscriptions to Chinese and international consumers, advertising to Chinese merchants and brands, and payment, wealth-distribution, and cloud services to consumers and enterprises, operating its Chinese licensed businesses partly through contractual VIE structures on top of the Weixin/WeChat social graph (1.42bn users). PASS — complex but statable; the VIE form and the investee portfolio (≈RMB 1,036bn listed plus unlisted at FY25) are noted as form and status features, not obfuscation.

## 3. Quality — past record
Worst-of-four normalized ROCE (consistent-presentation OI, FY2018–25) is 12.1% (end-of-period, with-cash); the cash-excluded reads run 16–18%. Worst-of-four FCFROCE (FY2021–25 only, record-limited) is 10.0%. Per-share compounding is emphatic: ΔOI/sh 18.3%, ΔBV/sh 20.4%, ΔTBV/sh 20.3% geometric, ΔFCF/sh 15.0% (4y). L/E 69%. The huge investment portfolio sits in CE and structurally depresses the with-cash reads. **ADEQUATE** — 12.1% clears P3 but not P2, and the record-limited window caps STRONG regardless.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth PASS: no customer near 10% of revenue (billions of consumers, millions of advertisers); no effective supplier above 10% of COGS+opex (owned studios, dispersed channel and infrastructure costs; the Apple iOS toll and Nvidia GPU constraint are noted, the latter capex-side). Forces: customer power WEAK; supplier power WEAK; threat of new entrants WEAK (network effects plus licence barriers); threat of substitutes **STRONG** — short video demonstrably took attention and ad share 2018–23 — but contained: Video Accounts re-accelerated ad revenue to +19%. Tally 1 strong, contained. Moat: network effects of the Weixin graph — dominant, stable-to-strengthening with AI integration. Market growth YES. **ADEQUATE**.
