# HEIA.AS — Heineken N.V.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future ADEQUATE, shareholder-friendliness PASS (capital allocation CAUTION, one flag), cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past)**.

Heineken owns one of the world's few truly global premium beer brands and operations in over 70 countries, but on the capital its shareholders have committed it earns too little. Goodwill and acquired brands make up most of the asset base, and operating profit has never covered it at a double-digit rate across the decade: the worst normalized ROCE is 8.4%, and the last three years ran at 7.6–7.9%. The price does not help: the stock trades at about 17–18× operating income and 14.6× free cash flow. To flip the verdict, returns on the whole capital base would need to climb back above 10% and stay there; neither the cost programme nor the FIFCO deal has shown that yet.

## 2. Understanding
Heineken N.V. brews, licenses and sells beer, cider and soft drinks — Heineken®, Amstel, Tiger and many local brands, a commodity category sold on brand — to wholesalers, retailers and on-trade outlets (who pay), as a vertically integrated brewer and distributor that also licenses brands and owns pubs and proximity retail, across Europe, the Americas, Africa & Middle East and Asia Pacific, headquartered in Amsterdam, a long-established, family-controlled (through Heineken Holding N.V.) global number two now restructuring under a new CEO from 1 October 2026. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
The worst-of-four ROCE is **8.4%** (end-of-period, with cash), with a range of 8.4–9.3% across all four constructions; worst FCFROCE is **5.8%**. Even beginning-of-period ROCE peaked at 12.5% (FY2021) and has run below 8% since FY2023 as acquired capital grew faster than profit. Taking associates out of capital employed does not rescue it (≈9.4%). OI per share compounded at only 1.3% a year, below the 3% floor; FCF per share grew 4.3% and BV per share 3.2%. L/E is 184%, inside the flag level. Tangible book is negative in every year. **WEAK**: below the ADEQUATE return floor, with a falling trend.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth passes. The annual report discloses no customer above a tenth of revenue, and malt, glass, cans and energy each have ready alternative vendors. On forces: customers WEAK at group level, though European retailer negotiations cut 2025 volume; suppliers WEAK; substitutes STRONG, with consolidated volume down 2.1% in FY2025 as moderation, spirits and no-alcohol options take share; entrants WEAK. That is one strong force, contained by premiumisation. The moat is brand: Heineken® volume grew 2.7% in FY2025 and 5.3% in H1 2026, so it is **stable**. Market growth is MIXED: emerging regions are growing and Europe and the Americas are flat. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
CEO pay was €6.55m in FY2025, against ceilings of €94m (fiscal-year read) and €86m (3-year read); the top Supervisory Board fee was €292k. Both pass. The family holds ≈23.7% economically and controls through Heineken Holding (50.005%); this is a documented caveat. The related-party items are the pro-rata buybacks from Heineken Holding and the 2023 FEMSA placement purchase, both at market price; no concern. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**. The 2019 CR Beer stake took an €874m impairment in 2024. The €2.8bn FIFCO deal was debt-funded and lifted net debt/EBITDA to 2.6×, above the company's own <2.5× target. The current buybacks run at about 15–17× and are funded from free cash flow. That is one flag. **PASS.**
