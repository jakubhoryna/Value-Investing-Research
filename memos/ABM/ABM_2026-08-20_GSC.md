# ABM — ABM Industries Incorporated: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; Shareholder-friendliness FAIL; Cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID**, failing three of four gates.

ABM is the largest listed US outsourced facility-services contractor: 100,000-plus mostly unionised frontline staff cleaning, maintaining and staffing some 20,000 clients' buildings, airports and plants. Scale, a 58-year dividend record and a real growth tail in electrical infrastructure make it look like a compounder. The arithmetic says otherwise. A decade of deals — GCA, Able, RavenVolt, Quality Uptime, now WGNSTAR — tripled goodwill and more than doubled capital employed while returns stayed in single digits: worst-of-four normalised ROCE 6.2% over FY2016–FY2025, against a 10% adequacy line cleared in exactly one year. Only a step-change in returns on the enlarged base — not a lower price — moves this verdict.

## 2. Understanding

ABM Industries is a Delaware-incorporated, NYSE-listed self-performing outsourced facility-services contractor that sells janitorial, engineering, parking, aviation ground-handling and higher-margin technical services (electrical infrastructure, microgrids, HVAC, mission-critical and semiconductor-fab support) to organisations that own or occupy buildings and infrastructure — commercial landlords and occupiers, airlines and airport authorities, school districts, manufacturers and distributors, data-centre operators — overwhelmingly in the United States with UK and Ireland operations, employing its labour directly rather than franchising, and standing since 1909 as one of the largest providers in a fragmented industry. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalised ROCE 6.2%, FCFROCE 5.2% (FY2016–FY2025); the four variants cluster in a 6.2–6.9% band, so no treatment choice rescues it. Only FY2023 (10.3%) touched the adequacy line. Returns are not in sustained decline, they are structurally low: GCA and Able lifted capital employed from $1.8bn to $4.2bn without lifting them. Per-share growth is real but flattered by a depressed FY2016 base (OI/sh +20.0% geometric, FCF/sh +7.3%, BV/sh +5.8%); TBV/sh is negative throughout, so n.m. Leverage 195% at FY2025, inside the flag — 223% on the April 2026 balance sheet. **WEAK** on level, not trend.

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth PASS (no client above 10% of revenue, FY2023–FY2025); supplier breadth PASS on the literal test, though the effective supplier is organised labour under sector-wide bargaining. All four forces call STRONG: customers re-bid short-cancellation contracts on price; labour is ~90% of cost with documented shortages; substitution is live in both forms, insourcing and simply cleaning less as office utilisation falls; local entry barriers are near nil. No moat among the six sources — the cost advantage that would be one is refuted by a 3.6% operating margin. Market growth MIXED: impaired commercial office (47% of revenue) against real growth in aviation, data centres and microgrids. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 hard strike: FY2025 CEO total $9.11m against $7.76m (fiscal-year) and $8.25m (trailing-3y normalised) — both breached, so no denominator distortion acquits. Absolute trigger (~$40m indexed) untouched; director pay tops out at $429k against a ~$1.02m chair stop. Capital allocation CAUTION: FY2025 and H1 FY2026 both returned more than free cash flow ($188m vs $155m; $129m vs $71m) on new borrowing that took leverage to 3.2× alongside WGNSTAR; repurchases at 14.9–21.2× EV/OI sit above the bargain lines but short of the overpriced class. **FAIL** on the strike alone.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth.**
- *Customers — PASS.* FY2025 10-K: no single client accounted for more than 10% of consolidated revenues in 2025, 2024 or 2023. The 2026 proxy puts the client count above 20,000, so the average client is ~$0.4m of revenue. Segment-level concentration is real but does not reach the consolidated test: the FY2015 10-K disclosed four clients at ~72% of Aviation-segment revenue, and the FY2024 release attributed a Manufacturing & Distribution decline to "rebalancing of certain work by a large client". Noted as force evidence, not a breadth failure.
- *Suppliers — PASS on the literal test.* No single vendor approaches 10% of cost of services plus operating expenses; roughly 90% of cost is direct labour, which is not a supplier in the contractual sense. Looking through to the effective supplier, however, organised labour under multi-employer collective bargaining is concentrated in ABM's core urban markets, and multiemployer pension exposure is a standing disclosed risk. This feeds the supplier-power call below rather than the breadth gate.

**Four forces (binary; P16):**

|Force|Call|One-line reason|
|-----|----|---------------|
|Customer bargaining power|**STRONG**|Short-cancellation, competitively re-bid contracts for a low-differentiation service; clients can and do insource; 3.6% operating margin is the price of that.|
|Supplier bargaining power|**STRONG**|~90% of cost is labour, sector-wide collective bargaining sets it, and wage inflation reaches ABM ahead of contractual escalators.|
|Threat of substitutes|**STRONG**|Both live forms: insourcing facilities staff, and doing-without as office utilisation falls — the company's own risk factors name hybrid work.|
|Threat of new entrants|**STRONG**|No permits, no scale minimum and no switching cost at the local level; thousands of regional operators already compete for the same buildings.|

**Tally: 4 strong — the book's stop signal; the memo is completed and Future is WEAK.**

**Moat.** None identified among the six sources. The candidate is *cost* — national scale in procurement, self-insurance and route density — but a 3.6% operating margin and a 6.2% normalised ROCE are direct evidence that no cost advantage is being captured. The technical-solutions franchise network (Linc Service, TEGG) is a genuine but small brand/ingrainedness asset inside a segment that is 11% of revenue. Direction: **stable at approximately zero**, with a slow positive drift as technical solutions grows.

**Market growth: MIXED.** Business & Industry, 47% of revenue, faces a structurally impaired commercial-office market; Aviation, Manufacturing & Distribution and Technical Solutions grew 8%, 4% and 10% organically in FY2025, with data-centre, battery-storage and semiconductor demand behind them. Directionally positive in aggregate, with the largest segment as the drag.
