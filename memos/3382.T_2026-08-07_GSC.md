# 3382.T — Seven & i Holdings Co., Ltd.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS · Past **WEAK** (worst-of-four ROCE 5.6% — never a single year ≥10% in the window) · Future ADEQUATE · SF **FAIL (flag pair: capital-allocation CAUTION + documented governance negative)** · Cheapness EXPENSIVE → **AVOID (Past and SF gates)**.

Seven & i owns the world's densest convenience-retail network and has spent two years in structural surgery — York superstores sold, Sogo-Seibu gone, Seven Bank deconsolidated, ¥600bn of a ¥2tn buyback executed, a 7-Eleven US listing planned — all catalysed by a takeover bid the board never let shareholders answer. The framework's read is unsentimental: a decade of 4–8% returns on capital (Speedway made it worse), free cash conversion near 4%, and a governance record in which the founding family's failed MBO and the Couche-Tard episode sit unresolved beside a criterion-free record buyback. Flip requires both a returns regime change (the slimmed group sustaining >10%) and a clean governance cycle.

## 2. Understanding
Seven & i operates convenience stores — franchised and company-run 7-Eleven networks in Japan (~21k stores) and North America (~13k, fuel-heavy since the 2021 Speedway purchase), plus international licensing and a shrinking tail of other retail — earning franchise royalties, merchandise margin and fuel margin from consumers; Tokyo-HQ'd, founding-family-influenced, mid-restructuring toward a c-store pure-play with a planned US sub-listing. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **5.62%** (end-of-period, with-cash; best variant 7.41%) — the window's *best single year* is 7.8%, so the 10% bar is never met even momentarily. Worst FCFROCE **3.97%**. Path: 7.2 → 7.8 → 7.7 → 7.7 → 5.6 → 4.7 → 5.1 → 5.3 → 3.9 → 4.9% — Speedway (FY22) structurally lowered returns and they have not recovered. ΔOI/sh geo +2.5%; ΔEPS +14.0% (divestment gains and buybacks, not operations); ΔBV/sh +5.9%. L/E **151.8%** (¥2.2tn debt against a TBV of **−¥959bn**). **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth PASS both sides (millions of consumers; thousands of suppliers; franchisee base fragmented). Forces: customer power WEAK · supplier power WEAK · **substitutes STRONG** — Japanese drug/discount formats and delivery eat conbini share of wallet, and US fuel demand (half of US gross profit's traffic driver) faces secular EV decline (doing-without form) · entrants WEAK (network density is the barrier). Tally **1 strong, contained**. Moat: **Ingrainedness/network density** — store density plus fresh-food supply chains competitors cannot replicate quickly; stable in Japan, **eroding in the US**. Market growth MIXED. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
Comp: passes at an order of magnitude (limb ¥13.1bn; Japanese-scale packages; exact figure to backfill). RPT/governance: **one flag** — the 2024–25 control contest: the founding family's ~¥9tn MBO attempt (special-committee reviewed, financing failed) ran while Couche-Tard's cash proposals were never put to shareholders; ACT withdrew (Jul 2025) citing lack of engagement — a documented governance negative independent of comp. Capital allocation: **CAUTION, second flag** — the live ¥2tn programme executed ¥600bn in FY26 at ≈19× MCap/FCF / ≈16× lower-EV EV/OI with no published worth criterion (though ~20% under the withdrawn ACT benchmark — said in its favour); Speedway (2021) at ≈13.7× trailing EBITDA (a hard lower bound of ≈17–18× EV/pre-deal-OI) is the era's defining overpriced deal, audited in App E. **Two independent flags → SF FAIL (§2·1C pair rule).**

- US c-store margins hostage to fuel cents-per-gallon and cigarette decline.
- Governance: the family's blocking stake and board history make a repeat control episode plausible; minority treatment is the open question the SF FAIL encodes.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review
