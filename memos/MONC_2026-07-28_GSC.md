Moncler is a good business run by people who pay full prices. Returns are high and self-funded — worst-of-four normalized ROCE 17.8% and FCFROCE 15.3%, on a balance sheet holding €1.46bn of net cash — and the brand held a 78% gross margin through a weak luxury cycle. It fails on shareholder-friendliness, not quality: Stone Island was bought at ≥16.6× pre-deal operating income, and the single repurchase tranche in the window went through above 20× implied MCap/FCF at any plausible price. Two independent flags from two tests is a FAIL; the gate is defined by the worst factor and never averaged. The shares are separately about twice the price the framework would pay. A flip needs demonstrated allocation discipline, not a lower price.

## 2. Understanding

Moncler S.p.A. is a Milan-headquartered, Ruffini-controlled Italian luxury-apparel group that owns and licenses the Moncler (1952) and Stone Island (acquired 2021) brands, designs high-priced technical outerwear plus adjacent ready-to-wear, footwear, bags and licensed eyewear, makes them in an owned Romanian plant and through contract *façon* manufacturers in Eastern Europe, and sells them mainly to affluent individual consumers — 52% of Moncler-brand revenue in Asia — through 390 directly operated stores and its own e-commerce (83% of group revenue), the balance to multi-brand retailers, department stores and e-tailers, who are the paying customers of the wholesale channel. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized averages (FY2021–25, strict NIBCL): ROCE 17.8%, FCFROCE 15.3% — well above the P2 lines, on capital employed over a quarter of which is cash. Growth is uneven: OI/share +11.7% and BV/share +11.0% geometric, but FCF/share −0.1%, under the 3% floor, because FY2021 paired a working-capital release with nine months of Stone Island. ROCE_wc has fallen two years running (20.1% → 18.5% → 16.8%) as capital outgrows profit; a third decline fires P4. Leverage is immaterial (L/E 55%, leases included). **ADEQUATE** — returns are STRONG-grade, but the FCF/share floor fails and a five-year window caps the verdict.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASSES both sides: no customer near a tenth of revenue (83% direct-to-consumer; wholesale spread across 70+ countries), no supplier near a tenth of spend (450+ suppliers, top 60 ≈ 70% of order value, stated multi-sourcing policy). Forces — customer power WEAK; supplier power WEAK; substitutes **STRONG** (a durable discretionary jacket, zero switching costs, against Canada Goose, Arc'teryx, Herno and every luxury house's outerwear); new entrants WEAK (brand legitimacy and prime-location scale take decades). One strong force, contained. Moat: **brand** — alpine heritage turned status good — stable, with erosion on watch (comparable-store sales −1% in 2025, EMEA −3%). Market growth MIXED. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation passes with wide headroom (top pay ≈ €7m against a 5%-of-lower(FCF, NI) line of €31.3m and an indexed absolute trigger near €40m). Everything else counts against. **Flag 1 (P19 buybacks):** the FY2022 tranche (€48.4m) implies MCap/FCF of 20.9–36.8× across the year's whole trading range — above the 16× line at any execution price, no rescue. **Flag 2 (acquisition audit):** Stone Island at €1,129m enterprise value on €68m of 2020 EBITDA is ≥16.6× pre-deal operating income by construction, past the 15× line. **Flag 3 (RPT):** the same deal was self-classified a major-importance related-party transaction, routing 10.7m newly issued shares to the controlling holder's vehicle. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Asia is 52% of Moncler-brand revenue; Chinese demand and tourist flows drive the P&L.
- A third year of negative comparable-store growth would move the moat call from stable to eroding.
- Capital employed compounds faster than operating income; a third ROCE decline moves Past to WEAK.
- Ruffini keeps creative direction while a first-ever Group CEO takes over from April 2026 — an untested split.

## C. Calculation notes

**NIBCL judgment call.** §2·1A defaults NIBCL to accounts payable + accrued expenses + income taxes payable + deferred income. The tier-2 balance sheet collapses income taxes payable and accruals into a single "other current liabilities" line (€262,474k in FY2025), and the statutory note that would split it was not retrieved. Burden of proof rests with the item, so the whole line stays in capital employed. The alternate reading is logged in B2; it raises every metric and changes no verdict.

**Goodwill** stays in capital employed: Moncler is acquisitive (Stone Island 2021, Moncler Japan minority tranches 2018–24, Stone Island Japan 2025), so the non-acquisitive deduction is unavailable.

**Leases.** Every window year is post-IFRS 16, so the reported balance sheet already capitalises; lease liabilities are treated as debt-equivalent in CE, EV and L/E. P18 is not engaged.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers.** Direct-to-consumer is 86.7% of Moncler-brand and 55.1% of Stone-Island revenue; group DTC ≈ 82.6% (€2,586.0m of €3,132.1m), sold to individual consumers across 390 directly operated stores and own e-commerce. Looking through to the effective supplier: fine white goose down is a specialised, DIST-certified input, but it is sourced from Europe, North America and Asia, and the *façon* stage is partly internalised in the owned Bacău plant (~1,100 employees). **YES.**

Both yes — breadth **PASSES**. No waiver used.

### 2. Related-party transactions — FLAG

The Stone Island acquisition was **voluntarily classified by Moncler itself as a related-party transaction of major importance** under CONSOB Regulation 17221/2010. The mechanism: the selling shareholders simultaneously contracted with Ruffini Partecipazioni Holding S.r.l. — wholly owned by the Chairman and Chief Executive — that all 10.7 million newly issued Moncler shares they received as half their consideration would be transferred into Ruffini Partecipazioni S.r.l., the vehicle already holding 22.5% of Moncler. Temasek sat on three sides at once: 30% of the target, a direct stake in Moncler, and 12.8% of the acquiring holder's vehicle. Moncler issued equity as acquisition currency whose destination was the controlling holder's own vehicle, and the Related Parties Committee approved it unanimously. That is a documented governance negative and counts as one flag.

Recurring related-party lines, FY2017 primary (the only year retrieved with the note): cost of sales €9,291k = **3.4%** of the line; general and administrative €7,441k = **6.8%**; stock-based compensation €8,300k = **35.3%**; revenue €884k. The blunt fairness test is uncomfortable on the G&A and compensation lines but not disqualifying on its own. **FY2025 note not retrieved — named re-test.**

### 4. Dividends, capital allocation and the acquisition audit — FLAG
