# DG — VINCI SA: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past **WEAK**; Future **WEAK**; SF **PASS** (one flag — capital-allocation CAUTION); Cheapness **FAIR** — **AVOID** on two independent gates.

VINCI is two businesses stapled together: concessions earning 66% of group operating income on 16% of revenue at a 48.6% margin, and contracting earning 34% of the income on 82% of the revenue at 4–8% margins. The concession half is excellent and finite — the French motorways anchoring it revert to the state between 2032 and 2036. The contracting half is competent and ordinary. Consolidated the arithmetic is unforgiving: ten-year worst-of-four ROCE of 8.4% sits below the 10% floor, liabilities are 321% of book equity. Nothing between here and a passing verdict is a price question.

## 2. Understanding

VINCI SA is a Nanterre-headquartered French société anonyme, single share class, that builds, finances, operates and maintains transport infrastructure — selling tolled road and airport capacity to motorists, passengers and airlines under long-dated public concessions (French motorways, airports in 14 countries) — and separately sells engineering, industrial services and construction works to public authorities (a third of revenue) and corporate owners in 120-plus countries, a 294,000-employee, €75bn-revenue world leader in both, whose French motorway concessions expire 2032–2036. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalised ROCE **8.4%** and FCFROCE **7.4%**; the flattering read is 10.8% / 9.4%. Direction is mildly positive — end-period ROCE 8.6% (2016) to 10.2% (2025) — around a 3.6% covid trough. Per-share growth is strong: geometric-mean ΔOI/sh **+9.4%**, ΔFCF/sh **+11.7%**, ΔBV/sh **+7.0%**. Leverage is the binding problem: **L/E 321%**, above 289% in every window year, with no P6 rescue — the returns are not great and the borrowing funds concessions, not repurchases of underpriced stock. **WEAK**, below the ADEQUATE returns floor *and* an unrescued leverage breach; either alone decides it.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS** both sides: the URD states no customer exceeds 10% of revenue, and a €66bn operating-expense base spread across local subcontractors and materials in 120 countries admits no 10% supplier. Forces by segment. **Concessions: 0 strong** — captive users, fragmented suppliers, weak substitutes, statutory entry barriers. **Energy Solutions + Construction: 2 strong** — customer power (competitive tender, a third public-sector; 4.1% and 7.6% margins are the evidence) and new entrants (local contracting has almost no barrier). Two strong forces in the unit carrying 82% of revenue is the stop signal. Moat: a single **Government** source in Concessions, none in contracting, **eroding by contract** — Escota 2032, Cofiroute 2034, ASF 2036 return roughly a third of group operating income to the French state on fixed dates. Market growth **MIXED**. **WEAK**.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

CEO Pierre Anjolras took **€4.64m** for eight months from 1 May 2025 against a 5%-of-lower(FCF, NI) cap of **€245m** (year) and **€241m** (3y normalised) — under 2% on both reads, and far under the ~US$40m indexed trigger. The richest board seat is near €150,000. Related-party dealings are declared immaterial at group and parent level. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: no repurchase read in any window year reaches the ≈25× calibration, the last three tranches plus H1-2026 execute at 9.8–10.6× EV/OI and 8.3–9.2× MCap/FCF, funding is internal and cancellations real — but the €5bn authorisation's €150 ceiling has been ratcheted up with the share price since 2019 and anchors to the market, not to worth. One flag, no pair. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Motorway concessions expire 2032–2036, retiring roughly a third of group operating income with no disclosed replacement.
- French fiscal policy taxes the concession rent directly — the 2024 transport-infrastructure levy, and an exceptional corporate-tax contribution extended again in the 2026 Finance Bill.
- Contracting margins of 4.1% and 7.6% on €63bn of revenue leave no cushion for Major Projects execution error.
- Goodwill rose €12.9bn across the window against a returns record that merely held flat.
- Airport traffic is severely cyclical: FY2020 operating income fell 57%.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To WATCH requires worst-of-four ROCE above 10% **and** L/E inside 200% — plausible late in the decade as ASF debt amortises into expiry, except that the same expiry removes the earnings. A contracted successor arrangement for the French motorways would reset the moat direction and is the most consequential catalyst; a separation of contracting would change the forces tally. Next review: FY2026 results (~early Feb 2027) and URD 2026 (~late Feb 2027) — returns base, leverage, the FY2026 tranche judgment and the acquisition INDETERMINATEs; earlier on a French decision on post-2032 concession arrangements.

## C. Calculation notes

**FY2025 CE build.** CE_wc = Total assets 133,065 − NIBCL 41,189 = **91,876**. NIBCL = trade payables 14,868 + other current liabilities 25,612 + current tax liabilities 709. CE_xc = 91,876 − (cash and cash equivalents 17,254 + cash management financial assets 1,244) = **73,378**.

**NIBCL burden-of-proof call (verdict-relevant, logged).** **Current provisions of €8,353m are left inside capital employed.** They are provisions for construction-contract losses, warranties, disputes and restructuring — none of them accounts payable, accrued expenses, income taxes payable or deferred revenue, which is the §2·1A default list. The burden of proof rests with the item and it is not discharged. **This is the single most contestable line in the memo.** Reclassifying current provisions to NIBCL lifts the four normalised averages to ROCE 11.2 / 14.4 / 11.4 / 14.1% (worst 11.2%), which would clear the P3 floor and make Past ADEQUATE on returns. **The verdict does not move**, because the 321% leverage breach is an independent WEAK trigger under P4. A future session disagreeing with this memo should locate the disagreement here first, and should note that it does not reach the verdict.

**Goodwill treatment.** Goodwill stays in CE. VINCI is demonstrably acquisitive (goodwill €7,296m at FY2015 → €20,177m at FY2025), so the non-acquisitive deduction is unavailable.

**Capex and FCF.** FCF = CFO − capex, where capex = "Purchases of property, plant and equipment and intangible assets" (gross, not net of the disposal proceeds VINCI nets in its own "operating investments") **plus** VINCI's own "Growth investments (concessions and PPPs)" subtotal, which comprises investments in concession fixed assets net of grants received and PPP financial receivables. FY2025: 3,011 + 1,173 = 4,184. This is deliberately darker than VINCI's published free cash flow — for FY2017 the memo reads €2,405m against VINCI's €2,525m, the €120m gap being disposal proceeds. Applied identically in all ten years.

**Maintenance-capex basis (P20): not active.** VINCI does disclose an expansion label ("growth investments"), satisfying condition (ii), but condition (i) fails — there is no multi-year capex ramp far above the pre-cycle capex/sales ratio. Total capex ran 4.0% of revenue in 2016 and 5.6% in 2025, a drift rather than a super-cycle, and growth investments have oscillated in a €0.8–1.5bn band throughout. All-capex basis is used for both the quality gates and the valuation gate.

**IFRS interest reclassification: not required.** VINCI presents "Net interest paid" inside net cash flows from operating activities in every year of the window. CFO is used as reported.

**Leases (P18).** IFRS 16 adopted 1 January 2019 with a €1,421m opening lease liability; from FY2019 lease liabilities are treated as debt-equivalent in CE, EV and L/E. For pre-adoption years, undiscounted operating-lease commitments were €1,237m at FY2017 and €1,230m at FY2016 — 1.8% of total assets against the P18 10% threshold. **No pre-adoption capitalisation performed.** Had it been, CE would rise and the returns reading would worsen, so the omission is not verdict-flattering.

**Captive finance / financial-institution substitution: not applicable.** VINCI has no captive-finance arm of scale; the OI/FCF/CE machinery applies directly.

**TBV.** TBV = BV − goodwill − all intangibles, including concession intangible assets, which is what the balance sheet line items require. FY2025: 30,752 − 20,177 − 11,258 − 29,007 = **−€29,690m**. Excluding concession rights on the argument that they are the operating assets gives −€683m — still negative. Both readings make MCap/TBV inapplicable rather than failed; stated in Gate 2.

**Bias check.** Consistency hazard checked — no position held, no prior memo, no register row. Cleverness bias noted and handled: the two-businesses-in-one framing is attractive and could tempt a sum-of-the-parts rescue in which the concession half is graded alone; the framework grades the consolidated entity and the segment split is used only where §2·1B directs (force calls), not to relocate the returns test. Hope bias noted on the 2032–2036 expiries: the memo does not assume a renewal on any terms, because none is contracted. No bias is live in the sense of having generated a judgment.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: PASS.** URD 2025 credit-risk note: no customer accounts for more than 10% of VINCI's revenue; roughly one-third of consolidated revenue is public or quasi-public sector, spread across national and local authorities in many jurisdictions. The disclosure sentence exists and is explicit.

**Breadth — suppliers: PASS (structural; no disclosure sentence — logged).** VINCI publishes no supplier-concentration statement. The pass rests on structure: FY2025 operating expenses were €66,145m, so a 10% supplier would have to bill roughly €6.6bn. VINCI's cost base is local subcontracting, aggregates, cement, bitumen, steel, cabling and labour, procured business-line by business-line across 120-plus countries; no plausible vendor approaches that scale, and no effective-supplier chokepoint of the fab-ecosystem or securitisation-market type exists. Absence of a sentence is noted as a disclosure-quality observation, not treated as evidence of concentration.

**Moat.** Single identified source, **Government**, and only in Concessions: exclusive, statutorily granted rights to operate defined networks with tariff formulae set in the concession contracts. No moat is identifiable in Energy Solutions or Construction — these are scale players in fragmented, price-competitive markets, which the margins confirm. Direction: **eroding, on fixed dates.** Escota expires 2032, Cofiroute 2034, ASF 2036. VINCI Autoroutes generated €6,733m of FY2025 revenue at a 49.2% EBITDA margin and is the largest single contributor inside the €6,160m Concessions operating income; on expiry the assets revert to the French state without compensation for the going concern. Airports (concessions of varying length in 14 countries) and VINCI Highways partially offset but are not contracted replacements for the French motorway rent.

**Market growth: MIXED.** Energy-transition and data-centre capex support Energy Solutions and Cobra IS volumes; French motorway traffic is mature and now separately taxed (2024 long-distance transport infrastructure levy); air traffic is growing but cyclical, as FY2020's 57% operating-income collapse demonstrated.

**Verdict: WEAK** — on two independent limbs of the §2·1B tiering: ≥2 strong forces in the dominant-revenue unit, and an eroding moat in the dominant-profit unit.
