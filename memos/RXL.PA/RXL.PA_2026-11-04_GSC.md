# RXL.PA — Rexel S.A.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS (two material units); Past WEAK (7.4% ROCE); Future WEAK (unit customer conc.); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (flag pair); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past + Future + SF)**.

Rexel is one of the world's two largest distributors of electrical supplies, selling the products of Schneider, Legrand, ABB and thousands of other makers to electricians, contractors and industrial buyers in Europe and North America. Decades of acquisitions left EUR 5.6bn of goodwill and intangibles, holding its ten-year return on capital near 7%. As a middleman between concentrated brand owners and business customers it shows no defensible pricing position, and its capital allocation carries two cautions.

Read as the board saw them, FY2024's repurchases now clear; late-2023 purchases, at the worth line, keep the caution.

## 2. Understanding

Group sentence: Rexel S.A. is a Paris-based electrical-supplies distributor, listed on Euronext Paris since 2007 and 23% owned by the activist fund Cevian, that buys cables, power-distribution and energy-management equipment, industrial-automation components, lighting, tools, conduits, wiring devices, climate (heating, heat-pump and air-conditioning) products, security and data-communication gear and solar and EV-charging equipment from some 7,000 manufacturers (28 of them half of purchases) and resells them, mostly from stock through about 1,876 branches and 64 logistics centres in 17 countries and increasingly online (about a fifth of sales), plus direct-shipped project sales, to electrical installers and contractors, industrial companies, commercial-building and infrastructure customers and utilities (commercial 50%, industrial 26%, residential 24% of FY2025 sales of EUR 19.4bn). ## 3. Quality — past record

STD lens. Over FY2016–FY2025 the worst-of-four ROCE is 7.4% (10.1% over five years) and FCFROCE 5.6% (6.6%); FCF uses all capex (0.6–0.9% of sales). Goodwill and acquired intangibles stay in capital. Returns rose from 4–6% before 2020 to 12–16% in FY2021–FY2023 on copper-led price inflation, then fell back to 8–10%. Per-share operating income grew 11.4% a year and FCF 11.7% (harsher reads). FCFROCE has fallen three years running. L/E is 166%, inside the limit. **WEAK** (`7.4% ROCE`).

## 4. Quality — future

Both units sell to businesses, and the filings carry no statement ruling out a customer above 10% of sales; the only statement concerns credit risk. Each unit therefore carries a customer-concentration flag. Rexel is not dominant: Sonepar is larger and Wesco and Graybar are comparable in North America. So the flag becomes a STRONG customer-power call, uncontained. Supplier power is also STRONG: brand-specified manufacturers could sell direct, as the filer warns, and the French competition authority found that Schneider and Legrand set Rexel's resale prices. Substitutes and entrants are WEAK. The moat (local density and purchasing scale) is plausible and stable. Two strong forces in each unit. **WEAK**.
