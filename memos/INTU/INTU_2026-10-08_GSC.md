# INTU — Intuit Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding **FAIL** (one-level decomposition: the material Consumer unit is irreducible) → **DO NOT ANALYSE FURTHER**. Past, Future, shareholder-friendliness and cheapness were not run.

Intuit's two franchises are easy to describe one at a time. QuickBooks and its add-on services sell software and money services to small and mid-market businesses. TurboTax sells tax preparation to consumers. The difficulty is how the filer now reports them. Since 1 August 2025 Credit Karma sits inside the Consumer segment, and the FY2026 10-K reports three revenue lines for that unit: TurboTax (61.7%), Credit Karma (30.8%) and ProTax (7.5%). TurboTax is paid by consumers. Credit Karma is paid by card issuers, lenders and insurers, a fee per approved card, funded loan, click or lead. Two sub-businesses above 10% of a material unit with different paying customers make the unit irreducible under MODEL v4.5.0, so Gate 0 fails. The DNAF is a parameter-driven result, not a finding of incomprehension or obfuscation. It flips back to a full analysis if the Consumer unit stops mixing paying customers above the line, or if a MODEL ruling changes how a platform segment of this kind is tested.

## 2. Understanding
**Group sentence.** Intuit sells financial software and related money services, mostly as online subscriptions and per-use fees, to small and mid-market businesses and accountants (QuickBooks accounting, payroll, payments, bill pay, small-business loans and Mailchimp marketing) and to US and Canadian consumers (TurboTax tax preparation). Through Credit Karma it also sells qualified referrals to banks, card issuers, lenders and insurers. It operates from Mountain View, California, sells about 92% of revenue in the United States, and is a four-decade incumbent of US tax and small-business accounting that grew by acquiring Credit Karma (2020) and Mailchimp (2021).

**Units (P22; FY2026 10-K, Note 14, segments recast from 1 August 2025).** Intuit does not track assets by segment, so materiality reads on revenue and Σ positive segment results.

| Unit | Filer's description | FY2026 revenue ($m) | Revenue share | Segment OI share | Material |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Global Business Solutions | serves small and mid-market businesses and accountants: QuickBooks, Intuit Enterprise Suite, payroll, payments, bill pay, financing, Mailchimp | 12,864 | 60.0% | 61.0% | yes |
| Consumer | "primarily serves consumers and professional accountants": TurboTax, Credit Karma and ProTax | 8,584 | **40.0%** | 39.0% | **yes** |

**Gate 0 test.** Global Business Solutions can be described in one six-parameter sentence: its reported revenue lines (QuickBooks Online Accounting, Online Services, QuickBooks Desktop Accounting, Desktop Services and Supplies) are all paid by small and mid-market businesses and their accountants. Consumer cannot:
- **Its sub-businesses are reported revenue lines for the unit.** FY2026: TurboTax $5,296m, Credit Karma $2,641m, ProTax $647m (FY2025: 63.6%, 28.4%, 8.0%; FY2024: 66.8%, 24.4%, 8.9%). TurboTax and Credit Karma are each far above 10% of the unit in every year shown.
- **They differ in paying customer.** TurboTax revenue is DIY and assisted tax preparation, e-filing and consumer money products, paid by the consumer filing a return. Credit Karma revenue is "cost-per-action transactions … such as credit card issuances and personal and auto loan funding" and cost-per-click and cost-per-lead transactions for home loans and insurance; the fee is "earned based on a predetermined fee for approved actions" and the filer describes the model as partnering "with financial institutions and financial product providers, connecting them with consumers on our platform". Credit Karma's members use it free; its payers are financial institutions. The industries differ too: tax-preparation software against consumer-credit and insurance lead generation.
- **No exception applies.** The exception for different sides of one marketplace covers Credit Karma's own members and partners, not TurboTax: a tax filing fee and a lender's referral fee are separate transactions. The same products sold through different channels are not in question. The filer's segment description does not name one industry ("primarily serves consumers and professional accountants").

The unit is above the P22 line on revenue and segment result. **FAIL → DNAF.** Where a reading was open, the harsher governs (J13); here the filer's own revenue lines and fee description decide it.

The lens test (STD on the evidence: no bank or insurer charter; the checking and savings accounts are offered "through an FDIC-member bank partner"; the small-business loan book, $1,647m of notes receivable at 31 July 2026, is a non-bank lending activity inside Global Business Solutions) and the group dependency check (no listed subsidiary or listed holding) were not needed for the verdict.
