# BH — Biglari Holdings Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Past **WEAK** — substitution-rule ROE of **1.6–3.1%** on every equity reading (Σ 4y net earnings $69.3m) · Future ADEQUATE · SF **FAIL**, overdetermined — comp hard strike, related-party service agreement, ADVERSE allocation · **FAIR** on the book anchor (1.09× gross, 0.44× look-through) → **AVOID**.

Biglari Holdings is the register's clearest case of a cheap asset that belongs to someone else. The book is real — Steak n Shake franchising, two insurers, oil and gas, roughly $1.1bn of investments — and at around book value the equity is not expensive on the anchor this class is judged by. What the framework cannot get past is who controls it and on what terms. Sardar Biglari holds roughly two-thirds of the vote through stock **bought with company money and parked in a fund he manages**; the company pays his entity $8.4m a year on top of a $900,000 salary, against four-year net earnings averaging $17m; the incentive agreement entitles him to a quarter of investment gains above a 6% hurdle; and he has taken well over $100m out over a decade. That is not a blemish attached to a compounder — it is the allocation record.

## 2. Understanding
A holding company: Steak n Shake (largely refranchised) and Western Sizzlin restaurants, First Guard and Southern Pioneer insurance, Abraxas Petroleum and Southern Oil, Maxim, and an investment portfolio held mainly through The Lion Fund partnerships managed by the chairman's own firm; San Antonio-HQ'd, dual-class since 2018. **PASS** — the parts are legible; the circular ownership makes the whole harder to read than its size suggests.

## 3. Quality — past record
The operating machinery is inapplicable — a securities-heavy holding company with insurance inside it — so returns run on equity. Net earnings attributable: **+$55.6m, +$54.9m, −$3.8m, −$37.5m** across 2022–25, a four-year sum of **$69.3m**. Two primary extractions disagree on the equity denominator (App A); the finding survives either — **3.1%** at the lowest equity reading, **1.6%** at the highest, every path far below the 10% floor. Operating earnings before tax fell $32.6m → $18.8m as a $225m note at 8.8% landed at Steak n Shake. The chairman's own scorecard — investments per share, compounding 42.4% since 2008 — runs on a count that shrinks partly because the company keeps buying its own stock into his fund. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth PASS both sides (franchisees, policyholders, oil offtake — all fragmented). Forces: customer power WEAK · supplier power WEAK · substitutes WATCH (Steak n Shake sits in retail's most substitutable category) · new entrants WEAK in niche insurance underwriting, WATCH in restaurants. Tally 0 strong. Moat: **Brand** at Steak n Shake, thin and much repaired; the insurers hold small underwriting niches; no group-level moat. Market growth MIXED. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
Comp: **hard strike.** FY2025 net earnings are negative and the free-cash-flow leg is n.m. under substitution — $9.3m against a loss year is categorically more than token; the trailing three-year ceiling is **$0.23m**, a **40.7× breach**. Related-party: the **$8.4m annual service agreement with Biglari Enterprises** is the pay and the related party at once, outside the shareholder-approved machinery — an independent second negative. Control: two-thirds of the vote, **most of it bought with company funds**. Capital allocation: **ADVERSE** — the defining act of the era was deploying shareholder capital into a chairman-managed fund that bought the company's own shares, delivering control he did not pay for, while the same structure entitles him to 25% of gains above a 6% hurdle and $8.4m a year regardless. Live, not historical. Any one of these fails the gate. **FAIL.**

## 6. Price
Primaries are inapplicable; the anchor is book. Class A equivalents trade at **$1,932.75**; gross market capitalisation **$1.21bn** against reported equity of ~$1.11bn = **1.09× book**, and **0.44× book** on the company's own look-through count of 254,176 shares. On the alternative equity reading, 2.12× and 0.86×. All sit inside the 3× qualifier and at or below the ~1.2× anchor this class trades against. **FAIR** — the cheapest thing this run has priced, and not actionable: two gates fail before price is reached, and the discount exists because a controller the record has already judged allocates the book.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers (context for the AVOID)
The bull case is the discount itself: half of look-through book, real insurers, an owner-operator compounding investments per share for seventeen years. The bear case is that the discount prices exactly what this memo does — a controller taking a fixed $9.3m plus a quarter of the upside, whose control the discounted shareholders financed. The disputed lines range the price ratios only.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review
Nothing moves without the arrangements changing: termination of the service agreement, unwinding the Lion Fund's BH holding, or an incentive paying on per-share value rather than fund gains. Quality needs four years of double-digit ROE, which needs the investment result to stop swinging the answer. Next review: **FY2026 annual report (~Mar 2027)**. Alert: n/a.
