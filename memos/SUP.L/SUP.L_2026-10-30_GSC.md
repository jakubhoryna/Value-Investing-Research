# SUP.L — Supreme plc: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past ADEQUATE (27.7% ROCE, capped by an FCFROCE decline); Future WEAK (customer conc.); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (flag pair); cheapness FAIR — **AVOID (Future + SF)**.

Supreme turns low-cost sourcing and UK manufacturing into value-priced vapes, drinks and household goods for discount retailers, and it has earned very high returns on little capital for a decade. Two things keep it off the watchlist. Its market position is weak: one retailer took 14.5% of FY2026 revenue, vaping is being narrowed by law and tax, and nothing in the filings shows pricing power. Capital allocation has also turned towards buying brands — Typhoo out of administration, Clearly Drinks, SlimFast and 1001 — without demonstrated pre-deal profits, while FY2026 pay ran above 5% of a free cash flow those purchases depressed. The verdict would flip only with the concentration carried by a demonstrated moat, the vaping category stabilised under the new duty, and two clean years of deals and pay inside the lines.

## 2. Understanding

Supreme plc is a Manchester-based, founder-controlled (Sandy Chadha, 54%) maker and distributor of value-priced branded consumer goods, listed on AIM since February 2021, that sells — about 90% in the UK — mainly to discount retailers, grocers, convenience and wholesale chains (such as B&M, Home Bargains, Tesco and Asda) and online: vaping products (55% of FY2026 revenue: its own UK-made 88Vape e-liquids and pod devices, plus third-party vape brands it distributes), drinks and wellness (26%: Typhoo tea, Clearly Drinks bottled waters and soft drinks, SlimFast meal replacements, Sci-MX sports nutrition) and electricals and household (20%: batteries under licensed Energizer, Eveready and JCB names and distributed Duracell, lighting, and the 1001 cleaning brand), a former battery-and-lighting importer turned manufacturer and brand owner through acquisitions since 2022. **PASS.**

The filer reports one operating segment, so Supreme is not a group (P22); the three categories are described, never weighted.

## 3. Quality — past record

On the STD lens and the J1 line (derivative gains, the Cuts Ice trademark gain and the Typhoo negative goodwill out), worst-of-four ROCE is 30.1% over FY2017–FY2026 and 27.7% over the latest five years. FCFROCE on maintenance-capex FCF (P20 constructed; all capex in FY2026) is 21.5% and 19.6% (all-capex 20.7% and 18.9%). The harsher growth reads are ΔOI/sh 15.1% and ΔFCF/sh 5.3%, and L/E is 83%. But beginning-of-period FCFROCE fell in FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026 (39.4% → 32.3% → 25.4% → 12.2%): a J6 limb (a) decline, from acquired capital, which caps Past below STRONG. **ADEQUATE — 27.7% ROCE.**

## 4. Quality — future

One customer took 14.5% of FY2026 revenue (two took 13.0% and 11.1% in FY2025): `customer conc.` flagged. No strong moat carries it — there is no evidence of dominance or of pricing power over the retailers, and gross margin fell from 32% to 29% — so customer power is a STRONG force. Substitutes are STRONG too: the disposable-vape ban (June 2025), the vaping products duty (October 2026) and illicit product press on the largest category, and batteries and lighting are shrinking. Entrants are STRONG (low barriers in value vapes and distributed goods); suppliers are WEAK. No moat is identified, and market growth is MIXED. **WEAK — customer conc.**
