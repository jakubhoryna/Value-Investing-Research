# PYPL — PayPal Holdings, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | STRONG | **WEAK** | PASS (caveats) | FAIR | **AVOID** |

PayPal is a statistically attractive paradox: a decade of 20%+ ex-cash returns on capital, net cash, shares shrinking 7% a year, and a price (6.6× FCF, ~6× EV/OI) that sits within ~10% of the mechanical trigger band. What it is not, on the evidence, is a *good* business going forward. The core profit engine — branded checkout — is losing share to OS-level wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay, Shop Pay) that own the device and the browser; PayPal's own board replaced the CEO in February 2026 explicitly because "results, particularly in branded checkout, fell short of expectations" — the second CEO change in 2.5 years. An eroding moat amid unfavourable forces is a Gate 1B failure regardless of price, so the verdict is AVOID, not WATCH: the cheapness is the market pricing the erosion, and the framework refuses to bet on which is mispriced. The verdict flips only if the moat question flips — sustained, multi-quarter evidence that branded-checkout share has stopped declining.

## 2. Understanding

PayPal Holdings, Inc. is a US-listed (Delaware) financial-technology company that earns volume-based transaction fees from hundreds of millions of consumer accounts and tens of millions of merchants for digital payment processing — branded checkout (PayPal, Venmo), unbranded processing (Braintree), P2P, BNPL, and value-added services — across ~200 markets with the US its largest revenue geography, as the incumbent leader in online branded checkout, growing revenue mid-single-digits while losing checkout share to platform wallets. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Exceptional and improving on Marshall's definitions: ROCE_xc ≥15% in 9 of 10 years (avg ~22%, FY2025 27.2%), FCFROCE_xc ≥8% in all 10 (often 20–40%). OI/share rose 5.3× over the decade (1.19→6.27) and FCF/share 3.8×, powered by operating leverage plus a 21% share-count reduction since 2021. Headline L/E is 296% — above the 200% flag — but ~$40B of liabilities are non-interest-bearing customer funds matched by segregated assets; ex-customer-funds L/E is ~96% and the company is roughly net-cash. With exceptional returns and L/E far below the 700% cap, leverage passes (judgment recorded, Appendix C). **STRONG.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: customers PASS (no concentration); suppliers **FAIL** — the largest cost line (transaction expense) flows to the Visa/Mastercard network duopoly and a concentrated set of issuing/processing partners. Forces: rivalry HIGH, substitutes HIGH (OS wallets, A2A rails, stablecoins), supplier power HIGH, customer power MEDIUM, entrants MEDIUM → **UNFAVOURABLE**. Moat: a genuine two-sided network (acceptance breadth) plus brand trust and ingrained vaulted credentials — but **eroding** where it matters: Apple and Google sit between PayPal and the consumer at the point of checkout, and the board has conceded the share loss. E-commerce growth is real but is exactly the growth that attracts capital. **WEAK** (breadth FAIL; eroding moat amid unfavourable forces).

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Comp is predominantly performance-linked equity (PSUs); single share class; insider ownership low (<1%, all NEOs) — typical large-cap, noted not struck. RPTs minimal. Buybacks: 2023–2025 repurchases (~$5–6B/yr at implied ~$63–71 avg, 8–12× FCF) are value-accretive; the 2021 repurchases (~$3.4B near peak multiples) and peak-priced M&A (Honey $4B, Paidy $2.7B) under prior management were not — **prominent caveat**, judged historical since that regime is gone and the board has twice acted on underperformance. Dividend initiated 2025; ~100%+ of FCF now returned. **PASS**, caveat flagged.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Branded-checkout share loss to Apple Pay/Shop Pay accelerates as agentic/one-tap checkout becomes default, hollowing out the highest-margin volume.
- Unbranded (Braintree) mix and network/issuer fee inflation keep compressing transaction margin.
- Third CEO strategy reset in four years (Lores) fails to fix execution, burning cash on transformation.
- Venmo monetisation and BNPL growth prove insufficient to offset the core's decline.
- Regulatory/interchange shifts or stablecoin rails disintermediate the wallet layer entirely.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

AVOID → WATCH/BUY requires the Future gate to flip: ≥4 consecutive quarters of branded-checkout TPV growing at or above e-commerce market growth (share stabilisation), with transaction margin dollars compounding — then re-run Gate 1B in a fresh REVIEW. Past STRONG and price near the band mean the re-test is worth doing promptly if evidence appears. Next review: FY2026 results (early Feb 2027) or upon such evidence.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth.** *Customers:* hundreds of millions of consumer accounts, tens of millions of merchants; no customer ≥10% of revenue → broad, unlikely to consolidate → **YES**. *Suppliers:* card networks (effective duopoly), issuing banks, processors; the dominant cost line has no substitute rail at scale today → **NO**. **Breadth FAIL.**

**Five forces.**

| Force | Threat | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer power | MEDIUM | Merchants can steer to cheaper rails; consumers multi-home across wallets |
| Supplier power | HIGH | Network duopoly sets interchange/fees on the largest cost line |
| Substitutes | HIGH | Apple Pay/Google Pay/Shop Pay, account-to-account rails, stablecoins |
| New entrants | MEDIUM | Scale/regulatory barriers high, but the dangerous entrants (OS platforms) are already in |
| Rivalry | HIGH | Stripe/Adyen in unbranded; platform wallets in branded; BNPL specialists |

**Overall: UNFAVOURABLE.**

**Moat.** Network (two-sided acceptance breadth) + Brand (buyer/seller protection trust) + Ingrainedness (vaulted credentials, habitual checkout). Direction: **eroding** — OS-level wallets intercept the checkout moment on devices PayPal doesn't control; the board's Feb 2026 DEFA14A concedes branded-checkout results "fell short of expectations" and replaced the CEO over it; independent analysis (Jan 2026) attributes per-account branded volume weakness to share loss rather than macro. Counterweights (Venmo momentum, BNPL, PayPal World/agentic-commerce deals incl. WeChat Pay interoperability) are real but unproven as moat-restorers.

**Market growth:** YES — global e-commerce and digital payments still expand mid-to-high single digits; per Marshall's caveat, that growth is precisely what attracted Apple, Google, and Shopify into the checkout layer.
