# ML.PA — Compagnie Générale des Établissements Michelin: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past **WEAK**; Future **ADEQUATE**; Shareholder-friendliness **PASS** under heightened scrutiny (one flag — capital-allocation CAUTION); Cheapness **EXPENSIVE**; final verdict **AVOID on the Past gate**.

Michelin is the best-branded tyremaker in the world and earns a poor return on the capital it now employs. On the as-reported operating income line the worst of four normalized reads is 8.39% against a 10% floor, returns have fallen three straight years, and operating income per share compounded at −1.4% since 2016 while capital employed grew about 40%. Nothing between here and a pass is a price question; even backvalued to the prior cycle's peak, EV/OI still fails. One accounting convention (Appendix C) would flip the verdict to WATCH.

## 2. Understanding

Michelin is a French *société en commandite par actions* — a partnership limited by shares whose Managing General Partner carries unlimited personal liability and is removable only with a non-managing general partner's (SAGES) consent, overseen but not appointed by an eleven-member Supervisory Board — which manufactures differentiated branded tyres and engineered polymer composites, selling roughly two-thirds of tyre volume as replacement product to independent distributors, dealers and its own 2,400-point retail and franchise network and the balance to vehicle and equipment OEMs that homologate its fitments, from about seventy plants with 122,600 employees, across North America 37%, Europe 26% and rest-of-world 37% of €25,992m of 2025 sales, in a definitively cyclical, capital-intensive, globally contested industry. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **8.39%** (beginning-CE, with cash) and worst-of-four FCFROCE **5.06%** over FY2021–FY2025; the reads cluster 8.39–9.69% and 5.06–6.15%, so this is no denominator artifact. ROCE_wc has fallen three consecutive years — 9.64%, 8.40%, 7.83%, 7.47% — firing P4 independently. The decade anchor confirms it: FY2016 ROCE_wc was 12.34% on €22.6bn of capital employed against 7.47% on €31.7bn now. Leverage is the clean number: liabilities are 94% of book equity, 105% at 30 June 2026. **WEAK** — returns below the P3 floor on every read, plus a sustained decline.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both sides: a replacement-dominated, fragmented distribution base with no customer near a tenth of revenue, and a €5.1bn materials bill spread across natural rubber 26%, synthetic 22%, fillers 21%, chemicals 15%, steel cord 9% and textiles 7% — commodities with many vendors. Three forces read WEAK: customers cannot backward-integrate, suppliers sell commodities, a tyre has no substitute. **New entrants read STRONG** — barriers to success have not held below the premium tier, and Road transportation's margin halved from 9.0% to 4.7% in one year under low-cost Asian competition. Tally one strong, contained. Moat: **Brand**, single-sourced, stable — homologation on premium fitments pulls branded replacement demand at a premium, still visible in Consumer 11.7% and Specialty 13.5% margins. Market growth MIXED. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation is not close: the Managing Chairman's €3.22m 2025 total is 3.9% of the fiscal-year threshold (5% of the lower of FCF €1,852m and net income €1,665m = €83.3m) and 3.5% of the normalized threshold; adding the €2.94m general-partner profit share still leaves 7.4%. The auditors' special report records no related-party agreement in 2025 or surviving from before. The SCA structure is a §2·1C·1 control caveat held **UNPAIRED** on the MC.PA/WMT/TRI line — one share class, no vote-versus-capital wedge, no block extracting value. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: repurchases execute at 8.8–11.2× on all four reads, but reinvestment has not sustained the returns record. One flag, no pair. **PASS**, under heightened scrutiny.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- The returns decline may be secular, not cyclical, with low-cost Asian capacity taking the value tier of truck and entry-level replacement demand.
- Capital employed grew about 40% since 2016 while operating income fell; a fourth consecutive ROCE decline at FY2026 would settle the pattern.
- Restructuring and impairment charges average €517m a year and recur, which is why the operating-income line convention is load-bearing.
- The €750m 2026 repurchase mandate is irrevocable and already charged €753m to equity — buying cannot stop if the price runs.
- FY2017–FY2020 balance sheets and FY2016–FY2020 cash flows were unretrievable, so the decade's middle is unmeasured and the window record-limited.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Past flips to ADEQUATE if worst-of-four ROCE regains 10%, needing operating income above roughly €3.15bn on the current capital base against €2,366m today. It also flips on the accounting convention: on *segment* operating income the worst read is 10.09%, Past reads ADEQUATE and the verdict becomes WATCH. ## C. Calculation notes

**FY2025 CE build.** CE_wc = total assets 35,018 − NIBCL 3,347 = **31,671**. NIBCL = trade payables 2,811 + trade payables under reverse-factoring agreements 536. CE_xc = 31,671 − (cash and cash equivalents 3,877 + current financial assets 371) = **27,423**.

**NIBCL burden-of-proof call (logged; not verdict-determinative).** Michelin presents a single combined current-liability line, **"Provisions and other current liabilities" (€3,287m at FY2025)**, which the retrieved documents do not disaggregate. Under §2·1A the burden of proof rests with the item and an ambiguous, materially sized "other" line stays inside capital employed; it is therefore **left in CE**. This is stricter than the SGO.PA and DG.PA treatments, where separately-presented "other payables" / "other current liabilities" lines went into NIBCL and only separately-labelled provisions stayed in CE — Michelin simply does not present them separately. **Sensitivity, run in full:** moving the whole line into NIBCL gives four normalized reads of 9.41% / 10.96% / 9.32% / 10.82%, worst **9.32%** — still under the 10% floor, so **the verdict does not move on this call**. A future session disagreeing with this memo should look here second, and note that it does not reach the verdict. Split the line from the FY2026 URD note at the next touch.

**Cash and short-term investments.** "Current financial assets" (€371m at FY2025, €506m at 30 Jun 2026) are treated as short-term investments and removed in CE_xc and in the all-cash EV variant. They are predominantly cash-equivalent instruments and derivatives; the line is under 1.5% of total assets and the treatment moves worst-of-four ROCE by under 10bp.

**OPERATING-INCOME LINE — logged, and verdict-determinative.** Michelin's consolidated income statement carries two operating subtotals. **"Segment operating income"** is gross income less selling and marketing, R&D, general and administrative and segment other income and expenses — i.e. revenue − COGS − SG&A. **"Operating income"** is that subtotal after **"Other operating income and expenses"**, which absorbs restructuring, impairments, acquisition and disposal effects and which has recurred in every year of the window at −189 (2021), −375, −920, −747 and −353 (2025), an average of €517m a year, roughly 2% of sales.

- **Convention applied (primary): the line as reported and as labelled "Operating income".** This is what DG.PA did — VINCI's FY2020 reads €2,459m, its charged line, not the €2,864m *résultat opérationnel courant* — and it is what §2·1A's "operating income as reported, no add-backs of impairments, litigation or one-offs" directs on its face. **Result: worst-of-four ROCE 8.39% → Past WEAK → AVOID.**
- **Alternative reading: segment operating income.** This is the *rationale* SGO.PA gave for its own choice ("matching the framework definition, revenue − COGS − SG&A"). On this line the four normalized reads are **10.09% / 11.55% / 10.27% / 11.74%**, worst **10.09%** — 9bp above the P3 floor. Past would read **ADEQUATE** (growth flat-to-positive at +0.54% on segment OI/sh, leverage in bounds) and, with Future ADEQUATE and SF PASS, the verdict would be **WATCH**, band unchanged in shape but computed on €2,719m of OI: EV/OI legs €13.13 (no-cash) / €18.08 (all-cash).
- **The two French precedents do not agree on the underlying principle, only on the label.** SGO.PA's "Operating income" is the *clean* line; Michelin's "Operating income" is the *charged* line. A label-based rule reaches opposite economic answers in the two cases, and SGO's stated *reason* (revenue − COGS − SG&A) would here overturn the verdict. Michelin's worst read sits 9bp above the floor on one reading and 161bp below it on the other — the same knife-edge SGO logged at 10.07%. **Open item for the next MODEL touch: codify the French OI-line convention as either label-based or definition-based before a third name is decided on it.** This memo applies the label-based rule for cross-register consistency and states the alternative in the body, not only here.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers.** No customer-concentration sentence appears anywhere in the retrieved disclosure, and the structure supports its absence: replacement tyres are roughly two-thirds of tyre revenue (auto and two-wheel replacement alone is 38% of group sales against 17% for auto OE), sold through independent distributors, dealers, and Michelin's own 2,400-point retail network of which 1,800 are franchised, across more than 170 countries; the OE balance is spread across every major carmaker, truckmaker and equipment OEM. No single counterparty plausibly reaches a tenth of €25,992m. **PASS.** Noted, as with PIRC.MI, that French issuers face no US-style mandatory 10%-customer disclosure, so absence is weaker evidence here than for an SEC filer and the structural argument is doing the work.

**Breadth — suppliers.** Materials cost €5.1bn in 2025, about 22% of cost of sales plus operating expenses, split natural rubber 26%, synthetic rubber 22%, fillers 21%, chemicals 15%, steel cord 9%, textiles 7%. Every category is a multi-vendor commodity with published market references; Michelin is additionally back-integrated into natural-rubber plantations. Looking through to the *effective* supplier changes nothing — no single vendor in any category approaches 10% of COGS plus opex. **PASS.** No waiver required on either side.

**Forces (binary).**

| Force | Call | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | WEAK | Fragmented replacement channel dominates revenue and includes Michelin's own retail; carmakers cannot backward-integrate and homologation locks a fitment for a model cycle. OE is run thin deliberately, as a funnel into replacement. |
| Supplier bargaining power | WEAK | Commodity inputs with market references and many vendors, plus own plantations. Input-price volatility is a margin risk, not supplier power. |
| Threat of substitutes | WEAK | No direct substitute for a tyre; doing without is unavailable to a vehicle owner; the wholly-different-function route (fewer vehicle-kilometres) is slow and marginal against a growing global vehicle parc. |
| Threat of new entrants | **STRONG** | Barriers to entry are capital, not knowledge, and barriers to success have not held below the premium tier: low-cost Asian capacity added in Morocco, Mexico, Thailand and Serbia has taken the value tier, and Michelin's Road transportation segment margin fell 9.0% → 4.7% in a single year, with segment sales −8.7%. This is measured erosion, not a forecast. |

**Tally: 1 strong** → judgment zone, contained.

**Moat.** Single source: **Brand**. Mechanism: a century of premium positioning plus motorsport and the MICHELIN Guide sustain consumer brand preference and win OE homologation on premium and prestige fitments; homologated fitments then pull branded replacement demand at a price premium, which is where the margin lives. Direction: **stable**, not strengthening — Consumer 11.7% and Specialty 13.5% segment margins still command a clear premium and the ≥18" mix keeps growing, while the erosion is concentrated in Transportation, 23% of sales. A second year of Transportation deterioration, or Consumer margin falling below roughly 10%, would re-read the direction as eroding and take Future to WEAK.

**Market growth: MIXED.** Global tyre unit demand is broadly flat with mix upgrading; the Polymer Composite Solutions business grew 13.6% in H1 2026 and is the declared growth vector, but it is a small share of group sales.

**Verdict: ADEQUATE** — breadth passes both sides, one strong force contained to one segment, plausible single-source moat, stable.

**Control structure — §2·1C·1 caveat, held UNPAIRED.** Michelin is a *société en commandite par actions*. The Managing General Partner (Menegaux) is appointed and removable only with the consent of the non-managing general partner SAGES, not by the limited shareholders; he carries **unlimited personal liability** for the company's obligations; the eleven-member Supervisory Board oversees but does not appoint. This is a genuine entrenchment structure and is documented as such. It is held **unpaired**, and the reasoning is stated so a later session can locate the disagreement:

- **There is one class of shares.** The double-voting-right regime (687,606,374 shares carrying 972,589,357 votes at 30 June 2026) is the standard French *droit de vote double* available to **every** holder who registers for the qualifying period — it is not concentrated in a family or a founder. There is no vote-versus-capital wedge of the kind that governed **KNEBV v2** (entrenchment counted as a flag) or **WISE.L v3** (9:1 Class B, 64.70% of votes on 20.37% of capital, a ~3.18× wedge created by an affirmative resolution).
- **No controlling economic block extracts value.** This is the **MC.PA v2** line (Arnault at 49.77% of *capital*, caveat unpaired), extended by **WMT v2** (Walton 44–46%, single class, votes = capital) and **TRI v2** (Woodbridge ~69%, one-share-one-vote). The Michelin family holds no comparable stake; the register at 31 December 2025 is 68.4% non-resident institutional, 17.9% French institutional, 10.7% individual, 3.1% employee.
- **The general partners' economic take is de minimis and disclosed.** SAGES received €2,943,900 for 2025 — 0.18% of net income to shareholders. The AGM's 4th resolution and the statutory auditors' special report record **no related-party agreements entered into in 2025 and none surviving from prior years**.
- **The structure supplies an offsetting alignment fact**: unlimited personal liability of the managing partner, which no ordinary listed company provides.

The caveat is therefore documented and carried as heightened scrutiny, not as a flag. Under §2·1C·1 a control structure becomes a strike only when paired with value-destructive allocation; allocation here is CAUTION, not ADVERSE, and the two are not merged — the caveat contributes nothing to the flag count. **A session that counts the SCA entrenchment as an independent governance negative reaches SF FAIL on the pair with the capital-allocation CAUTION, and AVOID on two gates instead of one.** That is the single most locatable disagreement in this memo and is named as such.
