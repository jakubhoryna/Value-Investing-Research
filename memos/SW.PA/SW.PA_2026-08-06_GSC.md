# SW.PA — Sodexo S.A.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past **WEAK** (worst-of-four normalized ROCE 9.34% against the 10% floor, on the most flattering window available); Future **WEAK** (three of four forces strong-unfavourable); shareholder-friendliness **PASS**, one flag (capital-allocation CAUTION); cheapness **EXPENSIVE** at 14.0×/12.8× EV/OI — **AVOID** on two failed quality gates.

Sodexo is worth owning only for what outsourced catering and facilities management durably is: a cash-generative service with a long runway of self-operated market to convert, run by a family owning 42.8% of it. What stands between here and a BUY is the returns engine, not price. Capital employed is €10.0bn against €985m of as-reported operating profit; the underlying margin is guided from 4.7% to 3.2–3.4% for Fiscal 2026 and 2027; net debt rose €2,687m to €3,582m in six months; and €1bn of non-recurring investment is committed to Fiscal 2030 to repair competitiveness already lost. To flip, Sodexo must earn back above 10% on capital employed on the line it reports as operating profit, and customer power and the self-operation substitute must stop setting its pricing — the first is possible, the second is not.

## 2. Understanding

Sodexo S.A. is a French *société anonyme* listed on Euronext Paris and controlled by the founding family's holding company Bellon SA, selling outsourced on-site food services and facilities management — a largely commodity service differentiated on price, reliability and local execution rather than product — under multi-year, competitively re-tendered contracts to client organisations in business, healthcare and seniors, education and sports-and-leisure, who pay rather than the diners who consume, with 46% of revenue in North America, 36% in Europe and 18% elsewhere, and which since spinning off Pluxee on 1 February 2024 does nothing else. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

The Pluxee demerger is a genuine basis break — a quarter of operating profit and €5.3bn of float-heavy assets removed, only Fiscal 2023 restated — so the consistent window is **three years, history-limited** and STRONG is unavailable by construction. On the as-reported *Operating profit* line the four normalized reads are 9.34% / 11.81% / 9.78% / 12.33%; the worst, **9.34%**, is below the 10% floor. FCFROCE worst-of-four is 6.31%. Per-share growth is positive (ΔOI/sh +7.6%, ΔFCF/sh +9.2%) but is a window artefact: profit fell in Fiscal 2025 and Fiscal 2026 is guided sharply lower. L/E 292% is a logged P5 flag. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth passes structurally; the supplier leg is unresolved and logged. Customer power **STRONG** — contracts re-tender on three-to-five-year cycles at 4–5% margins and net new business is negative. Supplier power **WEAK** — inputs come from many vendors through a purchasing organisation. Substitutes **STRONG** — self-operation still holds roughly half the market. New entrants **STRONG** — a local caterer needs no capital to take one site. Tally three strong-unfavourable. The moat is **cost**, from purchasing scale and site density, and it is **eroding**: margin guidance moved 4.7% → 3.2–3.4%. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 passes on both reads: the highest sourced corporate-officer total, €3.65m, against thresholds of €31.6m (fiscal-year), €32.5m (trailing-3y) and the ≈US$40m absolute trigger; director fees are far below any norm. The Bellon SA coordination agreement, at cost, is 0.29% of SG&A — a caveat; the €917.9m Sofinsod sale to Bellon SA is INDETERMINATE on price, forcing scrutiny but never completing a pair. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: repurchases are plan-bounded and reach 12.8× at worst, but reinvestment has not sustained returns while a 50% payout and a sterilising buyback run into rising leverage. The 1.355× voting wedge is held **UNPAIRED** per MC.PA. **PASS — one flag.**

## C. Calculation notes

**⚠ The French operating-income-line convention — computed, stated, and logged as an open MODEL item.**

Sodexo presents both lines, exactly as SGO.PA and ML.PA do:

- ***Underlying operating profit / résultat opérationnel courant*** — FY2025 **€1,139m**. It is revenue less cost of sales less SG&A costs, plus €5m of the operating share of equity-method profit (24,074 − 21,241 − 1,699 = 1,134; +5 = 1,139).
- ***Operating profit*** — FY2025 **€985m**, after *other operating income* €21m and *other operating expenses* €175m.

**This memo computes ROCE on the as-reported line labelled "Operating profit" (€985m), per the SGO.PA / DG.PA / ML.PA label precedent.** Sodexo's "other operating income and expenses" are not one-offs: the net charge has been −€129m (FY2023), −€58m (FY2024), −€154m (FY2025) and −€130m in H1 FY2026 alone, and **management has guided the Fiscal 2026 charge to approximately €300m** — a recurring, guided, budgeted line that the framework's no-add-backs rule exists precisely to keep inside operating income.

**What the alternative reading does, exactly.** On the underlying line the four normalized reads become **10.45% / 13.20% / 10.80% / 13.61%** in place of 9.34% / 11.81% / 9.78% / 12.33%. The worst read moves from 9.34% to **10.45%** — from 66bp below the P3 floor to 45bp above it. **Past would read ADEQUATE**, growth and leverage being unchanged. **The verdict would not move**: Future is WEAK on a three-strong-force tally that no operating-profit definition touches, so AVOID survives either reading. Cheapness would improve from 13.99×/12.77× to **12.10×/11.04×** EV/underlying-OP and the band from €10.07/€18.26 to **€17.41/€25.60**, moving the name from EXPENSIVE to FAIR and the distance from +212% to +122%.

**This memo therefore adds three findings to the ML.PA evidence base.**

1. **Sodexo is a third filer where the label-based and definition-based rules give opposite gate results, and it is a clean instance of the ML.PA type.** SGO.PA's *stated reason* for choosing its line was that it equalled revenue − COGS − SG&A. At Sodexo it is the ***underlying*** line that equals revenue − COGS − SG&A, and the line *labelled* "Operating profit" that carries the charges — the same inversion ML.PA logged. A label-based rule and a definition-based rule are now known to diverge on three of three French filers tested. **The convention is not cosmetic and cannot be left open much longer.**
2. **The knife edge is narrower than it looks on the rescuing side.** The alternative reading clears the floor by 45bp, and that headroom is erased by any stricter NIBCL call (below) — whereas at ML.PA the alternative cleared by 9bp and at SGO.PA the chosen line cleared by 7bp. Every French name tested so far lands within 70bp of the 10% floor on at least one reading; that is itself evidence that the choice of line is doing more work in this block than any other single judgment.
3. **Unlike ML.PA and SGO.PA, the convention here is NOT verdict-determinative** — Future carries the AVOID independently. This is the first French filer where the line can be decided at leisure without the verdict hanging on it, which makes it a useful calibration case rather than a forced one.

**NIBCL composition and the burden-of-proof call.** NIBCL = *Trade and other payables* + *Income tax payable*: €4,717m (FY2023), €4,973m (FY2024), €4,805m (FY2025). Left **inside** capital employed, per the burden-of-proof rule, are *current provisions* (€79m / €66m / €58m), *non-current provisions*, *employee benefits* (€259m FY2025) and *other non-current liabilities* (€256m FY2025) — all ambiguous, all material in aggregate, none proven to be non-interest-bearing operating liabilities. The note-level split of "trade and other payables" was not retrieved.

**The direction of this uncertainty is one-way and is the reason the call is safe.** Any stricter reading — moving part of "trade and other payables" out of NIBCL and back into capital employed — **lowers** ROCE. It cannot rescue Past on the as-reported line, and it would push the alternative underlying-line reading below 10% as well: removing just €430m from NIBCL (9% of the line) takes the underlying-line worst read from 10.45% to below 10.00%. The looser direction is not available: the whole line is already in NIBCL.

**Is ROCE flattered by an asset-light structure with client float?** This is the right question for a labour-intensive contract business and the answer is: **partly, and the framework's treatment already absorbs it.** Sodexo removes €4,805m of NIBCL from €14,849m of total assets — 32% — and runs structurally negative trade working capital (receivables €3,755m against payables €4,670m, a €915m supplier-and-client float). That float is genuine and free, and it is exactly what the with-cash / ex-cash bracket is designed to bound. Note the direction: the float **raises** the reported ROCE, and Sodexo still fails the floor. Note also the seasonality — at 28 February 2026 receivables had risen to €4,313m while payables were €4,714m, the float had compressed to €401m, and cash had fallen €915m. The Fiscal 2025 year-end capital employed is the *low* point of the year.

**Held-for-distribution treatment at 31 August 2023.** Both the €5,319m of assets and the €4,319m of liabilities held for distribution are removed in full, giving total assets of €15,475m and total liabilities of €11,921m. This is the continuing-operations construction and it is the most favourable of the three defensible treatments: leaving both in gives CE_wc of €11,758m and ROCE_wc of 7.20%; leaving the assets in but the liabilities out of NIBCL gives €16,077m and 5.27%. The chosen treatment gives 7.87%.

### Moat

**Single identified source: COST.** The mechanism is purchasing scale plus site density: aggregating roughly €21.2bn of annual cost of sales across a global network — and running it through a dedicated group purchasing organisation that Sodexo also sells to third parties — buys food, equipment and consumables on terms a single-site or regional operator cannot obtain, while multi-site density in a city or a health system lets one management and logistics structure carry many contracts. It is real: it is why three global players exist at all in an otherwise local trade.

**Direction: ERODING.** The evidence is the company's own guidance and actions, not inference. Underlying operating margin 4.7% (FY2025) → **3.2–3.4% guided for Fiscal 2026 and again for Fiscal 2027**; net new business negative in H1 Fiscal 2026; approximately €1bn of non-recurring investment plus ~€100m of incremental annualised operating cost committed through Fiscal 2030 to rebuild commercial capability; a new CEO installed on 10 November 2025 and the Chair and CEO roles split. A company spending €1bn to restore competitiveness is telling you the cost moat has been narrowing. Note that Brand, Switching costs, Network, Government and Ingrainedness were each considered and rejected: the brand does not command a price premium in tender, switching costs are precisely what the re-tender cycle destroys, there is no network effect, no licensing barrier, and the service is not ingrained in a client's own processes in the way an ERP system is.

### Market growth

**MIXED.** The long-run driver is genuine — roughly half of the addressable food-services market is still self-operated and converts slowly to outsourcing — but Sodexo is not capturing it: Fiscal 2026 organic growth is guided to **+1.2% to +1.5%**, of which management states a minimum of +2% comes from pricing, implying negative volume; nine-month Fiscal 2026 revenue of €18.2bn carried a −4.4% currency drag. Growth below food inflation, supplied by price rather than volume, is not a growing market for this operator.

**Gate 1B verdict: WEAK** — three strong unfavourable forces, an eroding moat, and one breadth leg unresolved.
