# MA — Mastercard Incorporated: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

One of the finest past records in this register — worst-of-four ROCE 42.7%, FCF converting at ~87% of operating income, per-share compounding in the high teens — attached to a genuine two-sided network moat. It is disqualified on the framework's own arithmetic: the CEO's 2025 total compensation of $35.4m breaches the $30m absolute stop (second consecutive year above it, and the March 2026 grant points higher), and the price sits at ~3.6× both primary thresholds. A flip to WATCH requires only comp reform; a flip to BUY additionally requires the price to fall roughly 75%.

## 2. Understanding

Mastercard is a large, prominent, 60-year-old, US-headquartered (Purchase, NY), NYSE-listed (2006 IPO out of a bank-owned cooperative) payment-network operator that sells differentiated transaction switching, clearing, authorisation and value-added services (security, analytics, consulting) — holding no credit risk and issuing no cards — to paying customers who are card-issuing and merchant-acquiring financial institutions (not cardholders or merchants) worldwide, with the US at ~29% of net revenue and no other single country above 10%, as one of the two dominant global card networks. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized 10y averages: ROCE 42.7% and FCFROCE 35.0% (both end-of-period, with-cash) — roughly 3–4× the P2 hurdles, with the cash-excluded variants at 65–75%. ΔOI/sh geomean 16.7%, ΔFCF/sh 18.6% — far above the 3% floor; the single down-year (2020, −15% OI/sh) recovered within one year and returns now sit at decade highs, so no sustained-decline downgrade. ΔBV/sh (+4.9%) and ΔTBV (negative since 2020) are artifacts of distributing more than earnings — itself a finding. L/E 600% breaches the P5 flag; P6 conditions applied and logged (Appendix C). **STRONG** — elite, still-rising returns on trivial capital.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: customers PASS — the 10-K's own sentence: no individual customer above 10% of net revenue in 2023–2025 (top five = 29%, flagged); suppliers PASS — fragmented technology/telecom/processing vendors, no concentration disclosed. Forces: customer power WEAK (thousands of institutions, improbable backward integration, portfolio-conversion switching costs — pressure surfaces as growing rebates, not defection); supplier power WEAK; substitutes **STRONG** (state-sponsored A2A rails — Pix, UPI, FedNow, SEPA Instant — plus wallets and stablecoins have displaced card rails at whole-geography scale); new entrants WEAK. Tally 1 strong — judgment zone, contained: cross-border and credit remain insulated and Mastercard owns A2A infrastructure (Vocalink) and joined the Open USD stablecoin consortium. Moat: network (two-sided acceptance), single source, dominant, stable — eroding only at the domestic-debit margin. Market growth YES. **ADEQUATE** (STRONG unavailable with one strong force).

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Comp: CEO Miebach's 2025 SCT total **$35,422,518 exceeds the $30m absolute stop** (2024: $30.1m; March 2026 LTI grant $29.5m target signals no retreat); the 5% leg passes ($748m). Second, marginal breach: Board Chairperson Janow ~$600k total (cash $257.5k + equity retainer) vs the $500k US stop (AAPL/Levinson precedent). RPTs immaterial. Dividends growing, ~19% payout, retention rational. **FAIL — strike: compensation.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Government-sponsored A2A rails and stablecoin settlement scale beyond domestic debit into credit and cross-border, compressing switched volume.
- Interchange/network-fee regulation (US CCCA-style routing mandates, EU caps) forces structural yield cuts.
- Issuer consolidation deepens rebate pressure; large renewals (e.g., Capital One) reprice economics.
- Comp escalation entrenches — the strike is trending away from the threshold, not toward it.
- Antitrust and merchant litigation re-opens the interchange settlement at material cost.
