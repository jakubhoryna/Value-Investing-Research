# SWEC-B.ST — Sweco AB (publ): GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS (seven material units); Past ADEQUATE (12.4% ROCE); Future WEAK (unit customer conc.); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (acquisitions); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** (Future + SF).

Sweco sells architecture and engineering design to public agencies, builders and industry across northern Europe, and grew from SEK 11bn to SEK 32bn of sales in ten years, much of it by buying smaller consultancies. Returns on capital are respectable but not high, around 12–13% on the worst reading, because goodwill now exceeds equity. Work is won in a fragmented trade, largely by public tender, and the Swedish unit depends on one government buyer for about a seventh of its sales. The 2023 deals cost far more than the acquired firms earned after the amortisation the purchase created. Price is well above range: the trigger is a third of today's price.

## 2. Understanding

Group sentence: Sweco AB is a Stockholm-based, Nasdaq Stockholm-listed consultancy (net sales SEK 31.6bn in 2025, about 23,000 experts, Europe's largest architecture and engineering consultancy by its own account, built through more than 160 acquisitions) that designs, plans and project-manages buildings and urban areas (38% of sales), water, energy and industrial plants (35%) and transport infrastructure (27%), paid by the public sector (39%), industrial companies (18%), property and construction companies (17%) and other private companies (26%) on fixed-price, time-and-material and percentage-of-construction-cost contracts, through eight country business areas.

- **Norway** (11%), **Finland** (11%; Finland and Estonia), **Denmark** (11%), **Netherlands** (11%) and **Belgium** (13%; Belgium and Luxembourg): the same services in each country, for public agencies, municipalities, developers and industry.
- **Germany & Central Europe** (9% of sales, 17% of segment assets): the same services in Germany, Poland and Lithuania, often paid as a percentage of construction cost.
- Immaterial: **UK** (5% of sales, 5% of segment assets, 3% of positive segment EBITA).

## 4. Quality — future

The Swedish Transport Administration paid SEK 1,327m in 2025, 14% of the Swedish unit's sales; no statement covers the other units, whose clients are organisations, so each carries `customer conc.`. Sweco is the largest consultancy in several markets but has peers of comparable size, and public tenders leave it no pricing power over that buyer: customer power is STRONG in every unit. Entrants are STRONG too: the filer calls the industry fragmented, and Sweco itself buys founder-built firms each year. Suppliers and substitutes are weak. No moat source is evidenced; operating margins before acquisition items sat between 8% and 10.5%. Market growth is MIXED. **WEAK**.
