INNIO is a genuinely good industrial asset attached to a genuinely bad ownership structure at an impossible price. The Jenbacher/Waukesha franchise has a 44 GW installed base and a service annuity that produced ~48% of FY2025 revenue, and the data-centre demand surge is real — Equipment revenue grew 46% in FY2025. But three years of audited history is all that exists (P1 asks ten), and within it: worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 9.1% (below the 10% ADEQUATE floor); liabilities are 21.5× book equity; tangible book is negative $2.2bn; and in the year before listing the sponsor took out $723.8m as a debt-funded capital repayment — $579.5m more than that year's net income, on the company's own disclosure — after $163.4m of dividends the year before. The IPO was 100% secondary: the company received nothing and the leverage stayed. Advent/ADIA retain 88% and Nasdaq controlled-company exemptions. Under P17's control-structure clause that pairing — control plus value-destructive allocation — is the strike. Nothing here flips on price: at 74× EV/OI the EV/OI ≤7 line is unreachable at *any* positive share price, because 7× FY2025 operating income is smaller than the debt itself.

## 2. Understanding

**PASS.** INNIO N.V. designs, manufactures and services differentiated reciprocating gas engines, gensets and containerised power systems under the Jenbacher and Waukesha brands (Equipment segment), and sells the multi-decade aftermarket that follows them — long-term service agreements, spare parts, overhauls, remanufacturing and digital monitoring (Services segment) — as a vertically integrated Dutch-domiciled, Munich-headquartered manufacturer with plants in Austria, the US and Canada, 88%-controlled by an Advent/ADIA vehicle, selling largely through distributors and EaaS integrators (Clarke Energy, VoltaGrid) who are the paying accounts on behalf of data-centre operators, industrial and utility power producers, gas-compression operators and biogas/CHP plant owners, carved out of GE in 2018 and listed in June 2026 at ~$2.6bn revenue.

## 3. Quality — past record

Three audited fiscal years only (FY2023–25) against P1's ten; two balance-sheet years, so beginning-of-period returns exist for FY2025 alone. **STRONG is unavailable by construction.** Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **9.1%** (end-period, with cash) — below P3's 10% floor; range to 12.3%. FCFROCE worst-of-four 9.6%, above P2's 8%, but flattered: FY2025 CFO of $547.9m contains a $506.1m contract-liability inflow (customer prepayments on the data-centre backlog); ex-swing, FY2025 FCF is **−$129.4m**. OI/share compounded 20.5% (shares flat at 25,000 retrospectively), but BV/share compounded **−49.5%** as the sponsor extracted capital. L/E **2,146%** at FY2025 (2,252% at Q1'26) — 10.7× the P5 flag and 3.1× the P6 hard cap, and P6's conditions plainly fail. **WEAK** — returns below the ADEQUATE floor and a leverage breach beyond the hard cap.

## 4. Quality — future

**Breadth FAIL.** Customer side: top-five 34% of FY2025 revenue rising to 39% in Q1'26; Clarke Energy alone 15.0% / 10.5% / 11.8% across FY2025/24/23 — above P15 in every year — and VoltaGrid was ~41% of FY2025 Equipment Order Intake, so the backlog underwriting the story is one account. The captive-customer waiver fails: these are channel partners and EaaS integrators who can and do switch OEM, and buyers are numerous — the concentration is commercial, not structural. Supplier side: the company discloses reliance on "a limited number of suppliers, and in some instances, a single supplier" for critical components, with those suppliers "heavily engaged with our competitors" — not demonstrated, no ready-alternatives evidence.

**Forces: 3 STRONG** — customer bargaining power (rising concentration, sophisticated buyers running competitive procurements); supplier bargaining power (single-sourced castings, generators, heat exchangers; documented copper and magnetic-steel shortages that delayed deliveries); substitutes (solar-plus-storage, fuel cells, gas turbines, grid interconnection — the prospectus's own first-listed risk). Only new entrants is WEAK. **Moat:** switching costs on the 44 GW installed base, stable — real, but confined to the aftermarket. Market growth YES. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**FAIL — hard strike: control structure paired with value-destructive allocation (P17 clause).** Advent/ADIA hold 88% post-IPO with board-nomination and board-resolution veto rights under the Relationship Agreement, and INNIO relies on Nasdaq controlled-company exemptions (no requirement for a majority-independent board or independent compensation and nominating committees). The paired allocation: $887.2m extracted to the sponsor across FY2024–25 (a $163.4m dividend, then a $723.8m capital repayment funded by $785.2m of new borrowing) against $233.8m of combined net income, taking equity to $228m, doubling interest expense to a $70.8m Q1'26 run-rate and pushing Q1'26 pre-tax income to −$4.5m — while management simultaneously describes an "elevated investment phase" to triple capacity. Comp is clean on the numbers: Berlien's FY2025 total $2.175m = 1.53% of net income (P17 strike line 5%, ≈$7.09m) and far below the ≈$40m indexed trigger; top outside director $231k against a ≈$672k stop. Buybacks n/a (no history). Additional flag, not needed for the verdict: $4.0m of one-time IPO inducement RSUs to three incoming non-executive directors ($2.0m to Mr. Linebarger alone) — named FY2026 re-test.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- The data-centre order book is concentrated in one EaaS integrator (VoltaGrid ~41% of FY2025 Equipment Order Intake) whose own funding and deployment schedule INNIO does not control.
- FY2025 cash generation is dominated by customer prepayments; if order intake flattens, the contract-liability tailwind reverses into a headwind while capex stays elevated.
- ~$2.6bn of floating-rate term debt against $228m of equity leaves no absorption capacity for a demand pause; interest expense already exceeds the Q1'26 operating result net of tax.
- The 180-day lock-up expires around early December 2026 with 660m sponsor shares becoming saleable — a structural supply overhang independent of fundamentals.
- Gas engines are a bridge technology; a faster-than-expected fall in grid-scale storage cost or a turbine capacity release would compress the scarcity premium the price embeds.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

**To ADEQUATE past:** worst-of-four ROCE through 10% *and* deleveraging that brings L/E inside 700% — arithmetically that needs several years of retained earnings with no further distributions, since the IPO added no equity. **To ADEQUATE future:** Clarke Energy and VoltaGrid both below 10% of revenue and of order intake respectively, with the supplier position quantified. **To SF PASS:** the strike stands while the control structure and the recapitalisation sit in the window; the credible path is sponsor sell-down below control combined with a full public-company governance set and a documented capital-allocation policy — not a price move. **Cheapness** is moot until the first two clear.

**Next review:** FY2026 10-K and the first DEF 14A (~Feb–Mar 2027) — first audited public-company year, first live P17/P19 test on the 2026 Plan grants, and the FY2026 capex print that decides P20 limb (i).
