# OR.PA — L'Oréal S.A.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past ADEQUATE; Future ADEQUATE; Shareholder-friendliness **FAIL**; Cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID**, on the shareholder-friendliness gate.

An excellent operating business attached to a repurchase record the framework cannot pass. The franchise is intact: worst-of-four ROCE 16.6% and FCFROCE 14.5%, operating income per share compounding at 7.9% and free cash flow per share at 9.4%, gross margin up 310bp since FY2015, modest leverage. What stands between here and a WATCH is not price. L'Oréal bought its own stock in seven of the ten window years at implied enterprise multiples of 22× to 40× operating income — including €8.9bn from Nestlé at €400 in December 2021, roughly four times the level the framework calls cheap. Unusual it is not; but the model scores it as value transfer, and it strikes on both limbs, seven times over.

## 2. Understanding

L'Oréal manufactures and sells differentiated branded cosmetics — skincare, haircare, makeup, fragrance and dermatological skin treatments — under owned and licensed brands, paid for by mass retailers, e-commerce platforms, pharmacies, hair salons, department stores and travel-retail operators as well as by consumers directly; it is a vertically integrated pure-play beauty group running its own research network and manufacturing base, organised in four divisions (Consumer Products, Luxe, Dermatological Beauty, Professional Products), selling worldwide from a French head office, family-influenced through the Bettencourt Meyers holding, and the largest beauty company on earth by revenue. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE 16.6% and FCFROCE 14.5% (FY2016–FY2025) clear the STRONG bars, per-share operating income and free cash flow compound at 7.9% and 9.4% against a 3% floor, and liabilities-to-equity ends at 77%. Level alone reads STRONG. But ROCE has now fallen three consecutive years on three of the four denominators — 20.2% to 16.8% end-of-period with cash — which forces the one-tier downgrade. The cause is denominator, not deterioration: acquisition goodwill plus a debt-funded cash build to €9.9bn. Ex-cash capital employed actually shrank in FY2025 and that read recovered. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

No customer reaches a tenth of revenue (absence of an IFRS 8 major-customer note is affirmative here) and the physical supplier base is fragmented against a self-manufacturing buyer; breadth PASSES both sides. Forces: customer power WEAK (brand pull makes the range must-stock), supplier power WEAK (input fragmentation, and gross margin has more than absorbed the 230bp rise in advertising intensity), substitutes WEAK (aesthetics is empirically complementary — Dermatological Beauty is the fastest-growing, highest-margin division through the injectables boom), new entrants **STRONG** (social distribution plus contract manufacturing has collapsed entry cost; buying the winners at 15×+ is the tell). One strong force, contained. Moat: brand, stable. Market growth YES. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation is restrained — the CEO's €12.0m total is 0.2% of the lower of free cash flow and net income against a 5% cap, clean on both reads; top outside director €135,100; chairman €950,000. Related-party dealings are trivial (€10.8m). Dividends are rational: €7.20 a share, 63% of diluted earnings, from a mature high-return business. The failure is entirely P19. Seven sourced tranche-years — FY2017, 2019, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 — each breach 20× on both reads of enterprise value to operating income, none within rescue distance, and the FY2023 Aesop purchase adds an independent flag. **FAIL** (P19 repetition and egregious-single-year, both).

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- The AVOID rests on capital allocation, not business quality — reject the buyback arithmetic and what remains is a high-return compounder at a full price.
- The P4 downgrade is denominator-driven and could reverse in FY2026 if the cash is deployed, restoring Past to STRONG.
- Galderma (20% for €4.2bn) and Kering Beauté/Creed are large, recent and untested here; both land in the FY2026 audit.
- Entry barriers are falling faster than the moat call credits, and the answer has been to buy brands at multiples that themselves flag.
- China and travel retail remain the Luxe swing factor; a second weak year pressures the division carrying the most goodwill.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To WATCH: strike years ageing out is not enough on its own, since FY2022–FY2025 strike independently — the route is a worth-referenced repurchase policy plus a demonstrated below-band or refusal year and allocator turnover. To worse: a third strong force, or a fourth straight ROCE decline taking Past to WEAK. Price is not a flip condition — a gate failure is not cured by a lower quote, and at 74% below spot the band is not a live alert. Next review: FY2026 annual report (~March 2027) — re-test the P4 count, audit Galderma and Creed, re-read the FY2026 tranche.

## C. Calculation notes

**Operating income basis — judgment call.** L'Oréal prints two subtotals on the face of the income statement: *Operating profit* (FY2025 €8,891.9m), struck before *Other income and expenses*, and *Operational profit* (€8,386.5m), struck after it. Other income and expenses carries impairments, restructuring and identified non-recurring items — exactly the add-backs §2·1A forbids. **OI is therefore Operational profit throughout.** Effect: worst-of-four ROCE 16.6% rather than 17.8%; the alternate is logged and does not change the Past tier. Note the segment table reconciles to the higher figure (divisional €9,993.8m less non-allocated €1,101.8m), so the segment note cannot be used as an OI source directly.

**Non-current financial assets (Sanofi/Galderma) — judgment call.** €12,867.3m at FY2025-end, 21% of total assets, held at fair value through OCI; the associated dividends (€351.9m) sit below operating profit while the asset sits inside capital employed. This is the asymmetry the captive-finance consistency rule exists to police. **Primary read is literal** — stake in CE, dividends out of OI — because §2·1A removes only cash and short-term investments for CE_xc and these are neither. Both symmetric alternates were computed and logged: excluding the stake from CE lifts worst-of-four ROCE to 23.9%; including the dividends in OI while keeping the stake gives 17.7%. Neither moves the Past tier, and the literal read is the conservative one.

**NIBCL composition.** Trade accounts payable + Other current liabilities + current income tax. Other current liabilities is substantially accrual in nature (tax and employee-related payables, credit balances on trade receivables, fixed-asset payables, prepaid income); the only ambiguous component is derivatives at €172.2m, 0.3% of total assets, immaterial and included. **Current provisions for liabilities and charges (€1,117.7m) are left in capital employed** under the burden-of-proof rule — they are ambiguous, and leaving them in is the conservative direction. Short-term debt and current lease debt stay in CE throughout.

**Leases.** IFRS 16 adopted 1 January 2019. Pre-adoption years were tested under P18 and **do not require capitalisation**: undiscounted operating-lease commitments were €2,697.2m/8.0% of total assets (FY2015), €2,840.4m/8.0% (FY2016), €2,385.0m/6.7% (FY2017) and €2,582.1m/6.7% (FY2018), all below the 10% trigger. Adoption-year artifact flagged: ~€2.16bn of lease debt arrived on the FY2019 balance sheet, and from FY2019 lease principal repayments (~€400–450m a year) sit in financing, so CFO and therefore FCF step up at 2019 on a basis break rather than an economic one. Lease liabilities are treated as debt-equivalent in CE, EV and L/E throughout.

**IFRS interest.** No reclassification required. L'Oréal's financing section carries no interest-paid line; interest flows through net profit into gross cash flow, so it is already inside CFO. Lease interest (€58.4m FY2025) likewise sits in operating — reconciled: total lease cash outflow €581.2m less €58.4m interest less €453.6m financing-section principal leaves €69.2m of short-term and low-value lease payments in operating.

**Comparability.** FY2015–FY2017 figures are taken on the IFRS 5 restated continuing-operations basis (The Body Shop, sold 2017, presented as discontinued), which is the basis consistent with FY2018 onward. FY2017 balance sheet is the IFRS 9 restated version from the FY2019 document.

**P20 not active.** No documented capex super-cycle; capex has run a stable 3.4–4.9% of sales across the window. FCF is all-capex throughout, in the quality gates and the valuation gate alike.

**Bias check.** Two live and handled. **Peculiarity/authority** — L'Oréal's reputation as a quality compounder creates pressure to find a way past the P19 record; the arithmetic was run on all ten window years before the verdict was written, and the strike count (seven) is far beyond any single-year judgment call. **Cleverness** — the temptation to rescue the FY2025 ROCE decline by declaring the €9.9bn cash pile "excess" and reading only ex-cash was resisted; the framework brackets the cash judgment rather than resolving it, and the P4 count was taken across all four reads, not the flattering one. Neither judgment is born of the bias.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: PASS.** No major-customer disclosure appears in the IFRS 8 segment note (note 3.1). Under IFRS 8.34 disclosure is mandatory where a single external customer reaches 10% of revenue, so in an IFRS filing the absence is affirmative evidence rather than mere silence — this is the distinction from a US filer, where absence would be weaker. Corroborating: the trade-receivables note reports the allowance at under 1% of gross receivables with third-party insurance cover across most subsidiaries, consistent with a dispersed book. A 10% customer would be €4.4bn of revenue.

**Breadth — suppliers: PASS (look-through judgment, logged).** No supplier-concentration figures are disclosed. Cost base is COGS €11,313.4m + advertising and promotion €14,177.8m + SG&A €8,288.3m + R&I €1,380.6m = €35,160.1m; a 10% supplier would be €3,516m. Physical inputs (packaging, chemicals, contract manufacture) are fragmented against a buyer that manufactures most of its own product — 1,039 suppliers completed EcoVadis assessments in 2025, and "strategic suppliers" is a defined subset of that. The look-through candidate is digital media: the effective supplier for demand generation is a three-to-four platform oligopoly. Advertising and promotion is €14.2bn, but that includes trade promotion, samples and point-of-sale as well as media, and no single platform plausibly reaches €3.5bn of L'Oréal spend. Passed, with the media-oligopoly dependence recorded as the item to re-test if A&P intensity keeps climbing.

Tally 1 strong, contained → judgment zone, not the book's stop signal.

**Moat.** Single identified source: **brand** — a portfolio of price-point-segmented brands whose consumer pull compels retail distribution, reinforced (but not sourced) by scale in research and media buying. Direction **stable**: share and margin are holding, which rules out eroding; the need to keep buying entrants rules out strengthening.

**Market growth: YES.** Global beauty compounds mid-single-digit through premiumisation, emerging-market penetration and dermatological skincare; L'Oréal has grown ahead of it.

**Verdict ADEQUATE** — breadth PASS, one strong force contained, plausible stable moat. STRONG is unavailable with a strong force on the board.
