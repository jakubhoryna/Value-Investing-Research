# AMZN — Amazon.com, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past ADEQUATE; Future ADEQUATE; shareholder-friendliness PASS (one flag — capital allocation CAUTION); cheapness EXPENSIVE — final verdict **WATCH**.

Amazon is worth owning for what the retail business obscures: a cloud franchise generating 58% of group operating income at a 37% segment margin, re-accelerating to 37% revenue growth, sitting on a marketplace that throws off advertising economics. Trailing operating income is $93.7bn and returns on capital employed are at decade highs. Only price stands between here and a BUY — $256.78 against a band near $31–42, a gap nothing plausible closes. The gates are secure but not distinguished: the ten-year record still carries Amazon's low-margin adolescence, and the capital-allocation call is genuinely open now that a roughly $200bn capex programme has pushed free cash flow negative and is being funded with debt rather than cash generation.

## 2. Understanding

Amazon.com is a US-headquartered, vertically integrated operator of three businesses — a first-party and third-party online and physical retail marketplace with its own fulfilment and delivery network, a cloud computing utility (AWS) selling compute, storage and AI services to developers and enterprises worldwide, and an advertising business monetising the retail traffic — earning money from consumers, third-party sellers, enterprises, advertisers and subscribers across North America, Europe and Asia. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized average ROCE is 11.1% over FY2016–FY2025, best 20.7%; worst-of-four FCFROCE is 6.3%. Growth is emphatic: operating income per fully diluted share compounded 41.2% annually over the decade, book value 39.1%, tangible book 42.9%. Leverage is comfortable at 0.99× liabilities-to-equity. The compression in FCF-return reflects two successive investment cycles — fulfilment, then AI — rather than operating decay: ROCE itself is at a decade high on the beginning-of-period read, and operating margin has widened from 3.1% to 11.2%. **ADEQUATE**, with STRONG blocked by the FCF-return leg.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both sides: no customer nears a tenth of revenue, and supplier concentration sits in capex — AI accelerators, memory — rather than COGS, with the ready-alternatives exception logged, since Amazon's own Trainium silicon is past a $25bn run rate and growing triple digits. Forces: customer WEAK, substitutes WEAK, new entrants WEAK, supplier **STRONG** — Nvidia pricing, named memory scarcity, data-centre power, and 1.6m staff in tight labour markets. One strong force, contained. The moat is switching costs at AWS — data gravity, committed spend, custom silicon — and strengthening; the marketplace network carries Stores and is stable. Market growth YES. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

No equity was granted to any named executive in 2025 and the CEO has had none since 2021; Jassy's reported total of $2.1m sits far inside both proportionality reads and nowhere near the roughly $40m absolute trigger. Realized compensation from prior-year vesting was not retrieved — logged INDETERMINATE. Director pay is restricted stock at historically modest levels. The Blue Origin launch contracts are a real related-party exposure but were competitively awarded alongside two unaffiliated providers, independently approved with Bezos recused, and immaterial against group operating expense — logged, not flagged. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**. **PASS, one flag.**

- AWS's re-acceleration leans on a few very large AI labs whose funding and compute strategies can change quickly.
- Reported net income is now dominated by non-operating marks — $53.4bn pre-tax in Q2 2026 — which flatter earnings without adding operating cash.
- Antitrust and consumer-protection proceedings across the US and EU remain live and unpredictable in remedy.

## C. Calculation notes

**Latest-year CE build (FY2025).** Total assets 818,042 − NIBCL 218,005 = **CE_wc 600,037**; less cash & short-term investments 123,029 = **CE_xc 477,008**.

**NIBCL judgment call (§2·1A burden of proof).** Amazon's balance sheet presents only three current-liability lines. Short-term debt, the current portion of long-term debt (2,748 at FY2025 year-end, from the Q1 2026 debt note) and current lease liabilities are embedded within "accrued expenses and other" and are not separately disclosed on the face. §2·1A requires those to stay in capital employed. Leaving them inside NIBCL understates CE by an estimated $16–19bn, biasing ROCE **up** by roughly 0.4 percentage points on a $600bn base. The direction is against conservatism and is disclosed rather than corrected, because correcting it would require estimating an undisclosed input. It does not move any gate.

**Lease treatment (P18).** FY2016–FY2018 precede ASC 842 and are not lease-capitalised in the tables above; undiscounted operating-lease commitments in those years exceeded the 10%-of-assets trigger. Rather than estimate the commitment schedules, the effect was bounded: pushing the worst-of-four normalized ROCE from 11.08% below the 10% P3 floor would require adding **$273,482m** of capital employed across the window — about $91bn per year if loaded entirely onto FY2016–FY2018. Sensitivity: capitalising $15/20/25bn gives 10.82%; $20/25/30bn gives 10.76%; $30/40/50bn gives 10.58%. Amazon's actual commitments in those years were an order of magnitude below the level required. **P18 cannot flip Past from ADEQUATE to WEAK**, and the gate is therefore robust to the gap.

**P4 judgment call — the decisive one for Past.** FCFROCE has fallen from roughly 25–30% across FY2016–FY2020 to 2.5% in FY2025, and is negative on a trailing-twelve-month basis. Read alone that resembles the "sustained multi-year decline" that P4 converts into WEAK. It is not applied here, for three reasons stated for the record. First, the decline is confined to the FCF-return measure; ROCE itself — the operating-return measure — is at a decade high on the beginning-of-period read (18.0% in FY2025 against 13.6% in FY2016), and operating margin has expanded from 3.1% to 11.2%. Second, the series is cyclical rather than secular: it troughed in FY2021–FY2022 on the fulfilment build, recovered to 12.0% and 10.5% in FY2023–FY2024, and fell again on the AI build — two investment cycles, not a trend. Third, the ten-year normalized worst-of-four of 6.3% **already embeds** both build-outs, and is precisely why STRONG is unavailable; applying P4 as well would penalise the same fact twice, which P20 expressly anticipates when it holds the quality gates on all-capex FCF because "multiyear averaging already dilutes the build-out". Two sessions disagreeing on Past should locate the disagreement in this paragraph.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers.** PASS. Revenue is spread across hundreds of millions of consumer accounts, millions of third-party sellers, hundreds of thousands of AWS accounts and a large advertiser base. The FY2025 10-K carries no customer-concentration disclosure because none is required; here the absence reflects genuine atomisation rather than a missing test, evidenced by the segment and revenue-line disclosures (largest single revenue line, Online Stores, is $269bn of $717bn and is itself millions of transactions).

**Breadth — suppliers.** PASS with the P15 ready-alternatives exception logged. No supplier approaches 10% of the $636,949m COGS-plus-opex base. The real concentration is at the capex layer: the FY2025 10-K states Amazon relies "on a limited group of suppliers for semiconductor products, including products related to artificial intelligence infrastructure such as graphics processing units", and the Q4 2025 forward-looking language names memory chips as a supply constraint. Looking through to the effective supplier, this is the Nvidia and advanced-foundry ecosystem. The exception applies because the concentration is deliberate, several vendors stand ready (AMD, in-house silicon), and Amazon is actively dis-intermediating it — Trainium and Graviton passed a combined $10bn run rate at FY2025 and the AI-chips business exceeded a $25bn run rate by Q2 2026, growing triple digits. Logged, not waived: this is the exception, not the captive-customer waiver, so it does not cap Future.

**Four forces (binary).**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | WEAK | Atomised consumer and seller base; AWS committed-spend contracts and re-architecture costs; no buyer moves group pricing |
| Supplier bargaining power | **STRONG** | AI accelerators, memory, data-centre power and a 1.6m-person workforce in constrained labour markets all price against Amazon at ~$200bn annual capex |
| Threat of substitutes | WEAK | Doing-without is not realistic in either unit; physical retail substitution is receding, and cloud alternatives are competitors within the same function rather than substitutes |
| Threat of new entrants | WEAK | Entry barriers (742m sq ft of leased fulfilment and data-centre space, $200bn annual capex) and success barriers (Prime ingrainedness, three decades of scale) are both extreme |

**Tally: 1 strong** — the judgment zone, not the stop signal. Containment evidence: the vertical-integration programme is measurable and growing triple digits, and AWS segment margin held at 36.8% on a trailing basis through the ramp, so supplier pricing has not yet reached the operating line.

**Segment-level calls.** AWS: customer WEAK, supplier STRONG, substitutes WEAK, entrants WEAK. Stores (North America and International): customer WEAK, supplier WEAK, substitutes WEAK, entrants WEAK. The strong force is AWS-specific and the group call inherits it because AWS is the dominant profit unit.

**Moat.** Single source, dominant profit unit: **switching costs at AWS** — data gravity, multi-year committed-spend agreements, and the re-architecture cost of moving production workloads, now deepened by custom silicon (Trainium, Graviton used by 98% of the top 1,000 EC2 customers) and by Bedrock's model catalogue. **Strengthening**: AWS revenue growth re-accelerated from 17% to 37% across five quarters, the fastest in eighteen quarters, while segment margin held. Second unit: Stores rests on the **network** effect of the marketplace (more sellers → more selection → more buyers), monetised through third-party seller services ($172bn) and advertising ($68.6bn, +26%); **stable**.

**Market growth.** YES — cloud and AI infrastructure, advertising, and continued e-commerce share gain. Guarded against late-pipeline glow: the growth call rests on reported segment revenue acceleration, not on narrative.

**Verdict ADEQUATE** — breadth PASS, one contained strong force, a strengthening moat on the dominant unit. STRONG is blocked by the supplier force alone.
