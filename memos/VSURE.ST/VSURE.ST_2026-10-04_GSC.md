# VSURE.ST — Verisure plc: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future ADEQUATE, shareholder-friendliness FAIL, cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID on two gates (Past, SF).**

Verisure runs a genuinely sticky subscription base: 6.4 million monitored homes and small businesses, attrition steady near 7.4%, and a portfolio segment earning a 74% EBITDA margin. The entity an owner buys is a different matter. The 2020 change of control put about €13bn of goodwill and customer-portfolio intangibles on the balance sheet, so the five-year record returns about 1.4% pre-tax on capital employed, and levered free cash flow was negative every year. Pay is the second problem: the CEO's 2025 package of €11.4m was earned against negative net income and negative free cash flow. The price is about 45× operating income. A flip would need several years of positive owner earnings large enough to lift returns on this capital base, and pay sized against them.

## 2. Understanding
Verisure designs, installs and remotely monitors intruder and fire alarm systems with guard-response services, sold as monthly subscriptions mainly to households and small businesses who pay for them; it is a monitored-security services business, vertically integrated from product design to alarm-receiving centres and field installers, operating in 18 countries across Europe and Latin America with head office finance in Malmö and a UK plc parent, and it is the European market leader, a Hellman & Friedman-controlled company listed in Stockholm since October 2025. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **1.4%**, FCFROCE **−1.0%**, FY2021–FY2025. Capital employed is about €15.4bn, of which about €13.2bn is goodwill and intangibles. Most of that was recognised at the December 2020 combination. Operating income rose from €130.9m to €298.7m; levered FCF was negative in every year (cumulative −€732m). Book value per share fell until the October 2025 €3.1bn primary raise; tangible book is negative throughout. L/E 85.7% after the raise. Two other readings stay below 10%: the Midholding predecessor basis averages 6.1%, and deducting all 2020 acquisition intangibles gives 6.6%. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth **PASS**: average revenue per subscriber is about €48 a month, so no customer can approach a tenth of revenue. Supplier concentration is not disclosed; the gap is logged. Forces: customers WEAK (millions of households, no collective leverage), suppliers WEAK (contract-manufactured electronics, own designs), substitutes STRONG (self-installed cameras and apps, and doing without), new entrants WEAK (response network density, licensed alarm-receiving centres, a company-claimed fivefold scale lead). One strong force, contained. Moat: cost/scale of the monitoring and response network, stable — though ten-year ARPU growth of about 2% a year shows little pricing power. Market growth YES. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
**FAIL — P17 hard strike.** CEO Austin Lally's 2025 full-year single figure was €11.38m, including €8.4m of one-off IPO restricted share units. Both owner-earnings legs were negative in the fiscal year (FCF −€74.5m, NI −€255.9m) and on the three-year read. Even the €3.0m fixed pay and bonus alone is more than token. Non-executive fees are modest (top €195k). RPT: Genesys services €2.7m, immaterial. Capital allocation **CAUTION**: no buybacks; the IPO raise deleveraged the balance sheet; a first €0.10 dividend (≈€103.6m) began before levered FCF turned positive over a full year. Hellman & Friedman holds 43.7% of votes.
