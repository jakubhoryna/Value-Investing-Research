# HHH — Howard Hughes Holdings: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | SF | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | **WEAK** | **WEAK** | **FAIL** | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** (triple gate) |

HHH is an asset story, not a business-quality story: a superb, irreplaceable land estate that has produced ~3% ROCE and cumulative **negative** free cash flow for a decade, now being rebuilt by Pershing Square (46.9%) into a Berkshire-style holding company. The model judges the record, not the narrative: Past fails on every metric, breadth fails on the homebuilder channel, and SF fails on two independent hard strikes (P17 comp, P19 2022 buyback), with the Pershing fee agreement and $1B related-party preferred logged as flags. The only credible path to a different verdict is years of Vantage underwriting record maturing HHH into a substitution-rule holdco (BRK.B treatment) — a different company, judged when it exists.

## 2. Understanding

HHH sells developed residential land (superpads and finished lots) to national homebuilders and condominium units to individual buyers inside master-planned communities it owns (Summerlin, The Woodlands, Bridgeland, Ward Village, Columbia, Teravalis), collects rent on ~9.3M sq ft of commercial space and 5,855 apartments built on that land, and — since 4 June 2026 — owns Vantage, a Bermuda specialty insurer/reinsurer; a 2010 GGP spin-off, HQ The Woodlands (TX), operating in NV/TX/HI/MD/AZ, reorganised 2025–26 into a Pershing Square-directed diversified holding company. **PASS** — complex but fully describable.

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four 10y normalized ROCE **3.2%** (all four variants 3.2–3.7% vs P3 ≥ 10%); FCFROCE **negative all four ways** (−2.9% to −3.4%; Σ10y FCF −$2.26B — MPC and condo development consumes CFO plus $216–730M/yr of investing capex). ΔOI/sh -2.8%/yr geometric; ΔBV/sh +1.3%/yr — both below the 3% floor. L/E 180% latest (breached P5 at 218%/229% in 2023–24; no P6 case). **WEAK** — misses ADEQUATE by a factor of three on returns.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: condo/rental customers broad, but the MPC profit engine (record $476M EBT 2025) sells to a concentrated, consolidating set of national homebuilders; the 10-K states the segment is "highly dependent on homebuilders" and carries **no** no-customer->10% sentence — burden unmet, and no waiver (homebuilders demonstrably self-develop land, so backward integration is live). **Breadth FAIL.** Forces: customer power STRONG (few large buyers, credible open-market alternatives); supplier, substitutes, entrants WEAK — the 20k+-acre entitled land bank is a genuine Government-source moat, stable. Market growth YES (Sun Belt). Tally 1 strong, but breadth FAIL forces **WEAK**.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**P17 hard strike:** CEO O'Reilly FY2025 total $7.83M > 5% of lower(FCF $246.6M, NI $123.9M) = $6.20M; repeats FY2024 on the FCF leg ($5.72M > $4.79M). **P19 hard strike:** FY2022 buyback $403.9M at implied EV/OI 20.1–21.1× and MCap/FCF ≈ 315–345× (FCF ≈ $14M); the below-worth defence rests on management NAV claims, not demonstrable conservative worth. Flags logged: Pershing services agreement ($15M/yr base + 1.5% p.a. of market-cap gain above an inflation-adjusted reference, paid to the 46.9% holder); $1B perpetual preferred placed with Pershing to fund Vantage; Vantage audit — $2.1B ≈ 1.5× YE2025 book for a 2020-founded insurer, full price on thin history (judgmental, insurer basis). **FAIL** — two hard strikes.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Housing downturn: land sales and condo closings, the FCF engine, are demand- and rate-cyclical.
- Leverage: ≈ $5.8B debt + $1.0B preferred against lumpy development cash flows.
- Holdco execution: capital now flows to acquisitions (Vantage $2.1B) judged by Pershing, with a fee structure that pays on market-cap appreciation.
- Insurance risk newly added: a five-year-old specialty book (2025 California wildfire losses) inside a levered developer.
- Governance: 46.9% holder is also the paid external manager; minority alignment depends on the standstill terms holding.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Past cannot flip on near-term data (10-year averages). The realistic path is transformation: if Vantage plus further deals make HHH an insurance-dominated holdco, the substitution rule (ROE, P/B anchor — BRK.B precedent) re-frames the test on a record that does not yet exist. P17 clears only if NI scales well past ≈ $160M with comp flat; the P19 2022 strike is never averaged away. Next review: FY2026 10-K (~Feb 2027) — first Vantage-consolidated annuals; re-test substitution applicability, RPT development, and the preferred's terms.
