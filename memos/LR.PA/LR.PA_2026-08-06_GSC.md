# LR.PA — Legrand SA: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS · Past ADEQUATE · Future STRONG · SF PASS (one flag) · Cheapness EXPENSIVE — **WATCH**.

What is worth owning is the electrician's habit: eighty local brands specified by tradespeople who learned them, stocked because installers ask for them, replaced in kind decades later — a reported margin never below 17.5% through a decade of acquisition. Between here and a BUY sit price and the balance sheet: net debt €5,755m at 30 June 2026, +74.7% y/y, funding a run that included Avtron at $1,125m, all inside the EV the trigger leg divides. BUY needs a fall of 81–87%. The gate likeliest to move is Past, at 11.62% against a 10% floor.

## 2. Understanding

Legrand designs and manufactures low-voltage electrical and digital building infrastructure — wiring devices, cable management, circuit protection and distribution boards, busways, rack power distribution, UPS, connected-building controls — sold under roughly eighty local brands principally to generalist and specialist electrical distributors, who pay and resell to the electricians and integrators installing it in residential, commercial and industrial buildings and, increasingly, datacenters; a French-headquartered, vertically integrated, serially acquisitive Euronext-listed manufacturer, 2025 sales €9.48bn (N&C America 42.2%, Europe 38.0%, RoW 19.8%), ~39,600 employees, datacenters 26% of sales. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **11.62%** (best read 15.25%), FCFROCE **9.05%** — above the ADEQUATE floors, short of the 15% STRONG line. Shape matters more than level: beginning-CE returns stepped down from 13.9–14.7% in FY2016–17 to a 10.2% trough in FY2020 and have recovered only to 12.8%, capital employed having grown faster than operating income on goodwill-heavy acquisition. No P4 breach — beginning-CE ROCE has risen three years running. Per-share growth clears the 3% floor: OI/sh +7.7% p.a., FCF/sh +8.0% p.a. over nine years. L/E 140.6%, under the flag but up from 99.7% in FY2016. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS both legs: no individual customer above 10% of net sales (stated), and raw materials and components (~69% of cost of sales) bought centrally on world markets from a fragmented base with no disclosed dependency. Four forces all **WEAK** — distributors are numerous, cannot backward-integrate and stock what installers pull; suppliers sell commoditised copper, zinc, plastics and electronics; buildings cannot do without electrical infrastructure and code-driven substitution moves at decade pace; entry needs per-country certification, shelf space and installer familiarity money does not buy. Tally 0 strong. Moat: **ingrainedness** — brand for the channel — stable, reinforced in datacenters where Starline, Raritan, ZPE and Avtron are specified by engineers, not chosen at a counter. Market growth YES. **STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation clears both P17 reads by an order of magnitude: CEO Coquart's 2025 total €3.39m against 5% of the lower of FCF and net income (€62.2m year, €59.3m trailing-3y) and a €34.7m indexed trigger; Chair €625k and top ordinary director €149.5k sit inside seat calibrations. Related-party disclosure is IAS 24 key-management pay only. Capital allocation **CAUTION** (one flag): no year approaches the ≈25× calibration and live activity is employee-plan delivery alone (€0.7m net, H1-2026), but the 2025 tranche at 19.0–22.7× is far above conservative worth and the new €2bn/10% mandate carries no price criterion. **PASS** — one flag, no pair.

## C. Calculation notes

**NIBCL composition and burden-of-proof calls.** NIBCL = trade payables + income tax payables + other current liabilities. Other current liabilities (note 4.8) is entirely non-interest-bearing accrual in character — FY2025: tax other than income tax 107.6, accrued employee benefits 402.4, statutory/discretionary profit-sharing 29.3, payables on fixed-asset purchases 41.6, accrued expenses 315.4, accrued interest 55.9, deferred revenue 69.7, other 36.4 = 1,058.3 — and is deducted in full. **Short-term provisions (FY2025: 162.1) are ambiguous and are therefore left in capital employed**, per the §2·1A burden-of-proof rule; deducting them would raise FY2025 ROCE_wc from 11.73% to 11.85% and would not move any verdict. Short-term borrowings stay in CE by rule.

**Latest-year CE build (FY2025, €m).** Total assets 17,592.8 − NIBCL (1,064.0 + 55.3 + 1,058.3 = 2,177.6) = **CE_wc 15,415.2**. Less cash & equivalents 2,381.2 (no short-term investments on the face; "other current financial assets" 0.8 are derivatives, not STI, and are left in) = **CE_xc 13,034.0**.

**TBV build (FY2025, €m).** Equity attributable to Legrand 7,294.2 − goodwill 7,629.7 − intangible assets 2,587.3 = **−2,922.8**. At 30 June 2026: 7,493.9 − 8,699.1 − 2,931.4 = **−4,136.6**. **EV build at 30 June 2026 (€m), both variants.** Market capitalisation 138.875 × 266,435,233 = 37,001.2 · plus long-term borrowings 6,711.0 · plus short-term borrowings 1,013.4 · plus minority interests 38.1 · preferred nil = **EV no-cash 44,763.7**; less cash & equivalents 1,969.1 = **EV all-cash 42,794.6**.

**Goodwill treatment.** Goodwill stays in capital employed. Legrand is the opposite of a non-acquisitive company — goodwill has grown from €2,776.3m (FY2015) to €8,699.1m (30 Jun 2026) — so the optional deduction is not available and would in any case flatter the very returns dilution the record is showing.

**Leases.** FY2019 onward the reported balance sheet capitalises under IFRS 16 and lease financial liabilities (FY2025 long-term 344.5 plus the short-term portion inside short-term borrowings) are already inside total borrowings, hence inside CE, EV and L/E as the framework requires. FY2016–FY2018 are pre-adoption and are left uncapitalised: the transition liability recognised on 1 Jan 2019 was €319.8m, ~3.1% of FY2018 total assets, against a P18 trigger of 10%. The FY2019 adoption-year artifact (CE_wc steps up €1.5bn, of which ~€0.3bn is leases) is noted; it depresses FY2019 ROCE_wc by roughly 0.4pp and is not adjusted.

**IFRS interest classification.** Legrand states explicitly that interest paid is included in net cash from operating activities (FY2025: €106.6m, of which €16.1m on lease liabilities). No reclassification into CFO is required, and FCF = CFO − capex is taken as reported.

**Capex definition.** FCF subtracts both "capital expenditure" and "capitalized development costs" — both are investing outflows creating balance-sheet assets. FY2025: 1,576.8 − (221.1 + 27.6) = 1,328.1.

**P20 maintenance-capex basis — NOT ACTIVE.** None of the three conditions holds: capex plus capitalised development has run 2.4%–3.2% of sales across the whole window with no ramp (FY2025 2.6%), management guides no step-down, and there is no documented expansion carve-out. All-capex FCF is used everywhere.

**Substitution rule (§2·1A) — not applicable.** Legrand is an industrial manufacturer with no captive-finance arm, no deposit funding and no securities-heavy balance sheet.

**Share count for Gate 2.** The FY2025 fully diluted weighted-average count of 266,435,233 (note 4.1.3) is used for market capitalisation, per-share metrics and the trigger band. That count includes 2,632,397 shares of OCEANE dilution while the €800m June-2025 convertible remains inside total borrowings, which double-counts slightly and is therefore the darker read; on the ex-OCEANE count of 263,802,836 the market capitalisation is €36,635.6m and the band moves to €18.56 / €26.03 — no verdict or ordering consequence. The OCEANE conversion price of €159.03 is 14.5% above the memo price, so it is out of the money today.

**Bias check.** One live: **hotness** — datacenter and AI-electrification demand is the loudest story in this sector and Legrand now derives 26% of sales from it. Handled by resting the Future verdict on the channel-ingrainedness moat in the legacy installed base rather than on the datacenter narrative, by making the market-growth call last and treating it as directional only, and by recording the mix shift as the first thesis-breaker. Also checked and not live: **peculiarity** (a French mid-cap compounder is not exotic), **consensus**, **cleverness**, and **windfallapathy**. Verdict was not born of a bias.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers.** Disclosure sentence present and explicit: "The Group derives the large majority of its revenue from product sales to generalist and specialist distributors. The Group considers that no individual customer represents more than 10% of consolidated net sales" (URD 2025, note 2.2). **PASS**, on the book's own test — the disclosure sentence exists and says no. Counter-evidence noted and carried into the force call: the effective buyers are a consolidating set of pan-regional distributors (Sonepar, Rexel, Wesco/Anixter, Graybar) and the disclosure is a threshold statement, not a concentration table.

**Breadth — suppliers.** No supplier-concentration disclosure sentence exists. Structural read: raw materials and components are ~69% of cost of sales (FY2025 cost of sales €4,662.0m → ~€3,217m, ~34% of revenue), split roughly 75% components / 25% raw materials; purchasing is centralised at group level explicitly to obtain scale on world markets; the inputs are copper, zinc, steel, engineering plastics and standard electronic components, all multi-vendor. Legrand's own risk disclosure frames raw materials as a *price* exposure, never a *dependency*. **PASS (structural)** — same treatment as the DG and SGO.PA precedents where the disclosure sentence is absent but the input base is demonstrably commoditised. No captive-customer waiver used or needed.

**Four forces (binary; P16).**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **WEAK** | Thousands of distributor customers, none >10%; backward integration by a pure distributor is implausible; the pull comes from installers who specify the brand, and the group's operating margin has widened from ~18.6% (FY2016) to 19.1% reported / 20.7% adjusted (FY2025) rather than compressed. |
| Supplier bargaining power | **WEAK** | Commoditised multi-vendor inputs bought centrally on world markets; forward integration into an eighty-brand, code-certified SKU business is implausible; switching costs on components are low. |
| Threat of substitutes | **WEAK** | Doing without electrical infrastructure is impossible; the wholly-different-function candidates (PoE/low-voltage DC distribution, wireless controls) face code, certification and installed-base adoption costs that move on a decade clock. |
| Threat of new entrants | **WEAK** | Barriers to entry are per-country certification and distributor shelf space; barriers to *success* are installer familiarity and an eighty-brand local portfolio — the reason low-cost entrants have taken share in emerging markets but not in the European or North American core. |

**Tally: 0 strong → favourable.**

**Moat — single identified source: Ingrainedness (brand for the channel).** The electrician, not the building owner and not the distributor, chooses the wiring device, the enclosure and the protection module; they choose the brand they were trained on and whose part numbers they know, the distributor stocks what they ask for, and the installed base pulls like-for-like replacement for decades. That is the book's ingrainedness mechanism in its cleanest industrial form, and it is what a high-teens reported operating margin on a commodity-input product actually rests on. **Direction: stable.** In the datacenter adjacency (Starline, Raritan, ZPE, Avtron — busway, rack power distribution, load banks) the mechanism changes rather than disappears: specification moves to design engineers and hyperscaler standards, where lock-in is real but the incumbency is younger and the competitive set (Vertiv, Schneider, Eaton, ABB) is stronger. That 26% of the business is the part of the moat least proven.

**Market growth: YES.** Electrification of buildings, datacenter power demand, and European renovation/efficiency retrofit (40–45% of sales are renovation) all point up. Directional only; guarded against late-pipeline glow per §2·1B.

**Future verdict: STRONG** — breadth PASS + 0 strong forces + a durable, stable moat.
