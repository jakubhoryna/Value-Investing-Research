## 2. Understanding

Group sentence: IMI plc, a Birmingham-headquartered engineering group listed in London for over a century, designs, makes and services fluid- and motion-control products — severe-service valves and actuators for power, oil and gas and process plants, pneumatic and electric motion components for factory machinery, hydronic balancing and control valves for buildings, precision fluid-handling parts for life-science instrument makers and components for commercial vehicles — and sells them to plant operators, contractors, machine builders, installers and original-equipment makers worldwide, about 45% of it as aftermarket parts and service (Europe 44%, the Americas 29%, Asia Pacific 19% of FY2025 revenue).

- **Automation** (65.3% of FY2025 revenue): Process Automation (67% of the unit) sells severe-service valves, actuators and their spares and service to operators of power, LNG, petrochemical and other process plants; Industrial Automation (33%) sells pneumatic and electric actuators, valves and fittings to machine builders and manufacturers.
- **Life Technology** (34.7%): Climate Control (51% of the unit) sells hydronic balancing and thermostatic valves to HVAC installers, contractors and wholesalers for buildings; Life Science & Fluid Control (29%) sells precision valves and pumps to analytical, diagnostic and critical-care instrument makers; Transport (20%) sells engine, chassis and thermal-management components to commercial-vehicle makers.

## C. Calculation notes

- **Economic hedge reversal.** IMI moves the result of its unhedged-for-accounting currency and metal forwards out of operating profit into net finance items. Where the reversal adds to statutory OI (a loss was taken out), the loss goes back in, under J3's one-way rule. That gives −7.6 (FY2015), −5.6 (FY2016), −1.9 (FY2018), −4.0 (FY2019) and −3.0 (FY2022). Where the reversal takes a gain out (FY2017, FY2020, FY2021, FY2023–25), statutory OI already excludes it.
