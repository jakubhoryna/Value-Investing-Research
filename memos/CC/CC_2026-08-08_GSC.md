# CC — The Chemours Company: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past **WEAK** (w-o-4 ROCE 3.39%, FCFROCE ≈ 0.8%, L/E 2,841%); Future **WEAK** (2-strong tally, eroding cost moat, regulatory substitution); SF **FAIL** (P17 hard strike — $8.6m against a $2.55m ceiling with no normalized acquittal available); cheapness n.m.-EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (four gates)**.

This is a leveraged commodity cyclical carrying an open-ended legal liability, and every gate says so at once. Operating income has been negative in two of the last three years, equity has shrunk from $1.1bn to $251m (and to roughly zero at the latest quarter) under $4.6bn of debt, and the PFAS docket converts any future good year into someone else's claim first. The stock's fall to $15 does not make it cheap: there is no positive operating denominator to be cheap against.

## 2. Understanding

Chemours manufactures titanium-dioxide pigment (Ti-Pure), refrigerants and thermal solutions (Opteon), and fluoropolymers and membranes (Teflon, Nafion), sold to industrial customers — coatings, plastics, HVAC/auto OEMs, chemical processors, electronics and hydrogen — worldwide; a Delaware corporation spun off from DuPont in 2015 carrying the legacy PFAS environmental-litigation estate, HQ Wilmington, Delaware, plants across the Americas, Europe and Asia; a formerly large-cap commodity-chemicals leader now small-cap under legal and leverage strain. **PASS.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: customer and supplier bases are broad and diversified (commodity chemicals; no >10% disclosure concern surfaced) — **PASS on its face**, unverified sentence flagged. Forces: customer power **STRONG** (TiO2 is a spec-identical commodity; buyers price-shop globally), supplier power WEAK (ores and fluorspar from multiple sources — with a China-fluorspar caveat), substitutes **STRONG in a specific form — regulatory doing-without**: PFAS restriction is substitution by force of law across the fluoro-portfolio, and TiO2 extenders keep nibbling; new entrants WEAK-to-contained in fluoro (permits are a real barrier) but the **demonstrated Chinese TiO2 capacity flood** already resets pigment pricing. Tally **2 STRONG** — the stop signal. Moat: **Cost** (chloride-process TiO2; Opteon patent estate) — real in places, **eroding** in the aggregate: the pigment cost edge no longer holds price, and the fluoro moat is the thing regulators are dismantling. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK.**
