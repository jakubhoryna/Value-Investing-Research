# EL.PA — EssilorLuxottica SA: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK (4.6% ROCE); Future STRONG; Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (buybacks); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past + SF)**.

EssilorLuxottica owns the eyewear chain end to end, from lens technology and Ray-Ban to 18,000 stores and AI glasses with Meta. Both units grade STRONG on the future. What it does not earn is a return on the €58bn of capital that the 2018 merger and later deals left on the balance sheet: 4.6% worst-of-four ROCE, 5.8% on free cash flow. In 2026 the board bought at least 5.8m shares, 1.2% of the company, mostly at €211.51 — 34 times trailing operating income — and kept buying in September, with no reference to worth. At €184.30 the shares trade at 22.0 times maintenance free cash flow and 30.6 times operating income, against a €13.72 trigger. A flip needs ROCE above 10%, beyond reach on this capital base, and repurchases held to worth.

## 2. Understanding

EssilorLuxottica (Charenton-le-Pont, France; formed on 1 October 2018 when Essilor, founded 1849, combined with Luxottica, founded 1961; 32.4% owned by the Del Vecchio family holding Delfin, whose votes are capped at 31%) designs and makes prescription lenses (Varilux, Transitions, Stellest), frames and sunglasses under owned brands (Ray-Ban, Oakley, Persol, Supreme) and licensed luxury brands (Prada, Chanel, Armani among them), AI glasses with Meta and medical devices, and sells them wholesale to about 300,000 opticians, optical chains, distributors and e-commerce platforms worldwide and directly to consumers through about 18,000 owned and franchised stores, online, EyeMed vision-care plans and eye clinics; North America is 45% of 2025 revenue and EMEA 38%; it is the world's largest eyewear and ophthalmic-lens group. **PASS.**

A group of two material units (the "central costs and other" column is reconciliation):
- **Professional Solutions** (48% of 2025 revenue): wholesale of lenses, frames, sunglasses, smart glasses and lab and diagnostic equipment to independent opticians, chains, distributors and third-party e-commerce platforms.
- **Direct to Consumer** (52%): retail of eyewear, sunglasses, contact lenses and eye exams to consumers (Sunglass Hut, LensCrafters, Pearle Vision, GrandVision banners), e-commerce, EyeMed plans paid by employers and plan sponsors, and eye surgery (Optegra, Signifeye).
