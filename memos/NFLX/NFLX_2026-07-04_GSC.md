# NFLX — Netflix, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Gate | Result |
|---|---|
| Understanding | PASS |
| Past | ADEQUATE |
| Future | **WEAK** |
| Shareholder-friendliness | **FAIL** (comp strike) |
| Cheapness | EXPENSIVE |
| **FINAL** | **AVOID** (Future WEAK + SF FAIL) |

Netflix is the profitability and scale leader of streaming, and its record is genuinely good — returns on the last three years are STRONG-grade, per-share OI has compounded ~49%/yr over the decade, and it de-levered from a debt-funded content build into a cash machine ($9.5bn levered FCF in FY2025). But the model stops it on two independent gates. The industry structure is hostile: content suppliers extract escalating rents and have forward-integrated into competing services, while substitutes are abundant and consumer switching costs are near-zero — two strong forces, the book's stop signal. And executive pay (~$53.9m for the higher co-CEO) is 1.8× the $30m ceiling, a shareholder-friendliness strike. Even after a ~45% drawdown from ~$130 to ~$78, the price sits at ~35× FCF / ~25× EV/OI — cheapness would require a fall to ≈ $17. Nothing here is close to a BUY; the analysis is filed so that a structural change (supplier de-escalation, real subscriber lock-in) plus comp reform would be the trigger to revisit.

## 2. Understanding

Netflix sells differentiated, ad-free-and-ad-tier subscription video-on-demand (plus games and nascent live/sports) directly to ~325m+ paying consumer households across 190+ countries; it is a vertically-integrated content studio *and* global streaming platform (Los Gatos HQ, US-listed), funding the industry's largest content budget from its own subscriber scale. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized-average ROCE is 14.9% (range 14.9–20.2%) and FCFROCE 4.9% (4.9–6.6%) — both *below* the STRONG bars (15% / 8%), dragged by 2016–2019, when Netflix deliberately burned cash (FCF −$1.6bn to −$3.1bn) building its library. The trailing three years invert that: ROCE 15–27%, FCFROCE 15–24%. Per-share growth is exceptional (OI/sh geo-mean +48.6%; BV/sh +29.2%; FCF/sh negative→+$2.18). Leverage fell from 407% to 109% (latest-year L/E, within bounds). Returns are on a sustained *up*-trend, so no downgrade — but the 10-yr average clears only ADEQUATE (ROCE ≥10%, growth strong, leverage fine). **ADEQUATE** — the mechanical average understates current quality; STRONG is unavailable on the full window.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASSES both sides: no customer near 10% (mass consumer subscription); no supplier >10% of COGS+opex (content spread across thousands of productions/licensors; AWS/infra well under 10%). Forces (binary): customer power **WEAK** (atomised subscribers, no backward integration); supplier power **STRONG** (A-list talent, sports/franchise IP and guilds extract rising rents, and studios — Disney, Warner, NBCU, Paramount — forward-integrated into rival DTC services and pulled content); substitutes **STRONG** (a dozen well-funded streamers plus free attention rivals YouTube/TikTok/gaming; consumers multi-home and churn at near-zero switching cost); new entrants **WEAK** (≈$15–20bn/yr content scale is a formidable barrier). Tally = **2 strong → WEAK** (book stop signal). Moat: cost/scale (largest base funds the deepest slate, spread over the most subscribers) — real and stable-to-strengthening, but it is not a switching-cost lock-in, so it does not neutralise substitution, and it does not blunt supplier rents. Market: growing (cord-cutting, international, ad-tier). **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Comp: higher co-CEO Ted Sarandos **$53.9m** FY2025 (Greg Peters $53.2m) vs the $30m P17 ceiling — **STRIKE (1.8×)**; the 5% test (~$473m) is not binding. Highest-paid outside director ≈$408k (all-option monthly grants) — below the $500k stop. Insider alignment adequate (Hastings founder stake; TCV's Hoag). RPT: standard policy, nothing material — clean. Buybacks $6.0/6.3/9.1bn (2023–25) genuinely cut the diluted count ~4.6% (SBC minimal, ~$368m) but were executed at ~25–40× FCF — overpriced vs conservative worth: a documented caveat, not the strike. No dividend (rational at high ROCE); no empire M&A (no goodwill). **FAIL — strike: executive compensation.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Content-cost inflation and sports-rights escalation (NFL, WWE) compress the very margin expansion that drove the recent returns.
- Low switching costs mean subscriber losses can be rapid in a downturn or when a rival lands a hit — retention is earned monthly, not locked.
- Studio-owned DTC consolidation (e.g., Paramount–Warner) could create a genuinely competitive scaled rival.
- Ad-tier and live/sports are unproven at the scale needed to change the structural force calls.
- Governance: co-CEO structure with founder-chairman and persistent >$30m pay; say-on-pay endorsement does not satisfy the framework.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Future flips toward ADEQUATE only if the structure changes: supplier leverage demonstrably eases (durable in-house cost control, weaker talent/sports bidding) *or* Netflix builds real consumer switching-cost lock-in (bundling, identity/ads, gaming, live) that recalls the substitute force to WEAK. SF flips only if top pay drops below the $30m ceiling. Cheapness is academic short of ≈ $18. Next review: FY2026 results (~late Jan 2027), or a named structural/governance change. This is an AVOID that stays on file, not a WATCH.
