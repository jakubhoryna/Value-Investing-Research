# NFLX — Netflix, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (compensation and buybacks); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID**, unchanged, now on three gates.

Netflix is the scale and profitability leader of subscription streaming, and its last three years are excellent: ROCE above 20% and free cash flow of $9.4bn in FY2025. The model still stops it on three independent gates. The ten-year record carries the 2016–2019 content build, when operating cash flow was negative, so the full-window FCF return is 4.8% and per-share FCF growth cannot be measured. Two competitive forces are strong: content suppliers and substitutes. And shareholder-friendliness fails twice over: co-CEO Ted Sarandos's 2024 compensation actually paid of $117.1m, and $32.2bn of repurchases since 2023, all clearly above worth, still running on a $22.3bn authorisation. The trigger is $16.36 against $68.69.

## 2. Understanding

Netflix sells differentiated subscription access, ad-supported and ad-free, to professionally produced series, films, live events and games, streamed to consumer households that pay monthly, directly or through partner billing, in over 190 countries, with advertising sold to brands a small second revenue line; it is a Delaware company headquartered in Los Gatos, a vertically integrated studio and global distribution platform reporting one segment, and a $45.2bn-revenue, 29-year-old company that just walked away from a $83bn studio acquisition. **PASS.** Lens STD. One reportable segment: not a group.

## 3. Quality — past record

On FY2016–FY2025 the worst-of-four ROCE is 14.8% (latest five years 18.4%) and FCFROCE 4.8% (10.5%), on maintenance-capex FCF, P20 constructed; all-capex FCFROCE 4.5%. The lower window governs, and 4.8% misses even the 5.3% bending bar. Operating cash flow was negative from 2016 to 2019, so the first three years' average FCF per share is negative and the FCF growth read is n.m. Operating income per share compounded 41.0% a year on the harsher read. L/E fell from 4.07 to 1.09. No sustained decline fires; returns rise. **WEAK** — FCFROCE.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes: consumers pay; no customer or single supplier is near 10%. Supplier power is STRONG: sports-rights and talent bidding, and studios that compete with Netflix and license to it on their own terms; Paramount's purchase of Warner Bros. makes one of them larger. Substitutes are STRONG: YouTube, a dozen subscription services and free attention platforms, at near-zero switching cost. Customer power and entrants are WEAK. Two strong forces is the stop. The moat is cost and scale, stable to strengthening, and it reaches neither force. Market growth YES. **WEAK** — 2+ forces.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

The hairiest number is Sarandos's 2024 compensation actually paid, $117.1m, more than twice the ≈ $40m line: a strike. Proportionality holds every year. Outside directors draw about $408k each, under the cap. Management holds about 797,000 shares outright, 0.02%, worth 0.47× the hairiest number: NOT ALIGNED. Repurchases in 2023, 2024, 2025 and the first nine months of 2026 ran at 25–36× OI and 28–49× FCF, clearly above worth, and the programme is live, price-blind and the stock still above worth: ADVERSE. **FAIL**, at strike-logic step 1 (compensation; buybacks).

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Paramount–Warner Bros. creates a scaled studio rival that is also a content supplier.
- Content and sports-rights costs keep rising: the NFL, boxing and live slates are bid for each year.
- View-hour growth has slowed while prices rose; switching costs stay near zero.
- The 2026 advertising forecast was cut to about $2.7bn from about $3bn.
- $22.3bn of authorisation remains for repurchases with no price criterion.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To **WATCH**, all of: the FY2017–FY2019 cash burn rolls out of the window and FCFROCE clears its bar (not before the FY2028 window at the earliest); supplier or substitute power demonstrably weakens; executive pay falls under the lines on every printed basis; and repurchases stop above worth, or the price falls below it.
