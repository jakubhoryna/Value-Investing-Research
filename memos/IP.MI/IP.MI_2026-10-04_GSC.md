# IP.MI — Interpump Group S.p.A.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past ADEQUATE, Future ADEQUATE, shareholder-friendliness PASS (one flag: capital-allocation CAUTION), cheapness EXPENSIVE: **WATCH**.

Interpump is worth owning for its Water-Jetting franchise, the world's largest maker of high- and very-high-pressure plunger pumps, which earns a 22.6% EBIT margin, plus a hydraulics roll-up that has compounded operating income per share at 9.6% a year. Returns are good but not exceptional: worst-of-four ROCE is 13.1% and FCFROCE 6.8%, held down by working capital and a FY2021–23 capex ramp. The price is far away: 16.0× FCF and 12.2–13.3× EV/OI against a band of €15.3–17.7. A BUY needs the price roughly halved at unchanged earnings, or free cash flow to rise well above €232m.

## 2. Understanding
Interpump designs, makes and sells high- and very-high-pressure plunger pumps, ultra-high-pressure systems and food, chemical and pharmaceutical process equipment (Water-Jetting, 35% of FY2025 revenue, 48% of segment EBIT), and power take-offs, hydraulic cylinders, valves, directional controls, hoses, fittings, orbital motors and steering units (Hydraulics, 65% of revenue, 52% of EBIT). The customers who pay are equipment OEMs (trucks, construction, agricultural and industrial machinery, cleaning and process plant builders), distributors and industrial end users, with the top customer about 1% of revenue. It is an Italian company headquartered in Sant'Ilario d'Enza, manufacturing in 15 countries and selling worldwide (North America 25%, Europe ex-Italy 35%). It is a founder-anchored (founded 1977), mid-sized serial acquirer listed on Euronext STAR Milan. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**ADEQUATE.** Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **13.1%** (end of period, with cash); the cash-stripped reads are 15.2–16.6%. Worst FCFROCE is **6.8%**. ROCE peaked at 16.2% in FY2022–23 and fell to 11.9% and 11.2% in FY2024–25 as organic revenue shrank. The halves are 13.6% and 12.9%, so the retreat is a named re-test, not a sustained decline. FCF is weaker than profit: FY2022 FCF was €71m on €384m OI after an inventory build and the capacity programme. Per-share growth clears the floor comfortably (OI/sh +9.6%, FCF/sh +11.0%, BV/sh +13.2% a year). L/E is 61%, with net debt 1.3× FCF.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth **PASSES**: the top customer is about 1% of revenue and the top fifteen about 13%; inputs are steel, castings and components with several vendors. In Water-Jetting all four forces are WEAK: fragmented buyers, commodity inputs, no substitute for pressurised water in cleaning and cutting, and a scale leader with a long-standing installed base. Hydraulics carries one **STRONG** force, substitutes: electrification of trucks and mobile machinery can remove the gearbox power take-off and replace hydraulic actuation. Its 12.8% margin against Water-Jetting's 22.6% shows the harder field. The moat is **switching costs**: components designed into OEM platforms and qualified over years. Stable. Market growth is MIXED: FY2025 Hydraulics −6.1% at constant perimeter, Water-Jetting +4.3%. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
Pay is within bounds. Executive chairman Fulvio Montipò received €7.85m in FY2025 (€2.93m cash plus €4.91m option fair value), against €10.4m on the fiscal-year read and €10.3m normalized. Directors' fees are €45–73k. The Montipò family and TIP hold 23.4% through Gruppo IPG Holding. Related-party dealings are 0.4% of cost of sales. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: buybacks since FY2018 ran at 12–22× EV/OI, mostly recycled into option exercises, and the 2026 €47m programme executed at 13.6× EV/OI and 16.5× FCF. The largest deal, White Drive (2021), cost about 5.2× EBITDA. **PASS** with one flag.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Truck and machinery electrification erodes the power-take-off and mobile-hydraulics installed base.
- Acquisitions are the growth engine; a large deal priced well above the White Drive multiple would turn CAUTION into a second flag.
- Founder succession: the executive chairman is the largest option holder and the group's architect.
