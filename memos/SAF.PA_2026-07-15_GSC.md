# SAF.PA — Safran SA: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | ADEQUATE | STRONG | **FAIL** | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** |

Safran is one of the best industrial franchises in the world — a civil-aerospace aftermarket annuity on a vast, growing installed base of CFM56 and LEAP engines, customer-funded (net cash despite €19bn of customer advances), with a dominant and strengthening moat. It fails this framework not on business quality but on capital allocation: Safran is repurchasing its own shares for cancellation at cycle-peak multiples — the FY2025 tranche (5.1m shares, €1.3bn, ~€255 average) implies **22.0–24.5× EV/OI and 23.8–27.2× MCap/FCF**, all through the P19 20× hard-strike line, and the same pattern holds across 2023–2025. That is a shareholder-friendliness hard strike → AVOID. The stock is separately EXPENSIVE (≈29–32× EV/OI, ≈35× FCF; trigger band ≈ €75 vs €331, +343%). The verdict flips to WATCH only if a full programme year runs below the flag lines (or abstains); a BUY additionally needs a ~77% price fall it is unlikely ever to offer.

## 2. Understanding

Safran designs, builds and services aircraft **propulsion** (via the 50/50 CFM International JV with GE — LEAP and CFM56 engines — plus helicopter turbines and military M88), **equipment & defence** (landing gear, nacelles, wheels & brakes, wiring, optronics, navigation) and **aircraft interiors** (seats, cabin), earning the bulk of its profit from the high-margin civil **aftermarket** (spare parts + RPFH service contracts) on its installed engine base; it sells original equipment to airframers (Airbus, Boeing) and services to the world's airlines, lessors and armed forces; a Paris-HQ, ~110,000-employee French aerospace-and-defence prime and CAC 40 constituent, formed 2005 (Snecma–Sagem), reshaped by Zodiac (2018) and Collins actuation (2025). **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalised-average ROCE over the clean 5-year window (FY2021–25, IFRS as-reported OI) is **9.9%** (end-of-period, incl. cash — the binding variant on a growing balance sheet; ex-cash 13.0%); FCFROCE **12.0–15.7%**, comfortably above the 8% floor. Returns are dragged by ~€13bn of Zodiac goodwill+intangibles that stay in capital employed (Safran is acquisitive) and by the COVID trough inside the window; the underlying engine business earns far more. Per-share OI and FCF grew strongly off 2021 (FCF/sh €4.66→€10.73). L/E screens 312% — above the P5 flag — but this is **operating float** (€19bn customer advances/deferred RPFH revenue), not financial leverage; Safran holds €1.7bn **net cash**. STRONG is unavailable (5y clean series; worst-of-four <15%). **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

**Breadth PASS:** the economic engine — aftermarket — is spread across thousands of airlines and lessors; OE is structurally concentrated at Airbus/Boeing but no single customer >10% of revenue is disclosed; supplier base broad (forging/casting bottlenecks are capacity constraints, not bargaining power). **Forces — 0 strong:** customer power WEAK (decades-long installed-base lock-in, RPFH contracts); supplier power WEAK; substitutes WEAK (no substitute for a certified engine; prohibitive switching); new entrants WEAK (certification + installed base + capital). **Moat:** cost + switching + ingrainedness on the CFM56/LEAP base — dominant and **strengthening** (LEAP fleet compounding; durability kits lengthening shop-visit intervals). Market growth **YES** (secular traffic + fleet renewal). **STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**Compensation PASS:** CEO Olivier Andriès total ≈ €3.1–3.6m (FY2023–24) — far below the ~€40m (2026, CPI-indexed) scrutiny line and below 5% of the lower of FCF (€3.9bn) and net income; outside-director fees within French norms; token insider ownership (neutral). **RPT:** none material. **Buybacks — HARD STRIKE (P19):** FY2025 repurchases-for-cancellation of 5.1m shares for €1.3bn (~€255 avg) imply EV/OI 22.0–24.5× and MCap/FCF 23.8–27.2×, both above the 20× line; 2023 (~20–23×) and 2024 (~21×) repeat it, and management is a quarter through a standing €5bn programme. At ~3.4× conservative worth these are overpriced acquisitions, not below-worth buybacks — the exception does not apply. **Acquisition audit** (BV≫TBV from Zodiac) clears: Zodiac 2018 was a distressed rescue at a low multiple, not a >20× deal. **FAIL — P19 buyback hard strike.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Air-traffic / aerospace-cycle downturn would compress OE volumes, though the aftermarket annuity is far more resilient (the 2020–21 trough recovered fast).
- The moat is so durable the market keeps Safran perpetually rich — the band may never be reached absent a broad market dislocation.
- IFRS net income (€7.2bn FY2025) is distorted by a +€5.7bn FX-hedge mark-to-market **in the financial result, not OI** — do not read it as operating strength.
- Buyback discipline is the live thesis-breaker: nothing signals management will pause repurchases at these multiples.
- Execution: LEAP aftermarket ramp, forging/casting supply chain, French corporate surtax, and Collins-actuation integration.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

**SF → PASS (verdict → WATCH):** a full programme year with repurchases below the P19 flag lines (implied EV/OI <14× and MCap/FCF <16×), or abstention — earliest testable at FY2026 results. **→ BUY:** additionally requires price into the ≈ €75 band (~−77%), realistically only in a severe cyclical/market drawdown. **Direction:** verdict can only improve via capital-allocation change or a large price fall; business quality is not the constraint. **Next review:** FY2026 results (~Feb 2027) — re-anchor band + re-test the FY2026 buyback tranche; H1 2026 (~Sep 2026); or price < ≈ €90. Not held — no sell discipline applies.
