# FQT.VI — Frequentis AG: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past ADEQUATE, Future WEAK, shareholder-friendliness FAIL, cheapness EXPENSIVE: **AVOID (Future + SF)**.

Frequentis is worth owning for its installed base: safety-critical voice and information systems in air-traffic and emergency control rooms, where 54% of revenue is follow-on business on systems already in place. Two gates stop it. In FY2025 one air-traffic customer took 15.3% of revenue, after seven reported years with none. And the CEO's €1.70m total exceeds 5% of the lower of FCF and net income on both reads. The price is far away too: 25.4× FCF and 18.6–20.9× EV/OI against a band at €18.50–21.20. The verdict could reach WATCH only if the large customer falls back below a tenth of revenue and pay comes back inside the owner-earnings line, or earnings grow past it.

## 2. Understanding
Frequentis designs, integrates and maintains communication and information systems for control centres: voice-communication switches, aeronautical-information and network systems, and command-and-control software. These are delivered mostly as customer-specific projects and then maintained. The customers who pay are civil and military air-navigation service providers (Air Traffic Management, 69% of FY2025 revenue) and police, fire, rescue, rail and maritime authorities (Public Safety & Transport, 31%). It is an Austrian AG headquartered in Vienna, where most staff work, selling into about 150 countries; 58% of FY2025 revenue came from Europe and 27% from the Americas. It is a founder-family-controlled mid-cap, listed since 2019. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**ADEQUATE.** Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **11.1%** (end of period, with cash); worst FCFROCE is 10.1%. The cash-stripped reads run 17.8–19.9%, because a large cash pile sits on a thin capital base. Returns dipped to 9–10% in FY2017–19, then rose to 15.8% in FY2025, so there is no sustained decline. FCF swings with customer advances: FY2022–24 FCFROCE was 2–6%. OI/share compounded 13.8% over FY2016–25, but FCF/share only 0.8%, below the 3% floor. L/E is 161%. STRONG fails on the ROCE level.

## 4. Quality — future
Customer breadth **FAILS**. The FY2025 segment note discloses one customer at €88.7m (15.3% of revenue) in Air Traffic Management, probably the large Americas project (identity not disclosed); FY2018–24 disclosed none. The captive-customer waiver fails because buyers are not few: every country has its own air-navigation provider. Supplier breadth passes (commercial components and subcontractors, no disclosed concentration). Forces: customer power **STRONG** (public tenders, one or two buyers per country, 5–9% EBIT margins); suppliers, substitutes and entrants WEAK. That is one strong force. The moat is **switching costs** on certified, installed control-room systems. It is stable. Market growth is YES: orders ran at 1.17× revenue. **WEAK** on breadth.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
The CEO received €1.70m in FY2025. The fiscal-year line is €1.42m (5% of €28.3m net income) and the normalized line €1.02m (5% of €20.4m average FCF). Both reads breach: a **hard strike**. Supervisory Board pay is token: the chair got €39k. The founder family controls about 68%. Related parties take about €7.9m a year, mostly headquarters rent (€3.9m), engineering and consulting. That is one RPT flag. Capital allocation is **SOUND**. Dividends are a 20–30% payout from FCF, and bolt-ons are small. Buybacks only supply incentive shares: €0.74m in August 2026, at 28× FCF. **FAIL** (P17 strike).
