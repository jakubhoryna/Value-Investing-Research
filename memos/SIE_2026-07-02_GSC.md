# SIE — Siemens AG: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | ADEQUATE | ADEQUATE | PASS | EXPENSIVE | **WATCH** |

Siemens is worth owning as the dominant Western supplier of factory automation and industrial software, with an installed-base moat measured in decades and a decade of per-share progress achieved while shedding an entire energy business. What stands between here and a BUY is price: at €273 the stock trades at 19× FCF and 27× EV/OI against thresholds of 8× and 7×, and the mechanical trigger band sits ~85% below. Conviction statement: the verdict flips to BUY only in a severe dislocation (trigger band €25–45 depending on treatment of the captive finance arm); it flips to AVOID if the post-Healthineers-spin remainder re-tests below ADEQUATE quality or if debt-funded software M&A resumes at scale.

## 2. Understanding

Siemens AG is a Munich-headquartered German Aktiengesellschaft (one class of shares, one vote each) that sells industrial automation hardware and software (Digital Industries), electrification and building-infrastructure products (Smart Infrastructure), trains and rail signalling (Mobility), and — pending a spin-off announced 12 Nov 2025 — consolidates a 67% stake in listed medtech maker Siemens Healthineers, plus a captive financing arm (SFS, €33bn assets); its paying customers are manufacturers, utilities, building and rail operators, and hospitals across Europe (~45% of revenue), the Americas (~30%) and Asia (~25%), and it is the global #1–2 leader in automation, growing modestly. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Strict Marshall math is depressed by SFS: the €29bn finance book sits in capital employed while its interest earnings are excluded from operating income. Strict ROCE_xc ran 5–10% (≥10% in only 1 of 10 years) — WEAK on the P3 rubric. Deducting SFS debt from CE (Appendix C judgment call; rating agencies and Siemens itself treat SFS as captive finance) gives ROCE_xc 7–14%, ≥10% in 8 of 10 years, and FCFROCE ≥8% in all 10. OI/share rose from €8.93 to €12.09 and FCF/share from €6.75 to €13.86 despite spinning off Siemens Energy (FY2020). L/E fell from 261% to 143% (101% ex-SFS debt). **Verdict: ADEQUATE** — a consistent low-teens-return industrial once the finance book is treated consistently; never STRONG (adjusted ROCE never reached 15%).

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: no customer concentration disclosed; suppliers broad. Forces MIXED: customer power MEDIUM (large utilities/OEMs), supplier power LOW, substitutes MEDIUM (open/software-defined automation), new entrants LOW (installed base, certification), rivalry MEDIUM-HIGH (ABB, Schneider, Rockwell, and rising Chinese entrants such as Inovance). Moat: switching costs plus ingrainedness — TIA/SIMATIC automation embedded in customer plants and engineer training for decades, deepened by the Xcelerator software layer (Mentor, Altair, Dotmatics) — stable-to-strengthening. Market growth YES (electrification, automation, grid and rail investment), with the usual caveat that growth attracts capital. **Verdict: ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation proportionate and performance-linked: CEO Busch's FY2025 total ≈ €12.3m ≈ 0.13% of OI, mostly variable; he holds ~210k shares (~€57m) under 300%-of-salary ownership rules; single share class. Related-party transactions minimal and arm's-length (Note 31). Buybacks FCF-funded and modest (€6bn program to 2029; FY2025 average ≈ €211/share — above framework-cheap, flagged, but share count is down ~4.5% over the decade). Capital allocation rational overall: progressive dividend (€3.60→€5.35), disposals at gains (Innomotics, Yunex, Energy/Healthineers stake sales) funding admittedly expensive software M&A (Altair $10.6bn ≈ 14× revenue) — caveat, not a strike. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Chinese automation demand weakness plus Inovance-class local competitors eroding Digital Industries' margin structure.
- The Healthineers deconsolidation (30% direct spin-off, stake to become a financial asset) removes ~€3.3bn of segment profit and reshapes every ratio — all quality metrics must be re-based.
- €41bn goodwill vs €9.4bn tangible book: impairment risk if Altair/Dotmatics software returns disappoint.
- SFS credit losses in a downturn (€28.9bn debt at 9× leverage) would hit the consolidated account directly.
- Rail (Mobility) long-cycle project execution risk.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To BUY: price into the trigger band (€25–45 depending on SFS treatment) with quality gates intact — a full REVIEW is mandatory first, especially post-spin. To AVOID: post-spin remainder failing the adjusted ROCE ≥10% test, a leveraged mega-acquisition, or moat erosion evidenced by automation share loss. Next review: Healthineers spin-off shareholder votes/completion (2026–27) or FY2026 results (≈ Dec 2026), whichever first; price alert < €60.

## C. Calculation notes

**FY2025 CE build (€m):** Total assets 166,202 − NIBCL 36,087 (= total current liabilities 47,261 − short-term debt & current maturities 11,174) = **CE_wc 130,115**; − cash & equivalents 14,495 = **CE_xc 115,620**; − SFS debt 28,898 = **CE_xc,adj 86,722**.

**FY2025 TBV build (€m):** Equity attributable to Siemens AG shareholders 62,244 − goodwill 40,670 − other intangibles 12,199 = **9,375**. Judgment call: TBV and BV use *attributable* equity (MCap is a claim on parent shareholders only), while all intangibles are deducted although a slice sits in 67%-owned Healthineers — conservative.

**FY2025 EV build (€m):** MCap 213,363 (= €273.45 × 780.264m shares outstanding) + total debt 56,015 (ST 11,174 + LT 44,841) + NCI at book 6,127 − cash 14,495 = **261,010**. No preferred stock. Pension provisions (€732m, down from €13.7bn in FY2016) not added, per framework EV definition.

**OI definition:** S&P template = gross profit − R&D − SG&A + other operating income − other operating expenses; excludes equity-method income, all interest and financial items, discontinued operations. FY2022 OI includes ≈ €1.1bn Yunex disposal gain inside other operating income (one-off, flagged). Company-adjusted "Industrial Business profit" FY2025 was €11.77bn — higher than template OI mainly because it includes SFS-like financing profit at Healthineers/segments differently and excludes PPA amortization; template figure used throughout. Reported IFRS pre-tax income FY2025: €10.83bn.

**SFS captive-finance judgment call (governs the Past verdict):** SFS runs €33.1bn of assets on €28.9bn allocated debt (9.1× D/E per Note 20); its earnings arrive as interest income, which the template OI *excludes*, while its assets sit fully inside CE. Strict Marshall ROCE therefore mismatches numerator and denominator, and strict L/E counts pass-through funding as leverage. Adjustment applied: deduct disclosed SFS debt from CE_xc (SFS's equity slice remains in CE unearned — conservative), and show L/E both raw and ex-SFS debt. Both strict and adjusted series are printed in B2/B3; the Past verdict of ADEQUATE rests on the adjusted series (precedent: BRK.B structural-screen substitution). **Strict rubric alone would grade Past WEAK — recorded plainly.** MODEL-mode question flagged: codify a captive-finance-arm rule (§2/§3) analogous to the insurer P/B substitution.

**Structural breaks:** Siemens Energy spun off Sep 2020 (FY2016–FY2019 flows include it); Innomotics discontinued from FY2024 reporting (sold Oct 2024, €2.06bn gain in FY2025 discontinued ops); Healthineers stake reduced by 7% in FY2025 (2% sale booked €1,250m other financial income — excluded from OI) with full deconsolidation announced Nov 2025. Figures are as-originally-reported per year; growth rates across FY2019→FY2020 and FY2023→FY2024 are break-affected.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth.** Customers: PASS — no ≥10% customer disclosure; base spans manufacturers, utilities, buildings, rail operators, hospitals worldwide. Suppliers: PASS — broad electronics/components base; semiconductor dependence diversified across suppliers.

**Five forces.** Customer power MEDIUM — large utilities, OEMs and state rail buyers negotiate hard, but the base is fragmented. Supplier power LOW — Siemens is the anchor customer for most inputs. Substitutes MEDIUM — software-defined/open automation and cloud-native PLM challenge closed stacks; Siemens hedges via Xcelerator. New entrants LOW — installed base, safety certification and service networks are decade-scale barriers. Rivalry MEDIUM-HIGH — ABB, Schneider, Rockwell, Honeywell, Alstom, plus price-aggressive Chinese entrants (Inovance) in mid-range automation. **Overall: MIXED.**

**Moat.** Switching costs + ingrainedness: SIMATIC/TIA automation is written into customer plants, processes and engineer training; ripping it out halts production. Software attach (Mentor, Altair, Dotmatics) adds a second lock-in layer. Direction: **stable-to-strengthening**, with the China mid-market the erosion front to watch.

**Market growth.** YES — electrification, grid expansion, automation/reshoring and rail investment are secular; Marshall's caveat applies (growth is attracting Chinese capital and competition).
