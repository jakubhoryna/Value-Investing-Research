## 2. Understanding
Badger Meter designs and makes differentiated water meters (mechanical and ultrasonic), ORION cellular and fixed-network radio endpoints, water-quality and sewer-monitoring sensors, BEACON SaaS (89% of FY2025 sales are utility water) and flow-measurement instruments (11%). Its paying customers are municipal and investor-owned water utilities, reached through a direct salesforce and distributors, plus industrial buyers of flow instruments. It is a vertically integrated manufacturer with a software layer, run from Milwaukee with plants in Wisconsin, Mexico, the Czech Republic and Switzerland. Ninety per cent of sales are in the US. It was incorporated in 1905, is listed on the NYSE and had $917m of FY2025 sales. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**STRONG.** The worst-of-four average ROCE is 18.9% (end-of-period, cash in) and the best 26.5%. Worst FCFROCE is 17.6%. Returns rose across the window: the first half averaged 16.4% and the second half 20.4% on the worst basis, as operating margin climbed from 13.4% to 20.0%. Per-share growth is well above the floor: geometric OI/share +15.5%, FCF/share +26.3% (from a weak FY2015 base) and BV/share +11.6%. There has been no debt since 2020. Liabilities to equity were 36% at FY2025 and 46% at 30 June 2026.

## 4. Quality — future
**STRONG.** Breadth passes. The 10-K states that no customer exceeds 10% of sales. Brass castings, resins and electronic subassemblies are single-sourced by choice, but the company states that alternative sources exist. All four forces are weak. Thousands of utilities buy on 15–20-year replacement cycles and stay with the endpoint and software installed. AWWA standards, utility qualification and installed-base compatibility hold back entrants. Meters are mandatory for billing. The moat is switching costs, built on installed endpoints plus BEACON software, and it is stable: gross margin held at 41% in H1 2026 even as sales fell. Market growth is YES: AMI conversion, sewer and quality monitoring, and SaaS ($74m, +29%).
