# CPR.MI — Davide Campari-Milano N.V.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future ADEQUATE; SF FAIL (P17 hard strike); price EXPENSIVE — verdict **AVOID** (Past, SF).

Campari owns a spritz ritual worth having: Aperol and Campari sell a cheap liquid at high margin into a serve the company built. The trouble is the record, which this memo measures over a decade of acquisitions. Ten years of buying brands (Grand Marnier, Wilderness Trail, Courvoisier) kept average returns on capital near 8%, and a capacity build-out since 2022 pushed free cash flow returns to about 3%. Pay failed too: in 2025 the departing finance chief received €32.5m, about twice 5% of net income on both the fiscal-year and three-year reads. Even ignoring both failures, the price is far from a bargain at 18× operating income. A flip needs returns lifted above 10% on the full capital base, which the disposal programme and the end of extraordinary capex could start to show from FY2026, with pay back inside proportion by FY2028.

## 2. Understanding

Davide Campari-Milano N.V. — a Dutch-domiciled, Milan-listed spirits group founded in 1860 and controlled by the Garavoglia family's Lagfin (51.0% of capital, 82.3% of votes), with €3.05bn of 2025 net sales — produces and brand-markets aperitifs (Aperol, Campari, Crodino), American whiskey (Wild Turkey), tequila (Espolòn), cognac and liqueurs (Courvoisier, Grand Marnier) and vodka (SKYY) from 24 plants, selling them mostly through its own distribution companies in 27 countries to retailers, distributors and wholesalers, with the Americas 44% and EMEA 50% of sales. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four ROCE is 8.1% and FCFROCE 3.3%, both end-of-period and cash-inclusive. The most generous read, beginning-period and cash-exclusive, is 9.9% ROCE, still below 10%. Returns sat at 8–11% in most years of 2016–2023 (5.3% in pandemic 2020) and fell to 6.6% and 7.5% in 2024–2025 after Courvoisier. FCF was squeezed by maturing-inventory builds and capex running at 10–15% of sales in 2022–2024. Operating income per share grew 5.3% a year and FCF per share 3.9%, both above the floor. L/E is 110%. **WEAK** — the return level fails the 10% line on every read.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes. No customer above a tenth of revenue is disclosed, though the company says sales are concentrated among a few key customers in some markets (alternate logged). Sugar, glass and agave are multi-sourced. Forces: customer power WEAK — a fragmented global base and must-stock brands; supplier power WEAK — commodity inputs; substitutes STRONG — moderation, GLP-1 drugs and ready-to-drink formats, with US sales flat in 2025 and SKYY declining; new entrants WEAK — brand spend, aged stocks and distribution are barriers. One strong force. Moat: brand, chiefly the Aperol spritz ritual, stable. Market growth is MIXED. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**Hard strike on pay.** Paolo Marchesini, the departing finance and operations chief, received €32.46m in 2025, mostly settlement and a last-mile incentive. Five percent of the lower of FCF and net income is €17.3m on the fiscal-year read and €14.6m on the three-year read, so pay breached both. The CEO's €3.9m is proportionate. Director fees top out at €325k, and related-party dealings with Lagfin are tax-consolidation balances. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: option-servicing repurchases ran at 20–44× EV/OI, alongside serial brand acquisitions, but a new CEO has turned to disposals. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Courvoisier (2024, US$1.2bn) adds a cognac exposure whose category is shrinking and which faces China and US tariff friction.
- Lagfin's 82% voting control means minority holders cannot change allocators or pay policy.
- Extraordinary capex is due to end in 2026; if spending does not fall back to about 4–5% of sales, FCF stays depressed.
