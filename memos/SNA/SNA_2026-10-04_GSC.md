## 2. Understanding

Snap-on makes and sells premium professional hand and power tools, tool storage, diagnostics, shop equipment and repair-information software (differentiated, branded goods plus subscriptions), paid for mainly by independent franchisees who resell weekly from mobile vans to vehicle-repair technicians, by repair shops and OEM dealerships, and by industrial customers in aerospace, military, natural-resources and education markets; it is a vertically integrated manufacturer-franchisor in the professional tools and vehicle-repair equipment industry, with a captive finance arm (Snap-on Credit) funding technicians' purchases and franchisees' vans; about 74% of net sales, manufacturing and HQ (Kenosha, Wisconsin) are in the United States, Europe the main other market; it is a 106-year-old, $19bn NYSE incumbent with about 13,000 employees, mature and slow-growing rather than transforming. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 18.9% (end-of-period, cash in) and worst-of-four FCFROCE 13.7%, both clear of the 15% and 8% STRONG lines; the cash-excluded readings run 21–24% and are flat across the decade, so the cash-in drift from 23.0% to 18.8% is the $1.6bn cash build, not an operating decline. Per-share growth compounded 6.8% (operating income) and 10.1% (free cash flow) against the 3% floor; book and tangible book per share grew 10.6% and 13.5% while the diluted count fell 10.8%. Liabilities-to-equity is 41%. Operating income includes the finance segment ($281.8m) with its $2.5bn of receivables in capital employed. **STRONG** — every P2 test passes on the worst reading.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: no customer reaches 10% of revenue; steel and components come from numerous suppliers, specialty alloys from an approved group of several mills. Forces: customer power WEAK (thousands of franchisees and technicians), supplier power WEAK (commodity steel), substitutes WEAK (50% gross margin held through the 2025 tariff year; the van's warranty, service and credit bundle keeps the value gap to cheaper tools), new entrants WEAK (lower-priced brands entered retail without denting margins; the van-plus-credit network is the barrier to success). Tally 0. Moat: brand, delivered through the ingrained van channel — called stable on held margins and finance yields despite two years of US van organic decline. Market MIXED: vehicle age and complexity rise, electrification trims repair content. **STRONG.**
