On its own numbers this is the best-executing listed retail broker in continental Europe: cost-income ratio 43.0%, ROE 17.9%, commission per trade up ~10% in 2025 on 13% account growth. The problem is the industry, not the execution. Retail execution is a commodity sold at near-zero price by funded substitutes, and the window itself proved the scale can be built from nothing. Two forces call STRONG — the stop signal — and the price is 2.8× the bargain line besides.

## 2. Understanding

flatexDEGIRO SE is a Frankfurt-headquartered, BaFin-licensed banking group running the DEGIRO, flatex and ViTrade self-directed online brokerage platforms, selling securities execution, custody and collateralised margin lending to ~3.5 million retail private investors who pay it commissions across 16 European countries, and earning a second, rate-dependent income stream on those clients' €6.2bn of on-balance-sheet deposits. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Eight years (FY2018–25); FY2016–17 not obtained, so **STRONG is unavailable** under §2·1A regardless of arithmetic. The financial-institution substitution rule applies: €6.2bn of customer deposits fund €4.4bn of central-bank balances, €1.0bn of bonds and €1.4bn of margin loans, making OI/CE returns meaningless. Worst-of-two normalised ROE is **13.8%** (end-of-period; 17.0% beginning) — clears the 10% line, misses 15%. No sustained decline: the annual series troughs at 8.2% (2019) and ends at 17.9%. EPS +29.5%/yr and BV/sh +20.3%/yr geometric, flattered by a pre-merger 2018 base. Non-deposit interest-bearing debt is 13.9% of equity. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS**: 3.5m atomised accounts, none near a tenth of revenue; the supplier side passes on the ready-alternatives exception (many venues and data vendors, in-house FTX:CBS core banking) — concentration undisclosed, logged in Appendix D. Forces: customer power **WEAK** (backward integration impossible; commission per trade rose in 2025); supplier power **WEAK**; **substitutes STRONG** — Trade Republic, Scalable, Revolut, eToro, Interactive Brokers and every incumbent bank sell an identical trade at near-zero commission, one custody transfer being the only switching cost; **new entrants STRONG** — the licence is a real barrier to entry, but the barrier to *success* has empirically failed. Tally 2 strong → stop signal. Moat: cost/scale, stable but not dominant. Market growth YES. **WEAK.**

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: PASS.** ~3.5 million customer accounts across 16 European countries (FY2025: 3.5m, +13.0%). No customer-concentration disclosure exists because none is required — no single retail account approaches a tenth of €559.8m of revenue.

**Breadth — suppliers: PASS on the P15 ready-alternatives exception, logged.** No supplier-concentration sentence appears in the annual report, which under P15 suggests the condition is absent rather than proven. The exception is applied because the material input categories all have several standing vendors: execution and settlement across more than 50 exchanges plus OTC direct trading (no single venue is contractually indispensable), market data from competing vendors, and — importantly — the core banking platform is built and run in-house (FTX:CBS), removing the largest single-vendor dependency a broker normally carries. The largest cost line is salaried staff (€127.4m). **Evidence quality is moderate; the fee split by venue is not disclosed. Named re-test at the FY2026 touch.**

**Moat.** Source: **cost**, supported secondarily by brand (flatex in DACH, DEGIRO across the Netherlands and southern Europe). Direction: **stable** — improving on its own trend, but not widening relative to competitors who have reached similar scale from a standing start. A moat that does not prevent a six-year-old rival from matching your scale is real but not dominant, and under §2·1B a dominant moat is evidence for weak force calls rather than an override.

**Market growth: YES.** European retail investing penetration is rising; customer assets under custody grew 33.5% to €95.5bn and transactions 19.5% in FY2025. The force calls concern who captures that growth, not whether it exists.
