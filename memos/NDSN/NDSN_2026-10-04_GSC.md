# NDSN — Nordson Corporation: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Nordson passes understanding, rates WEAK on its past record and STRONG on its future, passes shareholder-friendliness with one capital-allocation flag, and is EXPENSIVE — **AVOID**, decided at the past-record gate.

Nordson is worth owning for a precision-dispensing franchise whose installed base pulls proprietary parts and consumables through every customer line, earning 55% gross margins and 18–22% returns on capital for most of the decade. What stands in the way is what the last three years did to those returns: about $2.2bn spent on CyberOptics, ARAG and Atrion at 16.5–35× earnings took return on capital from 20.6% to 12.7% in three straight years, and the price, $333.54, sits about 470% above a band of ≈$56.5–58.5. The verdict moves to WATCH if FY2026 returns break the decline — the trailing twelve months already read about 14.6% — and BUY would additionally need a price roughly 80% lower.

## 2. Understanding

Nordson Corporation, incorporated in Ohio in 1954 (US GAAP, USD, Nasdaq), designs, makes and services differentiated precision dispensing, coating, fluid-control and inspection equipment, with the proprietary nozzles, pumps, components and single-use medical fluid parts that run through it, sold mainly direct in over 35 countries to manufacturers in packaging, nonwovens, electronics and semiconductors, medical devices, industrial coatings and agricultural sprayers; two-thirds of its $2.79bn FY2025 sales are outside the US, the industrial dispensing segment is the largest of three (48%), and the group has turned acquisitive since 2022. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 14.8% (year-end capital including cash); the best reading is 16.9%, and worst FCFROCE is 11.9%. The level clears the ADEQUATE line but not STRONG. Operating income per diluted share compounded 7.1% a year, free cash flow per share 10.4%, and liabilities run at 94% of equity. The trend decides it: beginning-capital ROCE fell from 20.6% in FY2022 to 19.8%, 14.0% and 12.7%, as roughly $2.2bn of acquired capital entered the base and earned far less than the legacy business. The FY2025 year-end uptick of 0.9 points is too small to read as reversal. **WEAK** — a sustained decline forces the downgrade from an ADEQUATE level.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes: no customer reached a tenth of sales in FY2023–25, and inputs are metals, plastics and electronics from many vendors. All four forces are WEAK — customers are fragmented and their lines are engineered around Nordson heads and pumps; suppliers sell commodities; no other way to lay down a precise bead of adhesive, coating or fluid at line speed exists; entrants face application know-how, installed bases and a global service network. The moat is switching costs: once a system is specified into a production line, its proprietary parts and service follow for years. Stable. Market growth is MIXED — electronics and medical growing, industrial and agriculture cyclical. **STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

The CEO's $8.15m is a third of the $24.2m cap on both reads; the chair's $482k sits under half the US chair stop, and the audit committee found no material related-party dealings. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: repurchases ran 13–29× across the decade, never at the bargain lines, and the live programme bought at about $257 in FY2026 (22–25× on FY2025 earnings) with $569m left and no price criterion; the 2022–24 deals were bought at 16.5–35× operating income on debt. No large deal has followed since August 2024, and debt is falling. **PASS (one flag).**
