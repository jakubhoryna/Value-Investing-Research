This is the archetypal good-stock-not-cheap: a founder-led, debt-free global payments platform compounding tangible book value per share at ~30% a year on ~25% with-cash returns, run by a management board that pays itself 0.8% of operating income, has never bought back a share, never paid a dividend it couldn't justify, and owns ~4.5% of the company. What stands between here and a BUY is purely price: even after a 33% one-year drawdown the stock trades at roughly 2–3.5× the framework's thresholds on the binding metrics. The verdict flips to BUY only on a severe dislocation toward ≈€286; it flips to AVOID if take-rate compression and rivalry (Stripe, bank-led rails, agent-led commerce) start eroding the moat — watch net revenue growth decelerating below ~mid-teens with falling EBITDA margin as the tell.

## 2. Understanding

Adyen N.V. is an Amsterdam-headquartered Dutch public limited company holding a European banking licence, operating a single, fully in-house global platform that provides payment acceptance, processing, acquiring, and adjacent financial products (issuing, business accounts, capital) to large enterprise and platform merchants, who pay per-transaction fees; revenues span Europe (largest region), North America, APAC and LATAM, and the business is a leading, still fast-growing challenger to legacy processors in global merchant payments. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Seven fiscal years exist in usable form (IPO June 2018; FY2019–FY2025 computed, flagged short). ROCE_wc was 24.6–45.3% in all seven years — exceptional levels — and FCFROCE_wc never fell below 20%. OI/share compounded ~24%/yr and TBV/share ~33%/yr; leverage is non-debt merchant float (total debt is €252M of leases against €10.8B cash), with L/E above the 200% flag in 2020–2023 but excused under the exceptional-returns provision and now down to 132%. The downgrade from STRONG: ROCE_wc declined from ~45% to ~25% as retained cash ballooned the capital base, and FCFROCE fell steeply (122% → 20%) as merchant-float inflows normalized — P4's declining-trend clause applies even at superb absolute levels. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASSES: top-10 merchants are 11% of revenue, none above 10%, churn below 1%; the supplier side's card-scheme duopoly is concentrated but its fees pass through to merchants. Forces are MIXED: high rivalry (Stripe, Checkout.com, Worldpay, J.P. Morgan) and real enterprise bargaining power (visible take-rate compression), against high entry barriers from global licensure and platform scale. The moat is Cost plus Switching costs, reinforced by licensing: one in-house platform with own acquiring/banking licences yields structurally low unit costs, and replatforming embedded, omnichannel payments is risky for merchants — stable-to-strengthening (>80% of growth from existing merchants). The market grows, which also keeps attracting capital. **ADEQUATE** (forces MIXED blocks STRONG).

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Management Board total pay was €9.2M in FY2025 — 0.8% of operating income — with no variable remuneration permitted; the co-founder co-CEO holds ~2.9% and the board ~4.5% combined; one share class, one vote (protective preference shares authorized but none outstanding — standard Dutch measure, noted). Related-party transactions are trivial. No shares have ever been repurchased; dilution runs ~1%/yr. No dividends — rational while reinvestment returns ~25% — though the €10.8B cash pile and the first-ever acquisition (Talon.One, ≈€750M, May 2026) make capital allocation the item to monitor. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Take-rate compression from enterprise bargaining power could outpace volume growth and cap net revenue economics.
- Stripe, bank-led account-to-account rails (e.g., European schemes), and agent-led commerce could commoditize acceptance.
- Reported FCF is structurally distorted by merchant-float swings, so the binding cheapness metric is noisy year to year.
- Talon.One marks a strategic shift to M&A; undisciplined follow-on deals would threaten the SF pass.
- Interest income (€268M FY2025) flatters net income; rate cuts remove a tailwind that sits outside operating income.

## C. Calculation notes

**FY2025 CE build (EUR M):** Total assets 12,257 − NIBCL 6,717.9 = **CE_wc 5,539.1**, where NIBCL = total current liabilities 6,787 − current lease liability 69.1 (Adyen's only interest-bearing current item; no financial debt exists). CE_xc = 5,539.1 − cash & STI 10,828.1 = **−5,289 (n.m.)**.

**FY2025 TBV build:** Equity 5,285 − goodwill 0 − other intangibles 7.5 = **5,277.5** → €166.95/diluted share.

**EV build at €817.40:** MCap = 817.40 × 31.611M diluted WAS = 25,839 + total debt (leases) 252.4 + preferred 0 + minority 0 − cash & STI 10,828.1 = **EV 15,263**.

Judgment calls (recorded for repeatability):
1. **ROCE_xc is n.m. in every year** because Adyen's cash includes merchant float whose offsetting payables are already netted out of CE_wc; subtracting all cash double-counts and turns CE negative. ROCE_wc is therefore used as the binding return variant. It is the conservative choice: CE_wc retains ~€4.1B of net own cash, so it penalizes the idle cash pile exactly as Marshall's with-cash measure intends.
2. **Past verdict downgraded STRONG → ADEQUATE under P4's declining-trend clause** (ROCE_wc 45% → ~25%; FCFROCE 122% → 20%), reinforced by the short record (7 years, all post-2018, including the COVID e-commerce boom). The decline is largely mechanical (cash retention growing the denominator; float normalization shrinking CFO), and ROCE_wc has been flat at ~25–26% for three years — a REVIEW showing stabilization plus three more years of record would support re-rating to STRONG.
3. **Leverage:** L/E breached the 200% flag in 2020–2023 but liabilities are overwhelmingly non-interest-bearing merchant payables; financial debt is leases only against a >€10B net cash position; returns are exceptional and L/E is far below the 700% cap → leverage passes under the exceptional-returns provision.
4. **Capex** defined as purchases of PP&E plus capitalized intangibles (the aggregator excludes the latter; difference ≤ €5M/yr).
5. **CFO includes interest received on the cash/float pile** (IFRS presentation choice; €260.8M in FY2025), which flatters FCF; conversely FY2025 CFO absorbed a −€313M merchant-payables swing. Both noted; framework formulas applied as written.
6. FY2019–FY2020 diluted shares derived as NI ÷ diluted EPS; FY2021–FY2023 likewise from the standardized series.
