# RMS.PA — Hermès International SCA: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS, Past STRONG, Future STRONG, shareholder-friendliness FAIL, cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** on the shareholder-friendliness gate.

This is one of the highest-quality operating records the framework has measured: 29.8% worst-of-four ROCE and 20.4% FCFROCE across a full ten-year window, 16.1% compound operating-income-per-share growth, near-zero borrowings, and a brand moat that has widened operating margin from 32.6% to 41.0% over the same decade. It fails anyway, and not marginally. Hermès has spent a decade buying its own shares at prices four to six times the framework's own bargain lines, and buying them for a single disclosed purpose — delivering stock into employee free-share plans. That is dilution sterilisation executed deep inside the overpriced band: a value transfer from owners to employees dressed as capital allocation. The strike is independent of price, so no fall in the share price repairs it. What would flip the verdict is a published, worth-referenced repurchase policy plus a completed programme-year executed at or below the bargain lines and turnover of the capital allocators who ran the strike era — all three limbs, none of which is in evidence.

## 2. Understanding

Hermès International SCA is a French *société en commandite par actions*, family-controlled through its Active Partner Émile Hermès SAS, that designs, manufactures in some sixty mostly-French workshops it owns, and sells almost entirely through roughly three hundred of its own stores worldwide, to individual affluent consumers, differentiated leather goods and saddlery, silk, ready-to-wear, watches, perfume and tableware, under a 189-year-old brand whose deliberate supply scarcity is the commercial mechanism. PASS.

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst of the four normalized averages is 29.8% ROCE and 20.4% FCFROCE, both on the end-of-period with-cash denominator; the ex-cash reads are 70.3% and 48.3%. Per-share growth compounds at 16.1% for operating income, 14.9% for FCF, 17.6% for book value — every one far above the 3% inflation floor. Leverage is 29.1%, and the only interest-bearing obligations are €2,312m of IFRS 16 lease liabilities against €12,773m of cash and short-term investments. One caution: ROCE ex-cash has fallen three consecutive years (89.3% → 73.9%) as the leather-workshop and store build-out lifts operating capital; the with-cash series, which is the project's operative P4 test, has fallen twice. A third decline at FY2026 fires P4. **STRONG.**

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth passes — direct retail to individual consumers, no concentration disclosure because none exists. Supplier breadth passes on vertical integration into tanneries, precious-leather sourcing and silk weaving, though the underlying spend is undisclosed. All four forces read WEAK: consumers are atomised and allocation-constrained; suppliers face a customer that owns its upstream; substitutes exist but have not touched a 41% margin; entry barriers compound with barriers to success, since brand equity of this kind cannot be bought. Tally zero strong — favourable. The moat is Brand, single-sourced, and strengthening: managed scarcity sustains annual price rises without volume loss and a secondary market clearing above retail. Market growth MIXED — personal luxury demand has softened, and Hermès' constant-currency growth has decelerated from 42% to 8.9%, but it still outgrows its sector. **STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation is unremarkable: Axel Dumas €7.83m against 5% of the lower of FCF and net income at €210.7m on the year read and €195.9m normalized, and far below the indexed absolute trigger; the Supervisory Board chair takes €181,500. Related-party dealing is disclosed but structural — a 0.67% statutory profit share to the Active Partner, RDAI's exclusive mandate over the internal design of every Hermès store at €20m, and family-owned Paris landlords. Buybacks fail three ways independently. FY2021 bought 142,131 shares at €1,139.33: 32.4× EV/OI and 41.6× MCap/FCF on the year read, 43.2× and 63.2× normalized — beyond 20× on both reads of both bases. FY2016 bought 319,621 at €342.94: 20.1× and 26.3×. Two flagged tranche-years, neither rescued, strike on repetition; and every disclosed acquisition purpose is "employee shareholding", so sterilisation escalates to a strike in years already breaching the flag lines. **FAIL — P19.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Asia-Pacific excluding Japan is 42% of revenue and 42% of operating income — a single-region demand shock is the dominant earnings risk.
- Succession and control: the entire governance model rests on Émile Hermès SAS and family cohesion, with no external check available to minority holders.
- The capex ramp has pushed ex-cash returns down three straight years; a fourth would fire P4 and take Past to WEAK.
- Exotic-skin supply is CITES-constrained and reputationally exposed, with animal-welfare campaigns a recurring headline risk.
- €12.8bn of idle cash earns near-nothing operationally and is the direct enabler of the repurchase behaviour that fails this gate.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

The verdict flips to WATCH only when the P19 strike is redeemed under all three strict limbs: a board-level, worth-referenced repurchase criterion; a completed programme-year at or below 8× MCap/FCF or 7× EV/OI, or a demonstrated refusal year while the stock traded above the flag lines; and documented turnover of the strike-era capital allocators. FY2024 and FY2025 repurchases of €37m and €8m are suggestive of restraint but are de-minimis years, not refusal years, and limbs (a) and (c) are unsatisfied. It flips further to AVOID-on-two-gates if FY2026 delivers a third consecutive ROCE_wc decline. Next review: the FY2026 URD, expected March 2027, testing both. Price alert below ≈ €400, which would put the P12 edge in reach and make the redemption question live rather than academic.

## C. Calculation notes

**FY2025 capital-employed build.** Total assets 24,322 − NIBCL 2,658 = CE_wc 21,664. NIBCL = trade and other payables 792 + current tax liabilities 452 + other current liabilities 1,414. Note 5.4 states that other current liabilities mainly comprise employee and tax liabilities excluding current tax, which places them squarely inside the accrued-expenses and taxes-payable definition. Burden-of-proof calls, all resolved by leaving the item in CE: current provisions 122, current post-employment obligations 19, current financial derivatives 61, other non-current liabilities 72. Goodwill of 180 is retained in CE — Hermès is only lightly acquisitive but the exception is discretionary and was not taken. CE_xc = 21,664 − 12,773 = 8,891, where cash and STI is cash and cash equivalents 12,239 plus cash investments with original maturity over three months of 534, per the note 10 reconciliation of the restated net cash position.

**FY2025 TBV build.** Equity attributable to owners 18,840 − goodwill 180 − intangible assets 231 = 18,429, or €175.45 per diluted share.

**FY2025 EV build.** MCap 178,090 (105,036,704 diluted shares × €1,695.50) + borrowings and financial liabilities 34 (of which the whole balance is put options over non-controlling interests) + lease liabilities 2,312 (1,987 non-current + 325 current) + preferred 0 + non-controlling interests 6 = 180,442 on the no-cash variant; less cash and STI 12,773 = 167,669 on the all-cash-excess variant.

**Leases.** FY2019–FY2025 are IFRS 16 as reported, with lease liabilities treated as debt-equivalent in CE, EV and L/E throughout. Hermès adopted IFRS 16 on a fully retrospective basis, so FY2018 and the 1 January 2018 opening balance sheet are restated in URD 2019 and are used as such — the FY2017 year-end balance sheet in this memo is that restated opening position (total assets 7,686.1, right-of-use assets 910.3, lease liabilities 1,006.8), not the as-originally-reported 6,768.2.

**P18 lease capitalisation, FY2016 and FY2017 income statements, FY2015 and FY2016 balance sheets.** FY2016 undiscounted operating-lease commitments were €937m (stores 810.5 + administrative and logistics 126) against total assets of 5,998.7 — 15.6%, above the 10% threshold, so capitalisation is mandatory. FY2015 commitments were €643m estimated against 5,187.6, or 12.4%, likewise above.

The disclosed commitment figures are described in the source as discounted minimum payments. That reading is validated by tie-out: FY2017's disclosed €997.3m against the actual IFRS 16 restated liability of €1,006.8m at 1 January 2018 is a 1.0% understatement, so the disclosed totals are used directly as the lease-liability present value without further discounting, and the 1% is left as a conservative bias.

The imputed rate is taken from Hermès' own restatement rather than from the 7.71% figure printed in the FY2016 commitment table, which is not a cost of debt. The FY2018 restatement moved €27.1m of lease interest into net financial income against an average lease liability of €1,061m, an effective 2.55%. Applying that: FY2016 imputed interest €24.4m on €937m, so OI becomes 1,697.0 + 24.4 = 1,721.4; FY2017 imputed interest €24.8m on the €971.9m average, so OI becomes 1,922.0 + 24.8 = 1,946.8. Cross-check: Hermès' own FY2018 restatement lifted operating income by €30.3m against €27.1m of interest, so this construction is marginally conservative.

For FCF, cash rent is calibrated at 19.7% of the lease liability, being the €208.9m of rent removed from FY2018's income statement over the €1,061m average liability. FY2016: rent €184.6m less interest €24.4m gives principal €160.2m, so FCF = 1,473.9 − 262.1 + 160.2 = 1,372.0. FY2017: rent €191.5m less interest €24.8m gives principal €166.7m, so FCF = 1,605.6 − 265.3 + 166.7 = 1,507.0. Right-of-use assets are added to total assets equal to the liability, leaving book value unchanged and therefore leaving the ΔBV and ΔTBV series unaffected.

**IFRS interest reclassification.** Not required. Hermès classifies only the principal element of lease repayments in financing; there is no material interest-paid line in financing, the group carrying €34m of borrowings against €12,773m of cash and STI.

**Captive finance / substitution rule.** Neither applies. Hermès is an operating manufacturer and retailer with no finance arm.

**P20 maintenance-capex basis — not active.** Capex over revenue across the window runs 5.0%, 4.8%, 5.2%, 6.9%, 7.0%, 5.9%, 4.5%, 6.4%, 7.0%, 7.3%. The recent level is the top of a range it has occupied before, not a ramp far above a pre-cycle ratio, so condition (i) fails and the remaining two are not reached. All FCF in this memo, in both the quality and the valuation gates, is on an all-capex basis.

**Backvaluing — not used.** Luxury is cyclical in demand but is not a definitively cyclical industry in the book's sense, and the tourniquet is not warranted where the current multiple exceeds the band by more than fourfold.

**Bias check.** The two live hazards on a name of this quality are affinity and hotness — the pull of a famous, admired brand and of a record that reads beautifully. Neither is load-bearing: the verdict turns on repurchase arithmetic computed from disclosed share counts and average prices, and it is a rejection, not an endorsement, so the pull ran against the conclusion rather than toward it. Miscontrast was checked in the opposite direction — 29.8% ROCE is high in absolute terms and must not be allowed to soften a shareholder-friendliness strike, which the framework forbids averaging away. **Bias check: none live.**
