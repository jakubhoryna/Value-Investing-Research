# EVK — Evonik Industries AG: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | **WEAK** | **WEAK** | PASS | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** |

Evonik is a large, competently run, conservatively financed specialty chemicals group that has not earned its cost of capital in any year of the past decade. Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **4.6%** and FCFROCE **3.5%** across FY2016–FY2025 — less than half the 10% ADEQUATE floor — and the trend inside that average is worse than the level: the company's own ROCE disclosure runs 14.0% → 6.1% with a −243m EBIT year in 2023. The balance sheet looks cheap (0.96× book) precisely because the assets do not produce; €4.4bn of goodwill from the 2017 Air Products and Huber deals sits on top of a business whose operating income has fallen 55% since 2016. Two gates fail independently, so no price flips this: at 7× operating income the whole enterprise is worth roughly what the net debt already is. What would need to change is not the multiple but the earning power — a sustained return of group EBIT to the €1.3bn area on a smaller capital base, which is a multi-year restructuring outcome, not a re-rating.

## 2. Understanding

Evonik Industries AG is an Essen-headquartered, RAG-Stiftung-controlled (≈53%) German specialty chemicals manufacturer selling differentiated additives, amino acids and health-and-care ingredients, silicas, high-performance polymers and catalysts — plus a near-commodity methionine business and a site-services and utilities arm running German chemical parks — to industrial, coatings, tyre, animal-feed and pharmaceutical manufacturers worldwide, with roughly half of sales in Europe/Middle East/Africa, as a long-established mid-cap listed producer currently mid-way through a portfolio restructuring into three segments (Advanced Technologies, Custom Solutions, Infrastructure). **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized average ROCE **4.6%** (end-of-period, cash-inclusive basis; range 4.6–5.0% across the four combinations) and FCFROCE **3.5%** (range 3.5–3.8%) — both far below P3. Yearly ROCE_wc falls 7.3% (2016) → 3.6% (2025) with a negative 2023, a sustained multi-year decline that forces a downgrade on level and trend independently. Per-share growth on a flat 466m share count is negative on three of four metrics: OI/sh −8.6%/yr, FCF/sh −3.5%/yr, TBV/sh −4.0%/yr, BV/sh +0.6%/yr — all below the 3% nominal floor. Leverage is the one sound feature: L/E 121%, inside the 200% flag. **WEAK** — returns roughly a third of the ADEQUATE threshold, declining.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS** on both legs: customer concentration disclosed as low, and ~33,000 suppliers with the largest 100 at ~40% of procurement expenditure. Forces: customer bargaining power **STRONG** (half the portfolio is price-taking, and pricing drove the 2023 collapse); supplier power **WEAK** (commodity propylene/methanol/ammonia inputs, many vendors, forward integration improbable); substitutes **STRONG** (methionine, silica and superabsorbents face continuous alternative chemistries); new entrants **STRONG** (Chinese capacity has repeatedly cleared the barriers to entry *and* to success). Tally **3 strong** — the book's stop signal. No moat with a single durable identified source survives contact with a 4.6% ROCE; where positions exist they are eroding. Market growth MIXED — sales are lower than 2017. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 clears comfortably: the highest-paid individual (CEO Kullmann) took **€3.706m** (€4.280m incl. service cost) against a 5%-of-lower(FCF €594m, NI €265m) line of **€13.2m** — 28% of the line — and far below the ~€34m indexed absolute trigger. Supervisory-board chair **€395k**, below the stop on any calibration. Related-party dealings are small and disclosed (all counterparties under €70m combined; the €252m RAG-Stiftung dividend is a pro-rata shareholder payment, not a transaction). Treasury purchases of €1–17m/yr serve employee programmes against a share count flat at 466m since 2013 — no sterilisation, no P19 buyback flag. The 2017 Air Products acquisition audit is logged **INDETERMINATE** (§2·1C, v3.4.0): the target's standalone pre-deal operating income is not disclosed by either party. **PASS** — no strike, no completed flag pair.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Methionine and silica pricing is set at the margin by Chinese capacity; a further leg down takes group EBIT below the 2023 trough.
- €4.4bn goodwill against €3.0bn tangible book — a second impairment cycle would wipe out several years of reported earnings, as 2023 already did.
- RAG-Stiftung's dependence on Evonik dividends to fund coal-legacy liabilities constrains the retention/redeployment choice, though the 2025 cut to €1.00 shows some flexibility.
- Energy cost structure at German sites is a standing competitive disadvantage against US and Middle East producers.
- The upside case — restructuring delivering a materially smaller, higher-return portfolio — is real but would take years and asset sales to show in the returns table.

Cheapness cannot flip on price alone at a sensible level while net debt absorbs the EV/OI band. ## C. Calculation notes

**NIBCL composition (§2·1A default, burden of proof on the item).** NIBCL = trade accounts payable + current other (non-financial) payables + current income tax liabilities. FY2025: 1,401 + 393 + 58 = **1,852**. Note 6.13 confirms the "other payables" line comprises contract liabilities from customer contracts, deferred income and miscellaneous accruals — all NIBCL-eligible. **Left in CE (logged calls):** current *other provisions* (€787m FY2025 — restructuring and environmental obligations, not operating trade credit) and current *other financial liabilities* (€689m — contains short-term debt, which always stays in CE per §2·1A, alongside rebate and customer-credit items). Both calls are conservative (larger CE, lower ROCE).

**Goodwill retained in CE.** Evonik is a serial acquirer over the window (Air Products PM 2017, Huber Silica 2017, Porocel 2019, PeroxyChem 2020) — goodwill stays in by default and no deduction is warranted.

**FY2025 CE build.** Total assets 17,981 − NIBCL 1,852 = **CE_wc 16,129**; less cash & equivalents 495 = **CE_xc 15,634**. No short-term investments are separately disclosed, so cash & STI = cash & equivalents.

**FY2025 TBV build.** Equity attributable to shareholders 8,110 − goodwill 4,411 − other intangible assets 686 = **TBV 3,013**. NCI of 64 excluded from BV per §2·1A.

**FY2025 EV build (note 6.12).** Bonds 2,441 + commercial paper 80 + liabilities to banks 299 + Schuldschein loans 176 + loans from non-banks 10 + lease liabilities 865 = **debt 3,871**. Derivative liabilities (190), rebate/bonus (33), customer credit (11) and miscellaneous financial liabilities (60) are excluded as non-debt. Company-reported net financial debt is €3,311m against my 3,871 − 495 = 3,376 gross-less-cash; the €65m difference is near-cash financial assets Evonik nets and I do not — conservative, and immaterial to the multiples.

**Pension provisions (€1,490m FY2025) are excluded from EV**, per the §2 Gate-2 formula as written. Noted as a conservatism gap running *against* Evonik: including them would raise EV/OI from 20.3× to 22.9×. It does not change any verdict.

**IFRS interest reclassification (mandatory, §2·1A).** Evonik moved "cash outflows for interest" from operating into financing activities with the FY2019 accounts. Interest paid is therefore deducted from reported CFO for FY2018 (restated basis) through FY2025; for FY2016–FY2017 it already sat inside CFO (verified in the FY2018 report's cash flow statement) and no adjustment is made. | Force | Call | One-line reason |

**Moat.** No moat with a single durable identified source can be sustained at group level. The company's claim that ~80% of sales come from market-leading positions is a market-share statement, not a returns statement, and Gate 0's marketing-message trap applies. No "moat-decides" floor is available: the moat is neither dominant nor stable.

**Market growth: MIXED.** Specialty chemicals broadly tracks industrial production; Evonik's own sales are lower in 2025 (€14.1bn) than in 2017 (€14.4bn), with the 2022 spike a pass-through artifact.

**Verdict: WEAK** (≥2 strong forces; no durable moat; eroding where positions exist).
