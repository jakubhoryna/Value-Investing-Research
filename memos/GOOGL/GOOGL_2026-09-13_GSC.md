# GOOGL — Alphabet Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past STRONG; Future WEAK; shareholder-friendliness FAIL; cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID**, on two independent gates.

The decade record is not in question: worst-of-four ROCE 22.7%, FCFROCE 17.6%, operating income per share compounding at 22.5% a year, leverage at 43% of book. Two other things are. The structure around the core product has changed — conversational assistants substitute directly for the query, the December 2025 Search judgment hands rivals data and syndication, and Apple still extracts an undisclosed payment for distribution Alphabet cannot replicate; that is two strong forces, the book's stop signal. And the compensation and capital-allocation tests each throw a scrutiny-level negative, which pairs into a gate failure. Nothing turns on price: at 33.3× EV/OI the stock would have to fall roughly four-fifths to reach the band. The verdict flips back only if the forces tally falls to one strong and the flag pair clears.

## 2. Understanding

Alphabet sells advertising placements and, increasingly, cloud compute: it auctions attention on its own search, video and mapping properties and on third-party publisher inventory to millions of advertisers worldwide; it sells consumption-based infrastructure, AI models and workspace software to enterprises; and it sells subscriptions, apps and devices to consumers — operating as a US-domiciled, founder-controlled dual-class holding company with about 199,000 employees, roughly half its revenue outside the United States, and a small portfolio of loss-making venture businesses (Waymo, Isomorphic) reported separately. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE 22.7% (best 47.3%) and FCFROCE 17.6%, both clear of the STRONG thresholds by wide margins on capital employed that keeps all goodwill. Per-share compounding: operating income 22.5% a year, free cash flow 14.0%, book value 14.6%, tangible book 15.5%. Leverage 43.3% of book, a third of the flag level. One blemish, recent and sharp — FCFROCE has fallen two consecutive years (21.7% → 20.1% → 14.9%) as capex reached 22.7% of sales, and the trailing read through June 2026 is already near 7%. Two years is not yet the sustained decline that forces a downgrade. **STRONG**, with the shortest fuse in the register.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both sides — millions of advertisers, no disclosed customer above a tenth of revenue, no disclosed supplier above a tenth of costs; the distribution-partner payment is not broken out by counterparty and is logged indeterminate. Customer power WEAK (fragmented, no backward integration); supplier power STRONG (one irreplaceable distribution counterparty, self-described limited-source accelerator supply, power and land constraints); substitutes STRONG (assistants replace the query at zero adoption cost; the remedy hands rivals data and syndication); new entrants WEAK (scale barriers absolute). Tally two strong — the stop signal. The moat is a network: auction liquidity across advertisers, publishers and users plus YouTube's creators, dominant in video and cloud, eroding at the query layer. Market growth YES. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Top disclosed pay was $42.2m (Schindler) against a CPI-indexed trigger of $41.0m — a breach, hence a flag; proportionality passes hugely on both reads (5% of the lower leg is $3.66bn). Chair fees $618k against a $1.02m stop; no related-party concern surfaced. Capital allocation is **CAUTION** — repurchases stopped entirely in the first half of 2026 after roughly $169bn spent in 2023–25 above the bargain lines, while the company raised $49.6bn of equity and preferred, opened a $40bn at-the-market programme, issued $56.2bn of debt, bought Wiz for ~$33.7bn cash and twice lifted 2026 capex guidance to $195–205bn. A new posture, no completed programme-year behind it. Two flags. **FAIL.**

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customer.** Alphabet's 10-K describes advertising solutions serving "millions of companies" and discloses no customer above 10% of revenue; more than 70% of FY2025 revenue is online advertising, spread across the advertiser base and Google Network publishers. **PASS.**

Breadth overall **PASS**; no waiver used.
