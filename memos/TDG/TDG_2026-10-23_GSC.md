## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS (two material units); Past WEAK (leverage: equity negative at every year-end); Future ADEQUATE (unit customer conc.); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (compensation); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** (Past + SF).

TransDigm owns about fifty niche makers of proprietary, certified aircraft parts and earns most of its profit selling replacement parts to airlines, distributors and armed forces over the 25–30-year life of an aircraft. Operating margins near 47% and per-share operating income compounding at about 13% a year show a business with real pricing power. Ownership is the problem: a decade of debt-funded special dividends ($9.6bn in FY2025 alone) has left a $9.8bn stockholders' deficit and $33.9bn of debt, more than seven times operating income, so no price passes the EV/OI test. The former CEO's realised pay reached $173.9m in FY2024, and repurchases in FY2025–26 ran clearly above worth under a live $5bn authorisation. A flip needs positive equity or P2-level returns, pay inside the lines, and repurchases held to worth.

## 2. Understanding

Group sentence: TransDigm is a Cleveland-based, 33-year-old US acquirer and operator of about fifty aerospace component businesses that design and make proprietary, FAA- and military-certified parts — actuators, ignition systems, pumps and valves, motors, batteries, sensors, microwave tubes, latches, seat belts, cockpit displays, lavatory and interior parts, parachutes, test equipment — sold to commercial airlines and maintenance shops (directly and through distributors), aircraft and engine makers and their system suppliers, and the US and allied armed forces; about 55% of sales are aftermarket, 37–43% defence, 63% to US customers.

- **Power & Control** (51.6% of FY2025 sales): power and motion components — actuators and controls, ignition and engine technology, pumps and valves, motors and generators, batteries, sensors, hoists and cargo systems, microwave electron devices — paid for by engine and system suppliers, airlines, maintenance providers and military buying agencies; now also PMA replacement parts (JPE, VSA, acquired April 2026).
- **Airframe** (46.6%): non-power airframe and cabin components — latches, rods, connectors and seals, cockpit security and displays, audio and antenna systems, lavatory parts, seat belts, interior surfaces, insulation, lighting, parachutes, test services and instrumentation — paid for by airframe makers, cabin-system suppliers, airlines, maintenance providers and military buyers.
