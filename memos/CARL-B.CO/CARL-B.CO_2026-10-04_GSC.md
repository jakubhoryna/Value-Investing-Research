# CARL-B.CO — Carlsberg A/S: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future ADEQUATE, shareholder-friendliness PASS (one flag: capital-allocation CAUTION), cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past)**.

Carlsberg is a durable, well-disclosed regional brewer with real brand and route-to-market strength, now one-third a soft-drinks bottler after the Britvic purchase. What disqualifies it is the record on the capital it actually employs: across ten years the worst normalized return on capital is 9.5%, and the balance sheet carries liabilities of 4.4 times shareholders' equity after a write-off in Russia, six years of buybacks and a debt-funded acquisition. The verdict flips only if returns on the enlarged base rise clearly above 10% while liabilities fall back under twice equity; at today's price the stock would also be expensive by a wide margin.

## 2. Understanding
Carlsberg brews, bottles and distributes beer (local and international brands) and, since January 2025, branded and PepsiCo-licensed soft drinks, sold mainly through grocery retailers, on-trade outlets and distributors that pay it, in Western Europe, Asia (China, Vietnam, Laos) and Central & Eastern Europe and India; it is a large, long-established, foundation-controlled Danish group mid-way through integrating its largest acquisition. PASS.

## 3. Quality — past record
Worst-of-four average ROCE is 9.5% and FCFROCE 7.2% on the conservative capital base; even the looser reading, which deducts every current accrual, reaches only 10.5% and 7.9%. Returns improved from about 8% in 2016–2020 to about 11% in 2021–2025, but the FY2025 base absorbed DKK 19bn of Britvic goodwill. Operating income per share grew 5.0% a year and FCF per share 2.6%, the latter below the 3% floor; book value per share shrank 3.0% a year, tangible book is negative throughout. Liabilities to equity rose from 144% to 443% (FY2025), helped by the DKK 40.8bn Russia loss in 2023, and no exemption applies. **WEAK** — leverage breach plus sub-10% worst-case returns.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth passes: no customer concentration is disclosed and inputs are commodities with several vendors, though the annual report carries no explicit 10%-customer sentence. Forces: customer power WEAK, supplier power STRONG (PepsiCo licences now underpin about a third of volume and are reassigned between bottlers, as the Royal Unibrew loss shows), substitutes WEAK, new entrants WEAK — one strong force, contained to the soft-drinks leg. The moat is brand — local-champion beer portfolios with entrenched distribution — and is stable. Market growth is mixed: flat beer volumes in Europe, growth in Asia and India. **ADEQUATE**.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
CEO pay of DKK 41.9m is well under 5% of owner earnings on both reads; the chair's DKK 2.19m sits below the US-calibrated floor. The Foundation's 77.5% voting control is a documented caveat; related-party dealings are dividends, research grants and arm's-length sales. Buybacks in 2019–2024 ran at 14–23× earnings measures, never at bargain lines, and stopped for Britvic, bought for about DKK 36bn, roughly 20× its pre-deal operating profit, on debt. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: one material, debt-funded deal whose returns case is unproven. **PASS** with that one flag.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers
- Britvic returns fail to cover the price paid, pinning returns near 10% on a far larger base.
- A PepsiCo licence loss in a major market would remove a third of the volume logic.
- Input-cost inflation in 2027 (aluminium, packaging) squeezes margins while leverage stays high.
- Further losses in war-exposed or hyperinflationary markets; DKK 1.3bn of cash in Ukraine is already unavailable to the Group.
- Hybrid issuance flatters reported leverage without reducing what is owed.
