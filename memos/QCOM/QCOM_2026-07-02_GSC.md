# QCOM — QUALCOMM Incorporated: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | STRONG | ADEQUATE | PASS | EXPENSIVE | **WATCH** |

Qualcomm runs two linked engines: a semiconductor business (QCT, ~87% of revenue) putting Snapdragon SoCs into premium Android handsets, cars and IoT, and a licensing business (QTL, ~13%) collecting a royalty on essentially every 3G/4G/5G device off a standard-essential patent portfolio. That combination produced ex-cash ROCE of 22–84% in most of the last decade and FCF/share compounding ~15%/yr, with a board that shrank the share count by a third. Price is the only thing between here and a BUY — at ~$189.50 the stock trades ~6× above the mechanical cheap band (~2.4× above the economically meaningful EV/OI band). The verdict flips to BUY only on a broad semis/market dislocation dragging price toward the band; it flips to AVOID if the Apple modem/licensing loss (2027–28) materialises and automotive/IoT/data-center diversification fails to fill the hole, taking Future to WEAK. On the shopping list, but the Apple cliff makes this a lower-conviction WATCH than a pure-moat name.

## 2. Understanding

Qualcomm is a US-incorporated (Delaware), NASDAQ-listed, fabless wireless-technology company that (a) designs and sells Snapdragon integrated-circuit platforms and system software — built by third-party foundries — to handset makers (Samsung, Xiaomi and, declining, Apple), automakers and IoT device makers, and (b) licenses its cellular standard-essential patent portfolio to virtually all wireless device makers for a per-device royalty; it earns most revenue in China/Asia, leads premium-mobile SoCs and dominates cellular licensing, and is growing but losing its largest modem customer. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Ex-cash ROCE ≥ 15% in 8 of 10 return years (22–84%), FCFROCE_xc ≥ 8% in all 10; the only breaks (FY2017–18) trace to the KFTC/antitrust fines, the Apple royalty dispute and the aborted NXP deal, all since resolved. OI/share compounded ~12%/yr and FCF/share ~15%/yr over the decade; diluted shares fell 33%. Leverage optically spikes (L/E "n.m." in FY2018 when buybacks + the $2B NXP break-fee drove equity to near-zero) but net-debt/FCF stayed ≤1.4× throughout with double-digit interest coverage — no distress. **STRONG.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **FAILS on customers**: Apple, Samsung and Xiaomi each exceeded 10% of FY2025 revenue, and Apple is designing Qualcomm out — its own modem replaces Qualcomm's across the iPhone line by ~2028 (~$7B hole), with the licensing agreement expiring 2027. Supplier breadth is adequate (multiple foundries). Forces are **MIXED**: the QTL licensing moat (government/patent + ingrainedness — every 5G device needs the SEPs) is wide and durable, but QCT faces MediaTek rivalry, the substitute of customer vertical integration (Apple now; Samsung/Google long-run), and an unproven data-center entry. Moat is **stable in licensing, eroding at the top of chips**. Applying the moat-decides floor, Future holds at **ADEQUATE** — the licensing annuity plus Android/auto/IoT breadth cushion the Apple loss — but this is the weakest ADEQUATE in the book.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

CEO pay $29.7M in FY2025 (+15% while operating income rose +23%), heavily equity/performance-linked; 10× base-salary ownership guideline met. No dual-class — one share, one vote. Related-party transactions minimal (a disclosed employed family member; nothing structural). Buybacks: the FY2018 $22.6B tender at ~$60–67 was disciplined (below intrinsic value and today's price); ongoing repurchases shrink the count, though FY2025's $8.8B at ~$155 avg is far less of a bargain. Dividend grown 12 straight years (~7%/yr), ~30% of FCF — rational given high ROCE. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- **Apple modem cliff (2027–28):** loss of ~$5.7–7B modem revenue plus the QTL Apple royalty on expiry — the single largest thesis risk.
- **Customer vertical integration spreads:** Samsung/Google following Apple in-house would hollow out QCT's premium position.
- **Data-center pivot unproven:** the AI-inference chip push (AI200/AI250; Meta/Microsoft named) is early, competes with Nvidia/AMD/Broadcom, and the multiple already prices optimism.
- **China concentration:** ~60%+ of revenue ships to China; export-control or geopolitical escalation is a direct hit (a China-risk shareholder proposal is on the 2026 ballot).
- **Licensing rate pressure:** ongoing antitrust/regulatory scrutiny of SEP royalty rates could compress QTL margins.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Flips to **BUY** only on a price move into the ~$31 mechanical band (or, on the economically meaningful FCF/OI reading, the ~$78–97 zone) with fundamentals intact — realistically a broad semis/market dislocation. Flips to **AVOID** if the Apple loss materialises *and* diversification visibly fails to replace it, pushing Future to WEAK. Next review: FY2026 quarterly results (watch non-Apple QCT growth and data-center ramp), the 2027 Apple licensing expiry, or price below ~$34 (NEAR).

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers:** *FAIL.* FY2025 10-K: Apple, Samsung and Xiaomi each ≥10% of consolidated revenue. Apple (largest modem customer, ~$5.7–7B) is transitioning to its in-house C1 modem across the iPhone line by ~2028; QTL licensing agreement expires 2027. Concentration is high and the top customer is actively exiting.

**Breadth — suppliers:** *PASS.* Fabless; wafers sourced from multiple leading foundries (TSMC, Samsung). No single-supplier chokepoint of the kind that flags pricing-power loss.

**Five forces:**

| Force | Threat | One-line reason |
|-------|--------|-----------------|
| Customer bargaining power | HIGH | Three ≥10% customers; largest is vertically integrating away. |
| Supplier bargaining power | MEDIUM | Foundry capacity is scarce/leading-edge but multi-sourced. |
| Substitutes | HIGH (chips) / LOW (licensing) | In-house customer modems substitute QCT; SEPs have no substitute for QTL. |
| New entrants | MEDIUM | High SoC R&D barrier, but MediaTek and hyperscaler silicon are real. |
| Rivalry | MEDIUM–HIGH | MediaTek in mid/premium Android; Nvidia/AMD/Broadcom in the data-center adjacency. |

Overall: **MIXED.**

**Moat:** Two-tier. QTL = **government/patent + ingrainedness** (every 3G/4G/5G device needs Qualcomm's standard-essential patents; the royalty is embedded in the industry's cost structure) — wide and **stable**. QCT = **cost/scale + engineering lead** in premium mobile SoCs — real but **eroding at the top** as Apple exits and customer vertical integration advances. Net moat: durable annuity underneath a contested chip franchise.

**Market growth:** MIXED. Premium handset ASPs rising and content-per-device growing (supports QCT), but unit smartphone growth is flat-to-low; automotive, IoT and (speculatively) data-center/robotics are the growth vectors, unproven at scale. Growth is a supporting factor, not the thesis — the licensing moat is.

**Verdict rationale:** Breadth FAIL on customers would mechanically push Future toward WEAK. The moat-decides floor (as applied to ASML/TSMC/BKE) holds it at **ADEQUATE**: the QTL licensing annuity is a genuinely dominant, stable moat that cushions the Apple loss, and non-Apple QCT grew 18% in FY2025 with auto+IoT +27%. But because the moat is strong on only one of the two engines and the other is actively eroding, this is a **borderline ADEQUATE** — materially weaker than a pure-moat name. Flagged for the open MODEL question on dominant-moat-plus-credible-substitute boundary (surfaced earlier by GOOGL/ASML).
