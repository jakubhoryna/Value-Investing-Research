# RRTL.DE — RTL Group S.A.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; shareholder-friendliness FAIL (flag pair: related-party concern + capital-allocation CAUTION); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past, Future and SF)**.

RTL Group is a well-run cash distributor sitting on a shrinking engine. Through FY2019 it earned about 21% on capital employed and paid out nearly everything it made. Since then operating income per share has collapsed: profit from operating activities fell from €1,147m (FY2019) to €204m (FY2025), and FY2025 ROCE was 2.7%. The stock is not cheap on any framework reading: the EV/OI leg admits no positive price at FY2025 operating income, and the FCF leg sits at €15.07, 47% below €28.55. To flip toward WATCH, Sky Deutschland and streaming would have to rebuild operating income to well above €360m, and hold it, while linear advertising keeps shrinking. On top of that, the shareholder-friendliness reading would have to clear.

## 2. Understanding

RTL Group sells audiences to advertisers through free-to-air TV, streaming, digital and radio in Germany, France and Hungary. It also sells subscriptions (RTL+, M6+, Sky in Germany, Austria and Switzerland) to households, and through Fremantle it produces and licenses TV formats and scripted content to broadcasters and platforms worldwide. It is a Luxembourg-incorporated, Frankfurt-listed, MDAX-sized broadcaster, 75% owned by Bertelsmann, in mid-transformation after selling RTL Nederland (2025) and buying Sky Deutschland (June 2026). **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE over FY2016–25 is 14.7% (end-of-period, with cash), and worst FCFROCE is 9.0%. Both averages hide the trajectory. ROCE ran 19.8–21.9% in FY2016–19, then 13.5%, 7.8%, 7.7% and 2.7% from FY2022 to FY2025. FCFROCE fell from 15% to 3.7–7.1%. Operating income per share compounded at −15.7% a year and FCF per share at −9.1%, both far below the 3% floor. Leverage is unremarkable: L/E is 106% at FY2025 and 136% at 30 June 2026. Verdict **WEAK**: a sustained multi-year decline in returns, and negative per-share growth.

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth passes: there are thousands of advertisers and no disclosure of any customer over a tenth of revenue. The supplier side is indeterminate, because Sky's sports-rights costs are undisclosed. Forces: customer power STRONG (agency-bought advertising, TV ad revenue −7.0% in FY2025 and −4.0% in H1 2026); supplier power STRONG (premium sports and studio rights are auctioned by few owners); substitutes STRONG (global streamers and online video); new entrants WEAK (scale, licences and rights libraries). That is three strong forces. The moat (reach and brand in national free TV) is eroding. Market growth is MIXED: linear TV is shrinking and streaming is growing. Verdict **WEAK**.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

The highest-paid executive took €7.47m (the departed deputy CEO, including a €4.0m termination indemnity). That is against 5% of the lower leg of €14.2m (fiscal-year read) and €19.0m (three-year read): no breach. Chair fee €400k, under the floor stop. Related parties weigh heavily. Bertelsmann is RTL's main lender (€600m of term loans), the depositary of its surplus cash (€468m at YE2025), its tax-pooling partner, and in 2022 sold it Gruner + Jahr for €228m: one flag. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: dividends exceeded FCF in each of FY2023–25. The 2025–26 buyback ran at about 20× FY2025 FCF and 33× OI, and net debt reached €1.0bn after the €5.50 dividend. Two flags: **FAIL**.
