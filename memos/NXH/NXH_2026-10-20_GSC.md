# NXH — Neighborhood Intelligence, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future WEAK, shareholder-friendliness FAIL, cheapness EXPENSIVE: **AVOID on three gates**, the same verdict as the prior version, now measured rather than bounded.

Since August the company renamed itself Neighborhood Intelligence (Nasdaq: NXH), bought The Container Store and a Florida home-services business with stock and convertible notes, and sold stock at $2.76; the shares trade at $1.52 (v1: $4.50). Operating income was negative in six of ten years and is −$86.7m over the trailing twelve months. A flip needs years of positive owner earnings, a moat and pay tracking earnings; nothing in the filings points that way.

## 2. Understanding

Neighborhood Intelligence, Inc. (Utah; formerly Overstock.com, Beyond and Bed Bath & Beyond, Inc.) sells home furnishings, décor, storage and organisation products to US consumers. It sells online through the Bed Bath & Beyond, Overstock and buybuy BABY websites, and offline through roughly 300 acquired Kirkland's stores being rebranded and through The Container Store's stores. It also makes Elfa shelving and closet systems in plants in Sweden and Poland, sells installation and home services (SFV Services), and holds a run-off portfolio of blockchain and venture stakes (tZERO, Medici Ventures, GrainChain notes). It is a loss-making serial re-inventor on its fourth name in three years. **PASS.**

One reportable segment (Retail); not a group under P22.

## 3. Quality — past record

The record now runs from filings, FY2016–FY2025. Lens STD; the venture and blockchain stakes sit above the P31 line, so both readings ran. Worst-of-eight ROCE is **−71.9%** (10-year, include reading, beginning-of-period, ex-cash). The 5-year figure is −63.2%. Worst-of-eight FCFROCE is −45.4%. Maintenance capex equals all capex in every year except 2016, 2018, 2020 and 2021, where it is constructed. Σ J1 OI is −$489.9m, positive only in FY2016 and FY2020–22 (the pandemic surge). ΔOI/sh and ΔFCF/sh are n.m. on negative endpoints. The diluted share count grew from 25.4m to 60.1m and is now about 115m. L/E is 95.4% at FY2025 and 195% at June 2026 after the acquisitions. **WEAK — losses.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: no customer concentration (consumers); no supplier above 10% is disclosed. Forces: customers WEAK; suppliers WEAK; **substitutes STRONG**, since the same goods are one click away at Amazon, Wayfair, Target and IKEA; **entrants STRONG**, since nothing guards online or store-based home-goods retail and the company's own losses show there is no barrier to success. Two strong forces is the stop signal. Moat: none. The brands are bought names of failed chains (Bed Bath & Beyond, Kirkland's, The Container Store after its 2024–25 Chapter 11), not a cost, network or switching-cost source. Elfa is a small manufactured line inside the retail segment. Market: MIXED. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

The highest pay figure is Marcus Lemonis's 2024 option award, $7.59m at grant-date value. It was granted in a year when FCF and NI were both negative and trailing negative, and it was above the prior year's highest pay ($6.14m in 2023), so it is a **hard strike**. The 2025 total ($5.93m, Lemonis) is a loss-year flag. The April 2026 TBHC acquisition bought a listed company that lost money in each of its last three pre-deal years: an **ADVERSE** acquisition finding. The 2025 buybacks ($6.2m, 1.6% of shares) bought with no positive earnings behind them and the programme has expired: CAUTION. Management holds ≈ 1.09m shares (0.95%; $1.66m, 0.2× the highest pay figure): NOT ALIGNED, although Lemonis bought 0.45m shares in 2026. **FAIL — compensation strike (step 1), with the acquisition ADVERSE beside it.**
