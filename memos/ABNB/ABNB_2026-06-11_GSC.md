# ABNB — Airbnb, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |

Airbnb is worth owning for its negative-capital business model: the two-sided network plus the brand lets it grow with essentially no capital employed ex-cash, converting ~38% of revenue to free cash flow. What stands between here and a BUY is price — at $132 every multiple misses by a wide margin, and EV/OI of 27× actually sits above the framework's *sell* signal (P9 = 25). Conviction: the quality verdicts flip down if Booking's alternative-accommodations push or city-level regulation visibly erodes booking share, or if the SBC-funded buyback treadmill accelerates; cheapness flips only on a ~65%+ drawdown or several years of compounding with a flat stock.

## 2. Understanding

Airbnb, Inc. is a Delaware-incorporated, San Francisco-headquartered online marketplace — the category-defining leader, still growing ~10%/yr — that connects 5M+ hosts offering short-term and longer-stay accommodations, experiences, and services with guests across 220+ countries, earning service fees paid by both guests and hosts on each booking, with revenues diversified globally across North America, EMEA, Latin America, and Asia-Pacific. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Only six fiscal years of computable returns exist (IPO Dec 2020); 2017–20 were operating-loss years (2020: −$3.6B, COVID). Since 2021, ROCE_wc runs 19–24% (2021: 7.9%) and FCFROCE_wc 42–47%; ex-cash capital employed is negative or negligible in five of six years — guest float and deferred fees fund the business, so ROCE_xc is n.m.-to-effectively-infinite. OI/sh rose 0.70 → 4.08 and FCF/sh 3.71 → 7.38 over 2021–25; TBV/sh rose 6.61 → 11.92 despite ~$11B of buybacks. L/E 149–188%, under the 200% flag, and most liabilities are customer float, not debt. **ADEQUATE** — returns and trajectory are STRONG-grade, but the short window and pre-2021 losses preclude STRONG under P2 (cannot show ≥8 of 10 years).

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: millions of guests and 5M+ hosts pay fees; neither side can consolidate (cloud-infrastructure dependence noted, minor). Forces MIXED: customer and supplier power LOW; substitutes (hotels, Vrbo, direct booking) MEDIUM-HIGH; rivalry HIGH — Booking Holdings is pushing hard into alternative accommodations; entrants MEDIUM behind the network barrier. Moat: **Network** (two-sided liquidity, uniquely cross-border long-tail supply) reinforced by **Brand** ("airbnb" is a verb; ~90% direct/unpaid traffic is a structural customer-acquisition cost edge) — stable overall, eroding at the edges in regulated cities (NYC-style bans). Market growth YES (alternative accommodations, experiences, secular travel), with Marshall's caveat: that growth is exactly what attracts Booking's capital. **ADEQUATE** (MIXED forces cap it below STRONG).

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Comp: CEO salary $1; 2025 reported comp $242K; the 2020 ten-year mega-option grant vests only above share-price hurdles; founders hold ~30% economic interest — strong alignment. **Flag 1:** dual-class control — Class B carries 20 votes/share and a founder voting agreement (Chesky alone 31.9% voting power) structurally subordinates outside owners. RPTs: founder nominating/voting agreements only; no material commercial RPTs. Buybacks: $1.5B→$3.8B/yr (2022–25), shrinking diluted count ~8% — more than dilution sterilisation — but executed at ~17–20× FCF. **Flag 2:** SBC of ~$1.6B/yr (13% of revenue) makes reported FCF flatter than owner reality. Dividends: none — rational at these reinvestment returns; M&A minimal. **PASS**, no strike; both flags recorded.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Booking Holdings' scale and loyalty flywheel takes alternative-accommodations share, eroding the network moat from the demand side.
- Regulatory contagion: more NYC-style short-term-rental bans in major cities shrink supply where it matters most.
- SBC treadmill: $1.6B/yr added back to CFO means framework FCF overstates owner cash flow by roughly a third.
- 2025 margin compression (operating margin 23.0% → 20.8%) as spend ramps into Experiences/Services; if the new verticals earn lower returns, quality dilutes.
- 2023's $2.7B one-off tax benefit distorts net-income comparisons; judge on OI and FCF only.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To BUY: price below ≈ $43, or fundamentals compounding the band upward (each year of OI/FCF growth lifts it ~10%) until they meet a drawdown. To AVOID: demonstrable booking-share loss to Booking, moat-relevant regulation in several top markets, or buybacks accelerating at rich prices while growth stalls. Next review: FY2026 results (~Feb 2027), price under ~$55 (NEAR band), or a major-market regulatory ruling.
