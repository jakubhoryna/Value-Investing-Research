This is a genuinely good business failing the framework on the arithmetic of its own capital deployment. ASSA ABLOY compounded operating income per share at 10.2% and FCF per share at 11.5% a year over the decade on a share count that has not moved since 2016 — no buybacks, no dilution, 1,110.8m diluted shares in every year of the window. But returns on capital employed have fallen for three consecutive years on the B2 beginning-of-period series (16.6% → 16.4% → 14.2% → 11.8%), corroborated by the company's own published ROCE (16.9% → 15.6% → 14.4% → 14.2%) and by FCFROCE (14.1% → 11.0% → 9.5%). The worst of the four normalized ten-year averages is 12.5% ROCE — comfortably above the ADEQUATE floor, comfortably below STRONG. Under P4 a sustained multi-year decline in returns is WEAK regardless of level, and the cause is not a mystery: roughly SEK 48bn went into Hardware & Home Improvement in 2023, and the asset has since annualised about SEK 1.3bn of EBIT in ASSA's hands. The price does not matter yet — at SEK 347 the shares trade at 19.5× EV/OI and 20.7× MCap/FCF against thresholds of 7× and 8×, roughly four times the band. What would flip this is FY2026–27 showing returns inflecting upward with HHI integrated and the restructuring programme finished; nothing about the moat or the growth record is in question.

## 2. Understanding

ASSA ABLOY designs and manufactures mechanical and electromechanical locks, cylinders, door hardware, entrance automation, and identity credentials, sold mainly to security wholesalers, locksmiths, systems integrators, OEMs and big-box retailers (not end users) across commercial, institutional and residential construction and its much larger aftermarket, as a Swedish-listed, dual-class-controlled, acquisition-led group operating five divisions in over 70 countries and standing as the world's largest supplier of access solutions. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ten-year ROCE 12.5% (end-of-period, with cash) and FCFROCE 9.8%; the four readings span 12.5–13.9% and 9.8–10.9%. Growth is strong and consistent: OI/share +10.2% and FCF/share +11.5% geometric mean over FY2016–25, both far above the 3% nominal floor; BV/share +8.9%. Leverage is unremarkable at 105% liabilities-to-equity, well inside the 200% flag. The problem is direction, not level: three consecutive down years in ROCE and two in FCFROCE, driven by the 2023 HHI purchase entering capital employed at full price. **WEAK** — P4's sustained-decline condition, not the level test.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes on both sides: the company discloses that no single customer exceeds 2% of Group sales, and the supplier base is explicitly described as scattered across the world with no concentration disclosed. Four forces, all called WEAK: customer power (sub-2% concentration, specification-driven channel, backward integration implausible — Americas residential big-box is a logged segment exception), supplier power (fragmented, commodity inputs), substitutes (the certified physical barrier cannot be done without; digital credentials are being absorbed through HID and Yale rather than displacing), new entrants (scale in a fragmented industry, fire and security certification, master-key and credential installed bases). Tally 0 strong. Moat: **ingrainedness** — the channel, not the end user, chooses the brand — stable, with switching costs reinforcing in Global Technologies. Market growth YES. **STRONG.**

## C. Calculation notes

**FY2025 capital employed build.** Total assets 208,371 − NIBCL 26,320 (trade payables 11,030 + accrued expenses and deferred income 15,290) = CE_wc 182,051; less cash and equivalents 1,398 + short-term investments 6 = CE_xc 180,647.

**NIBCL burden-of-proof calls (§2·1A).** Deducted: trade payables and accrued expenses/deferred income. **Left in capital employed:** other current liabilities (SEK 5,396m in 2025 — composition not disclosed line by line, so the item bears the burden and stays in), current provisions (824), derivative financial instruments (303), and — as a uniformity call — current tax liabilities (1,177 in 2025). Current tax liabilities are a default NIBCL component under §2·1A, but they could not be sourced from a primary balance sheet for FY2021–23; deducting them only where visible would put a break in the eleven-year series. Leaving them in throughout is the conservative direction (larger CE, lower ROCE) and moves the worst-of-four average by under 0.1pp. Short-term and current-portion borrowings and all lease liabilities stay in CE as debt.

**Goodwill.** Retained in capital employed. ASSA ABLOY is a serial acquirer — 23 businesses in 2025, 26 in 2024, and HHI in 2023 — so the non-acquisitive deduction permitted by §2·1A is unavailable, and deducting it would be the single largest possible distortion of this analysis: goodwill and intangibles are SEK 137,958m against SEK 182,051m of capital employed.

**Leases.** IFRS 16 applies from FY2019; lease liabilities (6,615 in 2025) are treated as debt-equivalent in CE, EV and L/E. For the pre-adoption years the P18 test is run and fails to trigger: disclosed operating-lease obligations at 31 December 2018 were SEK 4,144m against total assets of 106,568m = 3.9%, well under the 10% threshold. FY2015–18 are therefore left uncapitalised, and the FY2019 adoption artifact (right-of-use assets 3,731 appearing in CE) is noted rather than smoothed.

**IFRS interest reclassification.** Not required. ASSA ABLOY has classified interest paid inside cash flow from operating activities in every year of the window (FY2025: −3,282; FY2020: −699; FY2016: −613), so CFO needs no adjustment before deriving FCF.

**FCF basis.** All-capex throughout: CFO − investments in property, plant and equipment and intangible assets (gross, not the company's "net capital expenditure" measure, which nets disposals). P20 is **not** active — capex has run at 1.4–1.9% of sales for a decade with no ramp, no expansion-labelled spend and no guided step-down, so no maintenance basis is constructible or warranted.

**Operating income.** As reported, including FY2018's SEK 5,595m impairment and the FY2016 and FY2025 restructuring programmes. Management's ex-items measure (FY2025: 24,664, 16.2% margin) is stated for context only and is never used in a gate.

**Captive finance / substitution rule.** Neither applies. ASSA ABLOY has no material finance arm and is not a securities-heavy holdco or lender; the standard OI/FCF/CE machinery is appropriate.

**Bias check.** *Affinity and consensus* were the live hazards — this is a widely admired thirty-year compounder with an unblemished narrative, and the temptation was to read a three-year return decline as an accounting artifact of one deal rather than as the metric doing its job. Handled by testing the decline on three independent bases (my beginning-of-period series, my FCFROCE series, and the company's own published ROCE definition) and finding it on all three. *Miscontrast* was live in the opposite direction on price: 19.5× EV/OI looks cheap beside a US listed-industrials peer set and is nonetheless nearly three times the framework's threshold. The counter-reading is logged rather than suppressed: on the end-of-period basis FY2025 stabilised (12.4% → 12.7%), and adding back the FY2025 restructuring charge would lift the beginning-basis reading to roughly 12.6% and break the third leg of the decline. Neither changes the verdict; both are the flip conditions named in §8.
