# 7309.T — Shimano: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; Shareholder-friendliness PASS (1 flag — capital-allocation CAUTION); Cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** (Past and Future).

Shimano is the dominant maker of bicycle drivetrains and brakes with a second franchise in fishing reels, a 92.5% equity ratio and ¥464bn of cash — half its balance sheet. What fails is the trajectory, not the history: operating income per share is lower in FY2025 than in FY2016, return on capital has fallen three years running from the FY2022 boom peak, and the bicycle segment now earns 11–12% where it earned 20–22% before the pandemic, on higher sales. The FY2025 annual report also names, for the first time, a single customer — the German distributor Paul Lange & Co. — above a tenth of revenue. The verdict flips only if bicycle margins recover to the high teens on a sustained basis (rebuilding the per-share record) and the customer disclosure returns to "none over 10%"; even then the stock at 82× free cash flow is far from the band.

## 2. Understanding
Shimano designs and manufactures bicycle components (gears, shifters, brakes, hubs, cranks and e-bike drive units — 76% of FY2025 sales) and fishing tackle (reels and rods — 24%), sold mainly to bicycle and tackle makers and to distributors and retailers for the aftermarket, from plants in Japan, Singapore, Malaysia, China and Czechia to customers chiefly in Europe (44%) and Asia (31%); a Sakai-based, founder-family-led company founded in 1921, at the scale of ¥466bn of sales. **PASS**.

## 3. Quality — past record
Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **13.6%** (end-of-period, with cash; ex-cash reads 30.9–33.5%); worst-of-four FCFROCE **8.4%**. Both clear the ADEQUATE floor in aggregate, but the series is a boom and a slide: ROCE 22.5% in FY2022, then 10.1%, 7.2%, 5.8%; FCFROCE 11.1% to 2.0%. Operating income per share compounds at **−1.8%/yr** FY2016→FY2025 and FCF per share at **−5.6%/yr**; book value per share grows 10.0%/yr only because profits are retained as cash. L/E 7.9%. **WEAK** — negative per-share earnings growth and a sustained three-year decline in returns.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth **FAIL**: the FY2025 yūhō discloses Paul Lange & Co. OHG at ¥54.0bn, 11.6% of revenue; a distributor is replaceable, so the captive-customer waiver does not apply. Forces: customer power WEAK (thousands of bicycle makers and dealers, components a small share of bike cost); supplier power WEAK (commodity metals, in-house making); substitutes WEAK; new entrants **STRONG** — SRAM took the wireless high end and Chinese makers (L-Twoo, Wheeltop, Sensah) now sell electronic shifting well below Shimano's price. Tally 1 strong. Moat: ingrainedness — specification on bikes and dealer familiarity — eroding on the margin evidence. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK**.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
Top pay ¥122m each for the chairman and president (Shimano family) against ¥901m and ¥2,379m caps on the year and three-year reads; outside officers average ≈ ¥11.6m. Directors hold 0.9% directly, and family-linked holding companies another ≈ 11.7% (presumed). Related-party purchases ¥199m, 0.07% of cost of sales. Capital allocation **CAUTION**: ¥169bn repurchased FY2021–H1 2026 at 11–25× all-cash EV/OI with no worth criterion, and the ¥50bn programme live at ≈ 18.5×; funded from idle cash, not debt. **PASS** on one flag.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- The distributor concentration deepens, or a second customer crosses 10%.
- Yen strength cuts both reported sales and the yen value of the overseas cash pile; FY2025 carried a ¥19.6bn exchange loss.
- Repurchases continue above ≈ 20× operating income without a price discipline while operating income is still falling.
- A further product recall: the 2023 crank inspection programme put ¥16bn of warranty provisions on the balance sheet.
