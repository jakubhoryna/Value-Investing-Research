A competent, conservatively financed Tier-1 supplier that does not earn its cost of capital and cannot set its own prices. Worst-of-four normalised ROCE over FY2018/19–FY2025 is 7.1% against a 10% ADEQUATE floor, FCFROCE 2.3% against 8%. Operating income per share has compounded at −7.7% a period since FY2017/18, €5.17 to €2.73, while capex plus capitalised development ran €0.6–0.7bn a year against €0.3–0.5bn of EBIT: the capital goes in, the returns do not come out. Gate 1B fails independently — one customer was 22.6% of 2025 sales, and the captive-customer waiver is unavailable because OEMs dual-source at every nomination and no dominant moat protects the position. Shareholder-friendliness passes cleanly, which is worth recording but rescues nothing. The gates are structural, not price-dependent.

## 2. Understanding

HELLA develops and manufactures vehicle lighting (headlamps, rear combination lamps, body and interior lighting) and automotive electronics (sensors, actuators, body-control and energy-management units, radar) as engineered, programme-specific components sold under multi-year nomination contracts to global vehicle manufacturers, alongside a smaller Lifecycle Solutions arm selling HELLA-branded spare parts, workshop diagnostics equipment and special-vehicle lighting to independent aftermarket distributors and workshops; it is a Lippstadt-headquartered German KGaA (partnership limited by shares, managed by a general partner and supervised by a Shareholder Committee) 81.59% owned by FORVIA SE, with ~34,000 employees, €7.86bn of 2025 sales and manufacturing across Europe, Asia and the Americas — a mid-sized, technologically credible but price-taking Tier-1 inside a larger listed group. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Eight fiscal periods carry a full capital-employed series (FY2018/19–FY2025; the fiscal year moved to calendar in 2023 via a seven-month stub). Worst-of-four normalised ROCE **7.1%** (best basis, ex-cash, 10.0%); worst-of-four FCFROCE **2.3%** (best 3.2%). Annualising the stub lifts the worst reads only to 7.8% and 2.7% — basis-independent. No trend to lean on: 18.2% in FY2018/19 (flattered by a €286m disposal gain), −6.8% in FY2019/20, 6–9% every year since. ΔOI/sh −7.7% a period. Leverage is the one clean line: L/E 136%, net financial liquidity €381m. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **FAILS**: the segment note shows one customer at 22.6% of 2025 sales (20.0% in 2024). Waiver refused — OEMs resource programmes, switching costs reset at each nomination, no dominant moat. Supplier breadth is nominally broad but the effective chokepoints are not; the company names semiconductor export restrictions (Nexperia) and rare earths as live procurement risks. Forces: customer power **STRONG** (price-downs, multi-sourcing), supplier power **STRONG** (chokepoints), substitutes WEAK, new entrants **STRONG** (Chinese suppliers taking share; Lighting −8.2% in 2025). Tally 3 — the stop signal twice over. Moat: brand ingrainedness in Lifecycle Solutions (12% of sales), none in the other 88%; overall **eroding**. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17: highest-paid individual (CEO Schäferbarthold) €3.596m all-in = 4.32% of net income, under the 5%-of-lower-leg strike and far under the ~$40m absolute trigger — tight enough to name, not a strike. Highest outside seat €360k (Shareholder Committee chair) versus a ≈€610k CPI-indexed floor stop. P19: no repurchases in any window year (a 10% authorisation stands unused); no material acquisition, goodwill €4.8m. Related parties: FORVIA sales €78.4m (1.0% of revenue), purchases €56.6m (0.75% of the cost base), at market terms. Dividends ~30% of earnings, HBPO proceeds returned as a special. **PASS**, with a documented caveat on the 81.59%-controlled KGaA structure.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- FORVIA holds 81.59%; the credit facility contemplates a squeeze-out or domination agreement — a minority buyout at a price the minority does not set.
- Lighting (46% of sales) is losing volume to Chinese competitors, −8.2% in 2025; Electronics growth has not offset it.
- Capitalised development (€259m in 2025, rising) flatters earnings against cash and depresses tangible book.
- European vehicle production is the demand anchor and is structurally soft; tariffs are a named company risk.
- One customer at 22.6% of sales makes a single sourcing decision a group-level earnings event.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Past flips to ADEQUATE only on worst-of-four ROCE sustained above 10% — roughly €520m of EBIT on the current capital base, 71% above FY2025 — with capex normalising below depreciation. Future flips only if customer concentration falls below 10%, for which there is no visible path. Cheapness is academic until then. Next review: FY2026 annual report (~March 2027), or immediately on a FORVIA squeeze-out or domination agreement, which changes the security rather than the business.

## D. Future-performance worksheet
