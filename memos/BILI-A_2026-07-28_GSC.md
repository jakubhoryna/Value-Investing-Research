# BILI-A — Bilia AB: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Bilia runs an attractive service annuity (~70% of operational earnings) but fails all three quality gates. Past: worst-of-four 10y ROCE 10.5%, FCFROCE 0.9%, a three-year returns fade (16% → 7.7%), and L/E 289–405% every year with no P6 relief. Future: supplier breadth fails — five OEM general agents hold unilateral termination rights, and Volvo exercised them in November 2020, forcing Bilia out of Oslo, Germany and twelve facilities. SF: a P17 normalized-read comp flag pairs with a P19 flagged repurchase record. Price is moot: 23.5× FCF, 17.7× EV/OI. The AVOID is structural, not cyclical.

## 2. Understanding

Bilia sells new and used cars, transport vehicles and trucks under ~20 OEM franchise/agent authorisations (Volvo, BMW, Toyota, Mercedes-Benz, VW largest) and — the profit engine — services them through ~190 authorised workshops plus Swedish fuel/car-wash sites; customers are retail consumers and corporate fleets paying for vehicles, workshop hours, parts, tyre hotels and financing brokerage; industry: auto retail and aftermarket in Sweden (65% of revenue), Norway (24%), Luxembourg and Belgium; form: listed Swedish AB, ~30% Qviberg/Öresund-controlled, a franchised dealer group owning leased-out fleets and selling cars with guaranteed-residual repurchase commitments; listed since 1984, one of Europe's largest dealer groups, now absorbing the OEMs' agency-model shift. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four 10y normalized ROCE **10.5%**; worst-of-four FCFROCE **0.9%** — strict FCF (CFO − all capex incl. leased-vehicle purchases, P18-adjusted 2016–18) was negative in five of ten years. Returns in sustained decline: beg-CE ROCE 16.3% (2021) → 9.5% → 7.8% → 7.7% (2025), below every pre-COVID year. ΔOI/sh geo +5.0% clears the 3% floor; ΔFCF/sh n.m.; ΔBV/sh +9.1%. L/E breaches P5 all ten years (289–405%, leases in numerator); P6 conditions fail. **WEAK** — leverage breach plus returns fade.

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth PASS (retail/fleet, none >10%). Supplier breadth **FAIL**: new-car supply concentrates in five OEM general agents, each far above 10% of COGS (Volvo alone ~20% of 2020 turnover); no ready-alternative exception — authorisations are granted, and revocable, by the OEM, as Volvo's 2020 blanket termination proved. Forces: customer power WEAK; **supplier power STRONG** (demonstrated termination; agency-model forward integration); substitutes WEAK-to-moderate; new entrants WEAK (franchise permits, scale). Tally 1 strong, but breadth FAIL alone sets the verdict. Moat: none owned — the service annuity rides on revocable OEM authorisations; eroding. Market MIXED (stable parc; EVs need less service). **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

- L/E ~300% with leases leaves little cushion in a car-demand recession.

## C. Calculation notes

**FY2025 CE build (SEK m):** Total assets 20,229 − NIBCL 4,055 = **CE_wc 16,174**; − cash & STI 438 = **CE_xc 15,736**. NIBCL = trade payables 1,641 + accrued expenses & deferred income 1,633 + current tax liabilities 66 + current repurchase-agreement liability 715. **NIBCL rule (uniform, all 11 balance years):** the current "liability for cars sold with repurchase agreements" is deferred-income in substance (unrecognised sale proceeds matched by leased-vehicle assets) and is disclosed every year → included. VAT, personnel/source-tax items, customer advances, dividend payable and "other" stay in CE: dividend payable is financing, the rest are either ambiguous or not uniformly disclosed (AR2022 collapses the split for 2021–22) — burden of proof unmet; conservative. Sensitivity: adding the fuller bucket (~SEK 0.5–1.0bn/yr) lifts worst-of-four ROCE to ≈ 11%; verdict unchanged (leverage still fails).

**P18 lease capitalisation (2015–2018):** undiscounted commitments 3,089 / 3,102 / 3,054 / 3,787 — 30–42% of total assets, far above the 10% trigger. PV factor **0.692**, calibrated to Bilia's own IFRS-16 transition (opening liability 2,620 on 1 Jan 2019 vs 3,787 commitments at end-2018); a mechanical PV at the ~2.8% blended disclosed lease rate (SEK 2.47%, NOK 3.79%) would overshoot the company's own measurement by ~30% (commitment tables include terms beyond IFRS-16 lease-term judgments) — judgment call, logged. Imputed interest = 2.8% × PV (60/59/73); OI_adj = OI + imputed interest; CFO_adj = CFO + lease expense (448/464/509) − imputed interest; liability added to total liabilities and assets (RoU ≈ liability).

**FCF basis:** strict CFO − gross capex including leased-vehicle acquisitions (framework default; no company maintenance-capex disclosure in the book's sense — the AR's "replacement investments ~SEK 50m" line covers PP&E categories only and is rejected as a maintenance basis; P20 conditions absent — no super-cycle). Logged alternative (fleet-netted: + disposals of leased vehicles, +0.7–1.3bn/yr): worst-of-four FCFROCE rises to ≈ 7%, still below P2's 8%, and leverage still fails — **verdict robust to this call**. IFRS interest classification: Bilia's CFO starts at profit before tax with no interest add-back → interest paid already burdens CFO; no reclassification needed.
