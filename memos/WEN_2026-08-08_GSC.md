# WEN — The Wendy's Company: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS · Past **WEAK** · Future **WEAK** · Shareholder-friendliness **PASS** (one flag) · Cheapness **EXPENSIVE** → **AVOID** (Past and Future gates).

Wendy's fails on quality, not conduct. Ten years of operating income ran $315m → $343m — under one percent a year — on a capital base that grew $3.9bn → $5.0bn, for a worst-of-four return of **6.7%**; liabilities stand at **4,122% of book equity** after a decade in which distributions consumed the equity account. The franchise is contracting: US same-restaurant sales −5.6% in 2025 and −7.0% in Q2 2026, 245 net closures year to date, dividend halved, outlook withdrawn. At $7.51 the equity screens cheap on free cash flow at 5.9×; the $3.4bn of debt and leases above it makes the enterprise unownable at any price.

## 2. Understanding

Wendy's franchises and operates quick-service hamburger restaurants, earning most of its money as royalties, rents and franchise fees from roughly a thousand independent franchisees, plus sales from the minority it operates itself, across some 7,000 locations concentrated in the United States. Mid-cap US-listed franchisor financed through securitised whole-business debt, contracting at home while new management formulates a turnaround. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized average ROCE is **6.70%** over the full ten-year window (end-period with-cash; beginning-period 6.83%, ex-cash near 7.2%) — a third short of the floor. Operating income compounded at **+0.97%**/yr, $315m FY2016 → $343.5m FY2025; the last two years are declines (382 → 371 → 343.5), so **a third at FY2026 fires P4**. FCF return is bounded near 4.7–5.1%. Leverage stops being arguable: liabilities of $4.84bn against book equity of $117.4m is **L/E 4,122%**, roughly six times the cap, and the P6 let-out requires all other metrics to be great — they are not. **WEAK** on two independently sufficient limbs.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both sides — no franchisee or customer plausibly reaches a tenth of consolidated revenue, and proteins and produce come through a purchasing co-operative with many competing suppliers behind it. Customer power **STRONG**: diners face no switching cost, and a 7.0% same-restaurant decline against a value war is demonstration rather than theory. Substitutes **STRONG**: every competing chain, fast-casual format, grocery prepared food and delivery aggregator substitutes directly, and share is visibly moving. Supplier and new-entrant power **WEAK**. **Tally 2 strong.** Moat Brand, **eroding**: systemwide sales −3.5% in 2025, −6.5% in Q2 2026, guidance withdrawn. Market growth NO. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation passes cleanly: the highest fiscal-2025 total was **$4.59m** to departing CEO Kirk Tanner (interim CEO Ken Cook $4.50m) against a fiscal-year ceiling of **$8.26m** — five percent of net income, the lower leg — with the trailing ceiling bounded above $8.25m on any plausible FY2023 figure. Both reads pass with room. Capital allocation draws **CAUTION**: $329.6m returned in FY2025 against $242.6m of free cash flow — **136%** — after 108% in FY2024, funded by drawing cash from $450.5m to $300.8m while same-restaurant sales fell, corrected only in August 2026 by halving the dividend. Related-party transactions are disclosed with a Trian-affiliated director and a family-owned franchisee, neither sized, so the finding is **INDETERMINATE** and cannot pair. One flag stands alone. **PASS.**

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customer. PASS.** Wendy's is paid principally by franchisees — roughly a thousand independent businesses operating about 85% of the system — supplemented by consumer sales at company restaurants and by advertising-fund contributions. No single franchisee plausibly reaches a tenth of consolidated revenue at that fragmentation. The disclosure sentence was not directly retrieved and this is flagged; the structural read is secure.

**Breadth — supplier. PASS, with the effective-supplier note.** Purchasing runs through a system co-operative that aggregates beef, chicken, potatoes, produce, packaging and equipment. Read naively that is a single supplier; read properly it is a buying agent owned by the system itself, behind which sit many competing protein processors and distributors. The §2·1B instruction to look through nominal counts to the effective supplier cuts *in favour* here rather than against: the co-op is the franchise system's own bargaining vehicle, not a counterparty with power over it.

| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | Quick-service diners have zero switching costs and demonstrated price elasticity — a 7.0% US same-restaurant decline through a value war is the evidence, not the hypothesis. |

**Moat.** Source: **Brand** — Wendy's is a nationally known name with a distinct product position (fresh beef, a specific value construct) that has historically supported franchisee unit economics and therefore system growth. Direction: **eroding**, and not marginally. The one genuine counterweight, recorded because the model requires evidence to be weighed and not selected: **international is working** — systemwide sales +8.1% in 2025 and +3.4% in the second quarter of 2026, with positive same-restaurant sales. The repurchase authorisation had approximately **$35.0m remaining as of 6 February 2026 and expires in February 2027** — a small residual, not a fresh authorisation. The 2026 outlook has been withdrawn. There is no published price discipline for repurchases. Taken together: the over-distribution **is not live and is not expected to continue** — it has been corrected, under duress, by the company itself.
