## 2. Understanding

Winmark licenses five retail brands — Plato's Closet, Once Upon a Child, Play It Again Sports, Style Encore, Music Go Round — to independent owners running resale stores that buy used clothing, children's goods and sporting equipment from the public for cash and resell locally, and is paid principally a percentage of those stores' sales as royalties. Very small-cap Minneapolis franchisor; no stores of its own; negative book equity by design after decades of distributing more than it earned. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Returns are barely measurable in the ordinary way: FY2025 earned **$54.6m of operating income on roughly $19.2m of capital employed** — 284% with cash, 612% excluding it — because a royalty franchisor carries almost no assets; FCF return **233%**. Any worst-of-four sits far above the strong line, but these are artifacts of an asset-light structure, not comparable returns. Leverage n.m. — book equity **negative $53.7m** — so substitutes apply: net debt $49.7m = **1.11×** FCF, the deficit from distributions, not losses. **The binding limb is growth:** operating income +**1.55%**/yr FY2021–25, per-share **+2.4%**, both under the 3% floor. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both sides — 1,378 franchisees, none material individually, and essentially no supplier base. Customer power **WEAK**: franchisees sign multi-year agreements with no collective leverage over the royalty rate. Supplier power **WEAK**. Substitutes **WEAK**: online resale marketplaces compete for the same goods but demand listing labour, shipping and delayed payment where Winmark's stores pay cash on the spot and sort inventory for the buyer; royalties still grew 5.8% in FY2025. New entrants **WEAK**: resale is intensely local, and density must be built store by store against a thirty-year incumbent. **Tally 0 strong.** Moat: a **Network** effect, sellers creating the inventory that draws buyers and buyers the traffic that draws sellers — **stable**. Market growth YES. **STRONG.**

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customer. PASS.** Winmark's paying customers are its franchisees: **1,378 stores in operation** across five brands, plus 82 awarded and unopened. Royalties are a percentage of each store's retail sales. No single franchisee can approach a tenth of $76.4m of royalty revenue at that fragmentation, and no franchisee group is disclosed as material.

**Breadth — supplier. PASS.** There is essentially nothing to supply. | Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | WEAK | Franchisees are individually small, contractually committed for multi-year terms, and have no collective mechanism to press the royalty rate. |

**Tally: 0 strong — favourable.**

**Moat.** Source: **Network.** Each store is a two-sided local market: sellers bringing goods in create the inventory that draws buyers, and buyer traffic is what makes it worth a seller's trip. That loop is per-store and per-neighbourhood, which is why national online scale does not straightforwardly beat it and why an entrant must rebuild it in each location. Direction: **stable** — the franchise count and royalty base are growing modestly, and the format has now survived two decades of online marketplace competition.

The honest counterweight: **a stable moat is not the same as a widening one.** Operating income compounding at 1.55% a year while secondhand retail grows far faster says Winmark is defending its position rather than extending it. That observation is what caps the past verdict at ADEQUATE, and it belongs in the risk list rather than in the moat direction, because the evidence of erosion — falling royalties, closing stores, rate pressure — is absent.

**Market growth. YES.** Secondhand and resale retail is expanding structurally on both value-seeking and sustainability grounds.

**Verdict: STRONG** — breadth passes, zero strong forces, and a durable, stable moat. The only STRONG future rating produced in this run.
