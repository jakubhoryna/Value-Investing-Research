Axfood is the kind of business the watchlist exists for: Sweden's structural share-gainer in food retail (17% → 25% of the market over two decades, eleven consecutive years of gains), run on an integrated purchasing-and-logistics cost engine (Dagab) behind the country's leading discount banner (Willys), with a decade of ~17–21% Marshall-basis ROCE, FCFROCE above 20%, and ~83% of earnings paid out as a rising dividend by a committed 50.1% family owner. What stands between here and a BUY is price — and the gap is enormous: at SEK 268.70 the lease-loaded EV runs to 20.8× operating income against the 7× bar, so the binding trigger sits near SEK 46 (+478% distance); even the friendlier FCF leg (11.6×) needs SEK 185. Two soft spots keep the verdict honest: returns stepped down to 13–14% in 2024–25 on the loss-making City Gross consolidation (Past capped at ADEQUATE), and the only buybacks Axfood does are explicit LTIP-delivery hedges executed at 18–23× EV/OI — de-minimis in scale (SEK 25–115m/yr) and treated as one flag per the NVS/ADS precedent, but a strict book-pure P19 reading would strike them and flip the verdict to AVOID.

## 2. Understanding

Axfood is Sweden's #2 food retailer-wholesaler — a Stockholm-headquartered, Axel Johnson-controlled (50.1%) listed AB selling everyday groceries (commodity staples differentiated by banner price-position and a large private-label range) through group-owned discount (Willys), traditional (Hemköp), hypermarket (City Gross) and foodservice cash-and-carry (Snabbgross) chains, franchise/retailer-owned stores (Hemköp, Tempo, Handlar'n) and online, all supplied by its own purchasing-and-logistics arm Dagab, which also wholesales to independent convenience retailers; customers are Swedish households plus restaurants and independent merchants, and the 25-year-old, 25%-share incumbent is currently automating its national warehouse network. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

On the lease-consistent decade (2015–18 capitalised per P18 at the 2% IBR), the worst-of-four normalized averages are ROCE 16.6% and FCFROCE 21.1% — both clear the STRONG bars (15%/8%). Per-share growth: OI/sh +6.2% and FCF/sh +5.2% geometric, above the 3% floor. L/E 387% breaches P5 but is excused under P6's parenthetical: equity is lean because ~80% of earnings are rationally distributed; net debt ex-IFRS-16 is 0.4× EBITDA. The cap is the trend: ROCE held a 17–21% plateau through 2023, then stepped to 13.2%/13.9% in 2024–25 as the loss-making City Gross added ~SEK 4bn of capital employed — the no-decline condition isn't met cleanly. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Future

Breadth PASS both sides: millions of paying households and no supplier near 10% of COGS+opex (largest branded suppliers partly overlap private-label production; Dagab dual-sources). Forces, binary: customer power WEAK (fragmented households, no leverage); supplier power WEAK (broad base, 25% national share, private-label substitution); substitutes WEAK (food is non-discretionary; restaurants/meal-kits cost more); new entrants WEAK (Lidl needed two decades for ~6%; sites, planning permits, logistics scale). Tally 0 strong. Moat: **Cost** — Dagab's integrated purchasing/logistics scale behind the discount price position; strengthening (share gains, warehouse automation). Market growth YES, modest. **STRONG.**
