# 6752.T — Panasonic Holdings Corporation: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding **PASS** (per-unit route only, ~60% — the weakest instance in the register), Past **WEAK**, Future **WEAK**, SF **PASS** with one flag, Cheapness **EXPENSIVE** — verdict **AVOID**, carried independently by Past and by Future.

Panasonic owns two good assets — a century-old ingrainedness in the Japanese electrical-construction channel, and a US battery footprint few can replicate — wrapped in five businesses that earn little. What stands between here and a BUY is not price alone: six years of returns averaging **4.56%** on capital employed, three of four forces against the group, and a price tripled in a year to 50× the last audited operating income. The verdict flips only on a durable returns recovery, and even then Future needs a force call to change.

## 2. Understanding

*Group sentence:* Panasonic Holdings is a Japanese holding company that, through seven units, makes supply-chain software, avionics and rugged computers; wiring devices and lighting; air-conditioners and cold-chain equipment; lithium-ion cells; electronic components; appliances; and detached houses — for enterprises, electrical wholesalers, one dominant EV maker, set-makers, retailers and homebuyers, worldwide, in industries from declining to supply-constrained. Not unambiguous: the group test **FAILS**.

**PASS is carried entirely by MODEL's per-unit route** ("one sentence per unit if more equally weighted"), as at 6501.T and 7011.T, and I rely on it explicitly. No unit reaches 18% of revenue; five of seven are describable in one sentence; **Connect (17.2%, six limbs) and "Other" (17.6%, a housebuilder plus a consumer-electronics unit) are not** — two irreducible portfolios, ~35% of revenue, against one each at Hitachi and MHI. A session refusing the route prints **DNAF**; on register consistency (BRK.B, SIE.DE, 6503.T, 6501.T, 7011.T) **PASS** is repeatable. Worksheet in D1.

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **4.56%** (reads 4.56 / 4.97 / 5.54 / 6.16%) and worst-of-four FCFROCE **1.77%**, on a six-year IFRS window. The 10% floor is missed by 544bp; no NIBCL treatment closes it — stripping the entire "other current liabilities" balance reaches only ≈5.4%. Per-share growth fails twice: ΔOI/sh geometric mean **−1.78%**, ΔFCF/sh **−65.5%**, both below the 3% nominal floor. Leverage is the one clean part: L/E **91.9%**, falling every year from 157.2%, net debt ≈¥0.6tn against ¥5.2tn of book equity. **WEAK** — on level, not on a judgment call; and STRONG was unavailable anyway on a history-limited window.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASSES** on both legs: no IFRS 8 major-customer paragraph appears in any retrieved filing, and the arithmetic ceiling on the Tesla relationship is the whole Energy segment at **12.2%** of revenue — which would require Tesla to be 100% of a segment that also sells data-centre storage and consumer cells. Four forces: customer power **STRONG**, supplier power **WEAK**, substitutes **STRONG**, new entrants **STRONG** — **three strong, WEAK on the tally alone.** Moat: one source, **ingrainedness** in the Japanese electrical-construction channel, **stable**, covering ~14% of revenue and unevidenced in group returns. Market growth **MIXED**. **WEAK.**

### C2. The OI-line question — tenth instance, and the first where the label rule and the proposed substantive rule agree cleanly

**Panasonic presents a line labelled 営業利益 / "Operating profit", and it is the conservative one.** Under IFRS the group's income statement runs net sales → cost of sales → gross profit → SG&A → other income → other expenses → **operating profit**, so the reported subtotal *includes* restructuring, impairment and disposal items rather than excluding them. Per the ANALYSE brief's Japan note, this filer is precisely the case where "the convention does not bite," and it is worth one line for the standing MODEL item:

- **Label rule:** an object exists — "Operating profit" — and it is used. ¥236,407m in FY3/2026.
- **Definitional rule (revenue − COGS − SG&A):** would exclude other income and expenses and give a *higher* number.
- **TRI.PA's proposed substantive restatement — "the reported subtotal including the most operating charges":** points at the same line as the label rule.

So all three rules converge, and they converge on the conservative number. That is new: at 6501.T the clean definitional object was itself a prohibited add-back, at 7011.T the label rule had no object at all, and at ML.PA / SGO.PA the two rules gave opposite economics. **Panasonic is the first filer in ten instances where label, definition-by-conservatism, and the substantive restatement all coincide** — which is direct support for scoping the rule by a **materiality test** and restating it substantively, rather than by label or nationality.

**The wedge, measured.** Panasonic's *segment* profit is adjusted operating profit, which excludes the other income/expense items. Summing the seven segments gives ¥304,687m for FY3/2026 against ¥236,407m reported — a wedge of **¥68,280m = 28.9%**, the largest in the ten-instance series (TRI.PA 6bp, 7011.T 4.1%, 6501.T ~10%, SGO.PA and ML.PA verdict-determinative). For FY3/2025 the same construction gives ¥451,111m against ¥426,490m, a wedge of ¥24,621m = 5.8%. **Non-determinative here** — running the whole window on the flattering line lifts worst-of-four ROCE only to ≈4.8%, still 520bp short — so this is the eighth free calibration point of ten, and the largest wedge yet recorded without changing a verdict.

**特別損益 (J-GAAP extraordinary items) — does not arise.** Panasonic is an IFRS filer throughout the window; there is no below-the-line extraordinary category, and the mirror-image problem 7011.T documented on its FY3/2018 J-GAAP print has no analogue here. The pre-FY3/2021 US GAAP years *would* raise the equivalent question (US GAAP put impairments and restructuring in "other income (deductions)" below operating profit) — one more reason those years are excluded rather than spliced in.

**One further observation for the MODEL file.** Panasonic's own executive compensation is 80% weighted to **adjusted** operating profit (source 11) — i.e. management is paid on the line MODEL forbids. That is not a finding against the company; it is evidence that the choice of line is economically live at this filer and not a presentational accident.

**How it is presented, plainly.** The credit is a **US government production subsidy recognised inside operating income**, not below it: Panasonic records it in the Energy segment's adjusted operating profit, and separately records the portion it hands back to its customer as a **deduction from sales** — so the same programme both inflates operating profit and depresses reported revenue. **Why this sits in the Future gate.** A profit stream that is (a) a statutory credit with a legislated life, (b) partly contractually shared with the customer, and (c) attached to a customer that is simultaneously building its own cells, is not a durable competitive advantage; it is a policy transfer. It is the single strongest reason the Energy segment cannot supply the moat the group otherwise lacks, and it is why limb 2 of the captive-customer waiver fails at unit level as well as group level (D2).
