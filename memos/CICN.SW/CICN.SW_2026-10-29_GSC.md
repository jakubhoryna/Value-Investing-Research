Cicor builds electronics to other companies' designs for medical, industrial, defence and transport equipment makers, and has nearly tripled sales since 2020 by buying European plants. Returns on the capital it uses have stayed in single digits, liabilities are 2.7 times equity, and component makers squeezed its margins in the 2021–2022 shortage. The chief executive's 2025 pay was 12% of net profit. A move to WATCH would need returns above 10% across a cycle, leverage under 2×, pay in proportion to profit and a price far below today's.

## 2. Understanding

Cicor Technologies Ltd. is a Boudry-based contract electronics manufacturer, listed on SIX and 40% owned by One Equity Partners' OEP 80 B.V., that assembles printed circuit boards, cable harnesses, box builds and complete devices to customers' designs, with development and supply-chain services (EMS, 95% of FY2025 sales), and makes printed circuit boards and thin-film substrates (AS), for makers of industrial equipment (38.5%), aerospace and defence systems (25.7%), medical devices (19.2%) and transport equipment (10.2%), who pay per programme, from plants in Switzerland, Germany, the UK, France, Spain, Romania, North Africa and Asia; sales went from CHF 189m in 2016 to CHF 616m in 2025, mostly through acquisitions. PASS.

The AS division (5.7% of sales, 6.2% of segment assets, 6.0% of segment EBITDA) is immaterial; Cicor is one material unit.
