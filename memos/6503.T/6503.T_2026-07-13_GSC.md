A diversified Japanese electric-machinery conglomerate with genuinely good franchises (factory automation, defense & space, elevators) buried inside a group whose ten-year worst-of-four ROCE is 7.3% — a balance sheet stuffed with a ¥970bn pension surplus, cross-shareholdings, and ¥732bn of cash that management only recently began returning. ## 2. Understanding

Mitsubishi Electric is a 105-year-old, Tokyo-headquartered and TSE-listed diversified electric-machinery conglomerate — vertically integrated, ~256 subsidiaries, six segments — manufacturing differentiated capital goods and systems (power, rail, defense & space systems; factory-automation gear; elevators and air conditioners; power semiconductors; IT services) sold overwhelmingly to organisations (utilities, rail operators, Japan's MoD, factories, building owners, OEMs) and partly to consumers via HVAC/appliances, with ~52% of revenue overseas and the group mid-transformation: post-2021 quality-scandal governance reform, the automotive carve-out into Mitsubishi Electric Mobility, and a security push via Nozomi Networks. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four 10y normalized ROCE is **7.3%** (wc/end; best variant 9.3%) and worst-of-four FCFROCE **4.4%** — both far below the P3 10% line and the P2 15%/8% bars. Per-share growth is respectable (ΔOI/sh 5.9% geo, ΔBV/sh 9.7%, ΔTBV/sh 9.2% FY19–26) and leverage is pristine (L/E 61%, net cash ex-leases), and returns are inflecting upward (FY26 ROCE_xc/beg touched 10.1%, the first double-digit print) — but the framework tests the record, not the inflection. Capital sits idle: pension surplus ¥969.8bn, cross-holdings, cash ¥731.6bn, all inside CE by rule. **WEAK** — level failure under P4; no sustained decline, but a decade averaging ~7–8% is not a good business at the group level.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS both sides: no customer >10% of revenue (MoD is a monopsony inside defense but defense is <10% of group), diversified supplier base. Forces: customer power WEAK (fragmented end-markets), supplier power WEAK (scale buyer, partly internalised semis), substitutes WEAK (electrification/automation tailwinds; no doing-without for grid gear, elevators, HVAC), new entrants WEAK on the book's test (installed-base switching costs, scale, defense/rail permits) — though Chinese FA (Inovance) and HVAC (Midea, Gree) entrants are visibly succeeding in China, the caveat that keeps this ADEQUATE rather than STRONG. Tally 0 strong. Moat: switching costs/ingrainedness in the FA installed base and elevator-service annuities, plus a government moat in defense — stable. Market growth YES. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

- One-off noise is heavy: FY26 OI absorbed the ¥105.3bn Next-Stage early-retirement charge net of asset-sale gains — averaging dilutes, but single-year multiples mislead in both directions.
