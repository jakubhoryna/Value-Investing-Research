# RHIM.L — RHI Magnesita N.V.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future WEAK, shareholder-friendliness PASS, cheapness EXPENSIVE: **AVOID (Past + Future)**.

RHI Magnesita is the largest refractory maker outside China, integrated back into its own magnesite and dolomite mines, and its linings are consumed in steel, cement, glass and non-ferrous furnaces. The decade record does not clear the quality floor. Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 7.4% and FCFROCE 3.2%, and liabilities run at three times book equity. Returns peaked at 13.7% in FY2018, the first full year after the Magnesita merger, and have since fallen to 5.7% while acquisitions added capital. The live question is the Vesuvius proposal: a debt-funded cash-and-share bid, revised upward after repeated rejections, with leverage already above management's own target. Price is no help either. EV/OI is 14.8–16.7×, and the EV/OI band leg has no positive price until operating income reaches about €301m (FY2025: €223m). The verdict turns only if returns rebuild above 10% across the window and two of the strong forces weaken, which nothing in the record suggests.

## 2. Understanding
RHI Magnesita makes and installs refractory bricks, monolithic mixes, flow-control parts and related services, and sells magnesia raw materials. The customers who pay are steelmakers (about 70% of revenue) and cement, lime, glass, non-ferrous and chemical producers. The product is consumed in use and relined on a cycle. It is a Dutch N.V. run from Vienna, with mines in Austria, Brazil, Türkiye and the US and sales worldwide. It was formed in 2017 by the RHI–Magnesita merger and has added bolt-on acquisitions since; it is now the largest non-Chinese refractory group. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**WEAK.** Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **7.4%** (end-of-period, with cash), with the best read 9.5%. FCFROCE is 3.2–4.1%, held down by FY2021's working-capital build (FCF −€372m). Returns peaked at 13.7% in FY2018 and fell to 5.7% in FY2024–25 as capital employed rose from €2.9bn to €3.8bn. OI/share compounded 5.2% a year FY2016→FY2025 but fell 8.9% a year from FY2018. L/E is **310%** (FY2025) against the 200% flag, and the rescue conditions do not hold. Level, trend and leverage each fail.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth **PASSES**: no customer reaches 10% of revenue, and magnesia, the main input, is mined in-house. Forces: customer power **STRONG**, because concentrated steelmakers buy in an oversupplied market and refractory use per tonne of steel keeps falling. New entrants **STRONG**: Chinese exports set benchmark prices, and India is adding capacity into a market the company calls oversupplied. Supplier power and substitutes are WEAK. The tally is two strong forces. The moat is cost through vertical integration, and it is weak: the company's own vertical-integration ROIC was 4.0% in H1 2026. Market growth is MIXED: flat-to-falling in Europe, growing in India. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
Pay is moderate. The CEO's FY2025 total of €3.26m sits under the €4.30m fiscal-year threshold and the €6.55m normalized threshold. The chair's fee is £325k. No related-party trades with the two blockholders, MSP Stiftung (28%) and Rhône (21–24%), beyond dividends. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**, one flag. The 2019–21 buybacks ran at 11–21× EV/OI. About €0.9bn of deals in 2023–25 came as returns halved. Now a debt-funded Vesuvius bid is live at 2.9× net debt/EBITDA against the 1.0–2.0× target. **PASS**, under heightened scrutiny.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers
- A firm Vesuvius offer adds new acquisition debt (estimated at roughly €1.4bn) on top of 2.9× leverage, at a price not yet testable against Vesuvius's earnings.
- Steel is 70% of revenue, and European steel volumes and refractory intensity are both falling.
- Chinese raw-material and refractory export prices set the benchmark, so integration margins track Chinese policy.
- Company-adjusted EBITA (€373m FY2025) runs 67% above reported EBIT (€223m), and restructuring and similar exclusions recur every year.
- Pension liabilities (€229m) sit outside EV by rule.
