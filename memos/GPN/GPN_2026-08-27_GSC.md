# GPN — Global Payments Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future WEAK, Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (capital-allocation ADVERSE), price EXPENSIVE — **AVOID**, a three-gate fail.

The bull case is a scaled merchant-acceptance cash machine at 12× FY25 MCap/FCF. The framework rejects it at every layer beneath the adjusted print: returns on the actual capital base have never approached quality (worst-of-four ROCE 3.4% on ~$40–49bn of largely acquired capital), the moat is eroding under demonstrated entry by modern stacks, and management has spent seven years assembling empires with debt (TSYS, EVO, Worldpay — $50bn deployed) while ROCE sat in the low single digits. A verdict flip requires a different company, not a lower price: post-Worldpay, the debt stack alone exceeds 7× operating income, so no positive share price makes this CHEAP on the binding metric. The flip path (§8) runs through OI near $3.5bn, delivered deleveraging, and an end to serial M&A.

## 2. Understanding

Global Payments is a prominent, ~60-year-old Atlanta-headquartered payments-technology operating company (Georgia corporation, NYSE-listed) that, after closing the Worldpay acquisition and Issuer Solutions divestiture in January 2026, is a pure-play merchant acquirer and commerce-software provider — selling largely commoditized card-acceptance processing differentiated by distribution and vertical software (Genius POS, integrated payments) — to millions of paying merchants from SMBs to global enterprises plus platform/ISV partners, across ~175 countries with revenue concentrated in the Americas and Europe, and it is mid-way through the largest integration in its history. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 3.4% and FCFROCE 4.6% (FY2021–FY2025; both cash variants, beginning- and end-of-period) — far below the 10% ADEQUATE floor in every single year. The capital base is the problem: $21–37bn of goodwill and intangibles from serial acquisition. Per-share growth is noise around a churning portfolio (FY22 impairment, four divestitures, FY24–25 restated ex-Issuer): FCF/sh +6.2% geo-mean, BV/sh +1.1%, TBV/sh negative until FY2024. L/E 129% (171% post-Worldpay) is within P5. Window is 5y record-limited — the cap is moot; the failure is level, not length. **WEAK** — returns never cleared even the P3 floor.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes on the letter: no customer or single supplier over 10%, with the logged caveat that scheme access is effectively a two-vendor market. Forces: customer bargaining power WEAK (millions of fragmented merchants, software switching costs); supplier power STRONG (Visa/Mastercard duopoly plus bank sponsorship — no ready alternatives, real forward integration); substitutes WEAK today (pay-by-bank/A2A rising but card ubiquity and acceptance habits hold); new entrants STRONG (Stripe, Adyen, Toast, Shift4 have entered at scale and taken share — barriers to entry and to success both demonstrably breached). Two strong forces — the stop signal. Moat: distribution scale in legacy acquiring, eroding (merchant GAAP revenue roughly flat ex-M&A). Market growth YES, which is precisely what funds the entrants. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 passes both reads: CEO total $20.3m vs ceilings of $70.0m (fiscal-year) and $65.9m (trailing-3y normalized); absolute trigger ≈$40m not breached. RPT and outside-director fees not retrieved — gap logged, re-test queued, no pair formed. Capital allocation **ADVERSE**: three transformational deals in seven years (TSYS $21.5bn 2019, EVO $4.0bn 2023, Worldpay $24.3bn 2026) under continuous allocator leadership, with $0.87bn of goodwill impairments and loss-making exits as receipts and no course change — the largest deal came last, debt-funded at ~3.5× net leverage, alongside a live $2.5bn repurchase authorization, a $550m ASR, and a $7.5bn 2025–27 distribution commitment with no published price discipline, on a returns engine running at 3–5% ROCE. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Reported-OI interest coverage is ~1.3× (TTM OI $1.31bn vs ~$1.04bn annualized interest); the equity cushion is accounting goodwill, not tangible assets.
- Share migration to modern stacks (Stripe/Adyen/Toast/Shift4) and travel exposure — the Middle East conflict already forced a 2026 guidance cut.
- ≈$2.45bn of Issuer-gain taxes payable will drain cash through 2026–27, so today's EV flatters the all-cash variant.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Past WEAK is structural: even doubling reported OI leaves ROCE near 7% on this capital base — no realistic path inside the window. A verdict flip therefore needs, jointly: Future re-rated on evidence the entrant threat is contained; SF re-verdicted after the ADVERSE lapses (deleveraging delivered, no further platform M&A, repurchases under a published price criterion); and reported OI ≥ ≈$3.5bn to restore any band.
