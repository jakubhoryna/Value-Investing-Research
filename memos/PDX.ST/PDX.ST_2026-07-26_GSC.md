# PDX.ST — Paradox Interactive: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | SF | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | ADEQUATE | **WEAK** | PASS | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID (Future gate)** |

Paradox is an owner-operated, net-cash, dividend-paying niche IP compounder with genuinely excellent normalized economics — worst-of-four ROCE 25.3%, FCFROCE 17.7% — and among the cleanest shareholder-friendliness records in this register: the founder-CEO owns 33.4% and takes SEK 4 thousand in pay. What kills it is a single disclosed fact: **SEK 1,854m of SEK 2,192m FY2025 revenue — 84.6% — flows through one counterparty, Valve's Steam platform.** That is a customer-breadth FAIL an order of magnitude past P15, the captive-customer waiver is inapplicable (Valve depends on Paradox for nothing and faces no switching costs; the dependence runs entirely the other way), and the concentration is structural to PC gaming — so there is no realistic flip path. A great little business standing on someone else's land. At 93× EV/OI and 23× MCap/FCF it is also nowhere near cheap, but the price is moot: the verdict fails on quality of the future, not on price.

## 2. Understanding

Paradox develops (nine internal studios) and publishes deep, long-lived grand-strategy and management PC games — Crusader Kings, Europa Universalis, Hearts of Iron, Stellaris, Cities: Skylines, Victoria, plus the owned World of Darkness IP — monetised through initial sales and a decade-long tail of paid DLC, sold to consumers almost entirely via third-party digital storefronts (Steam above all, 86% of revenue is PC), collected from platform partners predominantly based in the USA (85% of revenue), from a Stockholm HQ, founded 2004, listed 2016, moved to the Nasdaq Stockholm main market June 2026, ~638 employees. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Normalized averages are strong on every variant: worst-of-four ROCE 25.3% (wc/end), FCFROCE 17.7% — both clear P2 levels. But STRONG fails on the growth leg: ΔOI/sh geometric 2016→2025 is **−8.0%** nominal, because FY2025 OI collapsed to SEK 146m under a SEK 355m Bloodlines 2 write-down and elevated amortisation (2016→2024 the same figure is +11.2%; 3-y average OI is up 38% on 2016–18). ΔFCF/sh +15.6%, ΔBV/sh +21.3%, ΔTBV/sh +20.8%. Per-year ROCE has stepped down from a hyper-profitable small base (62.8% in 2016) to the low-20s, with write-down years (2021, 2025) as recurring punctuation — a pattern, not a one-off. L/E 26.9%, net cash SEK 1.2bn. **ADEQUATE** — returns comfortably above P3, growth positive on averaged basis, but the OI/sh series and recurring capitalised-development write-downs (2022: 135m; 2023: 171m; 2024: 208m; 2025: 355m) bar STRONG.

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth **FAIL**: one platform, Steam, is 84.6% of revenue (disclosed, note 5); receivables and payment terms run against Valve. Waiver inapplicable — limb (a) fails outright. Supplier breadth PASS (talent, no concentrated input). Forces: customer/channel bargaining power **STRONG** (Valve sets the ~30% take rate and terms unilaterally); supplier power WEAK; substitutes WEAK (thousands of invested hours per player, no close substitute for the niche); new entrants WEAK (20-year IP, community, DLC catalogue — barriers to success are high). Tally 1 strong, but the breadth FAIL decides. Moat: real — ingrainedness/switching costs within the grand-strategy niche, stable. Market growth YES. **WEAK** — the moat protects Paradox from other publishers, not from its landlord.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Steam dependence at 85% of revenue is simultaneously the verdict and the existential risk — a take-rate change or delisting reprices the whole business.
- Hit-driven pipeline: four write-down years in the last four (135/171/208/355 MSEK) show new-IP execution is unreliable; the cash machine is the legacy catalogue.
- Capitalised development (SEK 1,015m on the balance sheet) makes reported OI systematically vulnerable to further impairments.
- Key-man/control: Wester is CEO, 33.4% holder, and board member; Tencent holds 10.1% with a board seat.
- Console/subscription (Game Pass) shift could dilute the DLC long-tail model.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Future WEAK is structural: a flip requires the single-platform share falling toward P15 territory — own-store, console, and mobile diversification on a scale nothing in the strategy suggests — or a framework change to how distribution platforms are read under P15. Neither is expected. Past could reach STRONG at FY2027 if FY2026–27 OI normalises (write-down endpoint rolls off), which changes nothing while the Future gate fails. Next review: FY2026 AR (~April 2027), informational, low priority.
