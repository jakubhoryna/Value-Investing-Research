# PAH3.DE — Porsche Automobil Holding SE: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | **WEAK** | **WEAK** | **FAIL** | FAIR | **AVOID** |

Porsche SE is the Porsche/Piëch family's holding vehicle — its worth is a debt-levered claim on ~31.9% of VW's capital (53.3% of voting shares) plus ~12.5% of Porsche AG, and little else. As a securities-heavy holdco the OI/FCF/ROCE machinery is inapplicable, so the §2·1A substitution rule governs (BRK.B / INVE-B lineage): ROE for ROCE, discount-to-NAV for cheapness. The bull case is the ~30% NAV discount and 5.3% yield — but three gates fail. Substitution-ROE is **negative** across FY2021–25: the −€20.0bn FY2024 non-cash impairment of the VW and Porsche AG carrying amounts (as-reported; add-backs forbidden) sits atop a declining ex-impairment trend and a dividend cut €2.56→€1.51. The look-through book is a concentrated ~87%-VW bet on a Chinese-EV-disrupted auto complex with an eroding moat. And governance fails: listed holders own **non-voting preference shares** while the family holds every voting share — a control structure the framework strikes **when paired with value-destructive allocation**, which the €10.1bn debt-funded 2022 Porsche AG purchase (now carried at ~half) supplies. Cheapness is only FAIR — ~30% is around its ~33% structural norm, not a margin of safety, and moot given the triple failure. A discounted holdco value trap; nothing flips it without a quality and governance change.

## 2. Understanding

Porsche Automobil Holding SE is a Stuttgart-Zuffenhausen–based German SE holding company controlled by the Porsche and Piëch families through 100% of its unlisted voting ordinary shares, whose economic substance is an equity-method claim on Volkswagen AG (~31.9% of capital / 53.3% of voting shares) and Porsche AG (~12.5% / 25%+1 voting) plus a small mobility-tech VC-PE portfolio, funded partly by ~€5.1bn holdco net debt and earning dividends and at-equity results from those two automakers. **PASS** — structure transparent; the stretch is that the value driver is VW, a sprawling 12-brand OEM, so understanding PAH3 is downstream of understanding VW.

## 3. Quality — past record

Substitution rule (securities-heavy holdco): **ROE replaces ROCE**; EV/OI, FCFROCE n.m. Worst-of-four ΣNI/Σequity over FY2021–25 is **≈ −1.0%** (ΣNI −€2,234m ÷ Σequity €222.4bn) versus a 10%-ADEQUATE bar — driven by the **−€20,017m FY2024 result** from non-cash impairments (as-reported; no add-back). The ex-impairment alternate ~9.4% is only borderline-ADEQUATE and moot: the write-downs reflect a real, ongoing decline (VW impaired again in FY2025 and Q1 2026) and the trend independently forces the downgrade (dividend €2.56→€1.51; equity €55bn→€37bn; Appendix B2). Leverage passes on the book test (liabilities €7.2bn ÷ equity €37.8bn = **19%**). **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

Assessed look-through under substitution (moat-floor method, per INVE-B). The dominant asset — VW at ~87% of NAV — is a mass-market OEM with an **eroding** moat: Chinese EV entrants taking its historic China profit pool, the EV transition compressing legacy margins, high fixed/labour costs, plant-restructuring conflict; adverse forces run STRONG (customer choice, substitutes, new entrants). Porsche AG (~13%) has a genuine luxury brand but is in a demand/strategy trough (China collapse, EV-plan reversal, ROS 8.6%→7.1%). PAH3's own durable feature is family voting control — a *control* moat, not a value moat, and one that cements the discount and blocks minority-led NAV realisation. Permanent capital is a stability positive but does not rescue portfolio quality; look-through market growth MIXED-to-negative. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**FAIL.** The listed instrument is **non-voting preference stock**; the families hold 100% of the voting ordinary shares (Wolfgang Porsche chairs a family-dominated supervisory board). The framework treats control as a caveat that becomes a **strike when paired with value-destructive allocation** — supplied by the **€10.1bn, ~€7.9bn-debt-funded 2022 Porsche AG purchase at its IPO peak, now carried at ~€5.7bn value-in-use (roughly half)**, alongside the serial ~€20bn VW impairments. Corroborating flag: dense interlocking structure (Pötsch chairs both PAH3 and VW supervisory boards; PAH3 is wholly VW's fortunes). P17 clean — no strike/flag (Pötsch €0.8m fixed + €0.75m bonus target + LTI, well under the 5%-of-FCF ≈ €35m line; board fees within German norms). Buybacks none (n/a). Verdict rests on the control-plus-allocation strike.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- **Value-trap mechanics:** the ~30% discount is the normal state, not a dislocation — with no catalyst and minorities holding no votes, it can persist or widen indefinitely.
- **NAV erosion:** VW impaired repeatedly (FY2024, FY2025, Q1 2026); further weakness cuts NAV, dividends, and the debt-service cushion together.
- **Dividend fragility:** the €1.51 payout depends entirely on VW/Porsche AG distributions, both under restructuring pressure; a further cut is plausible.
- **Look-through leverage:** net debt ~€5.1bn against ~€12–14bn market NAV (~40% LTV) magnifies downside even though book L/E (19%) looks benign.
- **Bear-case-breaker:** a sustained VW/Porsche AG earnings recovery would re-rate NAV and narrow the discount — the only realistic value path, and outside PAH3's control.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Flips toward WATCH only on a **quality and governance** change, not price: a durable recovery in look-through returns (substitution-ROE back above ~10% ex-impairment, impairment cycle closed) **and** evidence the family stops value-destructive debt-funded allocation. Cheapness separately requires the discount to widen past ~45% (≈ €22) to be a margin of safety against the structural norm. Not held — no sell discipline. **Next review:** FY2026 results (~mid-March 2027) for the impairment trend and NAV re-anchor; H1 2026 interim (~mid-August 2026); or price < ≈ €22.
