The world's largest sea/air freight forwarder is a genuine high-return, asset-light compounder: worst-of-four 10-year ROCE and FCFROCE both 23.1%, near-total payout of earnings (≈ CHF 8.7bn dividends over the decade against flat equity), and a 54.5% family owner whose interests are the shareholders'. ## 2. Understanding

KN sells freight forwarding — buying bulk sea, air, and road capacity from carriers and reselling it shipment-by-shipment bundled with customs and documentation — plus contract-logistics warehousing, to roughly 400,000 mostly corporate shippers worldwide; a prominent, 135-year-old, Swiss-headquartered, family-controlled (Kühne Holding 54.5%) listed group of ~85,000 staff, global #1 in sea and air forwarding by volume, operating asset-light: carriers own the ships and planes, warehouses are leased. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized averages (2016–2025, pre-2019 lease-capitalised): **ROCE 23.1%, FCFROCE 23.1%** — both clear P2 with room. Per-share growth is the thin edge: ΔOI/sh and ΔFCF/sh both **3.3%** geometric, exactly at the 3% floor; the record is a 2016–2020 plateau, a 2021–22 freight bubble, and normalization to a 2025 EBIT (1,242) only ~30% above 2016 adjusted. BV/sh flat at ~CHF 18 — the honest reading is that essentially all earnings left as dividends. L/E 446% breaches P5; passed under P6 (net financial debt only CHF 738m; equity thin by rational distribution, not leverage — logged). Yearly ROCE hit its decade low in 2025; 2024 interrupted the slide, so no forced downgrade — flagged for FY2026. **STRONG**, marginal on growth.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: customer PASS (400k customers, no IFRS 8 concentration disclosure); supplier PASS with the ready-alternatives reading (any liner or airline takes forwarder bookings). Forces: customer power WEAK (fragmentation), **supplier power STRONG** (ocean lineage consolidated into three alliances; Maersk and CMA CGM demonstrably forward-integrating into forwarding), substitutes WEAK-contained (carrier-direct/digital booking is real but two decades of predicted disintermediation haven't landed), new entrants WEAK (Flexport's struggles evidence the barrier to success; network density and buying scale). Tally 1 strong, contained. Moat: cost/scale via global network density, reinforced by ingrainedness in customer supply chains; stable. Market growth YES (trade ≈ GDP plus outsourcing). **ADEQUATE.**
