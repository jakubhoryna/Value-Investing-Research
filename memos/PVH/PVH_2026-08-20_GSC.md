# PVH — PVH Corp.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; shareholder-friendliness PASS with one flag; cheapness FAIR — **AVOID**, on both quality gates.

PVH owns two of the most recognised names in global apparel and collects high-margin royalties on them, and at 7.1× free cash flow the equity is not dear. What stands between here and a buy is not price but the business: capital employed carries $5.1bn of acquired goodwill and tradenames plus $1.9bn of capitalised leases, and six years of operating income averaging roughly $400m cannot earn an adequate return on it. Normalized ROCE is 4.0% on the worst of four reads — 7.1–8.0% even excluding the pandemic year. Three impairments in six years say what the returns already say. Only a durable step-change in operating income against a stable asset base flips this, not a lower price.

## 2. Understanding

PVH Corp. designs, sources and markets branded apparel, footwear and accessories under the owned *Calvin Klein* and *TOMMY HILFIGER* trademarks (over 95% of revenue) plus a residual *Heritage Brands* line, selling to wholesale customers — department stores, off-price chains, pure-play digital retailers, distributors, franchisees — and directly to consumers through its own stores and websites across EMEA, the Americas and APAC, while licensing the same trademarks to some sixty third parties for categories it does not operate; a New York-headquartered brand owner and licensor that manufactures nothing itself. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized average ROCE is **4.0%** (beginning-of-period, with-cash; range 4.0–4.5%) and FCFROCE **4.7%** (range 4.7–5.4%) across FY2020–FY2025. Excluding FY2020 entirely lifts ROCE only to 7.1–8.0%. The best single year in the window, FY2021, reached 10.7%; FY2025 was 2.4%. OI/share compounded −25.0% over FY2021–FY2025 (−0.6% adding back the FY2025 impairment); FCF/share −0.2%; BV/share +8.3%, but that is share count falling faster than equity. TBV is negative in every year — intangibles exceed equity — so ΔTBV is n.m. Leverage is the one clean pass: L/E 143.7%, inside bounds. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both legs comfortably: the five largest customers were 16.6% of FY2025 revenue, none above 5%, and production runs through ~1,000 independent factories in ~30 countries with alternatives available. Forces decide it — customers STRONG (no long-term agreements, order-by-order buying, allowance demands), substitutes STRONG (apparel is fungible; private label, resale, rental), new entrants STRONG (management cites low barriers for digitally native brands), suppliers WEAK. Three of four. The moat source is Brand; $1.83bn of write-downs against flat decade revenue makes it stable at best. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

CEO total was $16.17m in FY2025 against 5% of the lower of FCF and net income — $1.27m on the fiscal-year read, a 12.8× breach, but $21.5m normalized, which acquits. One flag, no strike; the absolute trigger is untouched. Outside-director pay is a retainer plus one RSU grant, far inside the stop. Capital allocation is SOUND: FY2025 repurchases of $561m for 7.7m shares at ~$72.86 imply 6.6× FCF (5.7× normalized), funded from operating cash flow, no empire-building M&A, token dividend. **PASS**, one flag logged.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Tariffs: ~$195m gross EBIT impact guided for 2026 (~$3.30/share), legally unsettled after the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling and the Section 122 order.
- China's List of Unreliable Entities listing (February 2026) leaves sourcing, selling and investing in China exposed; no measures imposed yet.
- EMEA is the profit engine (49% of FY2025 revenue, $749m segment EBIT) and is the region management flagged as deteriorating at Q1 2026.
- The G-III licence takebacks swap high-margin royalties for lower-margin wholesale revenue and working capital.
- A further discount-rate move impairs the remaining $4.7bn of goodwill and tradenames, as in FY2020, FY2022 and FY2025.

Future flips only if two of the three strong forces are argued down, which brand strength alone does not do. Neither is in prospect, so a lower price does not reopen the name. Next review at the FY2026 10-K (~March 2027): ROCE floor, window rolled towards ten years, P17 flag re-tested against the 2027 proxy.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: YES.** FY2025 10-K, Item 1 "Material Customers" and the matching risk factor: sales to the five largest customers were 16.6% of revenue in 2025, 15.1% in 2024 and 13.3% in 2023, and **no single customer accounted for more than 5% of revenue in any of those years**. The disclosure sentence exists and is explicit. Concentration is rising year on year, which is worth watching, but the level is comfortably inside the threshold.

**Breadth — suppliers: YES.** Products were made in approximately 1,000 factories in approximately 30 countries during 2025, all operated by independent manufacturers. The company states that an ample number of alternative suppliers exists should replacement capacity be needed, and separately that no single supplier or country is or is expected to become critical. Looking through to the effective supplier: production is concentrated in Asia and the China listing creates a jurisdictional rather than a vendor concentration — recorded as a risk in section 7, not as a breadth failure. Both legs pass; no waiver needed.

**Four forces.**

| Force | Call | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | No long-term wholesale agreements; purchases order-by-order; disclosed retailer pressure on price and demands for allowances and economic support; consumers face zero switching cost. |
| Supplier bargaining power | WEAK | ~1,000 independent factories across ~30 countries, alternatives available, finished-goods orders placed two to six months ahead. |
| Threat of substitutes | **STRONG** | Apparel is fungible across all three substitute forms — direct (any competing brand), doing-without, and wholly different (private label, resale, rental), all named by the company. |
| Threat of new entrants | **STRONG** | Management's own words: the industry has low barriers to entry for digitally native brands; competition is intense; wholesale customers compete directly through private label. |

Tally: **three strong** — beyond the stop signal at two. Future is WEAK on the forces alone, before the moat question.

**Moat.** Source: **Brand** — worldwide consumer recognition of *Calvin Klein* and *TOMMY HILFIGER*, monetised twice over, once through owned distribution and again through roughly sixty licence agreements whose operating profit carries almost no directly associated expense ($356.7m of Licensing segment EBIT on $421.2m of revenue in FY2025). That is a real moat and the reason the business earns anything at all. Direction: **stable at best**. Global retail sales under each brand are approximately $9bn and revenue has been flat for a decade; $1.83bn of goodwill and tradename write-downs across FY2020, FY2022 and FY2025 is the accounting confirming what the returns show. Not eroding fast; not strengthening.

**Market growth.** **MIXED.** Global branded apparel grows at low single digits at best; PVH's own 2026 guidance is approximately flat revenue with an operating margin held only by tariff refunds. Guarding against late-pipeline glow: there is nothing here that makes flat revenue into a growth story.
