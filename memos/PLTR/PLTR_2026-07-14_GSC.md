# PLTR — Palantir Technologies Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Gate | Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |

Palantir is a genuinely good *business trajectory* wrapped in a record too short and a governance structure too skewed to clear the framework, priced at a level that has no relationship to the model's arithmetic. Two gates fail independently. **Past is WEAK**: only ~6 public fiscal years (STRONG unavailable), and the worst-of-four normalized-average ROCE is 0.4% including the 2020 direct-listing loss and 5.4% excluding it — both under the 10% ADEQUATE floor — because three of the first four public years ran operating losses and the $7.2bn cash-and-Treasuries pile inflates capital employed. **SF FAILs** on a flag pair (entrenched dual-class founder control + the 2020–21 SPAC-ecosystem related-party revenue history), with ~15%-of-revenue stock comp and +162% share dilution since IPO as corroboration — though, in fairness, *not* on a P17 pay strike. Cheapness is academic: EV/OI ~224–229×, MCap/FCF ~154×, MCap/BV ~44×. Nothing here is a price question; a BUY would need a multi-year STRONG record *and* a governance re-rating *and* a ~95% price fall.

## 2. Understanding

Palantir Technologies is a U.S.-headquartered, founder-controlled (dual-class), asset-light, net-cash enterprise-software company that builds and deploys four data-integration and AI platforms — Gotham, Foundry, Apollo, AIP — on multi-year subscription/usage contracts to Western governments and defense/intelligence agencies (54% of FY2025 revenue, U.S.-led) and to large commercial enterprises (46%), in the data-analytics/AI-software industry. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Public history spans FY2020–FY2025 (~6y; FY2019 comparative) — under P1, so STRONG is off the table by construction. Worst-of-four normalized-average **ROCE = 0.4%** (incl. FY2020's −$1.17bn SBC-driven loss) / **5.4%** (excl.) versus the 10% ADEQUATE floor; the cash-heavy CE_wc denominator (>$7bn) is the drag, and OI only turned positive in FY2023. FCFROCE screens high (worst 16–19%) but is flattered by ~$684m/yr add-back of stock comp. Returns are sharply *improving* (FY2025 OI 17.5% on CE_wc), but the rubric averages the record and does not upgrade on trajectory. Leverage is pristine (L/E 19%, no debt). Verdict **WEAK** — thin history plus a sub-floor return average.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS**: the FY2024 10-K states no single customer ≥10% of revenue (2022–24); suppliers are the hyperscalers (multi-cloud, substitutable) and engineering talent. Four forces: customer power WEAK (ontology switching costs, land-and-expand), supplier power WEAK (multi-cloud pass-through), **substitutes STRONG** (Databricks/Snowflake + BI, hyperscaler-native AI tooling, systems integrators, in-house builds), new-entrant threat WEAK (security clearances, FedRAMP-High/IL accreditation, embedded deployments). Tally 1 strong → judgment zone. Moat: switching costs + government ingrainedness, stable-to-strengthening on the defense side. Market growing (YES). Verdict **ADEQUATE** — real moat, one contained strong force; guard against the late-pipeline "hotness" glow.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Comp does **not** strike: Karp's FY2025 summary-table total is ~$12.0m, far under 5%-of-lower(FCF $2.1bn, NI $1.63bn) = $81m and under the ~$40m absolute flag; the widely-cited "$6.8bn compensation actually paid (2024)" is SEC mark-to-market on his 2020 founder grant (no new grants since IPO), not a pay action. Buybacks are de minimis ($64m/$75m FY24/25, ~RSU tax settlement) — not a P19 program. FAIL comes from a **flag pair**: (1) entrenched dual-class super-voting control (founders steer ~49.9% of votes with no sunset); (2) the 2020–21 "Palantir ecosystem" related-party pattern (investing in SPAC-bound firms that concurrently signed Palantir contracts). ~15%-of-revenue SBC and +162% dilution corroborate. Verdict **FAIL**.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Momentum/narrative ("hotness") is the dominant bias risk here — the framework verdict is deliberately blind to price action and AI-cycle enthusiasm.
- SBC is the hidden cost: reported FCF is real cash, but ~15%-of-revenue equity issuance transfers ownership continuously; per-share economics lag headline growth.
- Government-revenue dependency (54%; U.S. 74%) ties the base case to defense/political budget cycles and procurement risk despite no single-customer >10%.
- Substitute pressure (hyperscalers, Databricks/Snowflake) is the one strong force and could compress the commercial moat faster than it strengthens.
- Founder control means minority holders have no governance lever if capital allocation ever turns value-destructive.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Past → ADEQUATE requires several more years of positive OI lifting the worst-of-four ROCE average through 10% (earliest ~FY2028, mechanically) *and* a full 10y window for STRONG. SF → PASS requires the governance flags to lapse (a dual-class sunset and clean RPT record) — unlikely near-term. Even both cleared, cheapness would need a ~95% de-rating. Next review: **FY2026 10-K (~Feb 2027)** extends the record and refreshes the band; **2027 proxy** re-tests governance. Not held — no sell-discipline section applies.
