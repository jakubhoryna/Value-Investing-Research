# DGE.L — Diageo plc: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future WEAK, shareholder-friendliness PASS (one flag), cheapness EXPENSIVE: **AVOID** (Past + Future).

Diageo owns some of the most recognisable brands in drinks, and that is what it would be worth owning for. The record does not support it. Returns ran mostly at 13–16% on capital until FY2024 and then fell to 7.4%, operating income per share shrank over the decade, and liabilities run at 312% of equity after seven years of repurchases at 17–48× free cash flow or operating income, funded largely with debt. In its largest profit pool, US spirits volumes fell 9% with negative price/mix in FY2026. To move toward WATCH, leverage must come back under 200% of equity through retained earnings and returns must recover above 10% without the brand erosion continuing.

## 2. Understanding
Diageo makes and markets branded spirits (Scotch such as Johnnie Walker, tequila such as Don Julio and Casamigos, vodka, rum, liqueurs; 75% of FY2026 net sales), beer (Guinness; 18%) and ready-to-drink products, selling them through wholesalers and distributors (including US three-tier channels and state control boards), retail chains, e-marketplaces and on-trade outlets in nearly 180 countries. North America is the largest region at 37% of net sales and 46% of pre-exceptional operating profit. It is a vertically integrated producer with distilleries, maturing stock and breweries, a UK-listed plc formed in 1997, headquartered in London, holding 34% of Moët Hennessy, and in a turnaround under a chief executive appointed in January 2026. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**WEAK.** Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **12.7%** and FCFROCE **8.2%**. Both are adequate in level, but returns fell from 15.7% (FY2024) to 11.0% and then 7.4% as impairments and restructuring hit operating income. OI/share fell 0.6% a year and FCF/share grew 2.9%, under the 3% floor. **L/E is 312%**, above 200% every year since FY2019, and the exemption does not apply: the other metrics are not strong, and the equity was spent buying back stock at 17–48× free cash flow or operating income rather than cheap stock. A leverage breach without the exemption makes Past WEAK.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth **PASSES**. No customer is disclosed above 10% of revenue, and inputs (grain, agave, glass, cream) come from many suppliers. Customer power, supplier power and new entrants are WEAK. **Substitutes are STRONG**: moderation, GLP-1 drugs and cheaper alternatives are named company risks, and FY2026 price/mix was −1.6%. The moat is **brand**, and it is **eroding** where it earns most: North America organic sales fell 8.4% (US spirits volume −9%, price/mix −2.5%, under "increased competitive pressure"), tequila fell 16% and $1.5bn of acquired brands were impaired. Guinness, Scotch and Europe held up. Market growth is **MIXED**. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
Pay is modest. The highest FY2026 total was £2.6m (Nik Jhangiani, CFO and interim CEO) against a $86.9m threshold. The chair is paid £704k against a £1.05m stop. Related-party trade with associates is about 1% of cost of sales. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**. About £9.3bn and $1bn were spent on buybacks in FY2018–FY2024 at 17–48× earnings. They stopped because leverage reached 3.4×, not because of a price rule, and no worth-based buyback criterion has been published. The 52% dividend cut and the disposals point to a change in course. **PASS** (one flag).

- Moderation, GLP-1 use and downtrading become a lasting volume and mix headwind across categories.
- More impairments of acquired brands (Türkiye, rum, US craft) cut operating income and book value.
