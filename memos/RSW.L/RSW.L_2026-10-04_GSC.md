# RSW.L — Renishaw plc: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past ADEQUATE, Future ADEQUATE, shareholder-friendliness PASS, cheapness EXPENSIVE: **WATCH**.

Renishaw is worth owning for a debt-free precision-measurement franchise: probes, gauges and encoders that are designed into machine tools, CMMs and motion systems, sold to a broad base of manufacturers with no customer above a tenth of revenue. Returns are good but no longer high. Worst-of-four ROCE is 13.7% and FCFROCE 7.2%, because about £290m of cash sits on the balance sheet and capital spending ran heavy in FY2023–24. The price is very far away: 70.7× FY2026 FCF and 30–32× EV/OI against a band of £6.74. A BUY needs FCF several times today's level at a far lower price; neither is in sight.

## 2. Understanding
Renishaw designs and makes, mostly in its own UK plants, precision measurement products: machine-tool probes and tool setters, CMM probes and software, gauging systems and calibration lasers (Industrial Metrology, 55% of FY2026 revenue); optical and magnetic encoders (Position Measurement, 32%); and metal additive-manufacturing machines, Raman spectroscopy and neurosurgical products (Specialised Technologies, 13%). The customers who pay are machine-tool and motion-system OEMs and manufacturers in electronics, semiconductors, automotive, aerospace and precision engineering; China and the USA are its largest markets. It is a long-established UK public company headquartered in Gloucestershire, still controlled by its founding families, of mid-cap size. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**ADEQUATE.** Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **13.7%** (end of period, with cash); the cash-stripped reads are 17.6–18.3%. Worst FCFROCE is **7.2%**. Returns peaked at 26.9% in FY2018, collapsed in FY2020 (restructuring and hedge losses), then slid from 19.6% (FY2021) to 11.0% (FY2025) as capacity spending raised capital employed faster than profit. FY2026 broke the slide at 13.2%, so the decline is ruled not sustained (Appendix C). The halves are 15.1% and 12.7%. OI/share grew 1.7% a year from FY2017 and BV/share 9.2%; FCF/share is flat. L/E is 25%; the only debt is a small yen loan.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth **PASSES**: no customer reaches 10% of revenue, and production is vertically integrated, with management removing single sources for long-lead materials. Customer power, supplier power and substitutes are WEAK in the two core segments: probes and encoders are designed into OEM machines and controller software, and precision measurement has no do-without option. New entrants are **STRONG**: the company itself raised low-price competition, chiefly Chinese, as a growing principal risk. Specialised Technologies carries two strong forces but earned 1% of FY2026 operating profit. The moat is **switching costs**, stable but under price pressure. Market growth: **YES**, with record FY2026 revenue (+14%) led by encoders. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
Pay is modest. The top FY2025 total was £1.48m (CEO Will Lee) against thresholds of £4.19m (fiscal year) and £2.29m (normalized). The chair is paid £325k and other non-executives £80k. The founding families hold about 53% through Deltam Holdings and personal stakes, in one share class: a positive, with a control caveat. Related-party dealings are a £187.5k lease to McMurtry Automotive, with Renishaw as landlord. Capital allocation is **SOUND**: no buybacks, no material acquisitions, a progressive dividend, a 70p special dividend and no net debt. FY2026 pay, including the finance director's £2.0m loss-of-office cost, is the named re-test. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers
- Chinese low-price competitors in probes and encoders could turn the stable moat into an eroding one; operating margin has fallen from 25% (FY2018) to 15–17%.
- Encoder demand is tied to semiconductor and electronics capital spending and can reverse quickly.
- FCF has been weak relative to profit: £598m over ten years against £1.20bn of operating profit, because of capex, inventory and tax timing.
- The new global ERP rollout has had deployment problems, which management names as a priority.
