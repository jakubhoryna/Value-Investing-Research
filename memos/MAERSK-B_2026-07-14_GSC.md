# MAERSK-B — A.P. Møller - Mærsk: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | SF | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | ADEQUATE | **WEAK** | PASS | FAIR | **AVOID (Future)** |

Maersk is a genuinely well-run, conservatively financed, shareholder-friendly operator of a structurally bad business. The 10-year record clears the ADEQUATE bar only because the 2021–22 COVID freight windfall contributes 72% of decade operating income; ex-windfall the group earns ~5% on capital. The verdict rests on Gate 1B: container shipping is a commodity with near-zero customer switching costs and no barrier to capacity growth — two strong forces, the book's stop signal — and the group has no durable moat (Terminals' concession moats are ~10% of revenue). No price fixes that; the AVOID is quality-based and stands even inside the band. A flip requires the industry, not Maersk, to change: durable capacity discipline or a genuinely moaty revenue mix.

## 2. Understanding

Maersk sells containerised ocean freight (commodity service, ~65% of revenue), freight-adjacent logistics (forwarding, warehousing, customs), and container-terminal handling to ~100k paying shippers — importers/exporters from FMCG to autos — worldwide; a vertically integrated Danish plc (Copenhagen HQ), foundation-controlled via dual-class shares, 120 years old, world #2 liner, transformed 2017–24 from oil-and-shipping conglomerate to pure container logistics. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four 10y normalized ROCE 10.8–12.2% (band: pre-2022 cash estimated; both exact ex-cash legs ≈ 15%) — above the 10% ADEQUATE floor, below 15% STRONG. Worst-of-four FCFROCE ≥ 12.0%, clears 8%. But composition is the story: FY16–18 ROCE ≈ 0%, FY23–25 ≈ 6–13%, with 2021–22 at 45–59% carrying the average. Not a sustained decline — a whipsaw. Per-share growth positive across the window (BV/sh ≈ +10% CAGR, plus heavy distributions; OI/sh from loss to $229). L/E 57% — trivial. **ADEQUATE** — mechanically earned, windfall-carried; underlying mid-cycle economics are sub-cost-of-capital.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS (no customer >10%; suppliers — fuel, charter owners, yards — fragmented or ready-alternative). Forces: customer power **STRONG** (undifferentiated service, near-zero switching cost — the 17% FY25 rate slide is the proof); supplier power WEAK; substitutes WEAK; new entrants **STRONG** (charter market lets capital in freely; industry added 7.2% capacity in 2025 with more coming — no barrier to success). Tally 2 strong → stop signal. Moat: none dominant — scale is shared with MSC/CMA; Gemini reliability is replicable engineering; Terminals' government-concession moat is real but ~10% of revenue. Market growth YES (container volumes +2–4%). **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17: CEO Clerc total DKK 45.0m ≈ $6.9m vs strike line $146m (5% × NI $2.9bn) and ≈ $40m absolute trigger — clean; director fees not extracted (gap logged, low risk vs Danish norms). RPT: foundation control (A.P. Møller Holding ~41%, dual-class) — documented caveat, allocation not value-destructive. P19: 2025 buyback DKK 13.1bn at avg ≈ DKK 12,530 → implied EV/OI 7.8×, MCap/FCF 5.9× — well under flags; the 2022–24 windfall was largely returned (specials + buybacks), not spent on ships — rationally rare in this industry. Payout policy 30–50%. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Overcapacity: 2025–27 orderbook deliveries against normalizing Red Sea routings can push Ocean EBIT negative for a prolonged period (company's own key risk).
- Freight-rate sensitivity: ±$100/FFE = ±$1.3bn EBIT — the P&L is a rate derivative.
- Balance sheet is the offset: ~$16bn net cash ex-leases funds survival and countercyclical distributions.
- Logistics & Services margin ambition (6%) still unmet at 4.8% — the "integrator" re-rating rests on it.
- Tariff/geopolitical rerouting cuts both ways: disruption props rates; resolution releases capacity.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To WATCH: Future would need re-rating — durable, evidenced capacity discipline (multi-year), or Terminals/L&S growing to dominate group economics with moaty returns. Cheapness alone can never flip a quality AVOID. To deeper AVOID: leverage creep or windfall-cash empire-building (watch M&A). Next review: FY2026 AR (~Feb 2027); earlier on a major capital-allocation event (large acquisition, CK Hutchison ports involvement).
