Morningstar is a genuinely good business having its best two years on record — FY2024–25 operating income roughly doubled off the 2022 investment trough, and Q1 2026 OI grew another 37% — while the stock halved on the AI-disruption narrative. The framework nonetheless says AVOID, and the reason is capital allocation, not quality: the FY2025 buyback repurchased 3.28M shares for $787.0M at an average $240.19, an implied 22.9× MCap/FCF and 20.6× EV/OI on the conservative EV variant — both above the P19 20× hard-strike line — and the FY2022 tranche ran at 65×/71×. The repurchasing continued into 2026, increasingly debt-funded: long-term debt jumped from $1.07B to $1.71B in Q1 2026 alone (buybacks plus the $365M CRSP acquisition), pushing pro-forma L/E to ≈291% against the 200% flag. A flip needs a program year executed inside the flag lines (or at a demonstrated discount to conservative worth) plus a leverage retreat — and even then the price sits 170% above the band.

## 2. Understanding

Morningstar sells subscription- and licence-based investment data, research, ratings, and indexes — Morningstar Direct/Data (managed-investment data and workflow software), PitchBook (private-market data), Morningstar Credit/DBRS (the fourth-largest credit-rating agency), Morningstar Indexes (now including CRSP, benchmarks for >$3T of Vanguard-linked assets), Sustainalytics ESG, plus Retirement and Wealth platforms — paid for overwhelmingly by institutional customers (asset and wealth managers, banks, PE/VC firms, advisors, plan sponsors, debt issuers), operating globally from Chicago; founded 1984 by Joe Mansueto (still executive chairman and 38.1% owner), a prominent mid-cap that has transformed from consumer fund research into institutional data infrastructure via 46 acquisitions since 2006. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized averages (FY2016–25, P18 lease capitalisation applied FY2016–18): ROCE 13.2% (beg/xc best at 18.3%), FCFROCE 13.6% — comfortably over the 10%/8% ADEQUATE floors, short of the 15% STRONG bar. The shape is a U: strong 2016–18, diluted 2019–23 by the DBRS/LCD acquisitions and a deliberate 2022 investment trough (ROCE_wc 6.4%), then a sharp 2024–25 recovery (20.5%/25.8% in 2025). ΔOI/sh +12.6% and ΔFCF/sh +13.0% geometric — far above the 3% floor. L/E 193.8% at FY2025 — under the 200% flag but only just, and the Q1 2026 borrowing puts the FY2026 print on course to breach. **ADEQUATE** — returns level, not the trend, is the binding constraint.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: largest customer <3% of consolidated revenue (10-K sentence present); suppliers broad (market-data licensors, cloud with ready alternatives). Forces: customer power WEAK (fragmented, workflow-embedded); supplier power WEAK; threat of new entrants WEAK (decades of accumulated proprietary data, NRSRO licence, index-linked assets); threat of substitutes **STRONG** — AI tools are a credible direct-substitution vector for the research/analytics layer and are already denting growth expectations (PitchBook growth slowed to 5.3% in Q1 2026). Tally 1 strong, contained by the proprietary-data layer. Moat: switching costs/ingrainedness — stable, with a contested AI-erosion vector. Market growth YES. **ADEQUATE.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Debt-funded buybacks at above-worth multiples are consuming the balance sheet: pro-forma Q1 2026 L/E ≈291% — a Past-gate WEAK risk at the FY2026 print.
- Serial acquisition at rich revenue multiples (LCD, CRSP) keeps refilling goodwill against negative TBV.
- Credit-ratings (DBRS) revenue is transaction-cyclical and rising as a mix share (15.7% transaction-based in 2025).
- Founder concentration cuts both ways: exceptional alignment, but capital-allocation conviction (Kapoor explicitly frames the buyback as value-buying) is unlikely to bend to price discipline.
