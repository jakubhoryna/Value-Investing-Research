# BC8.DE — Bechtle AG: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | SF | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | **WEAK** | ADEQUATE | PASS | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID (Past)** |

Bechtle is the business this project would like to like: Germany's №1 IT system house, founder-family anchored (Schick 34.03%), never a buyback at any price, clean related-party record, modest pay, ~70,000 customers, and a dense-local-presence moat no competitor replicates. The gate that fails is the trend, not the level: return on capital employed has declined for four to five consecutive years — end-CE ROCE 14.8% (FY21) → 14.4 → 13.3 → 11.3 → **10.2% (FY25)** — as serial acquisitions compounded capital employed faster than the German demand slump let operating income grow. P4's sustained-decline clause forces Past to WEAK regardless of the still-adequate 13.0% worst-of-four average (precedents: KBX, BNR). The verdict flips back to WATCH arithmetic if FY2026 arrests the decline (EBIT growth outpacing CE growth) with the worst-of-four holding ≥10%; Q4 2025 momentum (business volume +8% FY25) and the 2026 German public-sector/defence IT cycle make that a live possibility worth one scheduled re-test — not an excuse today.

## 2. Understanding

Bechtle sells IT products and services — vendor-neutral hardware/software resale plus consulting, integration and managed services (commodity products, differentiated delivery) — to paying business and public-sector customers from the Mittelstand up, through ~80 system houses in DACH and e-commerce subsidiaries in 14 European countries; a founder-family-anchored, MDAX-listed AG (with a captive leasing arm, Bechtle Financial Services), operationally a decentralised multichannel hybrid of local service branches and online trading, headquartered in Neckarsulm, Germany; prominent (№1 German system house since 1983), large (€6.4bn revenue, €8.6bn business volume, >16,000 employees), and currently in a leadership transformation (CEO Olemotz retires end-2026 after ~20 years). **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four 10y normalized ROCE **13.0%** (wc/end; xc/beg reads 17.8%) and FCFROCE **7.7%** — level-wise ADEQUATE, short of STRONG on both P2 legs. But the ROCE series declines monotonically from FY2021 on the end-CE basis (14.8→10.2%) and over FY2022–25 on the beginning-CE basis (16.1→10.7%): EBIT peaked in FY2023 (€382.3m) and fell to €335.3m by FY2025 while CE grew ~50% on acquired goodwill and working capital. ΔOI/sh geo 9.8%/y over nine years but negative in both FY2024 (−8.1%) and FY2025 (−4.6%); ΔBV/sh 12.8%; L/E 122.9%, comfortably within P5. FCF is violently working-capital-cyclical (FY22 €28m → FY24 €449m → FY25 €145m). **Verdict WEAK** — the sustained multi-year ROCE decline forces the downgrade (P4); the level alone would have read ADEQUATE.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: customer side PASS (~70,000 customers, Mittelstand to public sector, no concentration disclosed or plausible); supplier side PASS via the ready-alternatives exception — hardware vendors are interchangeable, though effective-supplier concentration in software (Microsoft) and vendor-terms power is real and logged. Forces: customer power WEAK (fragmented base, sticky services); **supplier power STRONG** (vendors set partner terms/rebates and can go direct — Broadcom/VMware demonstrated repricing through the channel); substitutes WEAK but watched (direct-to-cloud/vendor-direct disintermediation, contained by SME need for an integrator); new entrants WEAK (scale, certifications, procurement-framework qualifications, 120-location density). Tally 1 strong, contained. Moat: **ingrainedness** — Bechtle is the channel for German SME and public-sector IT; stable. Market growth YES (digitalisation, public/defence IT, digital-sovereignty spend). **Verdict ADEQUATE.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- German IT demand and public-sector budget cycles drive both legs of the P&L; FY2024–25 showed how fast margin and ROCE compress.
- Vendor power: partner-programme terms, rebate changes, and direct-selling moves (hyperscalers, Broadcom-style repricing) sit outside Bechtle's control.
- Serial M&A must keep clearing its cost of capital; the last four years say it hasn't — goodwill is now €984m vs TBV €873m.
- Leadership transition: CEO and CFO both new by 2027 after two decades of Olemotz continuity.
- Convertible (€300m nominal, 2023) adds latent dilution above the conversion price.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

AVOID → WATCH if the FY2026 report shows the ROCE decline arrested (EBIT growth outpacing CE growth, worst-of-four holding ≥10%) with Future intact; the Q4 2025 order-book inflection (backlog €2.7bn) is the evidence to test, not to anticipate. Cheapness would additionally need a roughly two-thirds derating or a step-change in normalized FCF/OI. Next review: **FY2026 annual report (~mid-Mar 2027)**, first year under the incoming CEO.

## C. Calculation notes

**NIBCL (Layer 1, applied all years, logged judgment):** current trade payables + contract liabilities (current + non-current) + current residual (= income-tax payable + current Abgrenzungsposten, derived exactly as current liabilities minus disclosed components; verified to the euro for FY2024–25). Everything else stays in CE per the burden-of-proof rule — deliberately conservative: **Sonstige Verbindlichkeiten remains wholly in CE** because it mixes lease liabilities (debt-equivalent, FY25 €250.3m — must stay) with personnel accruals, VAT and other operating accruals (FY25 ≈ €303m NIBCL-like per note 22) that cannot be split from the multi-year overview before FY2024. Sensitivity: shifting the FY25 note-22 accrual components into NIBCL would lift end-CE FY25 ROCE from 10.2% to ≈ 11.3% — the decline pattern and the WEAK verdict are unchanged. Pension and other provisions, deferred tax liabilities, and long-term trade payables (FY25 €66.6m, acquisition-inherited supplier loans) also stay in CE. Pre-IFRS-15 years (2016–17) carry deferred revenue inside Sonstige Verbindlichkeiten → in CE (conservative). Pre-IFRS-16 years (2016–18): operating-lease commitments not capitalised — undiscounted commitments were well under the P18 10%-of-assets trigger for this branch-office estate (adoption added ≈ €93m non-current lease liabilities against a €2.0bn balance sheet in 2019, ~4.6%); flagged as the FY2019 adoption artifact in the B2 series. Goodwill stays in CE (serial acquirer). IFRS interest-paid reclassification into CFO applied (mandatory); FY16–23 use the P&L finance-expense proxy (assumption, conservative); interest received left in investing (logged — excludes ~€15–17m/y of interest income from FCF, conservative). **CE build FY2025:** TA 4,574,655 − (AP curr 958,312 + contract liab 268,869 + 44,936 + residual 25,896 [tax payable 17,178 + Abgrenzung 8,718]) = **CE_wc 3,276,642**; − cash & STI 452,031 (liquid 378,771 + Geld-/Wertpapieranlagen 73,260) = **CE_xc 2,824,611**. **TBV build FY2025:** shareholders' equity 2,049,518 − goodwill 983,863 − other intangibles 192,413 = **873,242**. **EV build FY2025 (€m):** MCap 3,890.9 + FinVerb 472.6 (convertible 279.2 book / 300.0 nominal + Schuldschein 100.0 + other) + lease liabilities 250.3 + acquisition liabilities 20.7 + pension 12.1 + NCI 2.7 = **EV conservative 4,649.3**; − cash & STI 452.0 = **EV all-cash 4,197.3**. Shares: 126.0m basic = diluted (IS states "unverwässert und verwässert"); the 2017 (1:1) and 2021 (1:2) bonus issues are treated as splits — all per-share history restated to 126.0m.
