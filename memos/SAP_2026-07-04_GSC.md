# SAP — SAP SE: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | ADEQUATE | STRONG | **FAIL** (buybacks to service SBC) | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID (SF FAIL)** |

SAP is a genuinely good business — the textbook switching-cost moat on the corporate system of record, breadth on both sides, zero strong forces, and a past record that cleared its cloud-transition trough with a decade-high FY2025 (worst-of-four ROCE 12.2%, FCF €8.3bn). It fails the gate that is defined by the worst: the completed €5bn 2023–25 buyback program was, in SAP's own words, "designed primarily to service share-based compensation awards" — repurchases at EV/OI multiples of roughly 18–30×, far above conservative worth, to sterilise SBC dilution. That is the book's named strike, and the new €10bn program (Feb 2026–end 2027) entrenches it. Despite a ~45% one-year derating, the stock sits ~187% above the binding trigger. The verdict flips only on a genuine change in repurchase policy (buybacks conditioned on price, or retired capital returned via dividends) plus price; neither is in sight.

## 2. Understanding

SAP sells differentiated, mission-critical enterprise application software — ERP plus finance, HR, supply-chain, procurement and expense suites, increasingly delivered as cloud subscription (RISE/S/4HANA) with embedded AI — paid for by organisations of every size (~400,000+ business customers, none ≥10% of revenue); it competes in the enterprise-software industry as a German SE with a two-tier board, vertically integrated development delivered from own-plus-hyperscaler data centres through a large partner ecosystem; customers are global (Americas ~39%, EMEA ~46%, APJ ~14%) with HQ in Walldorf; a 54-year-old prominent incumbent — Europe's largest software company — late in a licence-to-cloud transformation. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized 10y averages: ROCE 12.2% (end-of-period, with-cash), FCFROCE 9.8% — clears the P3 10% floor, misses the P2 15% bar, so STRONG is unavailable. Growth is well clear of the 3% floor: OI/sh +8.7%/yr and FCF/sh +10.9%/yr geometric, BV/sh +6.9%/yr alongside €2–2.7bn annual dividends. Returns sagged through the cloud transition (ROCE_wc 13.9% → 8.6%, 2020–24, restructuring-laden) but FY2025 reversed decisively to a decade-high 16.4%/14.1% — the P4 sustained-decline downgrade is not applied (call logged, C). Leverage benign: L/E 57%. **Verdict: ADEQUATE** — solid, dividend-adjusted compounding at returns persistently below the STRONG threshold on Marshall's conservative capital base.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS both sides: no IFRS-8 major-customer disclosure across the record (none ≥10% of revenue); cost base is personnel-dominated with hyperscaler spend split across several ready vendors. Forces: customer power WEAK (hundreds of thousands of customers, prohibitive switching costs); supplier power WEAK (labour plus multiple substitutable hyperscalers); substitutes WEAK (doing without or in-house-building the audited system of record is not viable today; agentic-AI displacement is prospective, not demonstrated); new entrants WEAK (fifty years of process/compliance IP, integrator ecosystem, switching costs are barriers to success, not just entry). Tally 0 strong — favourable. Moat: switching costs, single source, dominant; stable, with RISE migrations deepening operational lock-in. Market growth YES (S/4HANA migration wave, enterprise AI budgets). **Verdict: STRONG** — AI substitution is the named watch item, not today's finding.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Comp passes P17: CEO Klein's FY2025 awarded-and-due €16.2m (FY2024 €19.0m) vs the €25.5m/$30m cap and €366m 5%-test; chair fee €600k within the German DAX-chair norm (calibration logged, E). RPT immaterial. Dividends rational (≥40% non-IFRS payout). **The strike is buybacks:** €5.0bn repurchased 2023–25 explicitly "primarily to service share-based compensation awards" at ~18–30× EV/OI, with a €10bn successor program running to end-2027. Repurchasing to sterilise SBC dilution at prices far above conservative worth is a one-strike FAIL. **Verdict: FAIL (buyback strike).**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Agentic AI eroding the application layer's pricing power faster than data-gravity and compliance walls defend it — the cause of the 2025–26 derating and the moat's only live threat.
- The €10bn 2026–27 buyback program continuing SBC sterilisation at high multiples — the standing SF strike.
- Cloud-transition margin gains proving cyclical: FY2025's decade-high ROCE rests on a single post-restructuring year.
- Teradata litigation (€387m expensed 2025) and management's habit of excluding such items from comp targets — a governance drift signal.
- USD weakness compressing reported EUR results (~55% of revenue is dollar-linked; EUR/USD moved 1.04→1.18 in 2025).

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To WATCH: SF flip alone. Past upgrades to STRONG only if the 15%/8% worst-of-four bars hold across the refreshed window (FY2026–27 sustaining ≥16% ROCE would move the averages slowly). Downside flip: demonstrated AI displacement of core ERP seats would force Future to WEAK. **Next review:** FY2026 results (~late Jan 2027), any buyback-policy announcement, or price < €55.
