# ARHS — Arhaus, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Past **WEAK** — an adequate 12.7% return level undone by a sharply negative growth limb and a leverage breach: operating income per share fell 46% in two years and L/E is 234% · Future ADEQUATE · SF **FAIL** (P17 hard strike both reads, $4.65m vs $2.95m/$2.90m ceilings; related-party leases and a 48.7% voting block alongside) · EXPENSIVE (23.1× FCF, 21.3×/18.4× EV/OI) → **AVOID (three gates)**.

Arhaus is what the post-pandemic furniture cycle looks like on the way down. Revenue held up — $1.23bn to $1.38bn over four years — but operating income went $185m → $164m → $87m → $89m, so the return on capital halved twice while the showroom estate grew: capitalised leases are now $527m against $418m of equity. The brand is real and there is no funded debt, which is why this is a three-gate AVOID rather than four. What decides it is a founder-chief-executive paid $4.65m against earnings that no longer support it, three facilities rented from entities he part-owns, his son as head of real estate, and 48.7% of the vote on 32% of the shares.

## 2. Understanding
Arhaus designs and sells premium home furnishings — upholstery, case goods, outdoor, lighting — through roughly 100 company-operated showrooms in 30 states plus catalogue and e-commerce, sourcing from a vendor network across North Carolina, Asia and Europe and delivering through its own white-glove logistics; Ohio-HQ'd, founder-controlled through Class B shares. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **12.72%** (end-basis, with-cash; best variant 19.66%) clears the 10% floor; worst FCFROCE **6.51%** does not clear 8%, so STRONG was never available. The gate fails on the other two limbs. **Growth is negative and steep**: operating income $164.3m → $87.0m → $88.9m, net income $125.2m → $67.3m, operating income per share down 46% in two years on a flat count; the return path reads **19.9 → 10.3 → 8.9%** — two consecutive declines standing, a third at FY2026 firing the trend rule formally. **Leverage breaches**: L/E **233.5%**, almost entirely showroom leases ($527m) and customer deposits ($236m) rather than borrowing — there is no funded debt — but the test reads all liabilities and P6 does not apply. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth PASS both sides (consumer base; diversified vendor network, no disclosed single supplier above the line). Forces: customer power WEAK · supplier power WEAK · **substitutes STRONG** — premium furniture is the definition of a deferrable purchase, and 2024's −2.2% demand comparable against an −8.0% reported comparable is the doing-without form in the data · new entrants WATCH (showroom capital and delivery are real barriers; brand entry at this price point is constant). Tally 1 strong, contained. Moat: **Brand** — a genuine aesthetic identity with pricing power in good years, but fashion-adjacent and heat-dependent, the Amer Sports caveat. Market growth MIXED. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Control: Class B carries ten votes; Reed holds 31.75% of shares and **48.69% of votes** — documented caveat. Capital allocation: **SOUND-leaning** — no funded debt, a $0.35 special dividend for March 2026, showroom capex already cut $107m → $78m as returns fell. The gate fails on the strike, with the related-party channel an independent second negative. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers (context for the AVOID)
The bull case is a cyclical trough: demand comparables have stabilised, 2026 guidance points to $1.43–1.47bn of revenue and $150–161m of adjusted EBITDA, and a recovering housing market would lift operating income back toward 2022–23 levels and the returns path with it. Plausible — and a cycle call, not a quality one. The bear case is operating leverage in reverse: a growing fixed lease base against a category customers can simply postpone.

The comp strike clears on a package under ~$2.9m or roughly a 60% rise in earnings. Price needs a fall of about three-quarters.
