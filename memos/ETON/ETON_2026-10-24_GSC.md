## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK (losses); Future WEAK (customer conc.); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (compensation); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** (Past + Future + SF).

Eton buys, licenses and relaunches small-market medicines for rare diseases and sells nearly all of them through one specialty pharmacy. Revenue grew from nothing in 2018 to $80.0m in FY2025, but operating income was negative in every fiscal year of the record, and leverage reached 252% of equity after the debt-funded Increlex purchase. One pharmacy takes 85–94% of revenue, and Increlex comes from a single source, Ipsen. The CEO's compensation actually paid rose to $7.9m in FY2024, a loss year. Operating income turned positive in H1 2026 ($15.2m), yet at $50 the shares trade far beyond any buy line. A flip needs several years of positive operating income, a wider customer base and pay set against profits.

## 2. Understanding

Eton Pharmaceuticals is a Deer Park, Illinois-based, Nasdaq-listed (IPO November 2018, founded 2017) rare-disease drug company that buys, licenses or develops off-patent and niche medicines made by contract manufacturers and licensors — Increlex (mecasermin injection for severe primary IGF-1 deficiency, bought from Ipsen in December 2024), Alkindi Sprinkle and Khindivi (hydrocortisone granules and oral solution for children with adrenal insufficiency), Galzin (zinc acetate for Wilson disease), Hemangeol (propranolol for infantile hemangioma, from 2026), generic carglumic acid, betaine anhydrous and nitisinone for inherited metabolic disorders, and the PKU GOLIKE medical food — and sells them in the United States mainly to the specialty pharmacy AnovoRx (84.6% of FY2025 net revenue), which stores, dispenses and ships them to patients whose treatment is paid by commercial insurers and Medicaid, plus licensing income from partners abroad (revenue $80.0m in FY2025, one reportable segment).
