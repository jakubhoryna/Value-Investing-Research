# MAERSK-B.CO — A.P. Møller - Mærsk: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS · Past ADEQUATE · Future WEAK · SF PASS · Cheapness EXPENSIVE → **AVOID (Future WEAK)** — unchanged from v1; cheapness moves FAIR → EXPENSIVE.

The delta since July is a rate shock, not a change in the business. The Strait of Hormuz closure absorbed tonnage and lifted Q2 loaded rates 22%; Q2 EBIT doubled to $1.6bn and FY2026 underlying EBIT guidance jumped from −$1.5–1.0bn (May) to $4.5–6.5bn (August). The share price followed, +45% since v1. None of this touches the deciding gate: a disruption premium on an undifferentiated service is not pricing power, the fleet is still growing ~5% a year with almost nothing scrapped, and on 28 September Maersk announced a return to Suez transits in both directions — the reversal mechanism is already in motion. Maersk remains a well-run, net-cash, shareholder-friendly operator of a structurally bad industry. A flip needs the industry to change — durable capacity discipline — or Terminals/Logistics to dominate group economics; cheapness alone can never flip it.

## 2. Understanding

Maersk sells containerised ocean freight (commodity service, ~65% of revenue), freight-adjacent logistics — reorganised in April 2026 into Forwarding, Solutions and Landside — and container-terminal handling to 100,000+ paying shippers worldwide; a vertically integrated Danish A/S, foundation-controlled via dual-class shares, the world #2 liner, transformed 2017–24 from conglomerate to pure container logistics. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

No new fiscal year; restated from v1, not re-derived. Worst-of-four normalized ROCE ≈ 10.8%, FCFROCE ≥ 12.0% — ADEQUATE, carried by the 2021–22 windfall (72% of decade OI; ex-windfall ≈ 5%). L/E 57%. Two notes for the FY2026 roll: vessel useful lives moved from 20 to 25 years on 1 January 2026, lifting reported OI ≈ $0.7bn a year (H1: $351m) — kept as reported, but FY26 OI is not like-for-like with the window; and the FY2016 loss exits the window, which will flatter the averages mechanically. **ADEQUATE.**

New entrants **STRONG**: nominal fleet +5.4% year on year, demolitions near zero for six quarters, 31 dual-fuel newbuilds on Maersk's own order book. Suppliers and substitutes WEAK. Tally 2 → stop signal. Moat: none at group level; Terminals earns a real concession moat (LTM ROIC 14.8%) on ~9% of revenue, and Logistics' 5.1% margin still trails the 6% ambition. Market growth YES (~4% 2026). **WEAK.**
