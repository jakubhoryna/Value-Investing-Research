## 2. Understanding

Booking Holdings is a Delaware-incorporated, NASDAQ-listed online travel agency — operating Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK and OpenTable — that earns commissions and merchant margins from hotels and other travel suppliers (plus fees from travelers) on accommodation, flight, car and restaurant bookings, generating the large majority of its revenue internationally (Europe-heavy, via Booking.com) as the growing global leader of the OTA industry. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

ROCE_xc cleared 15% in 9 of 10 years (21.8% → 41.6% pre-COVID; 33.8%–117% in the recovery; n.m.-positive in 2024–25 as ex-cash capital employed turned *negative* — customers and suppliers fund the business). FCFROCE_xc ≥8% in the same 9 years. Only 2020 fails (operating loss, FCF −$0.2B). OI/sh and FCF/sh compounded at 15.7% and 16.9% over the decade. L/E is optically broken (812% in 2022, negative equity since 2023) but the cause is $54B of cumulative buybacks, not losses; net debt is 0.0–0.3× FCF and interest coverage ~8× (judgment call documented, Appendix C). **STRONG** — exceptional returns on shrinking capital; the one bad year was an exogenous shutdown of global travel.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: hundreds of millions of travelers and ~3.5M properties; neither side can consolidate. Forces MIXED: customer power LOW, supplier power MEDIUM (chains push direct; Google controls paid traffic), substitutes MEDIUM-HIGH (direct booking, Airbnb, nascent AI agents), entrants MEDIUM, rivalry HIGH (Expedia, Airbnb, Trip.com). Moat: two-sided **network** effects reinforced by **cost** (scale in performance marketing and data) and **brand** (Booking.com in Europe) — stable, with rising direct/app mix offsetting Google dependence, but AI-agent disintermediation is a genuine new erosion vector. Market growth YES (travel ~GDP+, online share rising) — supporting, not load-bearing. **ADEQUATE** (forces MIXED caps it below STRONG).

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Comp: CEO Fogel $35.4M for 2025 (0.40% of OI), majority PSUs with 3-yr financial targets and TSR modifiers; 88% say-on-pay support. Single share class; insider ownership thin (~0.2%) but customary at this scale. RPTs: nothing significant flagged (verification gap noted, Appendix E). Buybacks: $30B+ since 2022, genuinely shrinking shares — but executed programmatically at ~14–20× FCF, well above framework-cheap levels. Dividends initiated 2024, growing; no empire-building (legacy OpenTable/KAYAK write-downs — $941M in 2016, $1.06B in 2020 — stem from a 2014 deal, ~1% of market cap). **PASS**, with the price-insensitive buyback caveat prominently flagged.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- AI agents (OpenAI, Google, vertical assistants) become the booking interface and commoditize OTA supply — the single largest moat threat.
- Google traffic economics: ~$8B+/yr performance marketing concentrated in one channel whose rules can change.
- Regulatory squeeze in the EU (DMA gatekeeper designation, rate-parity rules) compresses take rates in the most profitable region.
- Travel demand shock (pandemic, war, recession): 2020 showed OI can swing to a loss within quarters.
- Negative equity removes balance-sheet optics as a cushion; a funding-market freeze coinciding with a demand shock would be painful despite net debt ≈ 0.2× FCF.
