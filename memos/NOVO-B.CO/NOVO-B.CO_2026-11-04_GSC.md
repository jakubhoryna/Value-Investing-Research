## 2. Understanding

Novo Nordisk makes and sells prescription peptide medicines — mainly one molecule, semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy injectable and pill), plus insulins and a small Blood & endocrine disorders unit — bought by three US wholesalers on terms set by PBMs, insurers and government (US over half of sales) and by health systems elsewhere; a 103-year-old, Foundation-controlled (77% of votes) Danish group, integrated from API to fill-finish, selling in about 170 countries. PASS. Lens STD. Blood & endocrine disorders (formerly Rare disease) was 6.3% of FY2025 sales and 0.9% of segment profit, so one material unit; no material listed holding.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: `customer conc.` flagged.** Three US wholesalers took 23% / 18% / 14% of global net sales in 2025 (23/17/17 in 2024; 22/17/15 in 2023; 19/14/13 in 2022; 18/13/13 in 2021; 19/13/12 in 2020 and 2019; 20/13/13 in 2018; 21/13/12 in 2017 and 2016 — 20-F notes 2.1/2.2, carried from v11). One material unit, so the group-statement rule does not apply. **Strong-moat test:** (1) dominant — Novo claims more than 50% of US branded-obesity new patient starts for the Wegovy franchise (9M report, company data), but tirzepatide competes on every indication and semaglutide copies are on sale in Canada and other international markets: dominance is not evidenced; (2) stable or strengthening — no, the moat is eroding (below); (3) pricing power over the wholesalers and the payers behind them — no: realised prices fell in every region in 9M 2026. The pass fails, so the concentration is a STRONG customer-power call under P16. **Suppliers: no concentration** — API, device and fill-finish are in-house (the Catalent sites); no supplier disclosed above 10% of COGS + opex.

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | Payers, PBMs and the US government set net price (MFN agreement; Medicaid coverage cuts; list prices of Wegovy and Ozempic cut by about 50% and 35% from 1 Jan 2027), and the three wholesalers carry 55% of sales without a strong moat behind it. |

Tally **2 strong** → Future WEAK. Neither strong force has a containment mechanism that keeps it away from the moat's source; both act on the price the exclusivity protects.

**Moat.** Single source: government-granted exclusivity on semaglutide, supported by manufacturing scale. Direction **eroding**: the 9M report attributes the 2026 outlook to volume growth "countered by lower realised prices, including the MFN agreement in the US and the loss of exclusivity for the semaglutide molecule in certain markets in International Operations"; Wegovy injectable US sales fell 15% at CER in 9M 2026 on lower realised prices; the full-year adjusted sales outlook is −2% to +2% at CER. Counter-evidence logged: Wegovy pill US sales of DKK 9,575m in 9M 2026 and the claimed branded-obesity NBRx lead. *The line where a second analyst may disagree:* if FY2026 shows share holding with net price per patient stabilising through the January 2027 list-price cut, the moat could read stable — Future would still need a weaker customer or substitute call to leave WEAK.

**Market growth:** YES in volume (Q3 2026 obesity sales +22% at CER on volume) but price-deflating; **MIXED** in value.

**Declines (J6).** The FCFROCE decline of FY2023–25 is named: a capex build (DKK 133bn of PP&E spend in FY2023–25) and acquired capital (the Catalent sites, Akero), with competitive erosion behind the flat FY2025 operating income. The erosion part matches the eroding moat.

**Verdict: WEAK** — two strong forces and an eroding moat; reasons in order: moat eroding, 2+ forces (the concentration is inside the customer-power call).
