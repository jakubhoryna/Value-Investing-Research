## 2. Understanding

Novo Nordisk makes and sells prescription peptide medicines — mainly one molecule, semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy injectable and pill), plus insulins and a small Rare disease unit — bought by three US wholesalers on terms set by PBMs, insurers and government (US over half of sales) and by health systems elsewhere; a 103-year-old, Foundation-controlled (77% of votes) Danish group, integrated from API to fill-finish, selling in about 170 countries. PASS. Lens STD. Rare disease is 6.3% of sales and 0.9% of segment profit, so one material unit; no material listed holding.

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four ROCE is 56.0% over ten years and 49.1% over five; maintenance-capex FCFROCE (constructed) is 38.6% and 33.7%, with the all-capex reading at 33.6%. Operating income per share compounded 12.1% a year on the harsher read; maintenance FCF per share only 0.3%, because FY2025 FCF collapsed. FCFROCE fell in each of FY2023–25 on end-of-period capital, a sustained decline that counts despite the build-out. The level bars and path (a) growth hold, so the decline caps Past. Leverage 180%. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

A strong-moat pass is unavailable — the moat is eroding and net price keeps falling — so the concentration joins payer power in a STRONG customer-power call. Substitutes STRONG: tirzepatide leads and Novo answers by in-licensing. Supplier and entrant power WEAK. Tally 2. Moat: exclusivity on semaglutide, eroding; the company plans around its loss. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK.**

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: `customer conc.` flagged.** Three US wholesalers took 23% / 18% / 14% of global net sales in 2025 (23/17/17 in 2024; 22/17/15 in 2023; 19/14/13 in 2022; 18/13/13 in 2021; 19/13/12 in 2020 and 2019; 20/13/13 in 2018; 21/13/12 in 2017 and 2016 — 20-F notes 2.1/2.2). **Strong-moat test:** (1) dominant — the company led GLP-1 volume, but tirzepatide now leads obesity, so dominance is not evidenced; (2) stable or strengthening — no, the moat is eroding (below); (3) pricing power over the wholesalers and the payers behind them — no, net price falls in the company's own bridges. The pass fails, so the concentration is a STRONG customer-power call under P16. Containment of the distributor link is plausible (the wholesalers are pass-through and price is set with payers), but the payers hold the price, so the force stays STRONG. **Suppliers: no concentration** — API, device and fill-finish are in-house (the Catalent sites); contract manufacturers' DKK 71bn of purchase obligations are spread; no supplier disclosed above 10% of COGS + opex.

|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | Payers, PBMs and the US government set net price (rebates 69–75% of US gross sales; MFN agreement; Medicaid coverage cuts), and the three wholesalers carry 55% of sales without a strong moat behind it. Tally **2 strong** → Future WEAK. Neither strong force has a containment mechanism that keeps it away from the moat's source; both act on the price the exclusivity protects.

**Moat.** Single source: government-granted exclusivity on semaglutide, supported by manufacturing scale. Direction **eroding**: the CMD frames the plan as navigating semaglutide's loss of exclusivity (2028–30 "maximise semaglutide", 2030+ "grow beyond"), the sales bridge sets lower prices and loss of exclusivity against launches, and the 2030 ambition is growth only in line with large-pharma peers from a 2026 base below 2025's DKK 306bn adjusted sales. **Market growth:** YES in volume (branded obesity +81%, GLP-1 diabetes +29%, May 2026 MAT, company data) but price-deflating; **MIXED** in value.

**Declines (J6).** The FCFROCE decline of FY2023–25 is named: a capex build (DKK 133bn of PP&E spend in FY2023–25) and acquired capital (the Catalent sites, Akero), with competitive erosion behind the flat FY2025 operating income. The erosion part matches the eroding moat.

**Verdict: WEAK** — two strong forces and an eroding moat; reasons in order: moat eroding, 2+ forces (the concentration is inside the customer-power call).
