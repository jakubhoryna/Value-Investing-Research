# LSS.PA — Lectra SA: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future ADEQUATE, shareholder-friendliness PASS (one flag: capital-allocation CAUTION), cheapness EXPENSIVE: **AVOID (Past)**.

Lectra was a high-return niche franchise: cutting-room equipment, CAD software and the consumables and service contracts that ride on an installed base, earning 22–30% on capital employed before 2020. Since the 2021 Gerber Technology merger and the SaaS acquisitions of 2023–24 it has become a larger, much less profitable business: return on capital employed fell to 5.1% in FY2025 and operating income per share is below its FY2015 level. The past-record test fails on that sustained decline, not on leverage or volatility. For the verdict to move, ROCE would have to rebuild toward the mid-teens on the enlarged base; at €20.70 the price would also need to fall a long way, because FY2025 operating income (€35.4m) carries about €20m a year of acquisition amortisation (€22.7m in FY2024).

## 2. Understanding
Lectra designs, manufactures and sells automated cutting equipment, design and pattern-making (CAD/PLM) software, SaaS platforms for fashion-brand data, marketing analytics and supply-chain traceability (Launchmetrics, TextileGenesis, Retviews), and the spare parts, consumables and maintenance contracts that serve its installed base. Recurring revenue is 75% of the FY2025 total. The customers who pay are apparel brands and their subcontractors (55% of FY2025 software and equipment sales), automotive-interior and airbag makers (28%), furniture makers (6%) and other industrial users; no customer exceeds 4% of revenue. It is a French company headquartered in Paris with its main plant at Bordeaux-Cestas, manufacturing also in the US and China, and selling worldwide (62% of revenue outside Europe). It is a 1973-founded mid-cap, run since 2002 by its 12.7% shareholder Daniel Harari (chairman-CEO since 2017) and in transition from equipment toward subscriptions after absorbing Gerber, its main competitor. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**WEAK.** Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **10.2%** (end of period, with cash), just above the ADEQUATE floor, and worst FCFROCE is 10.9%. The level hides the trend: beginning-of-period ROCE fell from 30.0% (FY2016) through 22.2% (FY2019) to 10.7%, 7.6%, 7.9% and 5.1% in FY2022–25; the halves are 19.3% and 7.4%. That is a sustained multi-year decline, and it forces WEAK on its own. Operating income per share shrank 0.8% a year over the decade (€1.01 to €0.93) because 5.0m shares were issued for Gerber; FCF per share grew 9.4% a year. L/E is 133%, within bounds but up from 77–99% as minority put liabilities arrived.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth **PASSES**: no customer above 4% of revenue, the ten largest under 15%, and the company rates supplier dependency low. Customer power, supplier power and substitutes are WEAK. New entrants are **STRONG**: Lectra names the rise of regional competitors as a material risk, and its main equipment rivals build in China at lower cost. The moat is **switching costs**: an installed base of cutters and CAD seats tied to Lectra software, consumables and multi-year service contracts. Stable, on 2% recurring growth in FY2025, but not strengthening: non-recurring (new-system) revenue fell 12% in FY2025 and 26% in H1 2026. Market growth is MIXED (SaaS +14%, new systems falling). **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
The chairman-CEO earned €530k in FY2025 and holds no options; directors' fees total €425k for the board. The highest-paid employee is not disclosed: the group reports a 25× ratio to the undisclosed median, so the pay test is **INDETERMINATE**. No related-party agreements exist. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**. Gerber cost €339m, at least 15.6× its pro-forma EBITDA; Launchmetrics was bought at about 22× EBITDA; and TextileGenesis minorities were bought in 2026 at 2.6× the 2023 entry valuation. Returns on the enlarged base fell with them. The 2026 buyback, at about 10× FCF, is the counterweight. **PASS** with one flag.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers
- Equipment demand in fashion, automotive and furniture is in a cyclical and tariff-driven slump; another weak year pushes ROCE lower.
- The SaaS acquisitions may never earn their purchase prices; €66.6m of minority buy-out commitments remain at 30 Jun 2026, through 2030.
- Lower-cost Chinese equipment makers erode pricing on new systems, the feeder of the recurring base.
- Succession: the board plans for the chairman-CEO's foreseeable departure within three years (age limit), and his name is inseparable from Lectra's.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review
Toward WATCH: ROCE recovering to and holding above roughly 12–15% on the post-acquisition base, with operating income per share growing again. A BUY would additionally need the price at or below €1.1–2.3 at FY2025 earnings, or operating income near €100–140m at today's price. Toward a firmer AVOID: further acquisitions or minority buy-outs at rising multiples. Next review: 9M 2026 results (28 Oct 2026) and FY2026 results (10 Feb 2027).
