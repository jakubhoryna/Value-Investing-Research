Bajaj Auto earns high returns on its own Indian motorcycle and three-wheeler business, and its treasury book keeps leverage low. Three things stand between it and a WATCH. The filings carry no customer statement for a business that sells through dealers and importers. The captive finance arm is funded through one capital-market channel. And in November 2025 the company took control of a loss-making KTM group, after lending it ₹4,602 crore. Section 8 lists what would flip it. At ₹10,900 the shares trade about five times the ₹2,174 trigger.

## 2. Understanding

Bajaj Auto Limited, a Pune-based company controlled by the Bajaj family and listed in India since its 2008 demerger, designs, makes and sells motorcycles (Pulsar, Platina, Dominar, and KTM and Triumph models it builds for its partners), passenger and cargo three-wheelers (ICE, CNG and electric) and Chetak electric scooters, paid for by its Indian dealers and by importer-distributors in about 100 countries across Africa, Latin America and Asia; since 18 November 2025 it also controls the Austrian KTM group through Bajaj Mobility AG (74.94%), and its NBFC subsidiary Bajaj Auto Credit lends to Indian retail buyers of its vehicles. **PASS.**

- **Automotive (ex-KTM):** two- and three-wheelers and Chetak scooters, sold to Indian dealers and export importer-distributors.
- **Financing:** Bajaj Auto Credit Ltd, a captive NBFC lending to Indian retail buyers of Bajaj vehicles.
- **Bajaj Mobility AG (BMAG.SW, controlled listed subsidiary):** KTM, Husqvarna and GasGas premium motorcycles made in Austria, sold through dealers worldwide.
- Investments, a treasury book, is a securities segment, not a unit (Appendix C).
