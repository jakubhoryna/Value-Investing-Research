# VOW3 — Volkswagen AG (preferred): GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | **WEAK** | **WEAK** | **FAIL** | FAIR | **AVOID** |

Volkswagen is a genuine deep-asset-discount — it trades at **0.19× book / 0.38× tangible book**, and its listed stakes in Porsche AG (~75%) and Traton (~88%) are alone worth roughly the entire group market cap — but it is exactly the value trap the model is built to reject. The consolidated business earns a **~3–4% return on capital**, well below its cost of capital, on a €644bn balance sheet dominated by financial-services receivables; the competitive position is deteriorating (three of four forces strong, the China profit engine eroding under Chinese-EV entrants); and the capital is trapped inside a Porsche-SE- and Lower-Saxony-controlled structure whose priorities are stakeholder-political, not shareholder-returns. Statistically cheap on assets, structurally weak on quality and governance. The low multiple is the market pricing weak, deteriorating fundamentals — not an opportunity. Nothing at a *price* flips this; only a durable return recovery plus a governance simplification would, neither in evidence.

## 2. Understanding

Volkswagen AG is the Porsche-SE-controlled, Lower-Saxony-anchored (via the statutory "VW Law" blocking minority) German holding-and-operating group that designs, builds and sells increasingly commoditised passenger cars, light and heavy commercial vehicles and motorcycles under a ten-brand portfolio (VW, Audi, Porsche, Škoda, SEAT/Cupra, Bentley, Lamborghini, Ducati, plus Traton/MAN/Scania) to millions of retail and fleet/dealer customers worldwide through dealer networks, and additionally runs a very large captive Financial Services bank-and-leasing arm funding dealers and buyers — a vertically integrated, capital- and R&D-intensive volume automaker whose balance sheet is dominated by finance receivables, HQ Wolfsburg, historically the world's #1–2 carmaker by volume, now losing its former China lead. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE runs **~3.4% (with-cash) to 3.9% (ex-cash)** over FY2021–25 — a fraction of the 10% ADEQUATE floor — corroborated by S&P's 2.84% ROIC / 4.9% ROE. This is the captive-finance reality: ~€9–22bn of operating income against a €480–586bn capital base swollen by finance receivables and leased assets. FCFROCE is **~0%**, negative in three of five years (group cash flow consumed by leasing-book growth; even VW's cleaner "Automotive net cash flow" is modest). Per-share OI fell from €45 (2023) to €17.7 (2025); FCF/sh turned negative — the growth test fails. L/E **234%** breaches the 200% flag (finance-matched, but mechanical). **WEAK** — sub-cost-of-capital returns, deteriorating. (5-yr window; FY2016–20 unsourced — see App. A — but those dieselgate-tail years were weaker still, so the verdict only hardens.)

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS both sides (millions of retail buyers; a vast supplier base VW's scale dominates — with acute semiconductor/battery chokepoints, per the 2021–22 chip crunch). But **three of four forces are STRONG (unfavourable)**: customer power (low switching costs, fierce price competition), substitutes (low-cost Chinese EVs, Tesla, declining ownership), and new entrants (the EV transition has collapsed the old scale/brand barriers — Chinese OEMs are taking VW's China share, once its profit engine). Only supplier power reads WEAK. That is the book's stop signal. Moat: real at the premium end (Porsche, Audi brands) and in scale/cost, but **eroding** — no dominant, stable moat to floor the verdict. Market mature/contested. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Comp is genuinely disciplined: CEO Blume €7.42m FY2025 (down from €10.35m), ~0.1% of NI — far below both the 5%-of-lower(FCF,NI) hard strike and the ~€37m absolute trigger; the board is taking 11% waivers through 2029; supervisory-board fees modest; **no buybacks** (returns are via dividends — P19 inapplicable). What fails is control-and-allocation: VW is a formally *dependent* company of Porsche SE (53.3% of votes), with Lower Saxony's bespoke-statute veto (VW Law) and the Pötsch dual-role interlock (chairs VW's supervisory board while CEO of Porsche SE), **paired with evidenced value-destructive allocation** (the multi-billion Cariad software write-offs; restructuring repeatedly vetoed by state/labour; chronic sub-cost-of-capital returns). Per §2·1C the dual-class/control caveat converts to a strike here; equivalently, two independent governance negatives. **FAIL.** (Charitable read — normal German co-determined controlled company, rational dividends — would be PASS with 2 flags logged; either way, moot under AVOID.)

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- **China cliff:** VW's former ~40%-of-volume, JV-driven China profit engine is being taken by BYD and domestic EV makers — the single largest structural driver of the eroding moat.
- **Value trap:** the 0.19× book / SOTP discount can persist indefinitely while capital stays trapped in a state/family-controlled structure — cheapness without a catalyst.
- **Capital intensity:** simultaneous mega-programmes (PowerCo batteries, software, EV platforms, US localisation) against ~3–4% ROCE keep FCF thin and returns below cost of capital.
- **Governance lock:** the VW Law veto and Porsche-SE control make shareholder-value-maximising restructuring structurally hard (the failed 2024–25 plant-closure fight).
- **Cyclicality + tariffs:** US auto-tariff exposure and a mature, contested global market amplify an already low-margin base.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Verdict flips toward WATCH only on a *combination* the model would need to see, not a price move: (i) durable group ROCE recovery toward ≥10% (industrial mix re-based, China stabilised), **and** (ii) a genuine governance simplification (VW-Law repeal / Porsche-SE structural cleanup reducing the control discount and freeing capital allocation), **and** at least one WEAK force neutralised. Any single one is insufficient — Past, Future and SF all fail today. **Next review:** FY2026 results (~mid-Mar 2027); earlier only on a structural governance/ownership event. This is a structural AVOID; no price triggers a re-look.

## C. Calculation notes
- **Captive-finance treatment (default):** group OI (incl. Financial Services operating result) over CE that retains finance receivables and leased assets — the framework default; produces ~3–4% ROCE. **Logged symmetric-exclusion alternative:** stripping the finance arm both sides gives an industrial ROCE that is higher (~8–11% in good years) but still fails ADEQUATE on a normalized basis after the 2024–25 deterioration, and the Automotive Division carries net *liquidity* (~€30–40bn), which is why industrial EV is small. Not tabulated by year (App. A gap); does not change WEAK.
- **NIBCL conservative:** AP + accrued only; "other current liabilities" (tax, deferred income, current provisions) retained in CE per burden-of-proof. Current portion of long-term debt (€127.5bn FY25) always in CE.
- **FCF:** CFO − PP&E capex − capitalised development costs (an automaker's dev-capitalisation is economic capex; using PP&E-only, as some data vendors do, overstates FCF). Group FCF is distorted by finance-book growth; VW's "Automotive net cash flow" is the cleaner but still-modest industrial figure.
- **China JV profits** sit below the operating line (equity-accounted, in the financial result) — a downward distortion to reported OI, noted per framework; no add-back taken.
- **TBV** = Equity − (goodwill + intangibles €91.5bn, mostly capitalised dev costs) = €96.8bn.
- **Bias check:** none live. (Peculiarity/miscontrast checked — the striking asset discount is the temptation; the model's quality-first order handles it. No position; no endowment/consistency hazard.)
