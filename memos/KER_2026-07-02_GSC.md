# KER — Kering SA: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | WEAK | WEAK | FAIL | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** |

Kering is a portfolio of genuine luxury brands wrapped around one badly impaired asset. Gucci — roughly 60% of group profit — is mid-crisis, and the group's return on capital has collapsed from a ~27% ROCE_xc in 2022 to ~4.7% in 2025. This is a triple-gate failure, the strongest form of AVOID: the past record has deteriorated for four straight years, the profit-dominant brand moat is actively eroding, and management has spent the downturn levering the balance sheet with peak-cycle acquisitions and buybacks. A de Meo turnaround ("ReconKering") may well work — the brands are real and the plan is credible — but nothing in the framework rewards a bet on a turnaround that has not yet shown up in the numbers. The verdict flips off AVOID only when Gucci's recurring operating income visibly inflects upward across consecutive periods **and** the capital-allocation record cleans up; until then this is a name to leave alone, not a cheap-quality compounder.

## 2. Understanding

Kering SA is a Paris-listed luxury-goods conglomerate that designs, manufactures and sells — mostly through directly operated stores — leather goods, apparel, footwear, jewellery, watches and eyewear under a stable of Houses (Gucci, Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta, Balenciaga, Alexander McQueen, Boucheron, Pomellato, Qeelin, plus Kering Eyewear) to affluent consumers across Asia-Pacific, Western Europe, North America and Japan, and it is currently the challenged number-three European luxury group in a cyclical downturn and mid-turnaround. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

The decline is the story. ROCE_xc fell 26.7% → 19.3% → 7.7% → 4.7% (FY22–FY25); FCFROCE_xc followed 15.9% → 7.7% → 4.4% → 6.5%. Recurring OI/share dropped from €44 to €13, recurring operating margin from 27% to 11%, and net income to a near-zero €72m in 2025. Leverage climbed (L/E 126% → 165%, below the 200% flag but rising on acquisition debt). TBV/share swung negative in 2023–24 before a 2025 rebound driven by real-estate disposals, not operations. A four-year, unbroken collapse in returns forces the lowest rating regardless of the once-elite absolute levels. **WEAK** — sustained ROCE/FCFROCE decline.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASSES (millions of customers, diversified supply base). But forces are MIXED-to-unfavourable: rivalry is HIGH with LVMH and Hermès out-investing a share-losing Kering, and substitutes (resale, experiences, rival houses) are MEDIUM-HIGH. The moat is **Brand**, and at the profit-dominant House it is **eroding** — Gucci revenue fell 22% in 2025 amid a full creative reset. Saint Laurent, Bottega and the eyewear/jewellery platforms remain plausible moats, but ~60% profit concentration in an eroding brand, amid unfavourable rivalry, tips the group below the "plausible-moat" bar. Market is MIXED (structural growth, cyclical trough). **WEAK.** (ADEQUATE is arguable on portfolio diversification — see Appendix C — but does not change the final verdict.)

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Two clear strikes. **Capital allocation (named strike):** management pursued debt-funded, empire-building M&A into the downturn — Creed (~€3.5bn), 30% of Valentino (€1.7bn), and prestige real estate — driving net financial debt from near-zero (2021) toward ~€8bn while core ROCE collapsed. **Buybacks:** ~€1.0bn (2022) and €0.5bn (2021) repurchased near all-time-high prices of €500–700, versus €266 today. Add a control flag: Artémis holds ~42% of capital but ~59% of votes via double-voting shares, subordinating outside owners. Skin-in-the-game is high and the dividend was rationally cut (€14 → €3), but the strikes stand. **FAIL.**

## 6. Price

| Metric | Current | Threshold | Pass/Fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| MCap / FCF | 14.4× | < 8 | FAIL |
| EV / Operating income (recurring) | 29.2× | < 7 (sell > 25) | FAIL |
| MCap / Book value | 2.2× | < 5 | PASS |
| MCap / Tangible book value | 10.6× | < 3 | FAIL |

Only book value passes. EV/OI at 29× sits above the sell signal but is distorted by trough OI against large net debt (7× trough OI < net debt → negative implied equity), so it is **suspended** as mechanically unusable here. Binding constraint: MCap/TBV strict (≈ €75), with MCap/FCF (≈ €148) the more economically meaningful anchor since TBV is a weak gauge for a brand-driven business whose value sits in intangibles. **EXPENSIVE.** Trigger band ≈ €75–150 — moot while quality gates fail.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Gucci recovery stalls or the Demna creative reset misfires, extending the profit trough well beyond 2026.
- Artémis's own ~€7bn standalone debt constrains the parent and pressures Kering dividends/strategy.
- Rivals (LVMH, Hermès) permanently capture share Kering cedes during its self-inflicted reset.
- Further debt-funded M&A (Valentino call option, 2026–28) re-levers the balance sheet at the wrong time.
- Upside risk to the AVOID: a faster-than-expected margin recovery would re-rate the shares sharply.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Flips toward WATCH only when **both** (a) Gucci recurring OI inflects up across two consecutive halves and group ROCE_xc recovers toward double digits, and (b) capital allocation demonstrably normalises (no fresh peak-cycle debt-funded M&A; buybacks, if any, below intrinsic value). Cheapness is secondary until then. **Next review:** FY2026 results (late-Feb 2027), or earlier on clear evidence of a Gucci OI inflection.

## C. Calculation notes

- **Latest-year (FY2025) CE build:** Total assets 41,182 − NIBCL 4,502 = CE_wc 36,680; − Cash+STI 4,415 = CE_xc 32,265. NIBCL = total current liab 7,728 − current LT debt 2,046 − current leases 1,180 = 4,502. IFRS-16 lease liabilities treated as debt-like (not subtracted as NIBCL), consistent with the project's lease-as-debt convention.
- **FY2025 TBV build:** Common equity 14,706 − goodwill 3,666 − other intangibles 7,962 = 3,078. (TBV excludes the €809m minority interest.)
- **FY2025 EV build:** MCap 32,755 (266.30 × 123.0m) + total debt 18,564 (incl. €6,212m IFRS-16 leases) + NCI 809 − cash+STI 4,415 = **47,713**. Ex-lease convention (company's "net financial debt" ~€8.0bn) gives EV ≈ 41,501 and EV/OI ≈ 25.4× — still a fail; lease treatment does not change the verdict.
- **Judgment call — EV/OI suspension:** at FY2025 trough recurring OI (€1,632m), 7× OI (€11.4bn) is below net debt, so the EV/OI threshold-implied equity price is negative. The screen is therefore mechanically unusable at the cyclical trough (analogous to the project's MCap/FCF-suspension rule during elevated-capex phases) and is set aside for the trigger band, with the failure noted qualitatively.
- **Judgment call — Future WEAK vs ADEQUATE:** breadth passes and the group retains several intact brand moats (Saint Laurent, Bottega, eyewear/jewellery), which supports an ADEQUATE reading. WEAK is chosen because ~60% of profit sits in Gucci, whose brand moat is demonstrably eroding, amid unfavourable rivalry — an eroding profit-dominant moat is the §2 WEAK trigger. The moat-decides floor does **not** apply, as it requires a *dominant, stable* moat; Gucci's is unstable. The distinction is immaterial to the final verdict (Past WEAK + SF FAIL already force AVOID).
- **Recurring OI used for returns** (company definition, excludes non-recurring restructuring/impairment); statutory OI shown in B1 for reference. Using statutory OI would lower returns further and strengthen, not weaken, the WEAK verdict.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

- **Breadth — customers:** PASS. Millions of luxury consumers across four major regions; no customer concentration (directly operated retail is ~80%+ of sales).
- **Breadth — suppliers:** PASS. Diversified materials/leather supply base with ongoing vertical integration; no single-supplier dependency disclosed.
- **Five forces:** Customer bargaining power MEDIUM (aspirational buyers defer easily in downturns; top-end loyal). Supplier bargaining power LOW–MEDIUM. Substitutes MEDIUM–HIGH (rival houses, resale/secondhand, experiences). New entrants LOW–MEDIUM (heritage barriers high; niche/digital brands nibble). Rivalry HIGH (LVMH, Hermès, Richemont out-investing a share-losing Kering). **Overall: MIXED, tilting unfavourable.**
- **Moat:** Source = **Brand** (heritage price premium for like-for-like function). Direction = **eroding** at Gucci (−22% revenue 2025, mid-reset), stable at Saint Laurent/Bottega. Profit concentration (~60% Gucci) means the group-weighted moat direction is negative.
- **Market growth:** MIXED. Personal-luxury structurally grows long-term but is in a cyclical/tourism-driven trough; weak Asia and Gulf demand in 2025–26.

- **Inputs (FY2025):** FCF 2,270; recurring OI 1,632; common equity 14,706; TBV 3,078; cash+STI 4,415; total debt (incl. leases) 18,564; NCI 809; shares 123.0m; price €266.30; MCap 32,755; EV 47,713.

- **Threshold-implied prices (per share):**
  - MCap/FCF < 8 → 8 × 2,270 / 123.0 = **€147.6**
  - EV/OI < 7 → (7 × 1,632 + 4,415 − 18,564 − 809) / 123.0 = **−€28.7** (non-binding / suspended at trough OI)
  - MCap/BV < 5 → 5 × (14,706/123.0) = **€597.8** (already satisfied)
  - MCap/TBV < 3 → 3 × (3,078/123.0) = **€75.1**
- **Trigger-band logic:** with EV/OI suspended (mechanically broken at trough OI + high net debt) and BV already passing, the binding strict metric is MCap/TBV (≈ €75). Because TBV is a weak economic anchor for a brand/intangible-heavy luxury business, MCap/FCF (≈ €148) is flagged as the more meaningful constraint. Practical-vs-strict band ≈ **€75–150**. The band is moot: Past WEAK + Future WEAK + SF FAIL force AVOID irrespective of price.
