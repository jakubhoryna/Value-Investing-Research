# BTI — British American Tobacco p.l.c.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | **WEAK** | ADEQUATE | PASS | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** |

BAT is a cash-gushing global nicotine business — enormous, defensive, and generous with owners — but it is not a good business *on the capital it employs*. The 2017 Reynolds American acquisition (~£42bn) quadrupled capital employed and buried the balance sheet under ~£87bn of brands and goodwill. Returns on that inflated base are structurally mediocre (ROCE_xc clears 10% in only 1 of 8 post-acquisition years, never 15%), and the record carries a £27.3bn US-brand impairment (2023) and a £6.2bn Canada litigation charge (2024). Worst-parameter-wins makes Past WEAK decisive: **AVOID**, on quality, not price. This would only flip if returns on the post-Reynolds capital base structurally re-rated toward the mid-teens — years of amortisation, deleveraging, and a proven New Categories transition away — and even then the stock is EXPENSIVE at ~13× EV/OI. A 6% dividend yield is not a substitute for a quality gate.

## 2. Understanding

British American Tobacco p.l.c. is a UK-domiciled, NYSE-ADR-listed multinational that manufactures and sells combustible cigarettes (Dunhill, Lucky Strike, Pall Mall, Newport, Camel) and "New Categories" nicotine products — vapour (Vuse), heated tobacco (glo) and modern oral pouches (Velo) — to adult consumers in ~180 countries via retail distribution, ranking as one of the top three global tobacco players in a structurally declining combustibles market. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Reported ROCE_xc sits at high-single-digits every clean year (7–10%, FY2018–2025), clearing the 10% ADEQUATE floor only in FY2025 (10.1%) and never approaching 15%; even substituting company-adjusted operating profit (stripping impairments) lifts only three of eight years above 10%. FCFROCE_xc clears 8% in just two of eight. Per-share OI is flat post-Reynolds (£4.06 → £4.55) and FCF/share fell (£4.16 → £2.63, 2025 hit by the £2.56bn Canada payment). TBV is deeply negative (–£40.9bn). The record includes a catastrophic FY2023 (OI –£15.75bn) and near-loss FY2024. Leverage is contained (L/E ~127%, below the 200% flag). **WEAK** — sub-threshold returns on an M&A-inflated capital base, with two structural loss years.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: hundreds of millions of consumers, no customer concentration; broad leaf-supplier base. Forces MIXED-to-unfavourable — substitutes HIGH (vaping, illicit single-use vapes, cessation), rivalry MEDIUM-HIGH (PMI, Altria, JT), but customer power LOW (inelastic, addicted demand) and a regulatory moat that raises entry barriers. Moat: brand + ingrainedness + government (excise/regulation deters entrants) — real and cash-protective, but sitting on a combustibles base declining ~5–8%/yr while New Categories remain contested and unproven at scale. The January 2025 US menthol-ban withdrawal is a reprieve. Direction: **stable-but-narrowing**. **ADEQUATE** (moat floors it; does not change the final verdict).

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation reasonable (CEO ~£6m FY2024, LTIP-weighted, 500%-of-salary shareholding requirement); no dual-class. Related-party transactions minimal. Buybacks (£1.2bn 2025, £0.8bn 2024) executed at ~12–13× earnings and a ~6% yield — value-accretive, not dilution-sterilising. Dividend progressive (~65% of sustainable earnings), rational given a thin high-return reinvestment runway. **PASS** — but flagged: the Reynolds deal itself was value-destructive empire-building, and is the root cause of the quality failure; current management's allocation is disciplined, so this is not scored as a forward strike (judgment call, Appendix C/E).

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Combustible volume decline (~5–8%/yr) outrunning price/mix, eroding the cash engine faster than New Categories can replace it.
- Litigation/regulatory tail: Canada settlement drains ~85% of Canadian net income for ~40 years; excise ratchets and generational bans (UK, Australia) compound.
- Illicit single-use vapes undermining Vuse economics and the legal New Categories transition thesis.
- Further intangible impairments if US or other combustibles forecasts deteriorate again.
- Upside risk to an AVOID: a structural re-rating of returns as amortisation works through and leverage falls — worth monitoring, not owning.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Past could migrate to ADEQUATE only if reported ROCE_xc holds ≥10% for a sustained run (not one year), the Reynolds intangible/impairment cycle fully clears, and New Categories reach combustibles-grade returns — a multi-year "show me." Even then, price must fall to the trigger band for BUY. **Next review:** FY2026 results (~Feb 2027), or evidence of a durable returns re-rating.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

- **Breadth — customers:** PASS. Hundreds of millions of consumers, retail-distributed; no customer concentration disclosed.
- **Breadth — suppliers:** PASS. Broad leaf/tobacco and device-component supply; no single-supplier dependency.
- **Five forces:** Customer power LOW (inelastic, addictive demand, strong pricing history); Supplier power LOW; Substitutes **HIGH** (vaping, illicit single-use vapes, cessation/GLP-1 secondary); New entrants LOW–MEDIUM (excise + regulation are barriers, but disruptive nicotine startups exist); Rivalry MEDIUM–HIGH (PMI, Altria, JT, Imperial). - **Market growth:** Combustibles NO (structural decline); New Categories YES but contested/unprofitable-to-thin. Overall **MIXED**. Verdict **ADEQUATE** (moat floors it).
