Bilfinger is a competent, visibly improving industrial-services contractor whose shares have quadrupled in three years, and none of that survives a ten-year test. Operating income was negative in four of the last ten years and free cash flow in three; worst-of-four ROCE is 2.1% against P3's 10% floor. The forward gate fails on its own evidence — three of four forces STRONG, no moat — and shareholder-friendliness fails on a P19 hard strike: €150m of stock bought across 2017–2018, years producing neither operating income nor free cash flow on either read. The 2022 and 2025 programmes were disciplined by contrast. Nothing here is a price problem; at €85.35 the shares are FAIR.

## 2. Understanding

Bilfinger SE is a Mannheim-headquartered, MDAX-listed, widely-held industrial-services group that sells engineering, maintenance, turnaround, insulation, scaffolding, piping and electrical/instrumentation work plus plant technology — labour-and-project services differentiated only by local density, safety record and site qualifications — to the operators of process plants in chemicals and petrochemicals, energy, pharma and biopharma and oil and gas, overwhelmingly large industrial corporations rather than consumers, earning 68% of FY2025 revenue in European Engineering & Maintenance, 16% in Technologies and 14% in international E&M (North America, Middle East), and standing today as the ~30,700-employee, €5.4bn residue of the former Bilfinger Berger construction group after a decade of divestment and restructuring. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Ten years, FY2016–FY2025, as reported. Worst-of-four normalised ROCE **2.07%** (beginning-of-period, cash included) and FCFROCE **0.58%** — ΣOI €523.2m and ΣFCF €147.2m against capital employed averaging €2.4bn. Both are far under P3. The record is two businesses: FY2016–FY2020 lost money at the operating line in four years of five; FY2021–FY2025 compounds hard, EBIT €121m → €285m, FCF €24m → €296m. Per-share growth has no geometric mean — OI/share and FCF/share change sign — while book value per share fell 0.5% p.a. and tangible book 3.2% p.a., against roughly €1.0bn returned in dividends and buybacks. Leverage is fine, L/E 161%, inside P5. **WEAK** — the level test is not close, and averaging rescues nothing.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS** both sides: no IFRS 8.34 major-customer disclosure appears in the FY2025 report, affirmative evidence under IFRS that no customer reaches a tenth of revenue; the cost base is 43% personnel, the balance materials and subcontract labour from thousands of vendors. Forces: customer power **STRONG** (large process operators, competitive re-tendering, credible insourcing), supplier power **WEAK**, substitutes **STRONG** (in-house maintenance crews), new entrants **STRONG** (regional maintenance is people and equipment; the market's fragmentation is the evidence). Tally three — past the book's stop signal. No moat among the six sources; DACH density and framework incumbency are advantages; the 11.3% gross margin says so. Market **MIXED**: nuclear, pharma and carbon capture growing, European petrochemicals shrinking. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**FAIL — P19 hard strike.** The 2017–2018 programme bought 3,942,211 shares at €38.05 average (€150m) in years when EBIT was −€126.1m and −€12.2m, free cash flow negative in both; the trailing-three-year reads are negative too. The 2022 (13.0×) and 2025 (9.6×) tranches are clean; P17 passes wide, board total €5,581k against an €8.8m line.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers.** Question: does any single customer exceed a tenth of revenue? Evidence: the FY2025 report contains no IFRS 8.34 major-customer disclosure anywhere in the segment note or entity-wide disclosures. Under IFRS that disclosure is mandatory whenever one external customer reaches 10% of revenue, so its absence is affirmative evidence rather than silence — the opposite inference to the one drawn under US practice. A tenth of revenue is €543m against a customer base of process-plant operators across five countries and four industries. **PASS.** No captive-customer waiver needed or used.

**Breadth — suppliers.** Question: does any single supplier exceed a tenth of COGS plus operating expenses (€5,151.3m)? Personnel expense is €2,324.6m of that, contracted individually; the balance is materials and subcontract labour, and the company states that all subcontractor cost is booked to material expense. Inputs are scaffolding, steel, piping, insulation material, vehicles and hired crews — commodity purchases with many ready alternatives in every region. No supplier concentration is disclosed and no effective single vendor is visible behind the nominal count. **PASS.** Both answers yes → breadth passes.

**Forces (binary; no medium).**

| Force | Call | Reason |
|-------|------|--------|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | Buyers are large process operators purchasing through competitive tender and framework contracts they re-let on cycles; backward integration is not hypothetical but the industry's default alternative, and switching costs are a mobilisation cost, not a lock-in. |
| Supplier bargaining power | **WEAK** | Fragmented materials and locally contracted subcontract labour; no vendor holds a chokepoint on a €5.4bn cost base. |
| Threat of substitutes | **STRONG** | Doing-without in its purest form: the customer's own maintenance department. Insourcing is a proven substitute with an established cost comparison and no adoption switching cost of consequence; deferral of turnarounds is a second substitute in downturns. |
| Threat of new entrants | **STRONG** | Barriers to entry exist at the specialised end (nuclear qualification, biopharma skid engineering) but not in the regional maintenance work that is 68% of revenue, where entry needs people, equipment and references. The market's persistent fragmentation is the evidence that neither entry nor success barriers bite. |

**Tally: 3 STRONG.** Two or more is the book's stop signal → Future WEAK on the tally alone.

**Moat.** None identified among the six sources. Government — no; permits are per-site qualifications any qualified contractor obtains. Cost — no; the cost base is wages, and Bilfinger's 11.3% gross margin and 5.5% EBITA margin show no structural advantage. Brand — no; buyers are procurement departments. Network — no. Switching costs — present but modest, the source of framework-contract stickiness rather than pricing power. Ingrainedness — no channel to be ingrained in. Direction: not applicable in the absence of a moat; the closest thing to one, DACH density, has been narrowing as the group internationalises.

**Market growth: MIXED.** Nuclear services, pharma and biopharma capacity, hydrogen, carbon capture and district heating are expanding (Category A "sustainability-enhancing" revenue €701.9m, up from €583.8m). Against that, the company's own outlook describes the (petro)chemical situation in E&M DACH as tense on high energy costs and location disadvantages — and chemicals and petrochemicals is its largest customer industry.

**Future verdict: WEAK** — three strong forces and no moat, either of which is independently sufficient.
