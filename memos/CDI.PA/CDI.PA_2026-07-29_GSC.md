# CDI.PA — Christian Dior SE: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS · Past **WEAK** · Future STRONG · Shareholder-friendliness **FAIL** · Cheapness EXPENSIVE → **AVOID**, on two independent gates.

Christian Dior SE is LVMH seen through a holding company: 42.17% of the capital, 56.26% of the exercisable votes, no other operating asset since Dior Couture was sold into LVMH in 2017, no employees of its own. The underlying business is unchanged and Future stays STRONG; what differs is everything the holdco structure touches. Tiffany was acquired inside *this* consolidation at 22.1× pre-deal normalized operating income — a hard strike here directly, not inherited — and the Arnault family's 97.50% of capital / 98.63% of votes turns the documented control caveat into a strike once paired with that record. Leverage on the parent's own equity reads 296%. The 20.7% discount to look-through stake value is real and is the only thing here a value investor would want; it is not enough, and it addresses neither failed gate.

## 2. Understanding

Christian Dior SE is a French listed holding company, 97.50% owned by the Arnault family, employing nobody directly and buying its administration from the controlling shareholder's vehicle, whose sole asset is a controlling 42.17%/56.26% stake in LVMH — through which it fully consolidates the world's largest luxury goods group (leather goods and fashion, wines and spirits, perfumes and cosmetics, watches and jewelry, selective retailing), sold to affluent consumers worldwide. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Nine comparable fiscal years only — the June year-end moved to December in 2016 and the entity became a pure LVMH holdco in 2017 — so STRONG is unavailable by construction. Worst-of-four normalized ROCE 14.4%, FCFROCE 10.5%: both clear the ADEQUATE floors, neither reaches STRONG, and beginning-CE ROCE has fallen two straight years from the 2023 peak (19.5% → 15.3% → 13.2%). Per-share growth sits well above the inflation floor (ΔOI/sh +9.3%/yr, ΔFCF/sh +10.2%/yr geometric). Leverage decides the gate: total liabilities ÷ book value excluding minority interests is **296%**, past P5 with no P6 condition available. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

Identical to the underlying group and re-affirmed on its own evidence. Breadth PASS both sides — millions of retail payers with no concentration disclosure because none exists; supply substantially internalised with no vendor near a tenth of COGS plus opex. Forces: customer power WEAK, supplier power WEAK, substitutes WEAK for the dominant unit, new entrants WEAK — tally zero strong. Moat: Brand, portfolio-wide, stable; price and mix held through the 2024–26 softness without share loss. Market growth MIXED. The holdco layer neither adds nor subtracts operating exposure. **STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 proportionality is clean on both reads — the top disclosed total ≈€7.9m is 1.4% of the fiscal-year line and 1.2% of the normalized one, far from the ≈$40m indexed trigger; outside-director fees top out at €24.6k. Christian Dior SE repurchased none of its own shares across the window (treasury only ran down) — a finding, recorded. Everything adverse sits below: the **Tiffany acquisition audit reads 22.1×** pre-deal normalized OI inside this consolidation, a hard strike on its own; LVMH's 2019 and 2021–24 repurchase record passes through as transactions with minority interests; and 97.50%/98.63% control pairs with that record to strike under the control clause. Agache SCA bills the holdco €3.2m for its entire management function — logged. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- A third consecutive ROCE decline in FY2026 would add a sustained-decline reading to the leverage breach.
- Succession at the controlling shareholder governs both the moat and every allocation question flagged above.
- Chinese demand and tariff regimes remain the group's largest earnings swing factors.
