A good coffee franchise inside an ordinary small-appliance conglomerate, stopped at the future gate, not on price. The past record is respectable, not strong — worst-of-four ROCE 13.3%, growth clear of the inflation floor, leverage light. Forward, two forces read STRONG: customers, because the group sells through consolidating retail and platforms that reallocate shelf space at will; substitutes, because a premium bean-to-cup machine competes with capsules, filter coffee and buying coffee out. A tally of two is P16's stop signal, and the 2022 pass-through failure — EBIT margin 12.0% to 8.3% — shows it is not theoretical. To flip, the tally must fall to one, plausibly via Professional (La Marzocco, Eversys), where customers are cafés with real switching costs; that is still 13% of revenue.

## 2. Understanding

De'Longhi S.p.A. is a family-controlled Italian listed manufacturer of branded small domestic appliances — chiefly espresso and bean-to-cup coffee machines, plus food preparation, comfort and home care — sold under the owned De'Longhi, Kenwood, Ariete, Nutribullet, Eversys and La Marzocco marks and the perpetually licensed Braun mark, to retail chains, buying groups, platforms and distributors in 120-plus markets, with a smaller Professional division serving cafés and hospitality. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE 13.3% (end-of-period, cash in CE); FCFROCE 11.3%. ROCE clears P3's 10% but misses P2's 15%, so STRONG is unavailable. The shape is cyclical, not declining: 18.2% in 2016, 11.3% by 2019, a 9.3% trough in the 2022 cost and working-capital shock, back to 15.3% in 2025. Per-share geometric growth 2015–25 clears the 3% floor throughout — OI +7.0%, FCF +14.0%, BV +8.4%, TBV +3.3%. L/E 95.5%, well inside the P5 flag. **ADEQUATE** — real returns and real growth, never at the STRONG level.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both sides: no IFRS 8.34 major-customer disclosure anywhere in the FY2025 report — mandated, so its absence is evidence — and a ~3,000-supplier panel with documented second-sourcing. Forces: customers **STRONG** (retail and platform concentration, near-zero retailer switching costs, 2022 pass-through failure); suppliers **WEAK**; substitutes **STRONG** (capsule, filter, doing-without and cafés all contest the same occasion at low adoption cost); new entrants **WEAK** (brand, patents, distribution and service scale). Tally two. Moat is Brand, located in fully-automatic coffee where the group leads globally — stable, strengthening inside Professional, but not extending across Household, where Nutribullet fell in 2025 and Braun is licensed rather than owned. Market growth YES. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 clean on both v3.6.0 reads: top pay €4.34m (F. de' Longhi) against 5%-of-lower(FCF, NI) caps of €15.8m (year) and €14.6m (3y-normalized), far below the ≈$40m trigger; top outside-director fee €74k. ## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Retail and platform concentration keeps pricing power with the buyer, as 2022 showed.
- Coffee is roughly half the group; a capsule-versus-bean-to-cup shift moves the thesis.
- The controlling shareholder holds 26.5% of the Professional hub directly, beside the listed company's 61.6%.
- Household outside coffee — Nutribullet, Kenwood, licensed Braun — is commodity-adjacent; the 2025 weak spot.
- Euro reporting against Chinese and dollar cost bases; softening US demand and tariffs.
