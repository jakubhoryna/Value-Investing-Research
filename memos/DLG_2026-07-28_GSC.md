A capable operator inside a control structure that takes a disproportionate share of its own best asset. The business gates now pass: a decade of net-industrial-margin expansion, 47.8% to 51.7%, is not the P&L of a squeezed supplier, and it overturns the v1 customer-power call. What fails is the partnership. In the 2024 La Marzocco combination the listed company paid a full price to its own controlling shareholder for an asset that shareholder already controlled, and left it holding 26.6% of the hub directly plus rights to force an IPO from February 2028 and a demerger from February 2029. Outside holders funded 100% of the €327m and own 28.5% of what it bought, against 46.5% of everything else. Nothing here is theft — the process was rigorous, no director paid extra. It is not disinterested stewardship, and the flip needs structural change, not a better year.

## 2. Understanding

De' Longhi S.p.A. is a family-controlled Italian listed manufacturer of branded small domestic appliances — chiefly espresso and bean-to-cup coffee machines, plus food preparation, comfort and home care — sold under the owned De'Longhi, Kenwood, Ariete, Nutribullet, Eversys and La Marzocco marks and the perpetually licensed Braun mark, to retail chains, buying groups, platforms and distributors in 120-plus markets, with a smaller Professional division serving cafés and hospitality. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE 13.3% (end-of-period, cash in CE); FCFROCE 11.3%. ROCE clears P3's 10% but misses P2's 15%, so STRONG is unavailable. The shape is cyclical, not declining: 18.2% in 2016, 11.3% by 2019, a 9.3% trough in the 2022 cost and working-capital shock, back to 15.3% in 2025. Per-share geometric growth 2015–25 clears the 3% floor throughout — OI +7.0%, FCF +14.0%, BV +8.4%, TBV +3.3%. L/E 95.5% with €710m net cash. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

**Revised from v1.** Breadth passes both sides: no IFRS 8.34 major-customer disclosure — mandated, so absence is evidence — and a ~3,000-supplier panel with documented second-sourcing. Forces: customers **WEAK**, suppliers **WEAK**, substitutes **STRONG**, entrants **WEAK**. Tally one, contained. The v1 call of strong customer power rested on the 2022 EBIT fall from 12.0% to 8.3%; the industrial margin — the actual pricing-power readout — fell only 240bp, recovered within twelve months and has since made all-time highs, +390bp across the decade. Management attributes 2025 pricing pressure to *competitors*, not customers. Moat is Brand in fully-automatic coffee, strengthening inside Professional. Market growth YES. **ADEQUATE** — STRONG is unavailable while any force is strong.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

### E4. The La Marzocco combination — full audit [v2, expanded]

**Facts.** Announced 21 December 2023, completed 27 February 2024. De'Longhi contributed 100% of Eversys, paid **US$374m cash** for 41.2% of La Marzocco International — **US$200m to DLI, its own controlling shareholder** (22%) and US$174m to LMI minorities (19.2%) — and emerged with 61.4% of the combined hub; DLI retained 26.6%, LMI minorities 12%. DLI had taken majority control of LMI (62.6%) in **April 2021**, weeks after De'Longhi S.p.A. publicly denied "equity participation in La Marzocco" following a *Corriere Economia* report — literally accurate, since DLI and not DLG held the shares. DLI's own cost basis is undisclosed.

**Price — the deal mechanic.** Board-attributed EVs: LMI US$907.7m, Eversys US$494.6m, total US$1,402.4m. Against the RPT document's contractual Target EBITDAs:

|---|---|---|---|
| La Marzocco (net of minorities) | $907.7m | $53.711m | **16.90×** |

Identical. Netting target net financial positions (LMI +$0.076m, Eversys −$18.020m) reconciles to **61.44%** — exactly the agreed split. An identical multiple looks even-handed but is precisely the mechanism that transfers value between assets of different growth: Eversys was compounding revenue at 27%, La Marzocco at 14%. De'Longhi contributed the faster grower; DLI sold the slower one.

**Acquisition audit (P19 / test 4).** LMI 2023E revenue ≈US$261.6m. Applying De'Longhi's own group D&A ratio (3.45% of revenue) as the peer-normal anchor gives OI ≈$44.7m → **EV/OI ≈ 20.3×**; the defensible band on D&A of 3–7% of revenue is **19.8×–25.6×**. A normalized multi-year-average OI denominator, given 14% revenue CAGR, would push it higher still. **Flag confirmed above 15× on every reading; the >20× hard strike is logged INDETERMINATE** under the v3.4.0 estimates-of-undisclosed-inputs rule — it forces heightened scrutiny and a named re-test but cannot complete a pair. *(v1 stated ≈16–21×, computed off the presentation's gross adjusted EBITDA of €56.7m rather than the contractual net-of-minorities Target EBITDA; corrected here.)*

The DCF also carried a **WACC asymmetry in the seller's favour**: 10.0–11.0% for Eversys against 8.5–9.5% for LMI on identical terminal growth of 2.0–3.0%. Under symmetric rates the DCF would have pointed above 66%, widening the gap.

**Valuation inputs.** Rothschild disclosed, candidly, that the projections "non sono state esaminate e validate da una società di consulenza indipendente" and — pointedly — that **LMI's business plan was never approved by LMI's own board of directors**. Lazard's evaluation document for the DLG board ran **no sensitivity analyses** and conceded the two plans "might not be perfectly comparable" on inflation, FX and interest-rate assumptions. In a purely relative-value transaction, that concession is the whole determination.

**Was it a good deal for the business?** De'Longhi did not buy La Marzocco; it swapped 100% of Eversys for 61.4% of the combination plus cash. Netting that out:

| | |
|---|---|
| DLG economic EBITDA before | $29.27m (100% of Eversys) |
| DLG economic EBITDA after | $50.95m (61.4% of the $82.98m hub) |
| **Incremental** | **$21.68m for $374m = 17.3× incremental EBITDA** |
| DLG's net incremental claim on the hub | **26.1% of hub earnings** |

On FY2025 actuals (Professional revenue €488.2m, adjusted EBITDA €133.4m at 27.3% versus 21.6% in 2024, revenue +45.8%), the incremental return on the €326.8m net-financial-position cost is **10.7% on adjusted EBITDA and 9.3% on estimated EBIT** — against De'Longhi's own worst-of-four ROCE of **13.3%**, FY2025 end-period **15.3%** and ex-cash **26.1%**. **The acquisition dilutes returns on capital even after an excellent first full year.** It turns accretive only if Professional compounds hard for another three to five years — the genuine bull case, not yet demonstrated.

**The allocation alternative.** In December 2023, when the board approved the deal, De'Longhi's own shares closed at **€30.10** — a **10.3% FCF yield** on FY2023 free cash flow and a **10.7% OI yield** on FY2024 operating income (EV/OI 9.3×). Comparable headline returns, with no integration risk, no related-party conflict, no minority leakage and a permanent share-count reduction. The company proved in FY2025 that it can repurchase at 8.9× EV/OI, so capability was never the constraint — the choice was.

**Look-through ownership — the structural finding.**

| | Household | Professional |
|---|---|---|
| De Longhi Industrial | 53.5% | **59.5%** (26.6% direct + 32.9% via DLG) |
| All outside shareholders | 46.5% | **28.5%** |

Outside holders funded 100% of the cash and own 28.5% of the asset it bought, against 46.5% of everything else. The controlling family's economic claim on the fastest-growing division now **exceeds** its claim on the listed company. Capital pushed into Professional returns 59.5% to DLI and 28.5% to everyone else; capital pushed into Household returns 53.5% and 46.5%. That is a permanent structural bias in favour of the controlling holder, created by the transaction rather than inherited.

**Exit architecture.** From the fourth anniversary (**February 2028**) DLI plus a majority of LMI minorities may initiate an IPO of the hub; from the fifth (**February 2029**), absent an IPO, they may initiate a **demerger of the Professional business out of De'Longhi S.p.A.** A twenty-to-thirty-year hold on the group's best asset is therefore not underwritable from the outside.

**What acquits them.** The process was genuinely rigorous, not a rubber stamp: an RPT Committee of three independent directors (L. M. V. Collina chair, M. Le Divelec Lemmi, M. Benedetti) appointed Pedersoli Studio Legale on 24 February 2023 and Rothschild & Co on 28 March 2023 — ten months before signing — met repeatedly with a statutory auditor present and opined unanimously; Lazard advised the board; EY ran financial due diligence on both groups with symmetric ~5% materiality thresholds; Biscozzi Nobili covered the DLI vehicles; White & Case acted on legals. Giuseppe, Fabio and Silvia de' Longhi each declared an Art. 2391 conflict and **abstained**. **§2.6 of the RPT document confirms no change to the remuneration of any governing-body member resulted from the transaction** — no director was paid for doing this. The deal was funded entirely from own cash with no leverage; the balance sheet remains at €710m net cash and L/E 95.5%. The asset is performing. The strategic logic is coherent adjacency, not diversification — this is not classic empire-building, whatever the "without equal in the world of coffee" rhetoric suggests.

**What convicts them.** The conflicted CEO negotiated it: Fabio de' Longhi is DLG's CEO, Chairman of Eversys, a member of LMI's board of managers and a beneficiary of the trust owning DLI, and the RPT document names the CEO among management "appointed by the Company to carry out the negotiations". The CFO also sat on Eversys' board. Abstaining on 21 December is hygiene applied after the price was shaped.
