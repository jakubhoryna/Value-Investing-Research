A good industrial franchise at a compounder's price, run by a board that paid a software multiple out of an engineering company's cash flow. Two gates fail independently. Shareholder-friendliness takes a P19 hard strike on the 2021 purchase of CNC Software (Mastercam) at 29–38× the target's disclosed pre-deal EBIT — clearing 20× would need a 48% margin against the 25–30% Sandvik published. Cheapness misses by nearly four times: the band binds at ≈ SEK 88 against SEK 344. Neither is marginal. A flip needs the 2021 deal year out of the ten-year window with a clean record behind it *and* a price three-quarters below today's.

## 2. Understanding

Sandvik is a Swedish-listed, vertically integrated engineering group that manufactures and services rock-drilling and crushing equipment plus the consumable ground-support and cemented-carbide cutting tools those and other machines consume — increasingly bundled with manufacturing software — selling to mining companies, infrastructure contractors and metal-manufacturing firms across about 160 countries from a Stockholm base. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE 14.0% (end-of-period, with cash) against the 15% STRONG and 10% ADEQUATE lines; worst-of-four FCFROCE 9.5%, clearing its 8% test. Returns are cyclical, not declining — a 11.2–20.6% range with no sustained downtrend. Per-share growth clears the 3% floor comfortably: OI/share +13.1% and FCF/share +7.1% from a depressed 2015 base. Leverage conservative at 82.2%. **ADEQUATE** — missing STRONG on the ROCE leg alone, which also removes any P12 allowance.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both legs: no IFRS 8 major-customer disclosure against SEK 120.7bn of revenue, and Sandvik is backward-integrated into tungsten through its Austrian operation, selling powder rather than buying into a concentrated market. All four forces call WEAK — miners cannot backward-integrate into rig manufacture, fleet and aftermarket lock-in is real, rock breaking has no substitute, and carbide metallurgy plus remote service networks deter entrants. Mining's moat (52% of revenue, 61% of profit) is switching costs, stable. But revenue is near-evenly split and Machining — 40% of revenue, and the unit that absorbed the acquisition capital — is stable-to-eroding: four years of flat-to-down revenue, recurring impairments and restructuring. Market growth MIXED. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**FAIL — P19 hard strike.** CNC Software cost SEK 4,919m enterprise value in 2021 against a target disclosing USD 60m revenue at a 25–30% EBIT margin: **28.7–38.1× pre-deal operating income** on every FX and normalisation reading, versus a 20× strike line. DSI Underground (EUR 943m on EUR 516m revenue) is INDETERMINATE — pre-deal margin never disclosed, strike line at 9.1%. Compensation passes wide: the CEO's SEK 37.5m is 0.26% of the lower of FCF and net income against a 5% line; the chair's SEK 3.75m is below even the CPI-indexed floor default. Related-party dealing immaterial. The SEK 579m of four-year "repurchases" are equity-swap settlements of a performance-share plan against an unchanged share count — no issuance to sterilise, so no second strike.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Mining is cyclical and 2025–26 order intake is strong, so today's operating income is no trough denominator.
- Machining carries SEK 70bn of assets for SEK 7.4bn of profit — 10.6%, against Mining's 18.4%.
- Continued premium bolt-ons keep the P19 window dirty; ten acquisitions closed in the first nine months of 2025 alone.
- Automotive electrification structurally removes machined content — the largest secular threat to the cutting-tool franchise.
- Chinese tungsten export controls cut both ways: peer supply risk, Sandvik powder pricing tailwind.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To WATCH: the 2021 deal year must leave the P19 window with no repeat breach behind it — not before the FY2031 record. To BUY additionally needs price below ≈ SEK 88, a three-quarters fall on unchanged fundamentals; higher operating income lifts the band proportionately. Downgrade needs little: Past turns WEAK if worst-of-four ROCE breaks 10%, and Machining is where to watch. Next review: FY2026 annual report (~March 2027), or earlier if price approaches SEK 110.

**Goodwill** stays in capital employed — Sandvik is a serial acquirer, so the default holds and no deduction was considered.

**Leases:** Sandvik adopted IFRS 16 in 2019; lease liabilities (SEK 5,647m at end-2025 per note G29) sit inside interest-bearing liabilities and therefore already enter CE, EV and L/E. For pre-adoption years FY2015–FY2018 the P18 trigger does **not** fire: right-of-use assets recognised at adoption were SEK 3,172m against total assets of SEK 120,423m (2.6%), so undiscounted commitments cannot have approached the 10%-of-assets threshold. **Pensions in EV:** provisions for pensions are included as a debt equivalent, consistent with Sandvik's own net-debt definition and with the framework's conservative posture. Logged as a judgment call. On Sandvik's narrower "financial net debt" basis (excluding pensions) EV/OI reads 22.5×/22.3× and the EV/OI band leg lifts to SEK 90.35 — the verdict is untouched either way.

**IFRS interest reclassification:** none required. Sandvik's cash-flow statement begins at profit before tax with no add-back of net financial items and discloses interest paid and received within operating activities (note G29), so CFO is already levered.

**Capex definition:** acquisitions of tangible assets plus acquisitions of intangible assets. Rental-equipment investment is already netted inside Sandvik's operating cash flow and is not double-counted. Proceeds from asset sales are not netted against capex (conservative). ## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth (P15).**
*Customers — PASS.* No IFRS 8.34 major-customer disclosure appears in AR2025; since that disclosure is mandatory at the 10% threshold, its absence is affirmative evidence that no customer reaches it. A 10% customer would need to buy SEK 12.1bn. The base spans mining majors, infrastructure contractors, aerospace and defence primes, automotive tiers and tens of thousands of small machine shops across 160 countries. Consolidation risk is real on the mining side but the threshold is not close.
*Suppliers — PASS.* No single supplier above 10% of cost of goods plus operating expenses is disclosed. The effective-supplier lens matters here: tungsten is the critical input for cemented carbide and global supply is Chinese-dominated. Sandvik has resolved this by owning the upstream — its Austrian tungsten mining and processing operation plus carbide recycling — and AR2025 records Sandvik *selling* tungsten powder into a supply-limited market. Deliberate concentration does not arise.
Both legs yes → **breadth PASS**, no waiver needed.

**Forces (P16) — segment-level calls, multi-unit business.**

| Force | Mining (52% rev) | Rock Processing (9%) | Machining & Intelligent Mfg (40%) | Group call |
|-------|------|------|------|------|
| Customer power | WEAK — miners cannot backward-integrate into rig manufacture; fleet standardisation and parts/service lock-in | WEAK — same buyers, smaller ticket | WEAK — highly fragmented base down to job shops | **WEAK** |
| Supplier power | WEAK — steel and components are competitively supplied | WEAK | WEAK — backward-integrated into tungsten | **WEAK** |
| Substitutes | WEAK — no substitute for breaking rock; doing-without unavailable | WEAK | WEAK on the framework's test — additive manufacturing has not displaced production machining after fifteen years and adoption switching costs (qualification, throughput) stay high | **WEAK** |
| New entrants | WEAK — remote-region service networks, safety certification, installed base | WEAK | WEAK — carbide metallurgy, application engineering, distribution; Chinese competition is real but low-tier | **WEAK** |

**Tally: 0 strong → favourable zone.** Note the deliberate mapping choice: automotive electrification reduces machined content per vehicle, but that is a demand-mix shift and is recorded under market growth, not as a substitute for the machining function itself. A reader who maps it to substitutes would call one force STRONG in SMM and land in the same ADEQUATE zone.

**Moat.** Mining — **switching costs**, single source: equipment fleets standardise a mine on one supplier's parts, consumables and service, and automation systems integrate into mine planning; aftermarket revenue makes the relationship recurring rather than transactional. Direction **stable** — 2025 organic order intake +17%, adjusted EBITA margin 20.7% versus 20.4%. Machining — **ingrainedness/brand** (Coromant specified into customers' process sheets, Mastercam's ~270,000-licence installed base). Direction **stable-to-eroding**: revenue SEK 49.1bn (2024) to SEK 47.7bn (2025), segment impairments SEK 671m then SEK 376m, restructuring SEK 1,725m in 2024, and SEK 70.1bn of segment assets returning SEK 7.4bn of operating profit (10.6%) against Mining's 18.4%.

**Market growth: MIXED.** Mining supported by electrification, deeper orebodies and critical-minerals demand; aerospace and defence strong; general engineering subdued in Europe and North America; automotive machining content in structural decline.

**Verdict ADEQUATE.** Breadth passes and no force calls strong, but the group is not dominated by one unit — 40% of revenue sits in a segment whose moat direction is not clearly stable and which has absorbed the bulk of the acquisition capital. STRONG requires a durable stable-or-strengthening moat across the business; that is not demonstrated.
