A good industrial business that fails on the prices its board pays. The franchise is what the model looks for: a fragmented customer base, a commodity supply base, four weak forces and a stable switching-cost moat feeding a large service annuity. The record is respectable rather than exceptional on a subtractive capital base — worst-of-four ROCE 12.5%, FCFROCE 9.0%, OI/share compounding 16.6%. But allocation breaches P19 three times: the 2021 and 2022 repurchase tranches at 22–24× EV/OI on both reads, and the July 2025 Fives Cryogenics acquisition at 21–22× disclosed pre-deal earnings. Any one is decisive; none can be averaged away. At 22.2× EV/OI the price is not close either.

## 2. Understanding

Alfa Laval is a 140-year-old Swedish-headquartered listed manufacturer that designs, builds and services heat exchangers, centrifugal separators and fluid-handling equipment — differentiated engineered hardware plus a large recurring spare-parts and service annuity — sold through three divisions (Energy, Food & Water, Marine) to industrial and marine organisations in about 100 countries, with roughly two-thirds of revenue from equipment and the balance from service on an installed base it has been laying down for decades. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE 12.5% (end-of-period, cash-inclusive CE) against a best-of-four 14.7%; worst-of-four FCFROCE 9.0%. Both clear the ADEQUATE floor; ROCE misses the 15% STRONG line. The gap to the company's reported 23.9% is definitional — its capital employed of SEK 51,501m excludes cash, deferred tax and pension assets against the framework's SEK 75,112m. Direction is favourable: beginning-of-period ROCE_wc runs 7.0% (2016) to 17.8% (2025). Growth is solid — OI/sh +16.6%, BV/sh +9.1% geometric; FCF/sh only +4.3%, held back by the 2022 working-capital trough and rising capex. L/E 125%, inside the flag. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both legs: the report states no single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, and over 5,000 direct suppliers of commodity metals carry no disclosed concentration. All four forces WEAK — customers fragmented and unable to backward-integrate; suppliers many; substitute technologies exist but Alfa Laval sells across them and the duties cannot be done without; entry blocked by scale, class approvals and the service network. Tally 0 strong. Moat: **switching costs**, equipment approved into process lines and class-certified on vessels with parts and service following for decades; stable. Market growth YES structurally, though FY2025 order intake fell 11% and the book is shrinking. **STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation is clean: CEO total incl. pensions SEK 42.8m = 0.70% of the lower leg (FCF SEK 6,149m) against the 5% strike, and ≈USD 4.5m against the ≈USD 40m line; the non-executive board pool is SEK 8.76m across ten seats. No material related-party transactions. The failure is P19, three times. The SEK 2,000m of 2021–22 buybacks were for cancellation — a real return, not sterilisation — but ran at 23.7×/23.0× EV/OI (2021) and 22.3×/24.0× (2022), above 20× on both reads, twice. Fives Cryogenics — SEK 9,210m plus 50m debt less 327m cash acquired against disclosed pro-forma adjusted EBITA of SEK 421m — is 21.2×, rising to 22.2×. **FAIL.**

## C. Calculation notes

**Latest-year CE build (2025):** Total assets 98,161 − NIBCL 23,049 = **CE_wc 75,112**; less cash and equivalents 7,124 + current deposits 707 = **CE_xc 67,281**.

**NIBCL composition (2025):** accounts payable 5,444 + contract liabilities 12,293 + current tax liabilities 1,605 + accrued costs and deferred income 3,707 = **23,049**.

**NIBCL burden-of-proof calls (applied consistently across all eleven years):** left *in* capital employed — "Other liabilities" (2025: 3,194), current "Provisions" (1,936), derivative liabilities (133) and, in pre-2024 presentations, "Notes payable" (128–423). None is unambiguously a non-interest-bearing operating accrual of the four named types; the burden rests with the item and none discharges it. Liabilities to credit institutions and lease liabilities, current and non-current, always stay in CE per §2·1A.

**TBV build (2025):** BV (equity attributable to owners) 43,409 − goodwill 29,722 − other intangibles 6,723 = **6,964**.

**EV builds (2025, at SEK 578.80):** MCap = 578.80 × 413,326,315 = 239,233. Debt = non-current liabilities to credit institutions 9,626 + non-current lease 3,333 + current liabilities to credit institutions 7,590 + current lease 462 = 21,011. NCI 344. No preferred.
- Conservative (no cash excess): 239,233 + 21,011 + 344 = **260,588**
- All-cash-excess: 260,588 − 7,831 = **252,757**

The pension liability of SEK 984m is excluded from the EV build (it is not debt under the §2 Gate-2 definition) and noted rather than added; including it would worsen every multiple.

**IFRS interest reclassification (mandatory, §2·1A):** Alfa Laval classifies interest paid and received in financing activities in every year of the window — confirmed directly in the FY2017, FY2019, FY2021, FY2023 and FY2025 cash-flow statements. Net interest is therefore moved into CFO before FCF is derived, in all ten years. Amounts used: 2016 −80, 2017 −46, 2018 −50, 2019 −161, 2020 −184, 2021 −131, 2022 −191, 2023 −321 (all from received-less-paid lines), 2024 −337 and 2025 −357 (from the restated single "interest paid and received" line).

**Cash-flow restatement (AR2025 Note 39):** amortization of lease liabilities was moved out of operating and into financing, and all periods in the ten-year overview were restated on that basis. The restated series is used throughout, which is the standard IFRS 16 convention and matches the framework's pre-adoption formula (which likewise excludes lease principal from FCF). CFO for FY2024 is therefore 12,778, not the 12,159 originally published.

**P18 (pre-IFRS 16 lease capitalisation): not triggered.** Initial application in 2019 added SEK 2,766m of lease liabilities, ≈4.8% of FY2018 total assets — below the 10% test. FY2016–18 stand as reported; the adoption-year step in CE and L/E is flagged rather than adjusted.

**Goodwill treatment:** retained in capital employed. Alfa Laval is acquisitive (Fives Cryogenics 2025, NRG Marine 2025, MPS 2023, Scanjet 2022, StormGeo 2021, Desmet 2023-era additions), so the §2·1A carve-out for non-acquisitive companies does not apply.

**Company-reported ROCE reconciliation:** Alfa Laval reports 23.9% for 2025 on a capital employed of SEK 51,501m including goodwill (and 60.8% on SEK 20,287m excluding it). The framework's subtractive CE of SEK 75,112m is SEK 23,611m larger, the difference being cash and deposits, deferred tax assets, pension assets, derivative assets and the liabilities the burden-of-proof rule leaves inside CE. Both figures are correct on their own definitions; the framework's is the operative one.

**P20 (constructed maintenance-capex basis): not active.** Capex ran 1.7% of sales in 2016–17 and 3.8–5.0% in 2024–25, which is a step up, but management does not label the spend as expansion nor guide it down, and no company-disclosed maintenance figure exists. Condition (ii) fails; all-capex FCF is used throughout and MCap/FCF remains a live primary.

**Substitution rule (§2·1A financial institutions): not applicable.** No captive-finance arm, no deposit funding, no securities-heavy balance sheet.

**Bias check.** Tick-through run across the eighteen biases and both forms of akrasia. Two were live and are logged: **cleverness** — the temptation to treat the gap between the company's reported 23.9% ROCE and the framework's 12.5% as evidence the framework is mismeasuring rather than as the conservative reading it is designed to be; handled by computing all four normalized bases and reporting the worst without adjustment. **Hotness** — the marine-decarbonisation and energy-transition narrative attaching to this name; handled by confining market growth to a directional YES and noting the 11% order-intake decline against it. Neither judgment in this memo is born of a bias: the deciding finding is mechanical threshold arithmetic on disclosed figures.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: PASS.** AR2025 Note 32.3 states that no one customer represents more than 10% of revenue. The disclosure sentence is the test and it is present and explicit. Revenue is spread across three divisions and roughly 100 countries.

**Breadth — suppliers: PASS (evidence noted).** Over 5,000 direct suppliers disclosed in the sustainability section. Inputs are stainless and titanium plate, castings, motors and gaskets — commodity classes with multiple qualified vendors. No supplier above 10% of COGS + opex is disclosed. Looking through nominal counts as §2·1B requires: there is no single effective supplier, fab-ecosystem or securitisation-market analogue behind the count. No waiver used.

**Forces — four binary calls:**

|Force|Call|Reason|
|---|---|---|
|Customer bargaining power|WEAK|No customer above 10% of revenue; buyers are shipowners, refiners, dairies and utilities with no capability to backward-integrate into separators or plate packs; re-approval and downtime costs make switching expensive mid-life.|
|Supplier bargaining power|WEAK|5,000+ direct suppliers of commodity metals and components; forward integration into engineered thermal and separation systems is implausible; switching cost to Alfa Laval is low.|
|Threat of substitutes|WEAK|Competing technologies exist (shell-and-tube, decanters, membranes) but Alfa Laval sells across the set; the duties themselves — heating, cooling, separating, pumping — cannot be done without in the processes served.|
|Threat of new entrants|WEAK|Barriers to entry *and* to success: manufacturing scale, marine class-society approvals and process-line qualifications, and a global service network no entrant can replicate at the outset.|

**Tally: 0 strong → favourable.**

**Moat.** Single identified source: **switching costs.** Equipment is engineered into a process line or class-certified on a vessel; once installed, the spare parts, service intervals and re-approval economics bind the customer to Alfa Laval for the asset's life, which is where the aftermarket margin lives. Direction: **stable** — the installed base grows with each equipment cycle and no structural force is eroding the aftermarket attachment. Not strengthening either: competitors run the same playbook.

**Market growth: YES, with a near-term caveat.** Structural drivers are real (marine decarbonisation retrofits, LNG and hydrogen infrastructure, food and water processing). Guarded against the late-pipeline glow: FY2025 order intake fell 11% to SEK 66,742m and the order book fell to SEK 48,259m from SEK 52,319m, so the directional YES is a multi-year statement, not a current one.

**Verdict: STRONG** — breadth PASS on both legs, 0 strong forces, durable and stable moat.
