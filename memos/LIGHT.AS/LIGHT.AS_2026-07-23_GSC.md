# LIGHT.AS — Signify N.V.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | WEAK | WEAK | PASS (1 flag logged) | FAIR | **AVOID** |

Signify is the world's largest lighting maker and looks statistically cheap — 0.7× book, 5× FCF — but it is a structurally shrinking business earning commodity returns. Worst-of-four 10-year ROCE is 8.3%, below even the ADEQUATE floor, and the two forces that set price — customer bargaining power and Chinese entrant overcapacity — are both strong, with sales down 23% from the 2022 peak. The governance record is clean; the economics are the problem. A BUY would require the business to change, not just the price: sustained ROCE above 10% and evidence the overcapacity cycle has broken. Neither is in sight.

## 2. Understanding

Signify N.V. sells lighting — LED lamps and luminaires, connected systems (Philips Hue for consumers, Interact for professionals), and declining conventional lamps — to professional installers, distributors, municipalities, and businesses, and to consumers through retail, worldwide (Europe/Americas-weighted), as a vertically integrated Dutch-listed manufacturer spun off from Royal Philips in 2016, still the industry's largest player at €5.8bn sales but in structural transition as long-lived LEDs shrink the replacement market. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four 10y normalized averages: **ROCE 8.3%**, FCFROCE 8.4% (best-of-four ROCE only 9.8%) — below the 10% ADEQUATE floor on every cash variant. Returns never established a durable double-digit level: annual beginning-CE ROCE_wc ranged 6.1–12.8%, with the 2022 peak inflated by a €182m real-estate gain and 2023–25 back at 6–8%. Per-share growth over nine years: OI/sh +2.5%, FCF/sh +2.1% geometric — below the 3% nominal floor, and both peaked in 2021–22. L/E 148% (FY25) is within P5 after 2020's 245% acquisition-funded breach. **WEAK** — returns below the floor with a declining recent trend.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: PASS — no group-level >10% customer (no IFRS 8 major-customer disclosure; thousands of channel customers); suppliers broad with partial integration (Klite). Forces: customer power **STRONG** (price-buying channels, low switching costs outside connected); supplier power WEAK; substitutes WEAK (lighting essential, LED shift already internalized); new entrants **STRONG** — LED commoditization erased entry barriers; the AR itself cites "structural overcapacity" and sustained price pressure. Tally: 2 strong = the book's stop signal. Moat: Philips brand licence, Hue ecosystem, channel scale — **eroding** (sales 7.5bn→5.8bn since 2022). Market growth: NO in value terms. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Comp: top pay €2.41m (Rondolat 2025, incl. €1.05m contractual exit) = 0.9% of NI — far under the 5% strike and the ≈$40m trigger; SB chair fees ~€0.1m vs Dutch norms, no strike. RPT: no material transactions disclosed beyond key-management pay. Buybacks (P19 dual-read, all tranches 2017–2025 tested): capital-reduction tranches 2017/2018/2025 at 9.7–10.1× EV/OI or rescued below-worth (2025: 6.7× MCap/FCF); one **flag** — 2022 plan tranche at 21.4× year-read MCap/FCF on a working-capital-trough denominator (normalized read 9.6×, no strike). Cooper audit: ≈€1.25bn at ~9–11× estimated pre-deal OI — INDETERMINATE (estimate), no adverse finding. Dividends paid every year. **PASS** — one flag, no pair.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- LED longevity permanently shrinks the replacement base — the razor-blade economics of the bulb era are not coming back.
- Structural Chinese overcapacity keeps price/mix negative across professional and consumer.
- Goodwill €2,615m ≈ shareholders' equity €2,673m — impairment risk compounds any further decline.
- Philips brand licence dependency (renewal/royalty terms) for the consumer franchise.
- Connected/Hue growth remains too small to offset conventional and OEM erosion (OEM sales −25% in 2025).

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

AVOID → WATCH requires both quality gates to move: worst-of-four ROCE clearing 10% (needs sustained OI recovery toward €550m+ on today's CE) and a defensible re-call of the entrant/customer forces — industry consolidation that demonstrably ends overcapacity. Price alone can never flip this verdict. Next review: FY2026 annual report (~Feb 2027); re-test Past on the new year and re-read the forces. The 2022 P19 flag stands unless a below-worth demonstration at execution is documented (v3.5.0 worth check).
