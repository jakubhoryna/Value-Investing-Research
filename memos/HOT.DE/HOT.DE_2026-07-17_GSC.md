# HOT.DE — HOCHTIEF Aktiengesellschaft: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | **WEAK** | ADEQUATE | **FAIL** | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** |

The ownable core is Turner: a capital-light US construction manager, now the dominant data-center builder, financed by customer float. It is wrapped in a contractor holding whose balance sheet is a leverage machine (L/E 1,816%), whose nine-year worst-of-four ROCE of 7.2% misses even the ADEQUATE floor, and which is run as a strategic vehicle of its >80% owner ACS — assets passed through it (€13.2bn Abertis flows, 2018), placed into it (issue to ACS at €143.04), traded out of it (Flatiron into an ACS-controlled combination, 2025), under a CEO who simultaneously runs the parent. At €455.60, after a five-fold two-year re-rating on the AI-construction narrative, the market pays 22.5× EV/OI against a framework band of ≈ €30. Three gates fail independently.

## 2. Understanding

HOCHTIEF is a 153-year-old Essen-based, ACS-controlled (>80% ex-treasury) construction holding winning competitively tendered building, civil, and contract-mining projects — Turner in US commercial/data-center construction management (~half of sales), CIMIC/Thiess in Australian infrastructure and mining, E&C in Europe — plus 38.2% of FlatironDragados and ~20% of toll-road group Abertis, serving corporate and public-sector customers who pay per project across North America, Australia, and Germany; a prominent, newly DAX-listed group reshaped by the 2024 Thiess re-consolidation and 2025 Flatiron disposal. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Nine fiscal years available (FY2016 and the 2015/16 balance sheets unobtainable — P1 flagged, STRONG unavailable). Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **7.2%** — below the 10% ADEQUATE floor; worst-of-four FCFROCE 10.9% passes, carried by customer float. The record is jagged: 2019 OI −€790m as reported (Middle East exit −€1,695m), 2020–21 troughs, then the 2024–25 surge. OI/sh +7.6%/yr and FCF/sh +3.9%/yr clear the 3% floor, but BV/sh compounded −6.1% and TBV is negative since 2019. L/E **1,816%** breaches P5 (200%) and the P6 cap (700%) with no P6 conditions. **WEAK** — twice over: returns below floor, leverage past the cap.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: no IFRS-8 single-customer ≥10% disclosure exists (its mandatory absence is the evidence); subcontractor and materials bases are deep. Forces: customer power **STRONG** (competitive tender, price-based award); supplier power WEAK; substitutes WEAK; new entrants WEAK at mega-project scale (bonding, prequalification, track record). Tally 1 strong, contained. Moat: ingrainedness — Turner's repeat CM-at-risk relationships and #1 US data-center position — stable. Market growth YES. Verdict **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Comp clean: top pay €4.93m = 0.5% of net income (strike line €45m); SB chair €376k, under 2× the local norm. The 2020 buyback is rescued below-worth (implied EV/OI 5.5×). **FAIL on a flag pair:** (1) parent-directed related-party dealing — Flatiron exchanged into ACS-controlled FlatironDragados (2025), the €13.2bn Abertis pass-through and €143.04 placement to ACS (2018), a dual CEO of parent and subsidiary; (2) the accounting-quality record — BICC IAS-8 restatements, the 2019 Middle East exit, CFO propped by CIMIC supply-chain finance whose unwind collapsed operating cash to €387m in 2021.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Data-center capex is the entire re-rating; a hyperscaler pause hits Turner's backlog and the multiple at once.
- Contractor tail risk is asymmetric: one Middle-East-scale blow-up erases years of thin margin — 2019 proved it.
- ACS can restructure assets into or out of HOCHTIEF at terms minorities cannot contest; free float is <20% and shrinking.
- Customer float reverses when intake slows; CFO fell 76% in 2021.
- Negative tangible equity leaves no cushion; the model runs on counterparty confidence and bonding capacity.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To flip, all three must move: leverage inside P6 (implausible for this model), governance not directed by ACS (no catalyst — ACS is buying, not selling), and price below ≈ €30–124 depending on EV read. Structural AVOID; likeliest endgame: an ACS minority buyout. Next review: FY2026 Group Report (~Feb 2027), or immediately on any ACS squeeze-out or structural announcement.
