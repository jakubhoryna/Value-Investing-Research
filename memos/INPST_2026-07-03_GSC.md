# INPST — InPost S.A.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

|Understanding|Past|Future|Shareholder-friendliness|Cheapness|FINAL|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|PASS|WEAK|WEAK|FAIL (buyback strike)|EXPENSIVE|**AVOID** (Past + Future + SF)|

InPost is a genuinely impressive operating story — Europe's largest locker network, a love-brand near-monopoly in Polish out-of-home delivery, parcel volume quadrupled 2020–2025 — but it fails three gates. The balance sheet carries 4.3× liabilities-to-equity without the buyback justification the framework requires; over a third of revenue comes from two marketplace customers (Vinted 20.1%, Allegro 14.4%) that have both already built their own delivery networks, killing any captive-customer waiver; and treasury buybacks exist solely to sterilise share-plan dilution at market prices. The question is in any case being taken away: a board-recommended €15.60 all-cash take-private by Advent, FedEx, A&R (Brzoska) and PPF is in its tender period, closing 27 July 2026, with 48% of shares committed and only EC and Vietnam clearances outstanding. At €15.40 the stock is a merger-arb instrument, not a value candidate; on framework arithmetic it trades at roughly 15–25× its mechanical limit price. Nothing plausible flips this verdict — if the offer lapses, the quality failures remain at whatever price the stock reverts to.

## 2. Understanding

InPost is a Luxembourg-incorporated, Amsterdam-listed, founder-led (Rafał Brzoska, since 1999) parcel-logistics company that earns per-parcel fees — paid chiefly by e-commerce merchants and marketplaces, plus consumer C2C senders — for out-of-home delivery through Europe's largest automated-parcel-locker estate (61,196 APMs; 94,536 OOH points) with courier and fulfilment attached; it is the dominant, highly profitable incumbent in Poland (~49% of revenue) and a scaling challenger in France/Iberia (Mondial Relay) and the UK (Yodel, Menzies), reports in PLN, and is currently subject to a recommended all-cash take-private. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Only five fiscal years exist (2021 IPO; FY2020 balance sheet not sourced), so STRONG is unavailable by rule. The record itself is mid-tier: worst-of-four normalized ROCE 14.1% (end-of-period, with-cash; beginning-basis runs 17.7–19.0% on the FY22–25 subset), worst-of-four FCFROCE 9.1%; growth is emphatic (OI/sh +18.3% geo, FCF/sh +42.8%), though FY2025 returns dropped sharply (ROCE 17.7%→11.1%) on Yodel consolidation. The decider is leverage: L/E 431.7% at FY2025 — down from four-digit post-reorganisation levels but more than double the 200% flag — funding lockers and acquisitions, not repurchases of underpriced stock, so the P6 conditions do not hold. **WEAK** — leverage breach without P6 conditions, on a thin record.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **FAILS twice**: Vinted UAB 20.1% and Allegro Group 14.4% of FY2025 revenue (FY2024: 22.6% / 18.0%) per the annual-report concentration table. The captive-customer waiver is unavailable — both customers have demonstrably backward-integrated (Vinted Go; Allegro One Box), the first waiver condition verbatim. Forces: customer power STRONG (concentration plus proven self-supply); supplier power WEAK (fragmented hardware, sites, courier labour); substitutes WEAK in Poland (locker-first habit, app ingrainedness) but STRONG in the UK/Eurozone where home delivery by incumbents is the default; new entrants WEAK in Poland (density economics; Orlen Paczka contained) but STRONG in expansion markets (open field, Amazon/Evri lockers). Tally ≥2 strong at group level. The moat — network density plus ingrainedness — is real, dominant and stable, but only in Poland, now under half of revenue. Market growth YES (~4.6% CAGR European parcels, OOH share rising). **WEAK** — breadth FAIL, waiver unavailable; forces corroborate.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation passes P17: CEO Brzoska's FY2025 total PLN 17.9m (≈$4.9m) vs the PLN 26.3m cap (5% of NI 526.3m, the lower leg); SB chair €240k. RPT trivial (PLN 3.3m exec service companies; Menzies linehaul). No dividends ever — defensible at these reinvestment returns. The strike is buybacks: treasury purchases (PLN 196.0m in 2024, 23.6m in 2025, cumulatively ≈ the period's SBC) exist solely to deliver share-plan awards — the April 2025 tranche is explicitly disclosed as satisfying "obligations under employee incentive schemes," at €12.35 average, nowhere near a discount to conservative worth. Textbook dilution sterilisation. **FAIL — buyback strike.** One strike disqualifies.
