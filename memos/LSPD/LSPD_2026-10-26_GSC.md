# LSPD — Lightspeed Commerce Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS (single segment); Past WEAK (losses; record-limited); Future WEAK (customer conc.); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (compensation); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past + Future + SF)**.

Lightspeed rents cloud point-of-sale and e-commerce software, processes card payments and advances cash against future card sales for small and mid-sized retailers, restaurants and golf courses, two-thirds of it in the United States. It has made an operating loss in every one of the nine years retrieved, between USD 22m and USD 1.1bn, and has written off USD 1.3bn of goodwill since 2023. Three hard strikes fail it on shareholder-friendliness: top pay rose in a loss year; buybacks continue at prices no positive earnings support; and goodwill from the founder-CEO's 2019–2021 deals has been written down twice while he again runs the company. A move needs sustained operating profit, a customer statement and containment of supplier power, and capital allocation read against worth.

## 2. Understanding

Lightspeed Commerce Inc. is a Montréal-based software and payments company, founded in 2005 and listed on the TSX (2019) and NYSE (2020), that rents by subscription cloud point-of-sale, inventory, e-commerce (Ecwid), golf-course (Chronogolf) and B2B wholesale-ordering (NuORDER) software, processes its customers' card payments as principal through third-party processors (Stripe, Adyen and others), buys their future card receivables as merchant cash advances (Lightspeed Capital) and resells POS hardware, all paid for by single- and multi-location small and mid-sized retailers, restaurants and other hospitality operators, golf courses and brands, mainly retailers in North America and hospitality businesses in Europe (USD 1,227.0m FY2026 revenue: transaction-based 66%, subscription 30%, hardware and other 3%; by customer location United States 64%, Australia 7%, Canada 7%, United Kingdom 5%, other 16%). PASS.

The filer reports one operating and reportable segment, so there are no units and no group parts. The Upserve US hospitality line was sold on 28 April 2026.

## 3. Quality — past record

STD lens, on a record-limited nine-year window. Operating income is negative in every year. Worst-of-eight ROCE is −31.8% over the window and −30.4% over five years. FCFROCE on maintenance-capex FCF (P20 constructed in six years, all capex in the three deal years) is −5.1% and −3.6%; the all-capex reading is −5.4%. Both per-share growth reads are n.m. L/E is 12.3%, inside P5. No sustained decline fires. **WEAK** on losses. The five-year bar fails whatever FY2017 shows, so the missing year cannot lift the grade.

## 4. Quality — future

The customers are businesses, and no current filing rules out one above 10% of revenue: a customer-concentration flag. Card payments run through four named processors and make up 81% of direct costs, so at least one processor exceeds 10% of total costs: a supplier-concentration flag. Lightspeed is not dominant, so neither flag is carried by a moat. Each becomes a STRONG force, uncontained, because returns never turned positive. Entrants are STRONG as well: the filer itself calls barriers low. Substitutes are WEAK. Three STRONG forces. The switching-cost moat is undemonstrated. Market growth YES. **WEAK**.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Every test year is a loss year. The hairiest number is USD 4.83m (CFO Asha Hotchandani Bakshani, FY2024, grant-date basis). In FY2026 the top total rose from USD 2.67m to 3.38m (CEO Dax Dasilva) while trailing earnings stayed negative: a pay strike. Ordinary repurchases of 5–6% of shares a year, still running, are clearly above worth with operating income negative: ADVERSE. FY2025 wrote down 41% of the goodwill from Dasilva's deals, the second such write-down: ADVERSE. Dasilva holds 10.48% (ALIGNED). **FAIL** at step 1 (`compensation`; also buybacks and acquisitions).

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- The payments margin depends on processors that, by the filer's own account, also compete for its merchants.
- The merchant-cash-advance book (USD 116.5m) carries credit losses that rise when small-business sales fall.
- The 2026 buyback programme keeps spending cash while operating income is negative.
- A renewed fall in market capitalisation, which triggered the FY2025 test, could force a further goodwill impairment.
- The filer says artificial intelligence intensifies competition in a market with historically low barriers.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Past and Future must both clear before price matters. That means operating profit sustained into 10% returns on both windows, and a customer statement plus evidence that margins hold against processors and rivals. SF clears only when pay stops rising in loss years, repurchases stop above worth and the impaired deals' write-downs leave the trailing window. Next review: the FY2027 annual results (May 2027).
