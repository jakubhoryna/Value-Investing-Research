Thule is a good business at a price the framework cannot defend. Worst-of-four normalized ROCE 16.6% and FCFROCE 10.8% over FY2016–25 are real returns, earned on a brand that lets a maker of roof boxes hold a 46% gross margin and take price. Shareholder-friendliness is about as clean as this project sees: no buyback has ever been executed in the window, so P19's usual failure route does not exist; CEO pay is 2.0% of levered FCF against a 5% line; the chair's fee is below one Swedish norm; related-party dealings are intra-group only; and Quad Lock (Dec 2024) was struck at ~10.5× pre-deal operating income. What stands between here and a BUY is arithmetic — 29.7× MCap/FCF and 16.8× EV/OI against 8× and 7×. Past falls short of STRONG only because FCF per share compounded at −0.1%, which also forecloses P12. The verdict flips on price alone, and +271% is a long way.

## 2. Understanding

**PASS.** Thule Group AB designs and manufactures premium branded gear for carrying things during active outdoor life — roof racks and boxes, bike carriers, rooftop tents, RV awnings, child bike seats, strollers, dog-transport products, backpacks, luggage and, since December 2024, Quad Lock phone mounts — sold predominantly wholesale to specialist sport, outdoor, bicycle and automotive retailers, distributors, vehicle OEMs and RV manufacturers (direct-to-consumer is just over a tenth of European sales) in the consumer sporting-goods and leisure-hardware industry; it is a vertically integrated, single-segment, single-share-class Swedish manufacturer with its own factories, headquartered in Malmö, taking roughly two-thirds of sales from Europe and a quarter from North America, and it is the long-established global category leader in car-borne sport-and-cargo carriers, listed on Nasdaq Stockholm Large Cap since 2014 with FY2025 net sales of SEK 10.4bn.

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **16.6%** (end × with-cash) and FCFROCE **10.8%** both clear P2's 15%/8% lines. STRONG is lost on per-share growth: ΔOI/share compounds at **+5.9%** and ΔBV/share at +6.6%, but **ΔFCF/share at −0.1%** sits below the 3% nominal floor — an endpoint artifact of a working-capital-driven series (FCF/share SEK 1.63 in 2022, 19.32 in 2024, 7.27 in 2025). No sustained decline fires: ROCE_wc fell twice running (2022–23), recovered in 2024, fell again in 2025 to 12.1%, that low being a Quad Lock denominator step-up rather than operating decay. L/E 90%, inside the 200% flag. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS**: the AR states no single customer exceeds 10% of external revenue; supplier concentration is undisclosed (no Swedish requirement), so that leg rests on a structural argument — commodity metals, resins and textiles against a SEK 879m 10% line — logged as a named re-test. Forces: customer power **WEAK**, supplier power **WEAK**, substitutes **STRONG** (direct rivals at every price point, a cheap doing-without option on a discretionary purchase, wholly-different-function alternatives), new entrants **WEAK** (vehicle-fit engineering libraries, safety certification, shelf, scale). Tally 1 strong, contained — a record 46.0% FY2025 gross margin is what containment looks like. Moat: **brand**, stable. Market growth **MIXED**. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**PASS — zero strikes, zero flags.** P17: CEO SEK 15.31m FY2025 = 1.95% of levered FCF and 1.37% of net income against a 5%-of-lower-leg line of SEK 39.2m; ≈ US$1.6m, far under the ~US$40m indexed trigger. Chair fee SEK 1.581m ≈ 0.8× the Swedish large-cap norm. One share class. P19: **no buyback executed FY2016–25** — nothing to dual-read, and the share count rose, so no dilution-sterilisation. Acquisition audit (negative TBV): Quad Lock EV SEK 3,587m ÷ pre-deal OI SEK 341m = **10.5×**. RPT intra-group only. Payout ~80%.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Discretionary big-ticket demand: organic sales fell 9.7% in 2022 and 15.2% in 2023 — a downturn hits volume and mix together.
- North America has declined several quarters running (−7.2% in Q2 2026), with tariff and FX exposure on top.
- Input costs, aluminium especially, rise into H2 2026 while offsetting price increases are planned, not realised.
- Quad Lock carries the growth narrative plus SEK 2.4bn goodwill and SEK 0.7bn brand value; an integration stumble or a fashion shift impairs both.
- Cash conversion, not profitability, is the weak link — a decade of flat FCF per share is why the band sits so far below the price.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth (P15).**
- *Customers:* the AR2025 segment note states plainly that **no single customer exceeds 10 percent of external revenue**. The disclosure sentence exists and is affirmative — **PASS**. Structure supports it: wholesale to specialist sport/outdoor/bike/auto retail, distributors, vehicle OEMs and RV manufacturers across 140 markets, plus a direct-to-consumer channel of just over 10% of Region Europe sales; 30,369 known shareholders is not a customer statistic but the retail base is comparably fragmented. Consolidation risk is real (e-commerce platforms, big-box) but has not produced a >10% account.
- *Suppliers:* **not disclosed**; Swedish filers carry no such requirement. Structural argument: inputs are commodity steel, aluminium, plastics and textiles bought against COGS + opex of SEK 8,789m in FY2025, a 10% line of **SEK 879m** — no single vendor of commodity metal or resin plausibly reaches that against a vertically integrated manufacturer that makes its own carriers. The Q2 2026 commentary on aluminium prices is a commodity-market exposure, not vendor power. **PASS on the structural argument**, logged as a data gap and a named re-test. No captive-customer waiver used.

**Forces (P16) — binary.**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **WEAK** | Fragmented retail with no >10% account, brand pull that makes Thule a must-stock line, backward integration implausible for a safety-certified vehicle-fit product |
| Supplier bargaining power | **WEAK** | Commodity metals, resins and textiles from many vendors; low switching costs; forward integration into branded carriers implausible |
| Threat of substitutes | **STRONG** | Direct rivals at every price point (Yakima, Atera, Kuat, Bugaboo, UPPAbaby, Osprey, Peak Design), a cheap doing-without option on a discretionary purchase (bikes inside the car, a generic mount), and wholly-different-function alternatives (rental, car-sharing, flying instead of driving) |
| Threat of new entrants | **WEAK** | Barriers to *success* are high: vehicle-fit engineering libraries covering thousands of models, TÜV/ISO safety certification, dealer and specialist-retail shelf, and manufacturing scale — value-end entrants exist but have not taken the premium segment |

**Tally: 1 strong.** Judgment zone → ADEQUATE if contained. It is contained: gross margin reached a record 46.0% in FY2025 and 47.3% in Q2 2026 while organic sales grew, which is what containment looks like — the substitute set is broad but has not forced price.

**Moat.** Single identified source: **brand**. Mechanism: Thule is the default reference name in car-borne carriers, which lets it hold shelf space, command a premium against functionally similar hardware, and extend credibly into adjacent carrying categories (the Quad Lock and dog-transport extensions are brand-permission plays). Direction: **stable** — strengthening on margin and category extension in Europe, offset by several quarters of North American decline and by the growth narrative resting increasingly on an acquired brand rather than the core one.

**Market growth.** **MIXED.** Long-run tailwind from active-outdoor participation; near-term, organic sales fell 9.7% in 2022 and 15.2% in 2023 before +3.5% in 2024, −1.3% in 2025 and +3.1% in H1 2026, with RV registrations declining and North America soft.

**Verdict: ADEQUATE** — breadth PASS, one contained strong force, a plausible and stable brand moat.
