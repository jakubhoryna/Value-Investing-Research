# MMB.PA — Lagardère SA: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past **WEAK**; Future **WEAK**; Shareholder-friendliness **PASS** (allocation SOUND ~65%; control caveat UNPAIRED; RPT INDETERMINATE); Cheapness **EXPENSIVE**; verdict **AVOID on the Past and Future gates**.

Lagardère is two unlike businesses bolted together: an asset-light, copyright-rich publisher on a 10.4% margin, and a concession retailer renting its only real asset — the location — from landlords who set the price. Together they employ €8.3bn of capital to earn €613m: return on capital 5.11% against a 10% floor, liabilities 980% of book equity. Seven times operating income is less than the debt, leases and minority interest ahead of the equity, so **no positive price makes Lagardère cheap on either cash reading of enterprise value** — the first such name in this file.

## 2. Understanding

Lagardère SA is a French *société anonyme* on Euronext Paris, 66.3%-controlled by Louis Hachette Group, running two structurally different units of comparable size — **Lagardère Publishing** (Hachette Livre: differentiated, copyright-protected trade, education and illustrated books, paid for by retailers, wholesalers and education ministries, asset-light) and **Lagardère Travel Retail** (commodity duty-free, convenience and foodservice merchandise sold at a *differentiated location* airside and in stations, under fixed-term concessions won by tender from airport and rail landlords, paid for at the till by transiting passengers) — plus a residual Lagardère Live, on €2.3bn of borrowings and €2.7bn of IFRS 16 concession leases. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **5.11%** (end basis, with cash) over FY2021–FY2025; the four reads cluster 5.11–6.85%, nothing within 315bp of the 10% floor. The trend is favourable — ROCE_wc rose in every transition, 0.89% → 7.38% — but off a COVID-crushed FY2021 and never at the floor. Worst-of-four FCFROCE is 10.39% statutory, ≈7.2% once interest moves into CFO, 4.4% on the company's after-lease CFAIT. Per-share growth means (ΔOI/sh +72.3%, ΔFCF/sh +11.1%) are trough-base artifacts. Total liabilities are **979.6%** of book equity — 4.9× the P5 flag and 1.40× the P6 cap, the P6 conditions absent. **WEAK** on two independent grounds: returns level, and leverage breach.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASSES** on both limbs, supplier look-through unresolved. Forces are called per segment, the units being close in size. Publishing: customer power **STRONG** (Amazon and a few chains control access, as the 2014 dispute showed); the other three WEAK. Travel Retail: supplier power **STRONG** (the grantor supplies the only input that matters, sets the minimum guarantee and re-tenders — a 5.4% margin on €6.1bn of revenue is the evidence) and new entrants **STRONG** (entry is one tender away); the other two WEAK. Tally **3 strong**. Moat: **ingrainedness** in the Publishing backlist, stable — absent from Travel Retail, which carries ~90% of right-of-use assets. Growth MIXED. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Arnaud Lagardère's 2025 fixed plus annual variable of ~€3.80m is 37% of the fiscal-year threshold (5% of the lower of attributable net income €203m and interest-adjusted FCF €858m = €10.15m) and 44% of the €8.58m normalized threshold; the ≈US$40m trigger is remote. Louis Hachette Group holds **66.29% of capital and 69.88% of votes — a 1.054× wedge**, the narrowest in the file: a §2·1C·1 caveat held **UNPAIRED**, TRI governing. Related-party transactions are **INDETERMINATE** (URD note unreachable) and cannot pair. Capital allocation is **SOUND**: no repurchase in the window, a modest FCF-funded dividend, €301m of deleveraging. **PASS.**

- Travel Retail's economics reset at re-tender: a good year raises the minimum guarantee, not the margin.
- Publishing, the half earning an honest margin, is a third of revenue and cannot carry a 980% balance sheet.
- A 66% owner with ~70% of votes passes every resolution unaided; 2026 approvals of 99.6–100% measure the parent.
- The related-party note and every acquisition price are unread — and a controlling parent is where that matters.

## C. Calculation notes

### C1. The French operating-income-line convention — **sixth instance, and the first filer with no line labelled "operating income" at all**

Lagardère's consolidated income statement presents, in order: revenue; other income from ordinary activities; operating expenses in detail; **"Recurring operating profit of fully consolidated companies"** (*résultat opérationnel courant des sociétés intégrées*); income from equity-accounted companies; non-recurring/non-operating items; **"Profit before finance costs and tax"**. There is **no line called "Operating income"**, "Business income" or "Operating margin".

This breaks both rules in the standing MODEL open item at once:
- The **label rule** (SGO.PA, DG.PA, ML.PA — "compute on the as-reported *Operating income* line") has **no object to point at**. Nothing is so labelled.
- The **definitional rule** (SGO.PA's stated reasoning: the line equal to revenue − COGS − SG&A) also has no clean object: the recurring line already excludes restructuring, impairments, gains on disposal and amortisation of acquisition intangibles, while *including* right-of-use depreciation and IFRS 16 lease-modification gains, so it is neither the gross-margin construct nor a full operating result.

**The call.** ROCE is computed on **"Profit before finance costs and tax"** — the as-reported line with **no add-backs**, which is MODEL's plain instruction ("Operating income as reported — no add-backs of impairments, litigation or 'one-offs'"). This is also the line the aggregator labels "operating income", and it ties exactly to the filed figures for the two years where both are available (FY2025 €613m, FY2024 €578m). It includes income from equity-accounted companies, which is *consistent* rather than a distortion, because equity-accounted investments (€149m at 30 Jun 2025) sit inside total assets and therefore inside capital employed.

**The alternative reading, and why it decides nothing.** On "recurring operating profit of fully consolidated companies", the observed wedge is small and positive: FY2024 +€15m (593 vs 578), FY2025 +€28m (641 vs 613), H1-2025 +€16m, H1-2026 +€19m. FY2021–FY2023 wedges were not retrieved. Bounding rather than inventing: if the three unretrieved years averaged a wedge of **+€100m** (four times the observed average), Σ recurring EBIT would be €2,354m and worst-of-four ROCE **5.98%**; at **+€200m** a year, €2,654m and **6.74%**. **Neither reaches the 10% floor, and neither comes within 325bp of it.** The convention is therefore **non-determinative on this name — the fifth free calibration point in six instances**, and the object class is new again: not Michelin's recurring restructuring, not Saint-Gobain's business-income line, not Bureau Veritas's acquisition amortisation, not Valeo's above-the-line R&D, but the **absence of a labelled line**. That absence is itself the finding worth carrying to the MODEL item: a rule stated by label fails on filers that do not use the label, and English-language IFRS filers frequently do not.

### C3. Leases — IFRS 16 in force across the whole window; P18 not engaged

IFRS 16 has applied since FY2019, so every year in the FY2021–FY2025 window is on a capitalised-lease basis and the P18 pre-adoption capitalisation test is not engaged. No adoption-year artifact falls inside the window.

**Lease liabilities are treated as debt-equivalent in capital employed, enterprise value and L/E**, as the brief requires and as the register's lease-heavy precedents (AXFO.ST, DEC.PA) do. They are already inside total assets (as right-of-use assets) and total liabilities, so no adjustment is needed to CE or L/E; the EV build adds them explicitly.

Scale, for the record: right-of-use assets €2,456m at 30 Jun 2026, of which **€2,202m (89.7%) is concession stores** — i.e. Travel Retail. Lease liabilities €2,740m; lease interest €65m in H1-2026 (€122m in FY2025); the "cancellation of fixed rental expense — concession stores" adjustment is €279m in the half, so annualised fixed concession rent is roughly €560m. **The lease liability alone (€2,740m) is 102% of market capitalisation.** Operating income as computed is *after* right-of-use depreciation and *before* lease interest, which is the conventional post-IFRS-16 pairing with a lease-inclusive enterprise value.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

### Breadth (P15)

**Customer — no single customer above 10% of revenue.**
- The 10% line is **€935m** of FY2025 revenue.
- No disclosure sentence naming a customer above any threshold was found in any retrieved document. Under P15 the absence of the sentence suggests the condition is absent (i.e. no such customer).
- **Travel Retail (66% of revenue)**: the paying customer is the individual transiting passenger. Atomised by construction.
- **Publishing (32% of revenue)**: the paying customers are retailers and wholesalers, and the largest is Amazon. For Amazon to cross €935m it would have to be more than **31% of worldwide Publishing revenue** — plausible for Hachette Book Group's US trade sales alone but not for a portfolio that includes French and Spanish trade (where Amazon's share is far lower), UK, education (sold to schools and ministries) and illustrated/partworks.
- **Result: PASS**, with the exact percentage **INDETERMINATE on magnitude** because Lagardère discloses no customer concentration figure. Logged; not gate-determinative, because Future fails on the force tally regardless.

**Supplier — no single supplier above 10% of COGS + opex.**
- The 10% line is **€874m** (FY2025 revenue €9,353m less operating income €613m = €8,740m).
- **Look-through to the effective supplier.** In Travel Retail the effective supplier is not the goods vendor but the **concession grantor** — the airport or rail landlord who supplies the location. Annualised fixed concession rent is roughly €560m (from the €279m H1-2026 disclosure) spread across hundreds of concessions in more than 30 countries, so no single landlord approaches €874m. The largest single-airport relationships (Paris via ADP, Schiphol, major US hubs) are each a fraction of that. In Publishing the effective supplier is the author/agent complex, which is diffuse by construction, plus paper and print, which are commodities.
- **Result: PASS**, with the look-through **unresolved** in the sense that no filer disclosure confirms it — the same standing item logged for SW.PA and FR.PA. Must be resolved before any future upgrade of Future.

**Both limbs pass → breadth PASS.** No waiver used or needed.

### Four forces (P16) — binary, never medium, run per segment

Both units are run because they are close in size: Publishing is 32% of revenue and **48.7%** of recurring operating profit; Travel Retail is 66% of revenue and **52.1%** of recurring operating profit. Neither is dominant, so the brief's "or for each unit if more equally weighted" condition is met on the numbers.

**Lagardère Publishing (Hachette Livre)**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer power | **STRONG** | Amazon plus a handful of chains and supermarket groups control physical and digital access to the reader and set discount terms; the 2014 Hachette–Amazon dispute, in which Amazon degraded availability of Hachette titles for months, is the standing proof that the buyer can withhold distribution from the seller. |
| Supplier power | WEAK | Authors and agents are numerous, no single author is material to a €3.0bn imprint portfolio, and paper, print and freight are commodity inputs with alternatives. |
| Substitutes | WEAK | Physical and digital book demand has proved resilient through two decades of screen competition; the direct substitute (self-published and now AI-generated text) has not displaced curated trade or, especially, education publishing, where the buyer is a ministry buying a validated curriculum. **This is the call most likely to flip** and is named as such in the risks. |
| New entrants | WEAK | Barriers to entry are near zero but barriers to *success* are high — a backlist, editorial reputation, author relationships and a distribution network cannot be assembled quickly; the industry has consolidated rather than fragmented. |

**Lagardère Travel Retail**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer power | WEAK | The paying customer is a transiting passenger with short dwell time, no airside alternative and no ability to negotiate. |
| Supplier power | **STRONG** | The concession grantor supplies the only input that matters — the location — and prices it through minimum annual guarantees set at tender; **a 5.4% recurring operating margin on €6,133m of revenue, against Publishing's 10.4% on €3,001m, is the arithmetic evidence of where the rent lands.** |

**Tally: 3 STRONG at group level** (Publishing customer power; Travel Retail supplier power; Travel Retail new entrants). P16: ≥ 2 strong → **Future WEAK**. The verdict is robust to disagreement on any one call — even reversing the Publishing customer-power call leaves 2 strong.

### Moat

**Single identified source: Ingrainedness** — the Publishing backlist. Mechanism, in three sentences: Hachette owns perpetual copyright in a deep catalogue whose sales recur annually with negligible incremental capital, and the group's own H1-2026 commentary attributes performance to "robust international backlist sales". Authors, once established with an imprint, rarely move, because the imprint holds the rights to the works that make them worth publishing. That is ingrainedness in the framework's sense: the customer relationship persists because unwinding it is more trouble than continuing it.

**Direction: stable** — neither strengthening nor eroding; backlist share of Publishing revenue has not visibly moved.

**But the moat covers roughly one third of the business and a much smaller share of the capital.** Travel Retail carries 89.7% of right-of-use assets and 66% of revenue and has **no moat of any kind**: not government (concessions are competitively tendered, not granted), not cost (scale buys some purchasing leverage, competed away at tender), not brand (Relay and Aelia are signage, not preference), not network, not switching costs (the passenger switches for free), not ingrainedness (the counterparty is a landlord who re-tenders on schedule). Under Gate 1B a moat present in a minority of the capital base does not rescue a 3-strong-force tally.

### Market growth

**MIXED.** Global air passenger traffic is structurally growing and Lagardère's Travel Retail revenue grew 5.5% in FY2025 and 3.3% like-for-like in H1-2026 — but the growth accrues to the landlord at each re-tender rather than to the operator, which is why divisional recurring EBIT grew only 2.5% on 3.3% of revenue growth in H1-2026. Book markets are flat in units. Growth is real at the top line and largely absent below it.

### Future verdict

**WEAK** — three strong forces (any two suffice), and the identified moat covers a minority of revenue and a small minority of capital employed.
