Uniper is a European thermal generator and commodity trading house that was nationalised during the 2022 gas crisis and is now 99.12% owned by the Federal Republic of Germany. The record does not support ownership on any reading: cumulative ten-year net income is **−€18.4bn**, and even on management's own adjusted EBIT — which strips impairments and derivative swings — ten years of trading produced €4.2bn against capital employed in the tens of billions. The two clean post-crisis years earn 3.3–4.9% ROCE, and 5.7–7.3% if you strip the entire derivative gross-up out of the denominator. That is a business consuming capital at scale, not compounding it. Nothing here is a price problem; there is no price at which the gates pass, and the trigger band is not merely distant but arithmetically unattainable while FY2025 FCF is negative. The free float is 0.88% — about €158m of stock — so the name is also uninvestable at any size that would matter.

## 2. Understanding

**PASS.** Uniper SE is a Düsseldorf-headquartered European integrated power-and-gas company — 99.12% owned and controlled by the Federal Republic of Germany through UBG Uniper Beteiligungsholding GmbH, though still listed on the Frankfurt Prime Standard — that generates electricity from an 18.5 GW fleet of hydro, Swedish nuclear, gas and residual coal plants and sells power, natural gas, LNG, freight and emission allowances both to roughly 1,000 municipal utilities, industrial customers and grid operators across its core markets of Germany, the UK, Sweden and the Netherlands and to wholesale counterparties in more than 40 countries, operating through three segments (Green Generation, Flexible Generation, Greener Commodities) while executing an EU-mandated divestment programme under the state-aid conditions attached to its 2022 rescue.

The business is describable; the *accounts* are hard, and that is a separate matter recorded as a risk, not a Gate 0 failure. Reported revenue swung from €420bn (2022) to €73bn (2025) on derivative gross-up, and total assets from €128bn to €32bn, without the underlying fleet changing proportionately.

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized average ROCE over FY2022–25 is **−1.3%**; FCFROCE **−5.3%** (P3 requires ≥10% ROCE). Extending to FY2021–25 on end-of-period denominators gives −2.2% / −2.4%. This is not solely the 2022 catastrophe: the pre-crisis half of the window was also poor — net losses in 2016 (−€3,234m), 2017 (−€538m) and 2018 (−€442m), with adjusted EBIT eroding from €1,362m (2016) to €863m (2019). The best recent year, FY2024, returns 4.0–4.9%, rising only to 7.3% if every derivative and financial asset is stripped from capital employed. Per-share growth is **n.m.**: the share count went 365.96m → 8,329.51m in the 2022 recapitalisation, then a 20:1 reverse split, so no geometric mean is meaningful. Leverage is the one clean metric — L/E 164%, inside the P5 flag, with €3.9bn net cash. **WEAK**, on level, trend and a decade of cumulative losses.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **FAILS** on the supplier side: the effective supplier is the European gas-import complex, and the 2022 Gazprom default — which cost €15.6bn of negative free cash flow to replace — is the most vivid possible demonstration that ready alternatives did not exist at economic prices. Customer breadth passes (~1,000 named customers plus wholesale). Forces run **3 strong of 4**: customers strong (commodity product, and the report discloses gas customer attrition cutting UES volumes from 187.9 to 138.7 TWh), suppliers strong, substitutes strong (renewables, storage, corporate self-generation), new entrants weak — capital, permits and grid connections are genuine barriers. **No moat is identifiable** among the six sources; systemic-relevance designations and capacity payments are regulator-priced to preclude excess returns, which the 4% ROCE confirms. Market growth MIXED: European electricity demand rises while Uniper's own generation fell 17.4% in 2025. **WEAK** on three independent grounds.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**FAIL** — on a flag pair, not a hard strike. P17 is genuinely clean: the highest-paid executive took €2.011m (2024, Poppinga, inflated by a €999k make-whole) against a 5%-of-net-income line of €71.3m; variable pay has been eliminated entirely since 2022 under EnSiG §29(1a), base salaries are capped at €2.0m/€1.4m, and the supervisory-board chair takes €210k — far inside any German norm. No buyback programme exists (P19 not engaged), no acquisition audit triggers (BV ≈ TBV, goodwill nil). The two flags are: **(1)** related-party dealings with the controlling holder of a size no minority can ignore — €2,551m paid to the Federal Republic in 2025 to settle state-aid recovery claims, plus Gazprom damages proceeds contractually assigned away; and **(2)** capital allocation directed by non-owner objectives — ~€8bn of planned investment at 3–5% ROCE, forced disposals on a Commission timetable, and management's own summer-2025 admission that it was refocusing onto projects "making a reliable contribution to earnings."

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- **Float and forward supply:** 0.88% free float (≈€158m) makes the quoted price close to meaningless, and the EU requires the state to cut its stake to ≤25% by end-2028 — a known, large, price-insensitive supply overhang.
- **Earnings are a derivative artifact as much as an operating outcome:** reported OI (€950m) and adjusted EBIT (€568m) diverge by 67% in FY2025 alone.
- **Policy dependence is total:** the AR concedes the strategy "is fundamentally based on the assumption that the necessary political conditions will be established," and 2025's guidance cut followed regulatory delay to the German power-plant and hydrogen strategies.
- **Cash conversion has broken down:** FY2025 operating cash flow was −€814m even before capex, against €1,426m of reported net income.
- **The bull case is a backvaluing illusion:** against FY2023's crisis-peak economics the stock trades at 2.1× EV/OI and 3.0× MCap/FCF — see Appendix F for why that is inapplicable.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

The verdict is structural, not cyclical, and no price flips it. To reach even WATCH, Uniper would need: worst-of-four ROCE durably ≥10% across a full post-crisis window (three-plus clean years — earliest FY2028); a supplier-breadth answer that survives the P15 test; an identifiable moat; and an ownership structure in which minority capital is not subordinate to the controlling holder's contractual claims. The state sell-down to ≤25% would address the last of these and is the single event most capable of changing the analysis — but it resolves governance, not returns on capital. **Next review: FY2026 annual report (~March 2027)**, informational; earlier only on a re-privatisation announcement with disclosed terms.

## C. Calculation notes

**Latest-year (FY2025) CE build.** Total assets €31,604m − NIBCL €2,907m (accounts payable €2,750m + other current liabilities €157m) = **CE_wc €28,697m**; − cash & STI €5,517m = **CE_xc €23,180m**.

**NIBCL judgment call (§2·1A burden of proof).** Accrued expenses, income taxes payable and deferred income are not separately disclosed in the aggregated source and were left in CE, per the rule that ambiguous items stay in. Derivative and margining liabilities inside current liabilities (€9,818m total, of which only €2,907m is identified above) are **not** treated as NIBCL — they are trading positions, not operating payables. Both calls push CE up and ROCE down; the sensitivity below bounds the effect.

**Derivative gross-up sensitivity.** Uniper's balance sheet is inflated by trading positions ("other current assets" €66,715m in 2021 falling to €5,742m in 2025). Stripping *all* other current and other long-term assets from CE — a deliberately over-generous adjustment that assumes the entire trading book nets to zero capital: FY2024 CE €18,420m → ROCE **7.3%**; FY2025 CE €16,731m → ROCE **5.7%**. Even here P3's 10% is not reached, which is why the Past verdict does not turn on the NIBCL gap.

**TBV build (FY2025).** BV €11,887m − goodwill €0 − other intangibles €708m = **TBV €11,179m**.

**EV builds (FY2025 balance sheet, current price).** MCap = €43.20 × 416,475,332 = €17,991.7m. *All-cash-excess (lower):* 17,991.7 + debt 1,620 + preferred 0 + NCI 172 − cash & STI 5,517 = **€14,266.7m**. *No-cash-excess (upper):* 17,991.7 + 1,620 + 0 + 172 = **€19,783.7m**.

**Leases.** All in-window years are post-IFRS 16 (adopted 2019); lease liabilities are already capitalised on the reported balance sheet and sit inside CE, EV and L/E. P18 is not engaged — no pre-adoption year falls in the FY2021–25 computation window.

**Maintenance-capex basis.** Uniper *discloses* a maintenance/replacement split (FY2025: €525m maintenance and replacement, €408m growth, of €932m total investments), so the §2·1A company-disclosed rule applies rather than P20's constructed basis. Applied to FY2025 free cash flow: CFO −€814m − €525m = **−€1,339m**, still negative, so the maintenance basis changes nothing and MCap/FCF remains n.m. on either basis. Note total "investments" (€932m) exceeds cash capex (€774m) — different definitions; the quality gates use cash capex per §2·1A.

**IFRS interest reclassification.** Not applied — Uniper reports interest paid within operating cash flow, so no adjustment to CFO was required.

**Substitution rule (§2·1A).** Considered and **rejected**. Uniper is not a deposit-funded lender, insurer, securities-heavy holdco or captive-finance-dominated group; it is an industrial generator with a large trading book. The OI/FCF/CE machinery applies, with the derivative sensitivity above as the guard against denominator distortion.

**Bias check.** *Live: peculiarity and miscontrast.* A 99%-state-owned, nationalised, once-bankrupt utility invites reflexive dismissal, and the €18.4bn loss column makes any subsequent number look better by contrast. Handled by running the arithmetic on the most flattering defensible basis at each step — best single year rather than the average, derivative-stripped CE, management's own adjusted EBIT, and the FY2023 backvaluing read — and confirming each still fails. *Also considered:* incomprehensibility (the derivative gross-up is not a reason to fail Gate 0 — the business is describable; recorded as a risk instead) and consensus (the sector re-rated 34% in 2025; not treated as evidence either way). No judgment here is born of a bias.

The payout itself is unobjectionable. The allocation around it is not: Uniper plans **~€8bn of investment through the early 2030s** (€5bn through 2030) while earning 3–5% ROCE, which inverts the framework's rule to retain when returns are high and distribute when they are not. The programme's direction is set by state policy and Commission conditions rather than by return on capital — the AR states the strategy "is fundamentally based on the assumption that the necessary political conditions will be established" — and management conceded in summer 2025 that it was refocusing "even more strongly … on activities and projects making a reliable contribution to earnings," an admission that the prior programme included projects that were not. Disposals are executed to a regulator's timetable, which is the definition of a forced seller. **Second flag.**

**Strike logic applied.** No hard strike on any of the four tests. Two independent scrutiny-level negatives, arising from two different tests (RPT; capital allocation) → **SF FAIL** per §2·1C. *Recorded alternative reading:* the 99.12% control structure, paired with the 2022 recapitalisation that diluted pre-existing holders from 100% to 0.88% (365.96m → 8,329.51m shares issued at €1.70 against a €2.59 year-end price), could be read as the control-structure clause's "control plus value-destructive allocation" hard strike. The verdict rests on the flag pair rather than that reading, because the dilution was a rescue from insolvency where the counterfactual was plausibly zero. Either route reaches FAIL.
