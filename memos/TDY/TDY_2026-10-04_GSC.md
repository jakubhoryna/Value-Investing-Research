# TDY — Teledyne Technologies Incorporated: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future WEAK, shareholder-friendliness PASS (one capital-allocation flag), cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** (Past and Future gates).

Teledyne owns a broad set of specified sensors, cameras, instruments and defence electronics, with rising margins and a clean balance sheet. Two gates fail. Returns on capital fell from about 11% to about 7–8% after the $8.1bn FLIR purchase in 2021, putting the ten-year worst-of-four ROCE at 7.8%. And the US Department of War buys a fifth of everything Teledyne sells, through competitively bid contracts it can terminate for convenience. The price is also far from range: the band is near $138–146 against $617.17. The verdict could only move after several years of ROCE back above 10% on the enlarged base, and even then the customer concentration would still need a waiver it does not earn today.

## 2. Understanding
Teledyne designs and makes differentiated enabling technologies: digital imaging, including FLIR thermal cameras, infrared and visible sensors and X-ray detectors (52% of FY2025 sales); instrumentation for environmental, marine and electronic test (24%); aerospace and defence electronics (17%); and engineered systems (7%). Its customers are industrial and scientific OEMs, research institutions, defence primes and the US Government (25.5% of sales). It is a decentralised, acquisition-built Delaware holding of operating businesses, headquartered in Thousand Oaks, California, with about 48% of sales international. It was spun off from Allegheny Technologies in 1999 and had $6.1bn of FY2025 sales. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**WEAK.** The worst-of-four average ROCE is 7.8% (end-of-period, cash in); the best is 9.5%. Worst FCFROCE is 6.8%. Both sit below the adequate line. The cause is the purchase price of FLIR: capital employed nearly tripled in 2021, and returns on it fell from 10.9% (FY2016–20) to 7.0% (FY2021–25). That is a sustained decline, not a one-year dip. Operating margin rose from 11% to 19% over the decade, but the acquired goodwill outweighs it. Per-share growth is strong: OI/share +12.0% a year and FCF/share +17.5%, despite 32% more shares from the FLIR stock issue. Liabilities to equity are 40% at 28 June 2026.

## 4. Quality — future
**WEAK, carried by breadth.** The Department of War took $1,203.6m, or 19.7% of FY2025 sales, so breadth fails as written. The captive-customer waiver is denied. The DoD cannot build these sensors itself, but switching costs are not prohibitive: the 10-K says contracts are competitively bid, 81% fixed-price and terminable for convenience. Returns of 7–8% on capital do not show a dominant moat. Suppliers pass. Forces: government customer power is STRONG; the other three are WEAK. That is a tally of one, contained. The moat is switching costs from spec-in and qualification, and it is stable. Defence, space and imaging markets are growing.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
**PASS — one flag.** The top-paid executive, Executive Chairman Mehrabian, earned $9.79m. That is 22% of the ceiling on the year read ($44.7m) and 23% on the three-year read ($43.3m). Top director pay is $389.5k. The one related-party item, a spouse's employment at $547k, was ratified. Mehrabian holds 127,173 shares outright, about $78m. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**. FLIR cost 25.5–30.0× pre-deal OI and FCF across the four reads and diluted returns. The pending Varex deal ($1.1bn) costs 22.6× five-year OI and 40.5× three-year OI. The 2024–25 buybacks were bought at 17–26×.
