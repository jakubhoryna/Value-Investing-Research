# GTLB — GitLab Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | WEAK | WEAK | FAIL (P17 + P19) | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** |

A debt-free DevSecOps platform ($1.36bn liquidity) that has never printed a positive annual operating result in seven fiscal years. Triple gate: worst-of-four normalized ROCE −21.0% fails Past at any level; Microsoft/GitHub bundling plus AI-native entrants put two forces STRONG with the switching-cost moat eroding — GitLab's own June 2026 "agentic era" restructuring (14% of staff, 22 country exits, consumption-pricing pivot) is the confirmation; and SF strikes twice — top comp at 6.1% of FCF against a negative-NI basis (P17), and a $50m Q1 FY2027 buyback at 16.1× MCap/FCF with trailing OI negative, so no EV/OI ≤ 20× is demonstrable (P19). Nothing flips on price; a flip needs durable positive OI, below-worth repurchase discipline, and comp scaled to cash generation — multi-year at best.

## 2. Understanding

GitLab sells tiered subscriptions (Free/Premium/Ultimate plus Duo AI add-ons) to a single-application DevSecOps platform spanning the software-development lifecycle — GitLab-hosted SaaS on two hyperscalers, single-tenant Dedicated, or self-managed; customers are organisations' engineering functions (none >10% of revenue; ~82% billed from the US); an all-remote Delaware C-corp consolidating the JiHu China JV; a 2011 open-source project, 2021 IPO, mid-transformation under a Dec-2024 CEO with a June-2026 restructuring, competing chiefly with Microsoft's GitHub. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Seven flow years (FY2020–26), six balance-sheet years — under P1; STRONG unavailable, thin history flagged. OI negative all seven years (−$128m to −$70m); worst-of-four normalized ROCE **−21.0%** (beg-CE with-cash; both ex-cash variants n.m. — cash+STI exceed capital employed every year). Normalized FCFROCE 1.2% best-case vs the 8% floor; FY2026's first real FCF ($222m) is 97% covered by $215m SBC. Per-share OI growth n.m.; L/E 69% is the lone pass. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASSES both sides: no customer >10% of revenue (disclosure sentence present); hosting split across two hyperscalers ($131m committed), alternatives ready, under 10% of total cost. Forces: customer power WEAK (10k+ customers, sticky pipelines; DBNRR 122%→117% noted); supplier power WEAK; **substitutes STRONG** (GitHub bundled into Microsoft enterprise agreements at marginal cost; AI coding agents undermining seat-based pricing); **new entrants STRONG** (AI-native entrants, falling barriers). Tally 2 STRONG — stop signal. Moat: switching costs on installed CI/CD and compliance workflows — real, **eroding**. Market growth YES for AI dev-tooling, MIXED for seat-licensed DevOps. **WEAK.**
