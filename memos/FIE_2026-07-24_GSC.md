Fielmann is worth owning for one thing: an unusually durable scale-cost position in a fragmented, demographically-supported retail category — 57% of all spectacles dispensed in Germany, a record, from 6% of the country's optical stores, with 90% customer retention and vertical integration from lens grinding to till. What stands between here and a BUY is price, not quality. The shares at €39.20 sit 102% above the band, and the gap is structural: €600m of IFRS 16 lease liabilities sit in enterprise value, so the EV/OI leg will bind hard for as long as Fielmann rents its stores. The verdict flips to BUY only on a genuine dislocation toward the high €20s–€19 area; it flips to AVOID if the Shopko acquisition multiple resolves above 20× on disclosed data, or if the returns recovery of 2023–25 stalls back toward the 2022 trough.

## 2. Understanding

Fielmann Group AG designs, partly manufactures and retails prescription eyewear, sunglasses, contact lenses and hearing aids — plus eye-health services and a bundled "Zero-Cost" eyewear insurance — to individual consumers paying out of pocket (statutory insurers reimburse part of German hearing-aid sales), through a vertically integrated, 72.9% family-owned German listed AG running ~1,262 stores and digital channels across Germany (61% of sales), Switzerland, Austria, Spain, CEE and the United States, as Europe's largest and Germany's dominant optical retailer, now mid-way through a debt- and acquisition-funded transformation from a German price leader into a multinational vision-care platform. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized average ROCE 14.9% (CE_wc, end-of-period basis) on the retrievable FY2018–25 record; best read 17.5%. Worst FCFROCE 14.4%, comfortably clear of the 8% line — this is a cash-generative business. The shape matters more than the level: ROCE ran ~22% in 2018–19, halved to 10.5% by 2022 as the Vision 2025 acquisition and store programme loaded capital employed, and has recovered three consecutive years to 16.0% in 2025. OI/share compounded 3.8% and FCF/share 9.2% p.a., both above the 3% floor; TBV/share fell 9.8% p.a. on acquired goodwill. L/E 151.5%, inside P5. Not STRONG (returns below 15%, and only eight years are retrievable). **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS both sides: 30 million individual customers, none material; a broad sourcing network across frames, lenses and traded goods, with own lens grinding and own-brand collections as the structural mitigant. Forces — customer power WEAK (atomised buyers, no backward integration, though switching costs are low); supplier power **STRONG** (EssilorLuxottica supplies the category *and* competes in it, owning Apollo and GrandVision — forward integration is realised, not theoretical); substitutes WEAK (contacts sold in-house; LASIK is costly, irreversible and no answer to presbyopia); new entrants WEAK in Europe on scale and brand, STRONG in North America where Fielmann holds ~4% regional share. Tally 1 strong — judgment zone. Moat source: **cost**, scale-driven, stable and still widening at home, unproven abroad. Market growth MIXED. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 clears decisively: the *entire* Management Board's IAS 24 remuneration of €10,080k is 4.91% of the lower leg (net income €205.5m; 5% = €10,275k), so no individual can breach; Supervisory Board aggregate €914k, far inside any German norm. Related-party volumes are de minimis (€0.5m supplied / €0.4m received against a €1.9bn cost base) and the §312 dependency report is clean. No buyback programme, no SBC, no dilution to sterilise. The live issue is the acquisition audit: Shopko Optical at a deal EV of €312.5m against an estimated pre-deal OI of ~€13.1m ≈ **24× — above the 15× flag on every build tested**, and above the 20× strike on the central build, but the gross-up rests on estimated tax, PPA-amortisation and lease-interest add-backs, so the strike logs INDETERMINATE (§2·1C) while the flag stands. One flag, no second negative. Family control at 72.9% on a single share class is a logged caveat, not a strike. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- EssilorLuxottica is simultaneously Fielmann's largest supply-side counterparty and, through Apollo and GrandVision, its largest European competitor — the one force call that could turn a second gate.
- The US build-out is ~11% of sales, ~€400m of acquisition capital and unproven: three deals (Shopko, SVS Vision, Óptica Universitaria) at multiples the company has never disclosed.
- German consumer weakness plus a 57% unit share means domestic growth is now mostly price and mix, not volume — 2025 unit growth was 1.9%.
- Operating leverage runs through €1.02bn of personnel cost; German wage settlements compress margin faster than they compress revenue.
- €600m of lease liabilities plus a €275m Schuldschein have taken L/E from 88% to 152% in seven years; further debt-funded M&A pushes toward P5.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Next review: FY2026 annual report (~April 2027), which rolls the window to nine years, gives North America a second full year against the €197m goodwill balance, and is the named re-test for the acquisition audit. Earlier if the price breaks ≈ €26.

## C. Calculation notes

**FY2025 capital-employed build.** Total assets 2,485,206 − NIBCL 430,962 = **CE_wc 2,054,244**; less cash & equivalents 222,467 and short-term investments 4,400 = **CE_xc 1,827,377**.

**NIBCL composition and burden-of-proof calls (FY2025).** Trade payables 102,576 + current non-financial liabilities 217,478 (contract liabilities under IFRS 15 — Zero-Cost Insurance €130,851 current, hearing-aid repair lump sums, vouchers — plus social-security and wage/sales tax) + income tax liabilities 28,754 + current provisions 82,154 = **430,962**. Current provisions are included because their disclosed composition is dominated by accrued compensation (personnel provisions €52,780 of €82,154, being bonuses and special payments settling in H1 2026) and accrued invoices for construction and services (€17,144) — both named in the §2·1A NIBCL default. The €12,230 warranty leg is the ambiguous item and was left in NIBCL rather than split, since splitting it moves nothing. **Left in capital employed** (burden of proof unmet): current other financial liabilities €38,228, current financial liabilities €1,177, and all non-current provisions.

**Alternate NIBCL reading — logged.** Excluding current provisions from NIBCL entirely (the strictly conservative reading) gives worst-of-four ROCE of **14.25%** (end-of-period) and 14.72% (beginning-of-period). **The call does not flip the verdict**: both readings land ADEQUATE, between P3's 10% and P2's 15%.

**Goodwill.** Retained in capital employed throughout. Fielmann is a serial acquirer (four sizable deals since 2020) and the §2·1A carve-out for non-acquisitive companies does not apply.

**Leases.** FY2019–25 are under IFRS 16; lease liabilities are treated as debt-equivalent in CE, EV and L/E, and FY2019 carries the adoption-year artifact (CE steps up €387m). **FY2018 is pre-adoption and P18 triggers**: undiscounted rental commitments at 31.12.2018 were €377,882k against total assets of €944,793k = **40.0%**, far above the 10% test. Rather than construct a discount rate, the company's own IFRS 16 opening figure was used — lease liabilities as at 1.1.2019 of **€387,065k**, discounted at Fielmann's disclosed weighted-average incremental borrowing rate of **0.63%**. Applying §2·1A: imputed interest = 0.63% × 387,065 = 2,439; imputed depreciation = lease expense − imputed interest = 74,151 − 2,439 = 71,712; OI = 250,756 + 74,151 − 71,712 = **253,195**; FCF = CFO 192,978 − capex 76,001 + lease payments 74,151 − imputed interest 2,439 = **188,689**; total assets and total liabilities each grossed up by 387,065. Lease payments are the disclosed 2018 minimum lease payments of €74,151k; the €1,609k of contingent sales-based rent and €1,331k of sub-lease payments were excluded, matching IFRS 16 scope.

**IFRS interest reclassification (mandatory, §2·1A).** Fielmann reports interest paid within financing. CFO was reduced by interest paid before deriving FCF in every year: 2019 −2,390 (lease interest, embedded in the lease-payments line), 2020 −1,259 −2,110, 2021 −1,203 −4,057, 2022 −7,901, 2023 −14,272, 2024 −26,787, 2025 −31,252. FY2018 needed no adjustment — interest paid sat inside CFO under the pre-2019 presentation. Lease principal repayments remain in financing throughout.

**Restatements.** FY2024 comparatives are taken from AR2025 as restated under IAS 8 for the IFRS 15 revision to custom-glasses revenue recognition (contract assets €44,447k at end-2024; +€325k to FY2024 net income). FY2022–23 are on each report's own basis. The Zero-Cost Insurance contract-liability break described in A is not adjusted.

**Share count.** 84,000,000 shares issued, single class, no dilutive instruments in any year of the window ("no events that would result in any dilution of earnings per share"). Treasury holdings are 41,529 shares at end-2025 (0.05%). Per-share metrics use 84,000k throughout — within 0.2% of the exact weighted averages — and the market-capitalisation and band arithmetic uses the exact 83,958,471 shares outstanding.

**FY2025 OI quality.** Reported OI of 329,346 includes €5,858k of write-ups on tangible/intangible assets and €4,882k on right-of-use assets, plus a €9.6m reversal of an investment impairment inside other operating income. ## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth (P15).**
- *Customers* — **PASS.** ~30 million individual retail customers; no customer-concentration disclosure exists because none is possible. Statutory health insurers reimburse part of German hearing-aid sales, but they are not the paying customer of record for the eyewear business (89% of group sales).
- *Suppliers* — **PASS, with a logged caveat.** The annual report states that raw materials, components including lenses, and frames are sourced "from a variety of suppliers" across a broad network with long-term contracts; no single supplier is disclosed above 10% of cost of materials plus operating expenses (10% of that base is ≈ €190m against total material costs of €488m). Look-through caveat: the lens industry is a genuine oligopoly and EssilorLuxottica is the effective supplier behind a large share of category volume. The mitigants are structural rather than nominal — Fielmann grinds and coats lenses in its own facilities, designs its own frame collections, and assembles in-house.

**Forces (P16) — binary calls.**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|-------|------|-----------------|
| Customer bargaining power | WEAK | Atomised individual buyers with no backward-integration path; offset only partly by low switching costs on a 2–4 year repurchase cycle. |
| Supplier bargaining power | **STRONG** | EssilorLuxottica supplies the category and competes in it through Apollo Optik and GrandVision — forward integration is realised, not hypothetical. |
| Threat of substitutes | WEAK | Contact lenses are sold in-house; refractive surgery is expensive, irreversible and does not address presbyopia, the demographic driver; "doing without" is not available for corrected vision. |
| Threat of new entrants | WEAK (Europe) / STRONG (North America) | Europe: 57% German unit share, vertical integration, brand and regulated optician training dull both entry and success. North America: ~4% regional share against entrenched incumbents — Fielmann is itself the entrant. |

Tally on the dominant unit (Europe, 89% of external sales): **1 strong force → judgment zone**. The North America segment call is logged and carried, not averaged away.

**Moat.** Single identified source: **cost**. Mechanism — the largest store network and unit volume in its home market spread fixed advertising, lens-production and training costs across 9.5 million pairs a year, funding a price position (the "Nulltarif" zero-supplement promise) that smaller opticians cannot match while earning an adequate return. Direction: **stable, still widening at home** (German unit share at a record 57%, up again in 2025), **absent outside the core** — Spain, CEE and the US are bought share, not earned scale.

**Market growth.** **MIXED.** Ageing populations support presbyopia demand and audiology (6% of 2024 revenue, targeted at ~10% by 2030); German optical industry sales were roughly flat and unit growth was 1.9% in 2025, so domestic growth is share, price and mix. The US market is larger and growing but is where Fielmann has no cost moat.
