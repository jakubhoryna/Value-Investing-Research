# CBLL — Ceribell, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Past **WEAK** (operating losses of $39.7m and $58.4m; returns on capital −25%) · Future ADEQUATE · SF **FAIL** (P17: both legs negative and a $4.57m chief-executive package against them) · EXPENSIVE (8.0× revenue; neither primary has a denominator) → **AVOID (three gates)**.

Ceribell is a good young medical-device company priced as though it were already a great one. Revenue grew 36% to $89.1m at an 88% gross margin, the point-of-care EEG headband is genuinely differentiated in a workflow where speed decides outcomes, and the FDA has cleared Clarity from neonates to adults, added delirium monitoring and granted a breakthrough designation for stroke. But the operating loss widened from $39.7m to $58.4m as IPO proceeds went into a salesforce, cash and investments fell $194m → $159m, and the market pays $714m — eight times revenue, 4.6× book — for a business that has never earned an operating dollar. The compensation gate then fails independently.

## 2. Understanding
Ceribell sells a rapid-response EEG system to hospitals — a disposable headband, a bedside recorder and the Clarity algorithm that flags seizure activity in real time — placed in emergency departments and intensive-care units on a razor-and-blade model, with recent clearances extending the platform to delirium and, prospectively, stroke; Sunnyvale-HQ'd, IPO'd October 2024. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
Two years, both loss-making. Operating loss **$39.7m (2024) and $58.4m (2025)** on revenue of $65.4m and $89.1m; the two-year sum is −$98.1m against capital employed of $179–214m — a return of **−24.97%**. Free cash flow is negative on any reading (App A). What the record does show is gross margin of 87.9% and revenue up 36%, with the loss widening because sales and marketing spend is being added deliberately rather than because unit economics are poor. A legitimate growth story, and not a return record. Leverage is not an issue — L/E 26.1%. **WEAK** — by absence.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth PASS both sides on the evidence available (hospital customers across US acute-care sites; no single customer disclosed above the line — check limited). Forces: customer power WATCH — hospital purchasing consolidates into group purchasing organisations, though a low-cost disposable inside a reimbursed acute workflow is not where they push hardest · supplier power WEAK · substitutes WATCH — technician-read EEG is the incumbent, slower and scarcer, which is the gap the product fills · new entrants WATCH — Natus and the large monitoring vendors have distribution, and the barrier is clearance plus clinical evidence rather than technology. Tally 0 strong. Moat: **Switching costs**, early-stage — once a hospital standardises its protocol and trains staff, displacement is slow; strengthening on the clearance additions, never yet tested by a competitive attack. Market growth YES. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
Comp: **hard strike.** Net income and free cash flow were both negative in FY2025, and against no owner earnings the chief executive's **$4,565,821** total (salary $639,000, stock $1,707,132, options $1,574,179, bonus $619,313) is far more than token. FY2024 was the same picture. Related-party transactions: a consulting agreement with a chief medical adviser at **$450/hour up to 36 hours a month — $202,525 in 2025 and $192,450 in 2024** — small, disclosed, arm's-length in form; logged, not counted as a second negative. Capital allocation: **SOUND-leaning** — IPO proceeds are being spent on commercial expansion as stated, with no repurchases, no dividends, no acquisitions and modest debt; the criticism available is the size of the loss, which is a strategy question rather than an allocation abuse. The gate fails on the strike. **FAIL.**

## 6. Price
Neither primary has a denominator: operating income is negative in both years and free cash flow with it. What is measurable: **8.0× revenue**, **4.6× book**, and an enterprise value of $736m against $159.3m of cash and investments. The restoring condition is trivially close — with only $22.3m of notes and leases, **operating income above ≈ $3.2m produces a positive no-cash bound** — so the first profitable year makes this name properly measurable, and the guide points to $111–115m of revenue in 2026. **EXPENSIVE.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers (context for the AVOID)
The bull case may well be right: an 88%-gross-margin razor-and-blade device with three cleared indications, growing 25–36% a year into an installed base that gets stickier with each protocol adoption. The objection is not to the business but to paying eight times revenue for a company that has never earned an operating dollar. The bear case is that hospital budgets tighten, a large monitoring vendor bundles a rival product, and the loss curve does not bend before the cash does.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review
Analysable when operating income turns positive; investable on this framework only after several such years and a large fall in the multiple. Watch the loss trajectory against revenue growth — the crossing point, not the growth rate, is what matters here. Next review: **FY2026 results (~Feb 2027)**. Alert: n/a — no band exists yet.
