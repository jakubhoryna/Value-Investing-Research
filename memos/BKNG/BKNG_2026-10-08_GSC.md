## 2. Understanding

Booking Holdings is a Delaware-incorporated, Nasdaq-listed operator of online travel platforms (Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK, OpenTable) that earns commissions and merchant margins from about 4.4 million accommodation properties and other travel providers, plus traveller fees, on bookings made by consumers worldwide, with Booking.com in the Netherlands carrying most revenue; it is a large, mature, still-growing company that calls Booking.com the world's leading accommodation brand by room nights. **PASS.** One reportable segment, lens STD.

## 3. Quality — past record

STD lens. Worst-of-four ROCE is 27.0% over FY2016–25 and 35.6% over FY2021–25; FCFROCE on maintenance-capex FCF (P20 constructed, financial income out) is 26.6% and 35.4%, the all-capex reading 26.3% and 35.1%. No sustained decline. But equity has been below zero since FY2023 (−$5,578m at FY2025, −$10,783m at June 2026). The deficit came from $62.1bn of cumulative repurchases at 15–21× operating income, never at the strict CHEAP line, so the §2·1A pass is unavailable. Per-share growth through the FY2020 loss has no geometric mean (n.m.; endpoint reads +14.0% and +13.2%), which also fails P3. **WEAK — leverage.**

## 4. Quality — future

Supplier power is STRONG: search engines, "primarily Google", take most of $8.2bn of marketing and favour their own offerings. Substitutes are STRONG: direct booking and AI assistants. Entrants are WEAK. The network moat is stable and market growth YES. Three STRONG forces. **WEAK.**
