# MICC.AS — The Magnum Ice Cream Company N.V.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | WEAK | WEAK | PASS (2 flags logged) | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** |

The world's largest ice-cream company, demerged from Unilever on 6 Dec 2025 as a leveraged spin. Two independent gates fail. **Past WEAK:** the separation loaded ~€3.0bn net debt and stripped equity from €2,801m to €625m, so latest-year L/E ≈1,097% — above even the P6 700% cap — and the ~3-year carve-out record independently bars STRONG. **Future WEAK:** two of four forces are strong (retailer power with real private-label backward integration; a broad substitute set worsened by sugar/GLP-1 headwinds), against a genuine but pressured brand moat. The business is decent — mid-teens-to-low-20s ROCE, iconic brands — but neither statistically good here nor cheap: at €16.31 it trades at ≈21.7× EV/OI and ≈79× MCap/FCF (FY2025), ≈17×/≈13× on FY2024. BUY would need real deleveraging, a standalone year proving returns hold, and roughly a quarter of today's price.

## 2. Understanding

A century-old, Amsterdam-incorporated N.V. that owns, manufactures and sells branded ice cream (Magnum, Wall's/Heartbrand, Ben & Jerry's, Cornetto, Breyers, Talenti, Carte d'Or) to grocery, convenience and out-of-home customers across ~80 countries, spun out of Unilever in December 2025 as the world's largest standalone ice-cream group. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Carve-out/combined statements give only FY2023–25 of income and two real balance-sheet years, so **STRONG is unavailable (thin history)**. Returns are respectable but falling: ROCE ≈20.8% (FY2024) → ≈13.6% (FY2025, worst-of-two on wc basis), FCFROCE ≈21.6% → 2.9% as FCF collapsed. Revenue is flat (€7,618m→€7,910m); operating profit fell 22% in FY2025 (€764m→€599m) and FCF fell to €126m on separation costs and working capital. The decisive fault is **leverage**: post-demerger L/E ≈1,097% (€6,855m ÷ €625m), net debt €3.0bn (nil a year earlier, inside Unilever). That breaches P5 and exceeds the P6 cap; P6 relief is unavailable (returns not uniformly great). **WEAK — leverage breach; thin-history cap.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS (inferred): no single retail customer or commodity supplier is disclosed above ~10% across an 80-country footprint (not individually confirmed — logged). Four binary forces: customer power **STRONG** (consolidated grocers; private-label ice cream is real backward integration); supplier power **WEAK** (commodity dairy/sugar/cocoa/packaging, many vendors); substitutes **STRONG** (all frozen/snack/confectionery plus "do without", pushed by sugar taxes and GLP-1 appetite suppression); new entrants **WEAK** (freezer-cabinet placement + cold chain + brand deter scaled entry). Tally = **2 strong → WEAK**. Moat: **brand** (Magnum, Ben & Jerry's) plus cold-chain/freezer ingrainedness — real but stable-to-eroding (the unit under-grew inside Unilever). Market growth MIXED (LSD category; management targets 3–5% organic). **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Newly listed, so the record is thin. No buyback history (neutral). Comp: aggregate key-management €21m (FY2025); the single highest-paid figure is not isolable from XBRL — the P17 hard-strike line is 5% of the lower of FCF/NI = **€6.3m** (FCF-depressed), so a confirmed CEO total above that would strike — **flagged, unconfirmed** (needs the 20-F remuneration report). RPT: Unilever retains ~19.9% (to be sold down) plus transitional-service and IP arrangements — **scrutiny flag**. Capital allocation: the ~€3.0bn debt-funded return to the former parent is a structural feature new holders inherit. **PASS on the record assessable today; two flags logged — a confirmed comp figure >€6.3m paired with the RPT flag would flip this to FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Debt service and covenant headroom on €3.0bn net debt if ice-cream seasonality or a weak summer compresses FCF (FY2025 FCF was only €126m).
- Structural demand risk: sugar reduction, GLP-1 appetite effects, and private-label trade-down in a cost-of-living squeeze.
- Dis-synergies and stranded costs as Unilever transitional services roll off; the €800m restructuring program must deliver the promised productivity.
- Unilever's ~19.9% stake overhang pressuring the shares as it is sold down.
- Governance still forming — first proxy/remuneration cycle untested; a large founding CEO grant could trip P17.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Verdict flips toward WATCH only if: leverage normalizes (L/E back within bounds via retained earnings/debt paydown, lifting Past off WEAK) **and** a full standalone year shows returns holding and one strong force easing (Future to ADEQUATE). It flips to BUY only if those hold **and** price falls to the low-single-digit-euro band — a ~75%+ decline from here. Next review: **FY2026 results (~Feb–Mar 2027)**, the first clean standalone year (resets returns, FCF and the balance sheet); earlier on a major deleveraging event, an adverse comp disclosure, or price < ≈ €4.
