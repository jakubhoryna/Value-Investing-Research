# WEIR.L — The Weir Group PLC: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future STRONG; Shareholder-friendliness PASS (one flag — capital-allocation CAUTION); Cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past)**.

Weir makes slurry pumps, wear linings, hydrocyclones, comminution equipment and ESCO ground-engaging tools for mines, and earns about 80% of revenue from the spares and wear parts its installed base consumes. That aftermarket is a sound franchise, and FY2021–25 returns show it: ROCE averaged 10.4% on capital including cash. The ten-year record does not. Oil & Gas impairments in FY2019–20 (£829m inside discontinued results) and weak FY2016–18 profits leave the worst normalized ROCE at 5.5% and FCFROCE at 4.6%. The 2025 Micromine software purchase, at about 20× forward EBITDA and paid for with debt, has left tangible book negative. The verdict flips only once the window holds mostly mining-only years at today's returns. At 2,580p the stock is 27× FCF and 19.6× / 18.7× EV/OI in any case.

## 2. Understanding
Weir designs, makes and services slurry pumps, valves, hydrocyclones, rubber and polyurethane wear linings, crushers and high-pressure grinding rolls (Minerals), plus bucket teeth, lip systems and other ground-engaging tools (ESCO). Mine and quarry operators pay for these, mostly as replacement parts and service, worldwide; Africa & Middle East, South America and Australia are the largest regions. A small, growing mining-software business (Micromine, Fast2Mine) sits alongside. It is a Glasgow-headquartered FTSE 100 company founded in 1871, a mining-only group since it sold Oil & Gas in February 2021. **PASS**.

## 3. Quality — past record
Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **5.5%** and worst FCFROCE **4.6%**, both on end-of-period capital with cash included. Statutory operating income was negative in FY2015, FY2019 and FY2020 because of Oil & Gas impairments, and FY2017 FCF was £1m. Since the exit, ROCE has risen from 8.5% (FY2021) to 11–12% and FCFROCE to 6–10%. FCF per share grew 1.5% a year over FY2015–25, below the 3% floor. Book value per share grew 2.8% a year, after a 2018 equity raise. L/E is 138%. The continuing-operations reading also fails, at 8.0%. **WEAK** — returns below the floor on every reading.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth passes: no external customer reaches 10% of revenue. No supplier dependence is disclosed, inputs are multi-sourced, and Weir runs its own foundries. All four forces are WEAK. A mine cannot make its own Warman pumps or ESCO teeth, and the installed base ties it to OEM wear parts. Inputs are steel, alloys and elastomers. No process does without slurry transport or digging. Metso and Chinese makers compete as rivals, not as new categories; reference plants and the service network keep entrants out. The moat is switching costs on an 80%-aftermarket installed base, and it is stable. Market growth: YES. **STRONG**.
