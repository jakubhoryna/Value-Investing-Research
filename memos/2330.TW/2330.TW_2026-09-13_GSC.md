# 2330.TW — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS, Past STRONG, Future ADEQUATE (customer-breadth waiver caps here), shareholder-friendliness PASS, cheapness EXPENSIVE — final verdict **WATCH**.

TSMC is worth owning for a cost moat that widened rather than eroded across a full decade: 23.3% worst-of-four normalized return on capital employed, operating income per share compounding 19.9% a year, no dilution, and a dividend that quadrupled while capital spending tripled. Nothing in the business stands between here and a BUY. Price does: at NT$2,410 the shares trade at 63× last year's free cash flow and 33× enterprise value to operating income, against a trigger band of ≈NT$306. The verdict flips only on a severe repricing, not on anything management can do.

## 2. Understanding

TSMC is a Taiwan-headquartered, Taiwan-listed pure-play contract semiconductor foundry that manufactures, packages and tests integrated circuits to its customers' designs — selling wafer fabrication, mask-making and advanced packaging capacity to roughly five hundred fabless chip designers, systems companies and integrated device makers, three-quarters of them headquartered in North America — and which is the oldest, largest and technologically leading firm in that industry, operating fabs in Taiwan, China, the United States, Japan and Germany. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized average ROCE 23.3% (beginning-of-period, with cash), worst-of-four FCFROCE 11.9% — both clear of the STRONG bar on the strictest read, and the cash-excluded variants run 37.9–44.0% and 19.3–22.3%. No sustained decline: annual ROCE troughed at 19.1% in the 2019 and 2023 down-cycles and reached a decade high of 28.1% in FY2025. Per-share growth on a share count essentially unchanged for ten years: operating income +19.9%/yr, free cash flow +19.0%/yr, book value +16.3%/yr, tangible book +16.4%/yr — all far above the inflation floor, all while distributing a rising dividend. Liabilities-to-equity 45.6%, and the balance sheet carries net cash. **STRONG.**

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth fails: the ten largest customers were 78% of FY2025 net revenue, and the largest is reported near 19%. The captive-customer waiver applies — these buyers cannot backward-integrate into leading-edge fabs, face prohibitive requalification costs, and the concentration is structural because few firms buy leading-edge capacity at scale — and it caps this gate at ADEQUATE. Forces: customer power WEAK (gross margin rose from 50% to 60% across the decade), supplier power **STRONG** (ASML is the sole source of EUV lithography, with no alternative), substitutes WEAK, new entrants WEAK. Tally one strong force, contained. Moat source: **cost** — leading-edge volume no rival can match spreads process R&D and tool fleets over more wafers — and it is strengthening as node economics raise the minimum viable scale. Market growth YES. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation: 5% of the lower of FCF and net income is NT$49.6bn on the FY2025 read and NT$35.7bn normalized over three years; total pay for *all* directors and key management personnel combined annualises near NT$9bn, so the highest-paid individual cannot approach either line. Director pay is far below any plausible Taiwan seat norm. The absolute scrutiny trigger cannot be tested against a disclosed individual figure — logged INDETERMINATE, re-test at the 2026 annual report. Related-party dealings are small, operational and arm's-length (1.0% of revenue). Capital allocation is **SOUND**: no repurchases in ten years, no dilution, dividends at 47% of FY2025 free cash flow and rising, no acquisitions, and reinvestment that raised returns rather than diluting them. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Taiwan-Strait escalation would impair the asset base directly and is not diversifiable by fab geography on any near-term horizon.
- A single-supplier failure or allocation shift at ASML would stall the leading-edge roadmap outright.
- Export controls and tariff action are already a disclosed operating constraint and could cut China revenue or tool access further.
- Customer concentration now runs through AI accelerator demand; a capex pause at two or three hyperscaler-facing customers would hit utilisation hard.
- Under a technical cooperation agreement, the ROC government may call on up to 35% of TSMC's capacity — never invoked in 39 years, but a standing sovereign claim on the asset.

## C. Calculation notes

**Latest-year CE build (FY2025, NT$ thousands).** Total assets 7,933,023,878 − NIBCL 1,032,750,819 = CE_wc 6,900,273,059. NIBCL = accounts payable 82,551,595 + payables to related parties 1,778,730 + salary and bonus payable 63,872,882 + accrued profit-sharing bonus and directors' compensation 103,355,278 + payables to contractors and equipment suppliers 177,730,306 + income tax payable 202,337,872 + accrued expenses and other current liabilities 401,124,156. CE_xc = CE_wc − cash & STI 3,068,594,811 = 3,831,678,248.

**NIBCL burden-of-proof calls (applied identically in all eleven years, logged per §2·1A).**
- *Cash dividends payable* (NT$285,258,060 at FY2025) is **left in CE**. It is a declared distribution, not an operating liability, and is not in the framework's default NIBCL list; the ambiguous-item rule keeps it in. This is the conservative direction — it raises CE and lowers ROCE.
- *Payables to contractors and equipment suppliers* (NT$177,730,306) are **in NIBCL**. They are non-interest-bearing trade credit funding capital employed; the fact that the underlying purchase is capitalised does not change the nature of the liability.
- *Provisions — current* (sales returns and allowances, FY2016 and FY2017 only) are **in NIBCL**. IFRS 15 reclassified this exact balance into accrued expenses from FY2018, so including it preserves comparability across the adoption break.
- *Current lease liabilities* sit inside "accrued expenses and other current liabilities" and are therefore inside NIBCL, contrary to the debt-equivalent rule. The split is disclosed only from FY2019 (NT$3,833,015 at FY2025, ≈0.06% of CE_wc). Correcting it would move worst-of-four ROCE by under 0.1pp. Left uncorrected for cross-year consistency and logged.
- *Short-term loans* and *long-term liabilities — current portion* stay in CE throughout, per rule.

**Cash & STI definition.** Cash and cash equivalents + current financial assets at FVTPL + current financial assets at FVTOCI + current financial assets at amortised cost (+ current hedging financial assets where disclosed). The line "other financial assets — current" (restricted/pledged deposits and temporary payments; NT$59,702,922 at FY2025) is **excluded** — it is not clearly a liquid investment. Excluding it raises CE_xc and raises EV on the all-cash variant, both conservative.

**Capex definition.** Acquisitions of property, plant and equipment **plus** acquisitions of intangible assets, from the investing section, consistently across all years. FCF = CFO − capex, levered and derived. Two consequences worth naming: (i) including intangibles makes FY2025 FCF NT$992,418m rather than the NT$1,002,565m an aggregator using PP&E only would report — the darker figure is used; (ii) government grants received for PP&E (NT$76,258,790 investing inflow in FY2025) are **not** netted against capex, again the darker treatment. Acquisitions of right-of-use assets (a new FY2026 investing line, NT$9,441,034 in H1) are included in the TTM capex figure only.

**Leases (P18).** IFRS 16 was adopted 1 Jan 2019; from FY2019 the balance sheet capitalises and lease liabilities sit in CE, EV and L/E as debt-equivalents. The adoption-year artifact is NT$19,903,615 of lease liability recognised on 1 Jan 2019, disclosed in the FY2019 filing. For the pre-adoption years in the window, undiscounted non-cancellable operating-lease commitments at 31 Dec 2018 were NT$20,849,585 against total assets of NT$2,090,128,038 — **1.0%, far below the P18 10% trigger. No pre-adoption capitalisation performed.**

**P20 — maintenance-capex basis: NOT ACTIVE.** This is a deliberate departure from the v1 memo, which suspended MCap/FCF on an AI-capex super-cycle argument. All three §2 conditions were tested:
- (i) *A documented multi-year capex ramp far above the pre-cycle capex/sales ratio* — **fails.** Pre-cycle FY2016–18 capex/sales averaged 33.6% (35.1%, 34.3%, 31.3%). The ratio did spike to 53.4% / 48.1% / 44.2% in FY2021–23, but FY2024 and FY2025 print 33.3% and 33.7% — back at the pre-cycle level. Revenue grew with the spend. There is no elevated ratio at the test year to adjust away.
- (ii) *Management discloses the spend as expansion and/or guides capex down* — half-met. The FY2025 20-F describes 2026 spending as capacity expansion at 2nm/3nm and named fabs, but guides capex **up** to US$52–56bn from roughly US$40bn.
- (iii) *A defensible anchor* — the pre-cycle 33.6% anchor applied to FY2025 revenue gives maintenance capex of NT$1,279,842m against actual NT$1,282,557m. The adjustment would be NT$2.7bn, or 0.3% of FCF. Immaterial even if the basis were granted.
With (i) failing outright and (iii) yielding nothing, P20 is off and **both primaries run on all-capex FCF**. This is the single largest driver of the band moving from ≈NT$600 (v1) to ≈NT$306 (v2).

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customer side: FAIL, waived.**
Q: Is any single customer over a tenth of revenue? The FY2025 20-F discloses that the ten largest customers accounted for **78% of net revenue** and carries customer concentration as a named risk factor — the disclosure the test looks for is present and adverse. Press attribution puts the largest customer (Nvidia) near 19% and the second (Apple) near 17% for 2025; the filing-sourced top-ten figure alone is decisive. The Q2-2026 note that the ten largest customers are 84% of accounts receivable corroborates. **FAIL.**

*Captive-customer waiver (§2·1B) — all three conditions tested:*
1. *Cannot backward-integrate and face prohibitive switching costs.* A leading-edge fab costs US$20–30bn and requires process know-how accumulated over decades; the one well-funded attempt at re-entry has not produced competitive leading-edge external foundry volume. Switching foundries at an advanced node means re-porting the design to a different process design kit and IP library and requalifying — 12–24 months of work. **Holds.**
2. *Dominant and stable moat.* See below — cost moat, strengthening. **Holds.**
3. *Structural to the industry.* Only a handful of firms in the world buy leading-edge capacity at scale; the concentration is a property of the customer population, not of TSMC's sales effort. **Holds.**
**Waiver granted. Future capped at ADEQUATE.**

**Breadth — supplier side: PASS, qualified.**
No single supplier is disclosed above a tenth of COGS + operating expenses (FY2025 COGS + opex NT$1,873,410m). The framework's look-through clause ("a single fab ecosystem") is squarely aimed at TSMC's EUV dependency on ASML, and that dependency is real and sole-source. But the measurement the test specifies is COGS + opex, and ASML's billings are overwhelmingly capitalised — they reach the income statement only through depreciation spread over five years. On that measure the exposure is materially below the threshold, and TSMC discloses no supplier concentration. **Recorded as a PASS on the disclosed test, with the dependency carried instead as a STRONG supplier force, which is where it does its work in this gate.** Flagged for re-test if TSMC ever discloses a >10% supplier.

**Four forces (binary):**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **WEAK** | Gross margin rose from 50% (FY2016) to 60% (FY2025) while customers concentrated — pricing power demonstrably sits with TSMC. |
| Supplier bargaining power | **STRONG** | ASML is the sole source of EUV lithography; no alternative vendor exists and switching is not possible at any price. |
| Threat of substitutes | **WEAK** | No substitute for leading-edge logic silicon; doing-without is unavailable; chiplets and advanced packaging are complements TSMC itself supplies. |
| Threat of new entrants | **WEAK** | Barriers to entry (capital, permits, EUV allocation) plus barriers to success (yield learning, customer trust, ecosystem) have absorbed a decade of subsidised attempts without dislodging leading-edge share. |

**Tally: 1 strong → judgment zone.** Contained: ASML's incentives are aligned (TSMC is its largest customer), the relationship is long-term contracted, and TSMC's scale commands first allocation. Not a WEAK verdict, but not clearable to STRONG either — and the customer-breadth waiver caps at ADEQUATE regardless.

**Moat — single source: COST.** TSMC's annual capacity exceeds 17 million 12-inch-equivalent wafers with roughly three-quarters of wafer revenue at advanced nodes. Process R&D of NT$246bn (FY2025) and the tool fleet for each new node are spread over more leading-edge volume than any competitor can assemble, so TSMC's cost per good die at every node is structurally lower and its yield learning curve runs faster. **Direction: strengthening** — each node raises the fixed cost of entry and therefore the minimum volume needed to earn a return, and TSMC is the only firm with that volume. (Switching costs and ingrainedness are present but derivative; cost is the source.)

**Market growth: YES.** Leading-edge logic demand is growing on AI accelerator and premium-mobile silicon; HPC moved from 51% to 58% of revenue in FY2025 and to 60% in H1-2026.
