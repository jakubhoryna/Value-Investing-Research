# FLUT — Flutter Entertainment plc: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK (worst-of-four normalized ROCE 1.1%); Future WEAK (four of four forces STRONG); shareholder-friendliness FAIL (P19 ADVERSE); cheapness EXPENSIVE — verdict **AVOID**.

Flutter owns the world's largest online betting franchise and converts roughly 7% of revenue to operating cash, but earns no return on the capital that bought it: five years of US GAAP show $1.1bn of cumulative operating income against a capital base above $20bn, mostly goodwill and intangibles from the Betfair, Stars, Sisal, Snai and Betnacional deals. FY2024's $875m looked like the turn; FY2025 fell to $615m and the twelve months to June 2026 is negative. Net debt doubled to $11bn in one year, funding acquisitions plus $1.1bn of repurchases struck near $249. The 65% twelve-month fall has opened no band: 7× operating income no longer covers the obligation stack.

## 2. Understanding

Flutter is an Irish-incorporated, New York-run, NYSE-only online gambling group selling fixed-odds sports bets, casino, poker, bingo, Italian, Turkish and Moroccan lottery concessions, betting-exchange and pari-mutuel wagering, fantasy contests, CFTC-regulated prediction-market contracts and B2B odds services to individual adult bettors staking their own money — 15.9m average monthly players across ~100 countries, 88% online, plus 1,127 owned shops — as a vertically integrated multi-brand licence holder (FanDuel, Sky Betting & Gaming, Sportsbet, PokerStars, Paddy Power, Betfair, Sisal, Snai, tombola, Adjarabet, MaxBet, Betnacional) that must win and renew a separate concession per product per jurisdiction and remit duty on gross win, 43% of FY2025 revenue American and 57% International. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE 1.1%, best of the four 2.3%; worst-of-four FCFROCE 2.5% against the 8% line. Yearly ROCE ran −2.2%, −0.4%, +0.9%, +4.4%, +2.5% — a FY2024 peak, then reversal, trailing OI now −$62m. Book value per share compounded at −10.3% a year, $78.91 to $51.09, no dividend paid; tangible book negative throughout. L/E of 216.7% breaches the 200% flag with the P6 conditions absent — borrowing funded acquisitions, not underpriced stock. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth passes; supplier breadth **fails** on look-through — the licensing state sells the operating right, has no alternative vendor, takes the largest slice of gross win, and has repriced repeatedly (UK levy and stake limits, Illinois per-wager tax, India's ban, Italian re-tendering for 2028). All four forces call STRONG: customer power (the filing names low switching barriers; US marketing rose 61% in Q2 while US revenue fell 6%), supplier power as above, substitutes (prediction markets serve all 50 states under a lighter regime, and Flutter cannot offer sports contracts where it runs a sportsbook), new entrants (one federal registration replaces 31 state licences). Moat brand-plus-scale, **eroding** — US Adjusted EBITDA fell from $400m to $119m in a quarter. Growth MIXED. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 clears both reads: CEO total $19.70m against 5% of FY2025 levered FCF ($20.6m) and of trailing-3y normalized FCF ($28.1m), the net-income leg negative on both so both ran half-blind; the ≈$40m absolute trigger is not approached. Capital allocation is **ADVERSE**: FY2025's $1,123m of repurchases priced at 78–107× on all four reads, funded alongside $2,688m of acquisitions by $5,224m of net new debt against $412m of free cash flow; Boyd's 5% FanDuel stake at $1,553m implied ~$31bn for FanDuel against a $17.7bn group today; and the programme still executed through H1 2026 at 32–54× with no published price discipline. **FAIL.**

## C. Calculation notes

**NIBCL composition (documented call).** NIBCL = accounts payable + accrued expenses + current income taxes payable + other current liabilities + long-term unearned revenue. FY2025: 386 + 2,218 + 120 + 2,080 + 1 = 4,805. The primary balance sheet reconciles exactly: S&P's accrued expenses + other current liabilities + income taxes payable (4,418 at FY2024, 4,418 at FY2025 ex-unearned) equals the primary's "other current liabilities" plus "player deposit liability". Current portion of long-term debt, current lease liabilities, deferred tax liabilities and other non-current liabilities all stay in CE per §2·1A.

**Player-deposit pair (judgment call, logged).** Player-deposit cash of $1,932m (FY2025) sits inside total assets and the matching player deposit liability of $1,859m sits inside NIBCL, so the pair nets to +$73m in CE_wc — symmetric, immaterial, and the correct treatment for customer money the group cannot deploy. CE_xc deducts corporate cash and STI only ($1,851m at FY2025), leaving the netted pair; restricted cash ($72m) stays in CE.

**Goodwill treatment.** Goodwill stays in CE. The deduction option is reserved for non-acquisitive companies and Flutter is the opposite case — $22.8bn of goodwill and intangibles at FY2025 from Betfair (2016), Stars (2020), Sisal (2022), Snai, NSX and the Boyd stake (2025).

**Leases.** All window years are post-ASC 842, so the balance sheets already capitalise; lease liabilities are treated as debt-equivalent in CE, EV and L/E. P18 pre-adoption capitalisation is not engaged.

**FY2025 CE build.** Total assets 29,280 − NIBCL 4,805 = CE_wc 24,475; less cash & STI 1,851 = CE_xc 22,624.

**TBV build (30 Jun 2026).** Equity ex-NCI 8,750 − goodwill 15,622 − intangibles 6,480 = **−13,352**, i.e. −$76.97 a share. Both dead-company qualifiers are distorted by the same $22bn: MCap/BV reads a benign 2.0× only because acquired intangibles are carried at cost less amortisation. Marked inapplicable and said out loud.

**EV build (30 Jun 2026 primary; two variants).** Market capitalisation $17,663m (173.539m shares × $101.78) + long-term debt 11,872 + current portion 106 + lease liabilities 581 (153 + 428) + preferred 0 + non-controlling interests 605 (228 + 377 redeemable) = **EV no-cash $30,827m**. ## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth (P15).**

*Customer — PASS.* No single customer above 10% of revenue; the group serves 15.9m average monthly players across ~100 countries and the 10-K carries no customer-concentration disclosure. Largest country exposure is the US at 43% of FY2025 revenue, which is geographic, not customer, concentration.

*Supplier — FAIL on look-through.* Nominal supplier counts are irrelevant here. The effective supplier is the licensing and taxing authority in each jurisdiction: it sells the right to operate, no alternative vendor exists, its take is the single largest component of cost of sales, and it has exercised pricing power repeatedly and recently — UK statutory levy plus online slot stake limits from April 2025, financial-vulnerability checks at £150 net deposits, promotional-incentive restrictions from January 2026; an Illinois per-wager tax; India's Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act banning real-money games outright in August 2025, closing Junglee; Italian retail concession re-tenders expected late 2026/early 2027 with new concessions from January 2028, covering Sisal and Snai. Secondary concentrations point the same way: official league data mandates in the US route through a near-duopoly of data suppliers, and state market access runs through a finite set of tethered "skins".

*Alternative reading, logged.* If regulators are held not to be suppliers, supplier breadth passes on the diversity of game content, payments and data vendors, with the sports-data duopoly as a lesser concentration. The Future verdict does not depend on which reading is taken — the force tally alone is decisive. The captive-customer waiver is not invoked; it addresses customer-side failures and would not reach this one.

**Forces (P16) — four binary calls.**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | Many customers and no backward-integration threat, but switching costs are near zero — the 10-K names low switching barriers as a risk, and US sales & marketing rose 61% year-on-year in Q2 2026 while US revenue fell 6%: customer power showing up as price. |
| Supplier bargaining power | **STRONG** | The licensing state is a monopoly supplier of the operating right, takes the largest slice of gross win, and has raised its price in the UK, Illinois, Italy and Brazil while banning the product outright in India. |
| Threat of substitutes | **STRONG** | CFTC-regulated event contracts deliver the same customer job in all 50 states under a lighter tax and licensing regime; Flutter concedes it does not offer sports contracts in states where it runs a sportsbook, so rivals serve those states under different rules. |
| Threat of new entrants | **STRONG** | Barriers to entry exist but barriers to success have fallen in the adjacent prediction-market channel, where one federal registration substitutes for 31 state licences and entrants arrive with distribution already built. |

**Tally: 4 STRONG** — twice the book's stop signal of two. Future is WEAK on the tally alone.

**Moat.** Single identified source: **Brand**, with a supporting scale/cost element (FanDuel at roughly 41% of online sportsbook and 27% of iGaming gross gaming revenue where live; Sky Bet, Paddy Power, Betfair, Sportsbet and Sisal each locally dominant). Mechanism: brand recognition and parlay-pricing capability lower customer acquisition cost per player and let the group hold a wider margin than sub-scale rivals. Direction: **eroding**. The evidence is the cost of defence — US Adjusted EBITDA fell from $400m to $119m in a single quarter while US marketing rose by a third and revenue fell; a moat that requires escalating spend to hold share is not stable.

**Market growth: MIXED.** International revenue grew 10% year-on-year in Q2 2026 and Italy, Brazil and CEE are expanding. The US, which carries the equity story, is contracting on revenue with taxes rising, India was removed entirely, and FY2026 group revenue guidance was cut by $395m at the midpoint to $17.91bn.

**Future verdict: WEAK** — three independent triggers (four strong forces; breadth FAIL on the primary reading; eroding moat).
