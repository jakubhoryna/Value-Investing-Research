# TRI — Thomson Reuters Corporation: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | ADEQUATE | ADEQUATE | **FAIL** (P19 buybacks) | EXPENSIVE | **AVOID** |

Thomson Reuters is a genuinely high-quality professional-information franchise — Westlaw, Practical Law, ONESOURCE/UltraTax and Checkpoint are switching-cost, workflow-ingrained products with ~29% operating margins, ~27% FCF margins, low leverage (L/E 51%) and a controlled, long-term owner. It has been sold off ~49% in a year on the fear that generative AI erodes the value of proprietary legal/tax research, and now trades near its 52-week low. But two things keep it out of the shopping list. First, **shareholder-friendliness fails outright**: the company repurchased $1.0–3.1bn of stock every year from 2021–2025 at ~27–40× operating income / free cash flow — 4–6× the framework's conservative worth — a P19 hard strike, funded partly by monetising the legacy LSEG stake. Second, even after the crash it is **EXPENSIVE**, ~20× OI and ~20× FCF against ≤7×/≤8× thresholds. The verdict flips only on a full program-year of buyback discipline (or a halt) **and** price roughly a third of today's — the trigger band sits near $30.

## 2. Understanding

A content-and-technology company that sells subscription legal, tax/audit/accounting and compliance research-and-workflow software (plus Reuters news and residual print) to law firms, corporate legal/tax departments, accounting firms, governments and media worldwide; Toronto-HQ, USD-reporting, IFRS, single common share class controlled ~70% by the Thomson family's Woodbridge. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Operating returns are solid but not elite once the acquisition base is counted. Worst-of-four normalised-average ROCE over FY2021–25 is ~11% (Σ OI ÷ Σ CE, cash-in variant) — held down by the transition-era 2021–22 base still carrying the LSEG stake and heavy goodwill (GW+intangibles $12.7bn); excluding the non-operating LSEG investment lifts operating ROCE to ~15% (STRONG-adjacent). FCFROCE ~9–10% clears the 8% floor. OI/sh and FCF/sh compounded ~14–16%/yr; leverage is comfortable (L/E 51%). The **2018 Refinitiv divestiture** means a clean continuous record only spans ~7 years, so STRONG is additionally constrained by thin history. Verdict **ADEQUATE** — goodwill-laden ROCE ~11% worst-of-four caps below STRONG; everything else is strong.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: no single customer >10% (largest single relationship is the LSEG/Reuters news-supply contract, ~4–5% of group revenue — noted, not breaching); supplier base broad, with a growing but competitive cloud/AI-model dependency. Forces: customer power WEAK (high switching costs, no backward integration), supplier power WEAK, new entrants WEAK (content depth, brand, professional-trust barriers), **substitutes STRONG** — generative AI is a direct, structural threat to proprietary legal/tax research and the reason for the de-rating; adoption switching costs, accuracy and professional-liability needs contain it near-term but do not remove it. Tally = 1 strong force (judgment zone). Moat: switching costs + workflow ingrainedness + brand, historically stable, now **contested by AI**. Verdict **ADEQUATE**.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation PASSES: CEO Hasker's total ~$12m is far under both the 5%-of-lower(FCF,NI) hard line (~$76m) and the ~$40m absolute flag; meaningful insider/family alignment; single-class one-share-one-vote, so Woodbridge's ~70% is a logged control caveat, not a dual-class strike, and allocation has been disciplined. RPT: routine, immaterial. **Buybacks FAIL (P19 hard strike):** FY2021–25 repurchases of $1.0–3.1bn/yr executed at implied ~27–40× EV/OI and ~33–40× MCap/FCF — every year far above the 20× line and multiples of conservative worth; not below-worth. Verdict **FAIL** — P19 buyback hard strike.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- AI substitution is the central unknown: it could either hollow the research moat or, via CoCounsel/agentic products, deepen switching costs and re-accelerate growth — the market is pricing the former.
- Buyback discipline is unlikely to change while Woodbridge favours large capital returns (the May 2026 $605m return-of-capital + consolidation is the latest); the SF strike is structural, not a one-off.
- Heavy, ongoing M&A (Casetext, Pagero, SurePrep, Materia) into AI/tax — execution and price-paid risk; largely pre-profit targets, judgmental.
- Reuters News/print are structurally slower and cyclically ad-exposed; Global Print is in secular decline.
- Concentrated ~70% control limits activist pressure and outside accountability (offset by Reuters Trust Principles).

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

To AVOID→WATCH: a full program-year of buybacks at or below conservative worth (or a halt) clears the P19 strike; a BUY additionally needs price into the ~$30 band (≈ −67%) and, ideally, the AI-substitution question resolving toward moat-deepening. To a Past upgrade: sustained operating ROCE ≥15% on a stabilised post-Refinitiv base. **Next review:** FY2026 results (~Feb 2027) and the 2027 management circular (re-test the FY26 buyback table under P19); earlier on any AI-substitution inflection in legal/tax; price alert < ≈ $45.
