# HEI — HEICO Corporation: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS · Past ADEQUATE · Future STRONG · Shareholder-friendliness PASS (one P19 CAUTION flag) · Cheapness EXPENSIVE → **WATCH**.

HEICO is worth owning for a regulatory-licence moat (FAA-approved alternative jet-engine and aircraft parts, built part by part over decades) sold into a growing, fragmented aftermarket, run by a family that owns about $2.9bn of the stock and pays itself modestly. Price alone stands between here and a BUY, and the gap is enormous: roughly 38× operating income and 42× free cash flow against 7× and 8×. The verdict flips to AVOID if the acquisition engine stops earning its keep — returns on capital stepped down from 17–19% to about 13% after the $2.1bn Wencor purchase — or if OEM-bundled service contracts crowd alternative parts out of new-generation engines.

## 2. Understanding
HEICO designs, manufactures, repairs and distributes FAA-approved replacement parts for jet engines and aircraft components priced below the original manufacturers' (Flight Support Group, 70% of FY2025 sales), and makes niche electronic, microwave and electro-optical components for defence, space, aerospace and medical customers (Electronic Technologies Group, 30%); it sells to airlines, repair shops, defence primes and the US government in about 130 countries, operates as a decentralised holding of roughly 100 acquired subsidiaries (many with management retaining minority stakes), is headquartered in Hollywood, Florida, and has been run by the Mendelson family since 1990, now a $4.5bn-revenue, $36bn-market-cap company with two share classes. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 12.6% (end-of-period, with cash) and worst FCFROCE 10.5% — above the ADEQUATE line, below STRONG. The beginning-of-period reads sit at 14.9–15.5%, so the gap to STRONG is the goodwill from the 2023 Wencor deal: end-period ROCE fell from 13.4% (FY2022) to 9.5% (FY2023) and has recovered to 13.1% (FY2025), so no sustained decline is in evidence. Per-share growth is far above the floor: OI/share and FCF/share both compounded about 15% a year over the window; BV/share 17%. Leverage is moderate at L/E 85% (96% including redeemable minority interests), interest covered 7.8× by operating income. **ADEQUATE** — returns on the enlarged capital base have not yet regained the pre-2023 level.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth PASS: no customer reached 10% of sales in any of the last three years (top five 20%); no supplier concentration is disclosed and the decentralised structure makes one implausible (gap logged). Forces: customer power WEAK (fragmented operators for whom HEICO parts are a cost saving), supplier power WEAK (generic materials and components; the OEMs are competitors, not suppliers), substitutes WEAK (the OEM part costs more; OEM-bundled service contracts on new engines are the contained caveat), new entrants WEAK (each part needs its own FAA approval plus airline qualification; HEICO holds the largest independent library). Tally 0 strong. Moat: Government — accumulated part-by-part regulatory approvals; stable to strengthening as fleets age and OEM output lags. Market growth YES. **STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
Top pay (co-CEO Eric Mendelson) $13.0m in FY2025 against a $34.5m P17 line; trailing-3y $11.4m against $26.8m — clear on both reads, under the ≈$40m absolute trigger. Director fees $290–350k, within the US norm. The Mendelson group owns 7.3% of the economic shares (≈$2.9bn) and 14.7% of votes; dual class is a caveat, not control. Related parties (a son employed, a daughter's firm as customer) immaterial. Capital allocation **CAUTION**: no buybacks, dividends 4–7% of FCF, but $4.9bn of acquisitions in ten years, partly debt-funded, lowered returns from 17–19% to 13%; the Wencor multiple is undisclosed. One flag, no pair. **PASS.**
