# 6501.T — Hitachi, Ltd.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS (per-unit route, not the group sentence), Past **WEAK**, Future **ADEQUATE**, SF **PASS** with one flag, Cheapness **EXPENSIVE** — verdict **AVOID**, carried by Past.

Hitachi is an improving business priced as if the improvement were complete. A decade of divestment and reinvestment has narrowed a low-return conglomerate down to grid equipment, digital engineering and service annuities, and FY3/2026 is the record's best year. But the framework grades ten years, and on that record no defensible treatment of capital employed reaches the 10% floor. Between here and a WATCH stand three or four more years at current returns, and a price two-thirds below today's.

## 2. Understanding

**Group-level: FAIL.** No one sentence covers HVDC converter stations, core-banking systems, elevators and semiconductor metrology tools sold to grid operators, city banks, fabs and households across six industries.

**Per unit — MODEL's "more equally weighted" route: PASS.** **Energy (30%)** — differentiated, long-qualification grid hardware and nuclear service sold to utilities and states; wholly owned (Hitachi Energy); global, Europe-led; mature, supply-constrained. **Digital Systems & Services (28%)** — mission-critical IT, outsourcing and digital engineering sold to Japanese financial institutions, government and Western enterprises; wholly owned; Japan-weighted; mature-to-growing. **Mobility (12%)** — rolling stock and signalling sold to state rail operators on tenders; wholly owned; UK/Italy/Japan; mature. **Connective Industries (31%)** — a portfolio, not a unit, and the reason the group sentence fails. PASS at ~75% confidence, calibrated on BRK.B, SIE.DE and 6503.T; parameter table in Appendix D.

## 3. Quality — past record

**WEAK.** Worst-of-four normalized ROCE over FY3/2017–FY3/2026 is **8.42%** on the MODEL-conforming NIBCL (reads 8.42/8.44/9.88/9.95%) and **6.76%** on a trade-payables-only floor; the widest defensible NIBCL reaches 8.91%. Worst-of-four FCFROCE is **5.35%** (floor 4.29%). The 10% floor is missed on every combination of the two live judgment calls — not a knife edge. Trend contradicts level: ROCE_wc runs 6.31% (FY3/2019) → 1.93% (FY3/2020 impairments) → 7.58% → 11.88%, so P4 does not fire. Growth clears easily: ΔOI/sh +8.1%, ΔFCF/sh +14.3% geometric. L/E 125.9%, net cash. WEAK on level alone.

## 4. Quality — future

**ADEQUATE.** Breadth PASSES both legs: no customer-concentration disclosure appears (10% of revenue is ¥1.06tn) and no supplier approaches ¥939bn of COGS+opex. Forces: customer power **STRONG** — Mobility sells through public tenders at an 8.2% margin against the group's 12.4%, and CI's semiconductor-equipment and appliance-retail limbs face oligopsony buyers; supplier power **WEAK**; substitutes **WEAK**, though generative AI threatens part of digital engineering; new entrants **WEAK** — qualification cycles, homologation and service annuities raise both entry and success barriers. Tally 1 strong, contained at a quarter of revenue. Moat: **switching costs**, **stable** — 20–40 year equipment lives whose replacement needs re-certification and downtime, so service revenue recurs with the original supplier; not strengthening, because 8.42% shows the moat has not converted into a return. Market growth YES.

**PASS, one flag.** P17 is bounded, not measured — the yūhō would not open — but a flag needs one individual above ¥33.5bn (5% of normalized net income), twenty times the largest package ever disclosed in Japan. RPT clean: the AGM business report caps director-related transactions below 1% of each party's revenue. Capital allocation **CAUTION**: repurchases run at 17.3–27.8× across the four reads, 2.6–3.3× the trigger band, no published price criterion, on a programme enlarged 56% to ¥550bn at the record's highest price; and **GlobalLogic (2021) cost $9.5bn EV against $63.4m of pre-deal adjusted operating income — 149.8×, the company's own 37.4× forward EBITDA a hard lower bound**. Not ADVERSE: no read reaches ≈25× on all four, shares are cancelled, funding is free cash flow against net cash. One flag; no second independent negative.

## C. Calculation notes

**C1 — The operating-income line: a Japanese instance of the open MODEL item, and it bites.** Hitachi's IFRS statement of profit or loss contains **no line labelled "operating income"**. It runs Revenues − Cost of sales − SG&A = **"Adjusted operating income"**, then Other income, Other expenses, Financial income/expenses and equity-method results to **"EBIT"**, then interest to Income before income taxes. This is the **MMB.PA failure mode** (a label rule with no object) combined with the **SGO.PA definitional case** (a line that is exactly revenue − COGS − SG&A) — and it is the first non-French instance in the register, which is itself evidence that the item is not a French-nationality phenomenon.

The brief's Japan note predicted "a single unambiguous 営業利益 line". **That prediction fails on this filer.** Hitachi's "Adjusted operating income" is explicitly labelled *adjusted* and excludes impairments, restructuring, litigation and disposal gains — precisely the items §2·1A forbids adding back. Applying the definitional rule to it would be an add-back by the back door. The memo therefore computes on a **derived operating-income line = Adjusted operating income + Other income − Other expenses**, which restores every excluded item, stops short of financial income/expense and equity-method results (which belong below an operating line), and is the closest available construct to an as-reported operating income. The alternative reading — AOI as presented — lifts worst-of-four ROCE from 8.42% to 9.23% on the primary NIBCL and from 6.76% to 7.40% on the floor. **It does not change the verdict on any combination**, so Hitachi is a free calibration point, the second in a row after TRI.PA. The wedge is large in individual years (FY3/2020: ¥143,240m derived vs ¥661,883m AOI, a 4.6× gap driven by ¥570,635m of other expenses; FY3/2021: ¥798,910m vs ¥495,180m, driven by ¥476,137m of disposal gains) but averages out across ten years because Hitachi's restructuring charges and disposal gains genuinely offset.

Note the mirror-image observation the brief asked for: Hitachi is an IFRS filer, so there are no J-GAAP 特別損益 extraordinary items below the line. Instead the charges the French filers put above the line sit in "Other expenses", *below* the labelled subtotal — the same economic problem in a third presentational costume.

**C2 — Captive finance: the SIE.DE question does not arise, and it is closed.** Hitachi Capital Corporation, the group's leasing and financial-services arm, was **deconsolidated to equity-method on 3 October 2016** and Hitachi exited entirely when Hitachi Capital merged into Mitsubishi HC Capital in 2021. Within the P1 window it is consolidated for one half-year only: the Financial Services segment contributed ¥179,212m of revenue (2.0% of FY3/2017) and ¥21,336m of adjusted operating income (3.6%). Its balance sheet does affect one number — the 31 March 2016 opening balance sheet still carries the leasing book (total assets ¥12,551,005m, of which ¥1,329,974m current investments in securities and ¥2,081,520m long-term debt), which inflates the beginning-of-period capital employed for FY3/2017 only. **The symmetric-exclusion test was run**: dropping FY3/2017 entirely and running a nine-year window FY3/2018–FY3/2026 gives worst-of-four ROCE **8.44%** on the primary basis and **6.77%** on the floor — i.e. the captive-finance year moves the answer by 2bp and 1bp. No numerator/denominator inconsistency survives, no alternative basis needs logging, and unlike Siemens Financial Services **the treatment decides nothing here**. The SIE.DE open item is not extended by this name; it is answered in the negative.

**C3 — NIBCL, the burden-of-proof calls.** MODEL's NIBCL = accounts payable + accrued expenses (incl. accrued comp) + income taxes payable + deferred income/revenue. Hitachi's balance sheet maps onto this almost exactly for FY3/2019 onward: **Trade payables** + **Accrued expenses** + **Contract liabilities**. The semi-annual securities report confirms that **income taxes payable is reported inside "Accrued expenses"**, so the third MODEL component is captured rather than missing. Items deliberately **left in CE**: "Other current liabilities" (¥867,763m at FY3/2026 — undecomposed, includes provisions and other payables; burden of proof rests with the item), "Other financial liabilities" (¥317,342m — derivative and lease-related, debt-like), short-term debt and the current portion of long-term debt (always in CE by rule), and all non-current liabilities. Goodwill stays in by default (¥2,647,501m at FY3/2026).

Three bases were computed and all are reported because the pre-FY3/2019 disclosure forces a choice:
- **Primary** (used above): the MODEL definition where decomposable; for FY3/2016–FY3/2018 the aggregated "Other current liabilities" line stands in for accrued expenses + advances, which **overstates** NIBCL for those three years and therefore **flatters** ROCE there. Worst-of-four 8.42%.
- **Floor**: trade payables only, all eleven years — internally consistent, understates ROCE throughout (the BVI.PA treatment). Worst-of-four 6.76%.
- **Widest defensible**: primary plus the whole "Other current liabilities" line moved into NIBCL for every year (the MMB.PA "most aggressive defensible" sensitivity). Worst-of-four 8.83% (beginning-wc) / 8.91% (end-wc), ex-cash reads 10.49/10.55%.

**Every basis leaves worst-of-four below the 10% floor.** The gap on the widest basis is 117bp. Past WEAK is therefore robust to the NIBCL call, to the OI-line call, and to both together (widest NIBCL × AOI line = 9.67% worst, still short).

**C4 — Cross-shareholdings (政策保有株式).** The yūhō schedule could not be opened, so the balance is unmeasured. It is bounded by "Investments in securities and other financial assets (non-current)" of **¥593,559m** at 31 March 2026 (up from ¥349,567m — the increase is mostly the residual equity interests retained in divested businesses rather than new cross-holdings). That line is 3.9% of total assets. **Sensitivity**: stripping the whole line from FY3/2026 capital employed lifts ROCE_wc from 8.46% to 8.85% on the floor basis and from 11.88% to 12.67% on the primary basis. Applied across the window it would move worst-of-four by roughly 40–60bp — not enough to reach 10%. The reduction policy and the disposal record are unmeasured and are a named re-test at the FY3/2026 yūhō; they are logged as evidence inside the single §2·1C·3 test, not as an independent flag.

**C5 — Leases (IFRS 16 / P18).** IFRS 16 was adopted from FY3/2020, with right-of-use assets of ¥218,161m and lease liabilities of ¥221,832m recognised on transition — **2.2% of the FY3/2020 total assets of ¥9,930,081m**. The P18 test (undiscounted operating-lease commitments above 10% of total assets) therefore fails comfortably for the pre-FY3/2020 years and no capitalisation adjustment is made; the adoption-year artifact is immaterial. Current lease liabilities sit inside "Other financial liabilities" and non-current inside "Other non-current liabilities" and are not separately disclosed in any release; the two lines together (¥321,080m + ¥315,764m = ¥636,844m at 30 June 2026) are a hard **upper bound** on total lease liabilities and contain much that is not lease. Effect on the band is stated in F.

**C6 — Interest paid and FCF.** Hitachi presents interest paid (¥35,422m in FY3/2026) within operating activities in the Japanese practice, so no reclassification is needed and FCF = CFO − capex is levered as MODEL requires. No maintenance-capex breakout exists; all capex is subtracted, with the dark bias noted. **Tie-out**: the derived FY3/2026 FCF of ¥1,170,232m equals the company's own stated "core free cash flow" of ¥1,170.2bn exactly, which validates the capex definition (PP&E + intangibles, not PP&E alone). P20 is **not active**: capex/sales runs 4.0–5.0% across the window with no documented ramp, and the FY3/2027 guide of ¥670bn (5.7% of guided revenue) is a step up but is not disclosed as expansion-only nor accompanied by guidance to reduce.

**C7 — Latest-year CE build (FY3/2026, primary basis).** Total assets 15,041,246 − NIBCL (trade payables 1,658,755 + accrued expenses 799,334 + contract liabilities 3,054,661 = 5,512,750) = **CE_wc 9,528,496**; less cash and STI (1,323,480 + 355,304 = 1,678,784) = **CE_xc 7,849,712**.

**C8 — TBV build (30 June 2026).** Hitachi, Ltd. stockholders' equity 6,573,758 − goodwill 2,684,376 − other intangible assets 1,209,823 = **TBV 2,679,559**, i.e. ¥597.1 per diluted share. Goodwill and acquisition intangibles are 25.9% of total assets against a positive but thin tangible book — the Gate-1C acquisition audit is mandated and is run in E.

**C9 — Share count.** Per-share figures are restated to the current basis. Hitachi consolidated 5 shares into 1 on 1 October 2018 and split 1 into 5 on 1 July 2024, so pre-October-2018 counts (≈4,833m) and post-July-2024 counts (≈4,543m) are already on the same footing, while FY3/2019–FY3/2024 counts (≈930–967m) are multiplied by 5. Each year's diluted count is derived as net income attributable to Hitachi stockholders ÷ diluted EPS, and the FY3/2026 derivation (4,542.6m) ties to the disclosed 4,542,595,119. The Q1 FY3/2027 diluted weighted average (189,450 ÷ 42.21 = 4,487.8m) is used for market capitalisation and the band; the point-in-time count is lower (4,535,560,985 issued at 31 March 2026 less ~80m treasury after the Q1 purchases ≈ 4,456m), which would move the band by +0.7% — immaterial, and the higher count is the conservative choice.

**C10 — Substitution rule.** Not applied. Hitachi is not a securities-heavy holdco, an insurer or a deposit-funded lender; EV/OI and MCap/FCF are both meaningful and both are used.

**C11 — Bias check.** Tick-through of the eighteen biases and the two forms of akrasia was run before the verdict was fixed. Two were live and are recorded with their handling: **hotness** — Hitachi is up ~46% from the 52-week low and 18% in the four weeks since the guidance raise, and the temptation was to treat the FY3/2026 print as the new normal; handled by grading the ten-year record as MODEL requires and stating the flip condition arithmetically rather than narratively. **Peculiarity** — a 118-year-old conglomerate mid-transformation invites a "this one is different" reading; handled by testing Gate 0 against the register's own conglomerate precedents (BRK.B, SIE.DE, 6503.T, 005930.KS) rather than against intuition. **Consistency** was checked in the opposite direction from the usual: the register carries one DNAF name and the session was primed to expect a second, so the Gate-0 PASS was tested explicitly against the risk of manufacturing a dramatic result. No judgment here is born of a bias.

**E4 — Judgment log.**

**Verdict: CAUTION** (one flag under the §2·1C pair rule). Confidence ~70%.

Recency-weighted reading, ten lines:
1. The dominant evidence is the trailing three fiscal years and the live programme; the six repurchase-free years FY3/2017–FY3/2022 are context, and they cut in Hitachi's favour — there is no decade-long habit here, there is a four-year-old one.
2. Repurchase pricing has drifted above conservative worth every year since FY3/2023 and now sits at ~2.6–3.3× the trigger band, with no published price criterion at any point.
3. But no tranche reaches ≈25× **on all four reads**; the EV/OI reads never exceed 23.1×. On the register's own calibration this is the 6758.T (18.3×), SIKA (25.1×) and ESSITY-B pattern, not the SU.PA (24.8–26.9× on all four), WMT or RACE pattern.
4. The programme is live and freshly enlarged — ¥550bn for FY3/2027 against ¥352bn executed in FY3/2026, +56%, with ~¥400bn undrawn — and it was enlarged at the highest price in the record. That is the GEBN/RACE enlargement fact pattern and it is the strongest single argument for ADVERSE.
5. Against it: the enlargement follows a 30.7% rise in operating income per share and a 52.0% rise in free cash flow per share, so the programme grew roughly in line with the cash generating it, and it is funded from free cash flow against net cash of ¥821,919m.
6. Shares are cancelled, not warehoused; the count is down 6.1% over the window; the MSFT sterilisation test is passed cleanly.
7. **GlobalLogic at 149.8× pre-deal adjusted operating income — 37.4× on the company's own stated forward-EBITDA multiple — is a clear, sourced, single breach of the acquisition yardstick.** It cannot be argued away and it is said out loud here.
8.
