## 2. Understanding

**PASS.** Boliden mines zinc, copper, lead, nickel, gold and silver at seven owned mining areas in Sweden, Finland, Ireland and Portugal and refines its own and purchased concentrate plus recycled material at five Nordic smelters, selling exchange-priced metals to industrial customers, traders and LME warehouses worldwide — a century-old, Stockholm-headquartered, widely held SEK 94bn group.

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ten-year ROCE is **14.1%** (in a 14.1–17.2% spread) — clearing the 10% bar, missing 15%. Worst-of-four FCFROCE is **5.5%** against 8%: capex ran 16.6–19.8% of sales in 2023–25 versus ~11.5% in 2016–22, turning FCF negative in 2023. Per-share growth clears the 3% floor throughout — OI/sh +9.5%, FCF/sh +5.3%, BV/sh +10.8%, TBV/sh +11.8% geo 2016→2025. Leverage is a strength (L/E 87.4%). Returns are cyclical, not declining. **ADEQUATE**.

## 4. Quality — future

**Breadth FAIL.** Three Smelters customers at 15%/14%/13% of group external revenue — the disclosure sentence the framework treats as the test, failed three times; the third nearly doubled from 7%. Waiver refused: metal is fungible and exchange-priced, switching costs nil, buyers many. Supplier breadth fails on look-through — the effective supplier is a concentrate market whose TC/RC terms collapsed through 2024–25. **Forces: 2 strong** (customer and supplier power) — the stop signal independently. **Moat:** Government — Nordic, Irish and Portuguese permits take years and survive appeals; stable, but guards access to ore, not price. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK**.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

## C. Calculation notes

**NIBCL definition and burden-of-proof calls.** NIBCL = trade and other payables + other current liabilities (the accruals/deferred-income line, Note 30). Left **inside** capital employed in every year, and logged as such: current tax liabilities (not separable for FY2021–23 in the aggregated source, so excluded throughout for consistency), current other provisions, current derivative instruments, all short-term and current-portion debt, deferred tax liabilities and the non-current reclamation provisions. Boliden's own "capital employed" (SEK 98,412m for 2025) deducts non-interest-bearing provisions and liabilities in full and is therefore materially more generous than the framework basis (CE_wc SEK 123,209m); the framework figure is used throughout.

**Latest-year CE build (FY2025).** Total assets 143,396 − trade and other payables 14,172 − other current liabilities 6,015 = **CE_wc 123,209**; less cash and equivalents 9,237 = **CE_xc 113,972**.

**TBV build (FY2025).** BV (equity excluding NCI) 76,512 − intangible assets 3,278 = **TBV 73,234**; ÷ 283,955,454 year-end shares = SEK 257.90 per share. No goodwill is reported; goodwill deduction question does not arise.

**EV build (24 Jul 2026, on the 30 Jun 2026 balance sheet).** MCap SEK 480.40 × 283,955,454 = 136,412. Plus debt 22,722, plus NCI 24 → 159,158 (**no-cash-excess variant**); less cash 4,198 → **154,960 (all-cash-excess variant)**.

**Leases (P18).** Not triggered. Boliden states it has for many years taken a restrictive approach to lease financing and that IFRS 16 would not affect its accounting to any significant extent; lease charges paid were SEK 88m (2018) and SEK 59m (2017), orders of magnitude below the 10%-of-total-assets capitalisation trigger (SEK 5,873m in 2018). Post-adoption lease liabilities sit in "other interest-bearing liabilities" (SEK 358m at end-2025) and are carried as debt-equivalent in CE, EV and L/E.

**Maintenance-capex basis (P20): NOT activated.** Limb (iii) fails — Boliden discloses no maintenance-capex breakout anywhere in the annual report, and no defensible constructed anchor exists: capex/sales was already 11.0–17.6% across 2016–2020, so the "pre-cycle ratio" is not meaningfully below the 16.6–19.8% of 2023–25, and in mining, development capital and sustaining capital are not separable from disclosure. Limbs (i) and (ii) are arguable (the Odda expansion is disclosed as expansion spend and completes in 2026). Recorded because it is close: were P20 activated on an 11.5% 2016–22 anchor, maintenance capex would be ~SEK 10,763m, maintenance FCF ~SEK 9,923m and MCap/FCF ~13.7× — still a clear miss, with EV/OI binding the band at SEK 245–260. **The verdict does not turn on this call.**

**Other treatments.** Operating income is as reported, including the revaluation of process inventory (Boliden's own "excluding revaluation" measure, SEK 10,693m for 2025, is an alternative performance measure and is not substituted). Interest paid is already within CFO in Boliden's presentation; no IFRS reclassification required. No captive-finance arm; no financial-institution substitution. FY2016 capex derivation is stated in Appendix A.

**Bias check.** Live: **peculiarity** — a century-old Nordic miner with genuinely excellent safety and permitting culture invites a quality halo that the numbers do not support at the breadth gate; handled by testing the disclosure sentence mechanically rather than reasoning about how "diversified" a metals customer base feels. Also **miscontrast** — Boliden looks strong beside the Swedish industrials analysed this week, several of which failed on Past; that is a comparison against a weak field, not evidence of strength, and the gates were applied on absolute thresholds only. No judgment here is born of a bias.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: FAIL.** Segment note, FY2025: "Boliden's three major customers in the Smelters segment account for 15% (16), 14% (14) and 13% (7) respectively of Boliden's external revenue. Other customers each represent less than 5% (6)." Three separate breaches of the 10% line, totalling 42% of group external revenue, and the third customer's share nearly doubled year on year (7% → 13%). Captive-customer waiver tested and **refused**: refined metal is exchange-priced and fungible, switching costs are nil, buyers include traders and LME warehouses of which many exist, and the moat is not dominant. Waiver conditions (a), (b) and (c) all fail.

**Breadth — suppliers: FAIL in substance.** No single named supplier exceeds a tenth of COGS plus opex on disclosure, but the effective supplier is the seaborne and regional concentrate market that feeds the smelters, whose terms (treatment and refining charges) are set against Boliden and collapsed through 2024–25. Energy is contracted long-term and is not the binding constraint. The look-through test in P15 is what governs.

**Forces (binary).**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | Three buyers at 13–15% of group revenue each, buying a fungible exchange-priced product at zero switching cost. |
| Supplier bargaining power | **STRONG** | Smelters depend on purchased concentrate whose TC/RC terms are set by a tight concentrate market; own mines cover only part of the feed. |
| Threat of substitutes | WEAK | Zinc for galvanising and copper for conduction have no near-term substitute at scale; recycling is Boliden's own business, not a substitute. |
| Threat of new entrants | WEAK | Orebody scarcity, multi-year contested permitting in the Nordics, and multi-billion-krona capital scale. |

**Tally: 2 strong** — the book's stop signal; Future WEAK independently of the breadth result.

**Moat.** Single identified source: **Government** — mining concessions and environmental permits in Sweden, Finland, Norway, Ireland and Portugal take years, survive appeals, and cannot be replicated by a new entrant; Boliden holds a permitted, licensed footprint in jurisdictions that grant few new ones. Direction: **stable** (Kevitsa's renewed permit to 10.5 Mtonnes and the Garpenberg 4.5 Mtonne application both progressed in 2025; the new Finnish mining tax cuts the other way). The moat protects access to ore, not the price of the output.

**Market growth: MIXED.** Electrification and galvanised-steel demand support long-run zinc and copper volumes, and European supply security is a policy tailwind; against that, Boliden's own volumes are mine-plan constrained and smelter profitability is a function of concentrate-market terms rather than metal demand.
