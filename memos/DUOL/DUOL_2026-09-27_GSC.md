# DUOL — Duolingo, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future WEAK, shareholder-friendliness FAIL (capital allocation ADVERSE), cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** on three gates.

Duolingo is the world's most-used learning app and is now solidly profitable and cash-generative: $360m of FCF in FY2025, $1.31bn of cash and short-term investments, no debt. Three things stand between it and a BUY. The record is short and mostly loss-making, so returns on a cash-heavy capital base average about 1–4%. Apple collects roughly 60% of revenue and takes a fee large enough to make it a dominant supplier, while AI tools attack the product's reason to exist. And management spends cash buying stock at roughly 28× operating income to offset employee equity, with no price discipline. The verdict flips only after years of operating income on a scale the record does not yet show, a looser app-store grip, and repurchases confined to prices near conservative worth.

## 2. Understanding

Duolingo, Inc. is a Pittsburgh-headquartered, founder-controlled (dual-class) developer of a gamified, freemium mobile learning app — chiefly language courses, plus chess, math, music and the Duolingo English Test — whose revenue comes overwhelmingly from individual consumers worldwide (about 45% in the US) paying auto-renewing subscriptions collected mostly through the Apple and Google app stores, with smaller advertising, in-app-purchase and test-fee streams; founded in 2011 and public since 2021, it passed $1bn of revenue in 2025 and is deliberately trading near-term monetization for user growth in 2026. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Five usable years. Operating income was negative through FY2023 (−$60m, −$65m, −$13m) before turning to $63m and $136m. On capital employed swollen by cash, deferred-tax assets and long-dated bonds, the worst-of-four average ROCE is 1.4%; FCFROCE on the same basis is 19.7%, flattered by stock-based pay and prepaid subscriptions. FCF per share compounded from a tiny IPO-year base; operating income per share is n.m. across the loss years. Leverage is minimal: liabilities are 48% of equity and there is no debt. **WEAK** — returns sit far below the floor, and the short window would cap the gate at ADEQUATE anyway.

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth passes: the payers are millions of individuals. Supplier breadth fails: Apple processes about 60% of revenue and, even at its lowest 15% commission, costs more than a tenth of all operating costs, with no alternative iOS channel. Forces: customer power WEAK; supplier power STRONG, since Apple and Google set terms on about 83% of revenue; substitutes STRONG, from general-purpose AI tutors and translation; new entrants WEAK, given scale, brand and streak habits. Two strong forces. The moat is brand, stable for now — daily users grew 23% in Q2-26 — but monetization is under pressure. The market is growing. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Pay is restrained: the CEO takes salary only ($0.77m) and the top FY2025 total, $5.39m, uses 30% of the fiscal-year cap and 62% of the normalized one. Related-party dealings are trivial. Capital allocation is **ADVERSE**: $70m of 2026 open-market buybacks at $98–112 (about 28× EV/OI, 14× FCF), described as offsetting 2024–25 dilution, follow net-share-settlement withholding of $11m (2023), $49m (2024, ≈ $345/share) and at least $37m (2025). The programme continues with $330m left, no price criterion, and the stock three to eight times the band. Class B holds about 76% of votes. **FAIL** — capital allocation ADVERSE.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Apple and Google can change commissions or rules on about 83% of revenue at will.
- Stock-based pay near 15% of revenue in 2026 keeps dilution, and the buyback absorbing it, running.
- The user-growth pivot may not convert: bookings grew 8% in Q2-26 against 23% user growth.
- Founder voting control limits minority influence over all of the above.
