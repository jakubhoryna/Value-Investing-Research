# ZM — Zoom Communications: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future WEAK, SF FAIL, Cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID**, failing three gates independently.

Zoom is a cash machine attached to a balance sheet that does not work and a franchise being bundled away. It converts 40% of revenue to free cash and holds $7.7bn of securities against no debt, but that hoard drags worst-of-four return on capital employed to 9.9%, under the 10% floor, and management has spent $3.7bn on its own stock in three of the last four completed years at 14.8× to 89.3× EV/OI — never once inside the bargain zone. A BUY needs a smaller capital base, two strong forces neutralised, and a repurchase record that respects price. None is in prospect.

## 2. Understanding

Zoom Communications sells annual subscription seats to a cloud unified-communications platform — video meetings plus Phone, Contact Center, Rooms, Team Chat and AI Companion — billed to businesses, governments and schools direct or via resellers and to self-serve consumers by card; a Delaware-incorporated, Nasdaq-listed, dual-class software vendor running its own co-located data centres alongside rented AWS, Oracle and Azure capacity, taking 72.1% of revenue from the Americas, now a mature $4.9bn-revenue low-single-digit grower in a category its largest competitors bundle for free. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 9.9% (9.94% strict; 9.98% on the looser deferred-revenue treatment — below 10% raw on both) against the 10% ADEQUATE floor; worst-of-four FCFROCE is 21.0%. The spread is the whole story: ex-cash returns run 72–109%, with-cash returns 9.9–12.8%, because $7.7bn of marketable securities and $1.6bn of strategic investments sit inside capital employed earning nothing in operating income. Per-share growth is a COVID artifact — 83.0% geometric on OI/share across the window, but 1.3% over FY2022–FY2026, with FCF/share at 6.8%. Leverage is immaterial (L/E 21.9%). Returns are rising three years running (11.3% latest). **WEAK** — the ADEQUATE floor is missed on the deciding metric, by 0.06pp.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASSES both sides: no customer above 10% of revenue in any of FY2024–FY2026 (disclosed), and no supplier above 10% of cost, with own data centres and three interchangeable clouds. Forces: customer bargaining power **STRONG** — every seat can move at renewal to a substitute already paid for, and Zoom's answer was to give AI Companion away; supplier power **WEAK**; substitutes **STRONG** — Teams inside M365, Meet inside Workspace, Webex, plus doing-without; new entrants **WEAK**, dulled by scale. Tally 2 strong. Moat is brand plus real switching costs in Phone and Contact Center, but **eroding**: enterprise net dollar expansion 98%, 98%, 101% for FY2026/25/24, and online is down to 39.7% of revenue. Market MIXED. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation is clean: top employee $1.28m (Yuan) against 5%-of-lower(FCF, NI) tests of $95.0m and $59.1m, far under the ≈$40m absolute trigger; top director $374,860 against a $672k stop; Yuan holds 37.7% of votes outright. RPT is trivial ($219k nepotism hire, $200k/yr gift). Buybacks strike twice. Three tranche-years, none rescued: FY2023 at $89.52 → 89.3× EV/OI year, 33.4× normalized; FY2025 at $68.85 → 17.2× / 26.4×; FY2026 at $79.50 → 14.8× / 20.3×. Repetition strikes; FY2023 strikes again on the egregious-single-year test. Redemption fails all three limbs — no published worth-referenced criterion, no year at the bargain lines, no allocator turnover. **FAIL.**

## C. Calculation notes

**Latest-year capital employed (FY2026, USD m).** Total assets 11,960.4 − NIBCL 1,971.4 = **CE_wc 9,989.0**; less cash & equivalents 1,272.9 and marketable securities 6,544.0 = **CE_xc 2,172.1**.

**NIBCL build (FY2026).** Total current liabilities 1,999.2 (= accounts payable 6.3 + accrued expenses and other current liabilities 581.8 + deferred revenue current 1,411.1) − current operating-lease liability 27.8 = 1,971.4.

**Judgment calls, logged.**
1. **Deferred revenue, noncurrent stays in capital employed** ($13.2m FY2026; $175.6m cumulative across the window). NIBCL is by definition non-interest-bearing *current* liabilities, and the §2·1A default list enumerates items within current liabilities; a noncurrent item fails that test and the burden of proof sits with the item. **This call is verdict-relevant and is disclosed as such**: excluding it gives worst-of-four ROCE 9.938%; including it gives 9.977%. Both are below the 10% floor before rounding, so Past is WEAK on either treatment — the call changes the printed decimal, not the gate. Had the alternative treatment cleared the floor, this note would be the deciding disclosure of the memo.
2. **Current operating-lease liabilities stay in CE** as debt-equivalent (ASC 842 adopted FY2020; all window years are post-adoption, so no P18 capitalisation is required and no adoption-year artifact contaminates the window). FY2019, the only pre-adoption year with a balance sheet, is outside the returns window.
3. **Goodwill stays in CE.** Zoom is mildly acquisitive (Workvivo FY2024; goodwill 400.4 = 3.3% of assets), so the non-acquisitive deduction is not available.
4. **Strategic investments ($1,578.6m) stay in CE_xc.** They are equity stakes in private and public companies, not cash or short-term investments, and are not deductible under the CE_xc definition even though they generate no operating income. This is the single largest reason the with-cash and ex-cash reads diverge as far as they do.
5. **Deferred tax assets ($646.6m) stay in assets.** Arising from a valuation-allowance release, they inflate CE and depress ROCE; the framework permits no add-back.
6. **Operating income is as reported** — no add-back of the $760.8m stock-compensation charge, the $1.0bn-plus of FY2023 restructuring-era cost, or anything else. FCF is CFO less all capex, which adds stock compensation back; the ~$800m OI-to-FCF gap is therefore an accounting difference, not a cash one, and it is the reason FCFROCE (21.0%) reads more than twice ROCE (9.9%). Multiyear averaging is left to dilute the distortion, per §2·1A.
7. **Returns window is FY2020–FY2026 on identical year sets across all four reads.** Using FY2019 for the two end-of-period reads and FY2020 for the two beginning-of-period reads would make the four figures non-comparable and defeat the worst-of-four test. Sensitivity: dropping the FY2020 stub year gives worst-of-four ROCE 10.13% — above the floor. That variant is rejected (there is no principled basis for excluding an audited year with balance sheets at both ends) but is recorded because it is the one construction that changes the verdict.
8. **EV builds use the Q1 FY2027 balance sheet** (30 Apr 2026) per §4's timeliness rule, with FY2026 operating income and FCF as the latest full-year numerators.

**Bias check.** Live: **peculiarity** — a 0.06pp miss on a single metric is doing all the work at Gate 1A, and the temptation is to find a treatment that clears it. Handled by testing every treatment that could plausibly move the number (NIBCL variants, window variants) and disclosing all of them in C.1 and C.7 rather than selecting one; the verdict would be AVOID on Future and SF regardless, so nothing rests on the choice. Also live: **miscontrast** — 21.0% FCFROCE and a 40% cash margin read as excellence next to the typical WATCH candidate, and the ex-cash returns (71–109%) are genuinely exceptional; handled by holding to the worst-of-four rule, which exists precisely to stop a flattering read carrying a gate. Others: none live.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers.** PASS. Disclosed: no single customer at 10% or more of total revenue in FY2026, FY2025 or FY2024, and none at 10% of receivables at either of the last two year-ends. Corroborating shape: 4,468 customers each above $100k of trailing revenue, together 32.8% of the total; self-serve online is 39.7% of revenue. No waiver needed.

**Breadth — suppliers.** PASS. Zoom serves users from its own co-located data centres and rents from Amazon Web Services and Oracle Cloud, with Microsoft Azure for limited customer-specified managed services; no supplier is disclosed at 10% of cost of revenue plus operating expenses ($3,745m in FY2026, so the threshold is $374.5m). Three interchangeable hyperscalers plus owned capacity is the ready-alternatives condition satisfied on its merits rather than by exception.

**Four forces (binary).**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | Many small customers cannot backward-integrate, but every seat can move at renewal to a substitute the customer already pays for; Zoom's response — bundling AI Companion at no extra charge — is the price concession made visible. |
| Supplier bargaining power | WEAK | Owned data centres plus three substitutable hyperscalers; no forward-integration threat and low switching costs on infrastructure. |
| Threat of substitutes | **STRONG** | Direct (Teams inside Microsoft 365, Meet inside Google Workspace, Webex, Slack huddles), doing-without (return-to-office, telephony), and wholly-different-function (async video, chat) — the direct ones carry near-zero marginal price inside suites the customer already buys. |
| Threat of new entrants | WEAK | Real-time video infrastructure has commoditised, but barriers to *success* — enterprise compliance, reliability, channel, brand — are high and Zoom's scale dulls them; the dangerous attackers are incumbents, counted under substitutes. |

**Tally: 2 STRONG → Future WEAK by P16** independent of the moat call.

**Moat.** Source: **brand**, with a second, narrower source in **switching costs** for Zoom Phone, Contact Center and Rooms hardware deployments, where number porting, carrier contracts and installed devices make migration costly. The meetings brand — a verb, and the default in education and SMB — does not price, because the substitute is free at the margin inside a suite. Direction: **eroding**. Evidence: enterprise net dollar expansion 101% → 98% → 98% across FY2024–FY2026, disclosed as driven by lower hiring and seat downsells; online revenue share falling 42.1% → 41.0% → 39.7%; total revenue growth 4.4% in FY2026 and 5.5% in Q1 FY2027.

**Market growth.** MIXED. UCaaS and CCaaS seats grow modestly and the AI attach is early and unpriced; core meetings is a mature market with falling per-seat economics.

**Verdict: WEAK** — two strong forces, and an eroding moat, either of which is sufficient.
