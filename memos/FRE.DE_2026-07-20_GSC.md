Fresenius is a €41bn balance sheet earning €2.3bn of operating income. That is the whole story: worst-of-four normalized ROCE of **5.1%**, against a 10% ADEQUATE floor and a 15% STRONG floor, on a business whose dominant unit sells hospital care to a regulated payer that sets the price. Management's #FutureFresenius reset is real — the Fresenius Medical Care deconsolidation, the Vamed exit, net debt down to €10.3bn, EBIT margin recovering to 11.5% — but it is a repair of a decade of goodwill-funded expansion, not the creation of a high-return franchise. Two independent gates fail, and cheapness is not close: the EV/OI band binds at ≈ €6.35 against €42.63 today, because €11.9bn of debt and €0.7bn of minorities sit ahead of a €24bn equity on €2.3bn of operating income. For the verdict to flip, returns on capital would have to roughly double — which on this asset base means either a structural repricing of German DRG tariffs or a disposal of most of the capital employed. Neither is on the table.

## 2. Understanding

Fresenius SE & Co. KGaA is a Bad Homburg–headquartered German partnership limited by shares — controlled through a general partner (Fresenius Management SE) wholly owned by the Else Kröner-Fresenius-Stiftung, which also holds ~27% of the ordinary shares — operating two units: **Fresenius Helios** (60% of 2025 revenue), acute inpatient and outpatient hospital care in Germany, Spain and Latin America, paid overwhelmingly by statutory sickness funds and regional public health services rather than by patients; and **Fresenius Kabi** (38%), a manufacturer of generic injectable drugs, clinical nutrition, infusion therapies, biosimilars and infusion/transfusion devices sold to hospitals, wholesalers and group purchasing organisations worldwide — with a 29% equity-method stake in the listed dialysis company Fresenius Medical Care held as a financial investment since its 2023 deconsolidation. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **5.1%** and FCFROCE **3.4%** (4.5% on the company-disclosed maintenance-capex split) on the only internally consistent balance-sheet window the current perimeter offers — FY2024–FY2025. Every alternative read lands in the same band: the legacy group including FMC returned 5.9–8.3% ROIC on the company's own flattering definition (2017–2021), and FY2023 end-of-period ROCE was 2.7%. On the restated continuing series, EBIT before special items compounded 2.7%/yr and net income 1.7%/yr from 2021 to 2025 — both under the 3% nominal floor; BV/share is flat over four years and TBV/share is €4.24 against a €33.92 book. Leverage is the one clean line: L/E 113%, inside P5. **WEAK** — returns are half the ADEQUATE floor on every basis tested, and fewer than P1 consistent years makes STRONG unavailable regardless.

## 4. Quality — future

**Breadth FAIL.** No annual-report disclosure establishes that no customer exceeds a tenth of revenue, and looking through to the effective payer defeats the nominal count: Helios Germany's revenue arrives through the statutory health-insurance system at aG-DRG tariffs set by federal self-governance, and Helios Spain's through regional health services and concession contracts. The captive-customer waiver does not apply — the payer is not locked in, it legislates. Forces: customer power **STRONG** (price is set, not negotiated); supplier power **STRONG** (personnel is 45% of revenue at €10.1bn, priced by sectoral collective bargaining that has run ahead of tariff growth); substitutes WEAK for acute care; new entrants WEAK (hospital-plan licensing, capital intensity). Tally **2 strong** — the book's stop signal. Moat: government/licensing plus regional scale at Helios, **eroding** under the German hospital reform's Leistungsgruppen reallocation; Kabi has no identified moat beyond manufacturing scale. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

CEO Michael Sen's 2025 total was €5.1m including service cost — 0.41% of the lower of FCF (€1,469m) and net income attributable (€1,264m), against the 5% strike, and far under the ≈$40m scrutiny line. Highest-paid outside director (Wolfgang Kirsch, chairing both supervisory boards) €570k ≈ $652k aggregate, ≈$315k per seat — no strike on either the German norm or the CPI-indexed floor default. No Fresenius SE repurchases in the window (shares 563,237,277, unchanged), so P19's buyback test is not engaged; the only 2025 acquisitions were €392m of Merck-biosimilar milestone payments. Related-party dealings (Fresenius Management SE €24m; FMC service, product and lease agreements) are disclosed and proportionate. **PASS**, with one flag — a decade of retention and goodwill-funded M&A at sub-cost-of-capital returns, closed out by €1.9bn of Vamed discontinued-operations losses across 2023–25 — plus a logged KGaA/foundation control caveat and an INDETERMINATE on the 2017 Quirónsalud multiple.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- German hospital reform reallocates case volumes by Leistungsgruppen from 2027, with Helios Germany's €662m EBIT on an 8.2% margin carrying little absorption capacity.
- Personnel cost inflation set by collective bargaining is structurally decoupled from DRG tariff growth, compressing the dominant segment from both sides.
- The €2.8bn equity-method FMC stake is a non-operating asset whose income sits outside OI; a write-down or a disposal at a discount moves book value without moving operations.
- Kabi's growth vectors (Biopharma, MedTech, Nutrition) are the genuine bright spot at 16.4% EBIT margin, but at 38% of revenue they cannot carry group returns.
- Concentration of authority in the KGaA general partner means a future strategy reversal is not shareholder-checkable.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

The verdict flips only on returns: worst-of-four ROCE clearing 10% on a consistent perimeter — arithmetically requiring roughly €4.0bn of operating income on today's capital employed, or an equivalent shrinkage of the asset base through disposals. Breadth cannot flip while Helios dominates revenue; a Kabi-majority perimeter would require re-running Gate 1B at segment level. Price alone can never lift this above WATCH while both quality gates fail. **Next review:** FY2026 annual report (~March 2027), or earlier if the hospital-reform implementing regulations put quantified financial effects on Helios Germany into the public record.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: FAIL.** No annual-report sentence establishes that no customer exceeds 10% of revenue; under P15 the absence of the disclosure is itself the finding. Looking through to the effective payer confirms it: Helios Germany (€8.1bn revenue, 36% of group) is reimbursed through the statutory health-insurance system at aG-DRG tariffs negotiated centrally; Helios Spain (€5.4bn) sells substantially through regional public health services and concession contracts. Kabi's base is genuinely broad but is 38% of revenue and cannot carry the test. **Captive-customer waiver not applicable** — the concentrated payer is a legislator, not a locked-in buyer: it can and does reset prices unilaterally, which is the opposite of the waiver's premise.

**Breadth — suppliers: FAIL.** Personnel expense is €10,099m, 45% of revenue, and is priced by sectoral collective bargaining (ver.di / Marburger Bund in Germany) — a single effective supplier of the dominant input, with no ready alternative given documented nursing shortages and statutory staffing floors (PPUGV). Nominal supplier counts for goods are irrelevant against this.

| Force | Call | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | Price is legislated (aG-DRG / regional concession terms); the operator is a price-taker with no ability to pass through cost. |
| Supplier bargaining power | **STRONG** | Labour at 45% of revenue, collectively bargained above tariff growth; statutory staffing ratios remove the volume lever. |
| Threat of substitutes | WEAK | Acute inpatient care has no direct substitute; ambulatory shift is real but slow and partially captured by Helios itself. |
| Threat of new entrants | WEAK | Hospital-plan licensing, capital intensity and regional scale are genuine barriers to entry and to success. |

**Tally: 2 STRONG → the book's stop signal → Future WEAK** independently of the breadth result.

**Moat.** Source: **Government** (hospital-plan licences and concession contracts) reinforced by regional cost scale at Helios. Mechanism: a licensed bed in a planned region cannot be replicated by a new entrant, and density lets Helios spread overhead across a regional cluster. Direction: **eroding** — the German hospital reform's Leistungsgruppen system reallocates service permissions from 2027 and explicitly targets consolidation of case volumes, which puts existing licences back in play. Kabi: no identified moat among the six; manufacturing scale in generic injectables is a cost position without a barrier.

**Market growth: MIXED.** Volume demand from ageing demographics is durable; the funded price is not, and German hospital sector operating margins have been negative in aggregate for several years.
