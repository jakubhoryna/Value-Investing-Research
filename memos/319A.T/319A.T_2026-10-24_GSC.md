# 319A.T — Next Generation Technology Group Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK (4.7% ROCE); Future WEAK (no moat across a collection of small job shops); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (Advance bought clearly above worth); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past + Future + SF)**.

Next Generation Technology Group buys small Japanese manufacturers whose owners have no successor, keeps them, and runs them with a central improvement manual. It has bought about twenty since 2018, mostly with bank debt, and grew revenue from ¥6.8bn in FY2022 to ¥15.0bn in FY2025. The record is short and thin: the group has earned at most 4.7% on capital employed on the harshest reading, FY2025 maintenance free cash flow was negative, no moat runs through the businesses, and several deals were priced far above their pre-deal operating income. At ¥22,040 the group is valued at about ¥195bn, 162× FY2025 operating income: no CHEAP price exists at current earnings. A BUY would need sustained returns above 10%, positive free cash flow, deals at or below worth, and a price collapse.

## 2. Understanding

Next Generation Technology Group (株式会社技術承継機構, Tokyo; founded 2018 by its chief executive, who owns 65.5%; listed on TSE Growth in February 2025) is a holding-and-operating group that buys owner-retiring small manufacturers and never resells them, so its ¥15.0bn of FY2025 revenue (89% Japan, 9% Asia, mainly Thailand and China) comes from about twenty consolidated workshops that sell contract parts and processes — cold-forged automotive parts, precision machining, sheet metal and metal-foil work, castings, heat treatment, shot-blasting and surface lubrication, centreless grinding, brake and EV springs — and their own niche products — thin-film target materials for superconductor and battery research, automatic soldering machines, sheet and coil cutters, film winders, carbon-fibre racing parts, road-works safety equipment, railway power supplies, and used forklifts sold, rented and exported — to other manufacturers, contractors and railway operators, none of which pays 10% of revenue. **PASS.**

One reportable segment and no listed subsidiary or holding: not a group.

## 3. Quality — past record

Lens STD. On the four audited consolidated years (history-limited: no audited consolidated accounts before FY2022) worst-of-eight ROCE is 4.7% (G6 include reading, end-of-period with cash) and FCFROCE 3.8%, maintenance capex equal to all capex in every year. Operating income per share rose from ¥9.5 to ¥152.5, but FY2025 free cash flow was −¥164m (owner-director retirement allowances of ¥1.15bn paid out at acquired companies), so the ΔFCF/sh read is n.m. Liabilities are 237% of book equity, above the 200% line with no P6 case. **WEAK** — returns far below 10%, negative FCF growth read, leverage.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth is clean: the filer states that no customer reaches 10% of revenue, and no supplier concentration is disclosed. Forces: customer power STRONG (most workshops are subcontractors making parts and processes to larger manufacturers' drawings, which can re-source), supplier power WEAK, substitutes WEAK, entrants WEAK: one strong force, with no containment named. No moat runs through the group: a few companies have niche products, but no single source covers the whole. Market growth MIXED: Japanese small-batch manufacturing is flat, while semiconductors, EV and rail parts grow. **WEAK** (no moat; an uncontained customer-power force).

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

The two executive directors received at most ¥23m together a year, far below every P17 line. The chief executive's 65.5% stake, worth about ¥128bn, makes management ALIGNED; one share class, so votes follow economics. No material related-party transaction is disclosed. The acquisition stop fails: Advance (forklifts, October 2025) cost ¥1,610m against its only disclosed consolidated pre-deal operating income of ¥61m, 26.4× before any assumed debt, clearly above worth. Suntec's price was 20.1× normalised income, Alpha System's 15.8×, and two FY2025 targets' group results were never disclosed. **FAIL** at step 1 (`acquisitions`).

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- The model depends on buying owner-run companies below book with cheap regional-bank debt; higher Japanese rates or competition from funds would raise prices.
- Bargain-purchase gains (¥2.3bn in FY2025) flatter net income; operating income and cash flow are the honest measures and are far smaller.
- Owner-director retirement allowances at acquired companies (¥1.58bn still provided at June 2026) will drain cash as owners leave.
- Automotive parts makers (Taga, Toshima, Suntec) carry the EV transition and customer price pressure.
- A controlling founder decides capital allocation; minority holders cannot check it.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Up: returns on capital employed above 10% on the enlarged base for several years, positive maintenance-capex FCF, deals priced at or below 14× pre-deal operating income, and a price at which a no-cash bound exists. Down: further deals priced above worth, or a write-down of the FY2025 goodwill. Next review: FY2026 results (~February 2027) and the FY2026 annual report.
