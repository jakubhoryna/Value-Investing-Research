# BBWI — Bath & Body Works, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; Shareholder-friendliness PASS; cheapness FAIR — **AVOID**, unchanged, now on two gates.

Bath & Body Works still earns high returns on the capital its stores need, about 29% at the worst reading, and its buybacks were bought below conservative worth. That is no longer enough. Operating income per share has fallen at 7.5% a year since the spin-off, the return on capital excluding cash has fallen four years running, and two decades of buybacks have left shareholders' equity negative. Sales have now shrunk four years in a row in categories the company itself calls growing, and the new chief executive's plan sets out to win back product leadership it has lost: the brand is losing ground. At $20.42 the shares trade at 8.1 times operating income against a buy line of $14.42, so even a turn in the business would leave the price above the line.

## 2. Understanding

Bath & Body Works, Inc. designs and sells differentiated branded body care, home fragrance (candles, wallflowers), soaps and sanitizers under the Bath & Body Works and White Barn names, made largely by about 90 contract vendors (many co-located in its Ohio "Beauty Park"), to individual North American consumers who pay at 1,927 company-operated US and Canadian stores (76.6% of FY2025 sales, mostly off-mall) and online (19.1%), with franchise, licence and wholesale partners in more than 45 countries paying royalties and wholesale prices (4.3%); it is a Columbus, Ohio, Delaware corporation, the continuing entity of L Brands after the August 2021 Victoria's Secret spin-off, mature and prominent in a sales decline under a chief executive hired in May 2025. One reportable segment, so not a group. **PASS.**
