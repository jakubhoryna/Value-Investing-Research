Yokogawa owns a forty-year installed base of plant control systems in refineries, LNG trains, chemical and pharmaceutical plants, and it earns lifecycle work on that base for decades. For most of the window the returns were ordinary: 7–10% on capital employed with cash through FY3/23. They stepped up from FY3/24, when operating income nearly doubled to ¥79–84bn on a 13.6–14.9% margin. The ten-year average therefore clears the adequate floor (worst read 10.8%) but not the strong one. At ¥4,707 the stock trades at 20.1× FCF and 14.7× / 12.5× EV/OI. ## 2. Understanding
Yokogawa designs and sells distributed control and safety systems, field instruments (flow and pressure transmitters, analysers) and the engineering, upgrade and maintenance services around them, plus a small test-and-measurement instruments line. Its paying customers are operators of continuous-process plants — oil, gas and LNG, chemicals, power, pharmaceuticals, food and materials. About 27% of sales are in Japan and the rest worldwide, led by the Middle East and Africa at 19%. It is a widely held Tokyo company founded in 1915; the Control segment carries 94% of sales. **PASS**.

## 3. Quality — past record
Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **10.8%** (end-of-period, cash in CE; the others are 11.6–15.7%). Worst FCFROCE is **6.8%**. Returns ran 7–10% through FY3/23 and 13–15% since FY3/24, so there is no decline to answer for, but the strong average is not yet earned. Per share over ten years, operating income grew 8.0% a year, FCF 12.8%, and BV and TBV 8.4% each, all above the 3% floor. L/E is 51% and the company holds ¥186bn of cash against ¥34bn of debt and leases. Acquisition write-offs of about ¥34bn sit below OI by construction. **ADEQUATE** — the level falls short of 15%/8%.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth passes. The FY3/26 and FY3/24 yūhō both state no customer above 10% of sales, and no supplier concentration is disclosed. Customer power is **WEAK**: buyers are many, they cannot build control systems themselves, and a running plant rarely changes its control platform. Supplier power and substitutes are **WEAK**. New entrants are **STRONG, contained**: Supcon holds about 39% of China's control-system market (secondary), and China fell from 16.4% (FY3/23) to 11.1% (FY3/26) of Yokogawa's sales. That is one strong force. The moat is switching costs on the installed base, and it is stable. Market growth is YES: energy, LNG and AI-related demand. **ADEQUATE**.
