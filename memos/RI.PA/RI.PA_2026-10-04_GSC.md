# RI.PA — Pernod Ricard SA: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future ADEQUATE, shareholder-friendliness PASS (capital allocation CAUTION, one flag), cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past)**.

Pernod Ricard owns some of the best-known spirits brands in the world, and they still earn a 26% operating margin. The capital tied up in them, though, has never earned enough. Acquired brands and goodwill make up about half of total assets, and maturing whisky and cognac stock adds another fifth. Against that base, operating profit has returned 7.8–8.4% across the decade, and only 6.5% in FY2026. The price has fallen hard but is still not cheap on the framework: about 13× operating income and 12.5× free cash flow, because €12.7bn of debt sits underneath a €14.8bn market value. To flip the verdict, returns on the whole capital base would have to hold above 10%. The only years that got there were the FY2022–23 post-COVID peak.

## 2. Understanding
Pernod Ricard SA distils, ages, blends and markets branded spirits and champagne — Jameson, Absolut, Chivas Regal, Martell, Ballantine's, Royal Stag and Mumm, a differentiated product sold on brand — to wholesalers, retailers, on-trade outlets and travel-retail operators (who pay), as a vertically integrated producer that holds years of maturing inventory and runs its own distribution affiliates in 73 countries, selling in about 160 and headquartered in Paris, a mature, family-anchored global number two in spirits now cutting costs through a two-year US and China downturn. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
The worst-of-four ROCE is **7.8%** (end-of-period, with cash), with a range of 7.8–8.4%. Worst FCFROCE is **4.1%**. Beginning-of-period ROCE crossed 10% only in FY2022–23, then slid to 6.5% in FY2026 as profit fell and capital stayed put. OI per share compounded at 0.8% a year and FCF per share at 2.2%, both below the 3% floor; BV per share grew 2.0%. Tangible book is negative in every year. L/E is 134%, inside the flag level. **WEAK**: below the ADEQUATE return floor, with a falling recent trend.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth passes. No customer is disclosed above a tenth of sales, and the company states it has "no significant dependence on its suppliers". On forces: customers WEAK at group level, though US distributors destocked hard in FY2026; suppliers WEAK; substitutes STRONG, with moderation and cheaper formats cutting into premium spirits; entrants WEAK, because aged inventory and distribution scale are hard to replicate. That is one strong force, contained. The moat is brand. Operating margin fell only 35bp organically on a 3.9% organic sales decline, so it is **stable**, but FY2026 price/mix turned negative. Market growth is MIXED: India is growing (+7%), the US (−14%) and China (−19%) are falling. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
The CEO's FY2026 total was €5.25m, against ceilings of €59.4m (fiscal-year read) and €54.4m (3-year read). The top director fee was €181k. Both pass. The Ricard family holds 15.3% of the capital and 21.5% of the votes, and added shares in FY2026; double voting rights are a control caveat. No regulated related-party agreements are in force. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: €2.3bn of repurchases in FY2020–24 ran at roughly 19–20× EV/OI and 28–41× FCF on figures knowable at execution, and were partly debt-funded. The programme has been dormant since FY2025, but the €4.70 dividend still absorbs about all FCF at 3.7× leverage. One flag. **PASS.**
