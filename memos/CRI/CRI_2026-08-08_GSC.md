# CRI — Carter's, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS · Past **WEAK** · Future **WEAK** · Shareholder-friendliness **PASS** (one flag) · Cheapness **EXPENSIVE** → **AVOID** (Past and Future gates).

Failed by trend, not level. Worst-of-four returns run **14.8–19.0%** across the full ten-year window depending on the working-capital treatment, but operating income has fallen four years running — $497m FY2021 to **$143.9m** FY2025, −71% — and per-share operating income compounded at roughly **−8%**/yr. Still the dominant US baby-apparel brand at a 16% wholesale margin, and H1 2026 is the first encouraging half in four years: sales +6.7%, US retail comps +7.8%, adjusted operating income roughly flat. One half-year against four bad ones.

## 2. Understanding

Carter's designs and sources children's apparel — bodysuits, sleepwear and playclothes under the Carter's, OshKosh B'gosh and Skip Hop names — selling roughly half through its own US stores and website, a third wholesale to mass and online retailers, the rest internationally through Canadian and Mexican retail and third-party distribution. Atlanta-headquartered small-cap; manufactures nothing, sourcing from third-party Asian factories; one year into a CEO transition after a four-year profit decline. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Normalized average ROCE FY2016–25 is **14.8%** on the harshest working-capital treatment and **16.8%** on the most flattering, ex-cash variants higher — **worst-of-four ≈ 14.8%**. FCF return is bounded near 11% and clears. Liabilities $1,640m against $925m equity = **L/E 177%**, mostly capitalised operating leases. The failure is trend and growth: operating income fell **four consecutive years** (497 → 379 → 323 → 255 → 143.9); per-share operating income compounded at roughly **−8%**/yr against the 3% floor, net income $256m → $91.8m. **WEAK** on both limbs independently.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth is unverified and structurally tight: US Wholesale is 34.5% of sales into a short list of mass and online retailers, and the annual-report disclosure sentence was not retrieved this session — a >10%-of-revenue customer would fail it outright and is plausible. Forces: customer power **STRONG** — the mass retailers buying the wholesale line compete against it with private label; substitutes **STRONG** — private label, a large and growing secondhand market, and hand-me-downs, where garments are outgrown before they wear out, compounded by a falling US birth rate; supplier power **WEAK** (fragmented Asian contract manufacturing); new entrants **WEAK** (sourcing scale, shelf, brand). **Tally 2 strong.** Moat **Brand**, direction **eroding** — 71% fall in operating income, 4.9% US retail margin. Market growth **NO**. **WEAK.**
