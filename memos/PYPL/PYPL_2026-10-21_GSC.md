# PYPL — PayPal Holdings, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (flag pair); cheapness FAIR — **AVOID**, unchanged, now on three gates.

PayPal earns real money on a huge payments network, but the business needs the customers' own money to run. Read with the customer balances it holds as capital, the ten-year return on capital is 6.0%, and the balance sheet carries three dollars of liabilities per dollar of equity. Half of revenue goes to card networks and two processors PayPal cannot bargain with, and the account base has stopped growing. Pay for the former chief executive's first year and the repurchases of 2023–25 are two separate negatives. At $57.14 the stock is under eight times free cash flow but 11.1 times operating income on the strict reading; the price is not what fails.

## 2. Understanding

PayPal Holdings is a US-listed Delaware payments company that charges merchants volume-based fees, and consumers fees for currency conversion, instant transfers and credit, for branded checkout (PayPal, Venmo), unbranded card processing (Braintree), person-to-person transfers and stored balances, buy-now-pay-later and merchant loans, and earns interest on the customer balances it holds and on its loans, across about 200 markets with the US 57% of FY2025 revenue; it is the incumbent in online branded checkout, growing revenue 4% a year and run since 2026 by a new chief executive. One reportable segment; not a group. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

**WEAK.** Lens STD. The $40bn of customer balances are float: PayPal earns interest on the assets behind them, so they stay in capital employed. On that reading the worst-of-eight ROCE is 6.0% over ten years and 6.4% over five, below the 10% floor; FCFROCE is 7.5% (maintenance capex constructed, all-capex 7.5%). The yearly returns have risen from 5.1% to 8.2%, and there is no decline. Per-share growth is strong: operating income 18.2%, free cash flow 13.0% a year. L/E is 296% at FY2025 and 317% at June 2026, above the flag with no cap available. Read the prior way, with the balances netted, ROCE is 12.6% and leverage still fails.

## 4. Quality — future

**WEAK.** No customer reaches 10% of revenue, as the filer states. Supply is concentrated: two payment processors took 56% of FY2025 transaction expense, at least one above 10% of total costs, and behind them sit Visa and Mastercard. With the moat eroding, that concentration is a strong supplier-power force. Customer power (merchants steer, consumers multi-home) and substitutes (platform wallets, account-to-account rails) are also strong; entrants weak. Three strong forces. Active accounts sit at 439m, flat for five quarters, while volume grows 10%. Market growth YES.

## C. Calculation notes

**C.1 Lens.** FI does not fit: PayPal publishes no regulatory capital ratio for the listed group (its Luxembourg bank, PayPal (Europe) S.à r.l. et Cie, S.C.A., is supervised by the CSSF, but no consolidated ratio covers ≥ 90% of assets), and it has material non-financial activity (processing). **STD.** The STD rules for customer funds apply: customer balances count in L/E; the $39,743m of funds receivable and customer accounts is never cash; the funding of the lending activity is a debt-equivalent (C.6). Not a captive arm question: PayPal's credit products are its own product, inside its one segment. Not a group (one reportable segment; no material listed subsidiary or holding).

**C.2 Capital employed — the customer-funds call (the load-bearing judgment).** Funds payable and amounts due to customers ($40,198m at FY2025) are stored balances and funds in transit that PayPal holds for consumers and merchants. They are left in CE (not NIBCL), for three reasons: (i) the framework names gift-card float as an ambiguous item that stays in CE when material, and these are the same kind of stored customer value; (ii) PayPal earns interest on the assets underlying customer balances and books it in revenue, so the operating income the ratio uses already carries the float's earnings, and netting the float out of CE would count the income without the capital (adjustments shaping one number shape the others); (iii) an insurance activity's float is never NIBCL. Where two readings exist the harsher governs (J13). Reading B (the prior chain's: funds payable in NIBCL) gives worst-of-eight ROCE 12.6% and is printed as context; it fails Past anyway on leverage. The funds receivable and customer accounts ($38,198m at FY2025) therefore stay in CE in both cash variants.

**C.3 FCF build.** FCF = CFO − maintenance capex ± the lending-book rule. PayPal books originations and repayments of its held-for-sale loans (European and US buy-now-pay-later sold under forward-flow agreements) inside CFO; held-for-investment originations sit in investing. The held-for-sale flow beyond ±3% of the prior year-end loan book (held for investment plus held for sale) leaves FCF: FY2017 +1,148 (outflow 1,308 less 3% of 5,348); FY2018 −1,176 (inflow 1,407 less 3% of 7,712: run-off, not cash generation); FY2023 +452 (outflow 675 less 3% of 7,431); FY2024 0 (outflow 146, within 3% of 5,996); FY2025 +1,107 (outflow 1,316 less 3% of 6,963); TTM +1,243 (outflow 1,476 less 3% of 7,755 at 30 Jun 2025).
