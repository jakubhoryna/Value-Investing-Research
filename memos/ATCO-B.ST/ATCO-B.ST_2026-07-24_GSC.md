One of the highest-quality industrial franchises this model has examined — 24.6% worst-of-four return on capital through a demerger, a pandemic and a semiconductor cycle, on 83.5% liabilities-to-equity. It fails anyway, and not on price. Every krona spent on its own shares in ten years, SEK 9,955m, went to deliver stock into personnel option programmes, matched by SEK 10,841m as those options were exercised; the share count is unchanged (+0.12%) and no capital has been returned by repurchase. P19 strikes dilution sterilisation at any price, and the tranches separately breach 20× on both dual-read legs. The business needs no repair for this to flip — the option-settlement mechanism does.

## 2. Understanding

Atlas Copco AB is a Nacka-headquartered, dual-class Swedish industrial group that engineers, manufactures and services differentiated capital equipment — compressors and air treatment (45% of revenue), vacuum and abatement (22%), power tools and assembly systems (16%), portable power and flow (17%) — sold with a 38%-of-revenue aftermarket to industrial organisations (manufacturers, semiconductor fabs, contractors, utilities) in over 180 countries through a decentralised direct sales-and-service network, under the long-term influence of Investor AB (17.1% of capital, 22.4% of votes) and focused on air, vacuum, tools and power since demerging Epiroc in 2018. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized 10-year averages: ROCE **24.6%** (P2 ≥ 15%), FCFROCE **18.0%** (P2 ≥ 8%), worst basis end-of-period/with-cash in both. The post-demerger FY2019–25 sub-window is materially identical (24.1% / 17.6%), so the Epiroc perimeter break does not carry the result. Per-share growth clears the inflation floor: OI/sh +6.2% and FCF/sh +5.8% geometric p.a. over nine compounding years, BV/sh +8.4%, TBV/sh +9.0%. FY2025 is the first down year (OI/sh −10.5%) on currency and soft short-cycle demand, not trend. L/E 83.5%, far inside P5. **STRONG.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both legs: the credit-risk note states sales are dispersed with no single customer representing a significant share, and no IFRS 8 major-customer disclosure exists; component suppliers are globally distributed with no disclosed dependence. All four forces **WEAK** — hundreds of thousands of industrial buyers facing switching costs on installed equipment; fragmented suppliers against a large buyer; no functional substitute for compressed air or vacuum; entry blocked by scale, service density and installed base. Tally 0 strong. Moat source **switching costs** — 15–20 year equipment lives, proprietary parts, 38% recurring service revenue; **stable**. Market growth MIXED. **STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

CEO Vagner Rego's SEK 28.3m total recognised is 0.107% of the lower leg (FCF SEK 26,379m; 5% line SEK 1,319m); the chair's SEK 4.44m is under even the floor-default director stop. Related parties clean. Buybacks fail twice. **Strike 1 (price-independent):** the mandate exists "in order to fulfil the obligations under the performance stock option plan" and treasury shares are "held for possible delivery under the 2019–2025 personnel stock option programs" — a decade of repurchases matched by option-exercise divestments. **Strike 2:** the FY2024 and FY2025 tranches breach 20× on both dual-read legs (FY2025: EV/OI 27.2×/25.4×, MCap/FCF 34.4×/34.1×), no rescue reachable. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- The bolt-on machine is both growth engine and audit blind spot: undisclosed deal multiples leave P19's acquisition limb permanently unclearable.
- The option-hedge repurchase is structural — when shareholders withheld the 2025 purchase mandate, the board reproduced the economics through a third-party swap.
- Roughly 95% of revenue is earned outside Sweden; SEK strength cut FY2025 reported revenue 4.8% against flat organic volumes.
- At 28.6× EV/OI the multiple carries the entire return case; a de-rating to 15× implies roughly −45% with the business unchanged.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Shareholder-friendliness is the only gate between this name and WATCH, and clearing it takes two things. The sterilisation strike ends only when option obligations are settled by fresh issuance or in cash, or the swap stops making the company the buyer of its own shares. The price strike either ages out of the window or is redeemed under the v3.5.0 clause, which demands all three limbs — published worth-referenced repurchase policy, a bargain-line or refusal year, and strike-era allocator turnover. None holds today. Even on SF PASS the name enters as WATCH, not BUY: the band is roughly a quarter of the current price. Next review at the FY2026 annual report (~Mar 2027), earlier if the repurchase mechanism is restructured or the B share approaches SEK 48.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: PASS.** AR2025 note 27: "Since the Group's sales are dispersed among many customers, of whom no single customer represents a significant share of the Group's commercial risk…" No IFRS 8.34 major-customer disclosure appears in the segment note, which under that standard is affirmative evidence that no customer reaches 10% of revenue. *Caveat logged:* within Vacuum Technique (22% of group revenue) semiconductor customers are structurally concentrated; the group-level call is unaffected but the segment-level exposure is named in §7. No captive-customer waiver invoked or needed.

**Breadth — suppliers: PASS.** Components are sourced from a globally distributed base with no disclosed dependence; the group describes long-term relationships with qualified suppliers across ~50 significant direct suppliers in its sustainability reporting, none identified as material to COGS. Atlas Copco is an assembler with high in-house engineering content and is the large party in these relationships. Evidence is qualitative — logged as a data gap in A.

**Four forces (binary):**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|-------|------|-----------------|
| Customer bargaining power | **WEAK** | Hundreds of thousands of buyers in 180 countries, none material; installed equipment locks in 38% service revenue |
| Supplier bargaining power | **WEAK** | Fragmented global component sourcing against a SEK 168bn buyer; no single-source dependence disclosed |
| Threat of substitutes | **WEAK** | No functional substitute for compressed air or vacuum in manufacturing; efficiency-driven demand reduction is marginal and slow |
| Threat of new entrants | **WEAK** | Barriers to entry *and* to success: manufacturing scale, global service density, application engineering, installed base |

**Tally: 0 strong → favourable.**

**Moat.** Single identified source: **switching costs**. Compressors and vacuum pumps run 15–20 years in process-critical duty; uptime is worth more than the equipment, spares and service are proprietary, and displacing an installed unit means requalifying a production line. The mechanism shows up as 38% of revenue in service and as operating margins of 20.3% that held through the 2022–23 input-cost surge. Direction: **stable** — neither strengthening (service penetration is already high) nor eroding.

**Market growth: MIXED.** Short-cycle industrial demand was flat-to-down in FY2025 (revenue −4.8%, organic roughly flat); vacuum and semiconductor-linked demand is structurally growing but violently cyclical; energy-efficiency retrofit supports replacement. A directional call is sufficient here and does not change the tier.

**Verdict: STRONG** — breadth PASS + 0 strong forces + a durable, stable moat.
