## 1. Understanding
Novo Nordisk is a Danish pharma built on one molecule — **semaglutide** — sold as **Ozempic** (DKK 127.1bn 2025 sales) and **Wegovy** (DKK 79.1bn) across diabetes and obesity, plus a smaller Rare Disease unit. Economics are extraordinary: ~81% gross margin, ~41% operating margin, R&D ~17% of sales. The business is simple to understand; the risk is concentration in a single franchise now facing competition, patent erosion and price cuts.

## 2. Past returns — STRONG
Returns are exceptional and well above the 15% bar, though decelerating hard. ROCE (ex-cash, beginning capital) ran 82% → 104% → 106% in 2022–24 before falling to **54% in 2025**; the company's own ROIC fell 88.5% → 63.9% → 39.3%. Split-adjusted EPS roughly **tripled over a decade** (DKK ~7.5 in 2016 → 23.0 in 2025). The warning sign is recent: 2025 sales grew only +6.4% (vs +25–31% prior), operating profit was **‑0.5%** reported, OI/share was **flat (‑0.2%)**, and FCF/share **collapsed ‑58%** as capex and M&A surged. The historic record passes STRONG; the trajectory is rolling over.

## 3. Future & moat — ADEQUATE
The moat is real but contested. **Strengths:** vast GLP-1/insulin manufacturing scale (DKK 60bn PP&E capex in 2025 alone), household-name brands, an entrenched diabetes franchise, high biologics barriers. **Erosion:** Eli Lilly leads on next-gen efficacy (tirzepatide ~25.5% weight loss vs CagriSema ~23%; oral orforglipron beat Novo in diabetes trials), Lilly guides 2026 revenue >$80bn (~25% growth) while Novo guides a *decline*. Semaglutide's compound patent runs to ~2031–32 in the US/EU but is **already expiring in India, China, Canada, Brazil and Turkey** (generics launched March 2026). US list prices for Wegovy/Ozempic drop to **$675 WAC from Jan 2027** (~50%/~35% cuts), and 2025 US sales were flattered by one-off 340B/gross-to-net recognition that won't recur. Novo is fighting back (Wegovy HD approved Mar 2026, oral semaglutide, amycretin into late stage, CagriSema phase 3), but the forces trend mixed-to-unfavourable. ADEQUATE, not strong.

## 4. Stewardship — PASS (with flags)
Controlled by **Novo Holdings / the Novo Nordisk Foundation: ~28% of capital but ~77% of votes** via 10×-vote A-shares — a long-term, research-funding steward, but one that subordinates B-share votes (Flag 1). Pay is modest and performance-linked: total executive remuneration fell 38% to **DKK 193m** (≈0.15% of operating profit); new CEO Doustdar ~DKK 35m annualised. Capital allocation is the main concern: **buybacks were run at peak prices** (avg DKK 871 in 2024) versus DKK ~264 today — value-destructive in hindsight, though management cut the programme 93% in 2025 and resumed at ~DKK 264 in 2026 (Flag 2). The 2024 Catalent transfer via Novo Holdings was disclosed with fairness opinions but is a related-party item (Flag 3). Dividend discipline (~50% payout) is rational. Net: PASS.

Mechanical FCF is crushed by a once-in-a-cycle build-out (DKK 60bn PP&E + DKK 30bn Akero intangibles); normalised FCF ≈ DKK 59bn. EV/OI at 10.2× is the cleanest read and only ~46% above target. Quality gates all pass, but it is **not yet cheap** — hence WATCH, not BUY. It would need ≈ DKK 105–135 to clear the normalised cash-flow bar, or FCF must normalise upward sooner.

## 6. Bottom line
A genuinely high-quality, Foundation-stewarded franchise caught mid-transition: decelerating growth, a credible competitor ahead on efficacy, a patent cliff already underway abroad, and 2027 US price cuts pending. The drawdown is large but the price still isn't a GSC bargain. **WATCH** with the trigger band above.
