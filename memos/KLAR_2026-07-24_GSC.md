# KLAR — Klarna Group plc: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | SF | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | WEAK | WEAK | FAIL (P17 hard strikes) | EXPENSIVE (anchor n.m.) | **AVOID** |

Klarna is a genuinely large two-sided commerce network — 118m active consumers, ~960k merchants, a real Swedish banking licence funding a $10.5bn loan book with $13.0bn of retail deposits — but as an investment it fails every gate the framework runs. The audited record is a loss machine: operating losses in all three 20-F years (−$323m, −$121m, −$230m), net losses in six of the last seven years, worst-of-four normalized ROE ≈ **−7.3%** against a 10% ADEQUATE floor. The competitive position offers no shelter — substitutes and entrants are both STRONG forces at a checkout where consumer switching cost is zero. And governance decides the SF gate on its own: the CEO's FY2025 remuneration of **$45.1m** was extracted against a $273m loss year (and would be 43× the P17 line even against the sole profitable year), while the chair and two directors each breach the outside-director fee stops. Nothing here is price-fixable: the cheapness anchor is not even computable while net income is negative. What would flip it: several consecutive years of durable positive NI, a normalized comp posture post-IPO, and evidence the network can price against Affirm/PayPal without giving the margin away. That is a different company, years out.

## 2. Understanding

Klarna is a Stockholm-founded, UK-incorporated, NYSE-listed (Sep 2025) digital bank and payments network that earns transaction fees from ~960k merchants and interest plus service fees from ~118m consumers on short-term pay-later and instalment credit across 26 countries — revenue centred on Germany, the US and the UK — funded chiefly by retail deposits gathered under its Swedish banking licence, with a growing merchant-advertising side-business; a two-decade-old firm newly public after a loss-making 2019–23 US expansion. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

§2·1A substitution rule applied (deposit-funded lender): ROE replaces ROCE; FCFROCE n.m. — CFO (+$808m, +$587m, −$1,032m) swings with deposit-funded receivables growth and is economically meaningless. Audited USD record covers FY2023–25 only (flagged; STRONG unavailable) — but the direction is settled: normalized ROE −7.3% beg / −6.9% end, worst **−7.3%**; yearly −10.5% / +1.0% / −12.1%. EPS −$0.69 / +$0.01 / −$0.79 — no growth record exists. Press-sourced pre-window record confirms losses 2019–2022 (FY2022 op loss ≈ $1.65bn). Non-deposit interest-bearing debt is trivial (≈14% of equity) — irrelevant to the verdict. **WEAK** — returns are not merely below the floor; they are negative.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: PASS — no customer >10% of revenue (disclosed); no merchant >10% of GMV in any major market; funding side is an atomized retail-deposit market with Nelnet/Santander forward-flow alternatives. Forces: customer power WEAK (atomized, though enterprise merchants multi-home and extract terms); supplier power WEAK; **threat of substitutes STRONG** (cards, PayPal, bank instalments, doing-without — zero switching cost at checkout); **threat of new entrants STRONG** (Affirm, Block/Afterpay, PayPal at scale; the EU banking licence is no barrier to the US model). Tally 2 strong = the book's stop signal. Moat: a claimed two-sided network, but several BNPL networks coexist and merchants multi-home — no single dominant source. Market growth YES, and irrelevant. **WEAK.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Credit-cycle exposure: a $10.5bn consumer book of near-prime short-duration credit has never traversed a full unemployment cycle at current US scale.
- Competitive margin ceiling: Affirm/PayPal/banks cap take rates; merchants multi-home freely.
- Governance: dual-class B (10 votes, no economics) plus CEO Class C options entrench control at 7% economic ownership.
- SBC dilution treadmill ($156m FY25) against a ~$6.5bn MCap.
- Regulatory: BNPL-specific rules (UK FCA regime live 2026, EU CCD2) compress late-fee and underwriting economics.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

AVOID is structural — three gates fail independently and none is price-sensitive. Flips require, cumulatively: sustained positive NI across ≥3 years (rebuilds the ROE record toward the 10% floor), a post-IPO comp normalization (FY2026 is the first clean year after IPO-grant fair values), and force-structure evidence (durable pricing against substitutes). Next review: FY2026 20-F (~Feb–Mar 2027), informational, low priority.
