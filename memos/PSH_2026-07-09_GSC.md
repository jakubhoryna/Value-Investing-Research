PSH is a permanent-capital closed-end fund giving minority holders exposure to Bill Ackman's concentrated activist portfolio. The operating-business machinery does not apply; under the §2·1A securities-holdco substitution rule the analysis reduces to manager return, the NAV discount, and the fee — and fails on the fee. ## 2. Understanding

Pershing Square Holdings is a Guernsey-domiciled, LSE/Euronext-listed closed-ended investment company (permanent-capital vehicle, FTSE 100 constituent, part-financed by investment-grade bonds) whose public shareholders pay Pershing Square Capital Management — Ackman's New York activist manager — a 1.5% management and 16% performance fee to hold a concentrated 8–12-name portfolio of principally North American large-cap equities, and whose shares have traded persistently at a ~30–35% discount to a ~$18bn NAV since the October 2014 IPO. **PASS** — the model is comprehensible (consistent with BRK.B passing despite manager-dependence); forecastability of the return stream is low and is carried into Future.

## 3. Quality — past record

Substitution rule applied (securities-heavy holdco): net-of-fee NAV return is the levered ROE analog. Trailing NAV CAGR is 17.3% (10y) but only 10.5% (5y), on violent dispersion — 2015–18 fell four straight (−20.5/−13.5/−4.0/−0.7%) before 2019–20 (+58.1/+70.2%). Critically, **net of fees PSH has trailed the S&P 500 over 1y, 3y, 5y and since inception (12.7% vs 15.0% ITD); it beats the index only over 10y.** Raw 10y clears the ≥15% STRONG bar, but the mandatory downgrade for the sustained mid-decade decline plus the fee-driven benchmark lag put it at **ADEQUATE** (STRONG-on-raw-metric alternate logged, App. B).

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth/forces/moat don't map to a fund; assessed by analog. The durable edge is structural, not a business moat: **permanent capital** (no redemption runs — a genuine advantage over open-end peers) plus the PSCM/Ackman brand and access to activist situations — a stable Brand/access moat. Against it: total key-man dependence with no defined succession; a 12-name book with idiosyncratic, less-liquid exposures (FNMA/FMCC GSEs, HHH-holdco, SPARC, Seaport); and a 16% fee that structurally caps *net* compounding. Underlying-equity market: MIXED. Net **ADEQUATE**, key-man- and fee-caveated.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

The fee is the strike. Management 1.5% p.a. + performance 16% of NAV appreciation above a high-water mark (verified, PSH fact sheet). In substance this is manager compensation: order-of-magnitude ~$650–750m in 2025 (≈$190m mgmt + ~$480m performance; labelled inference) against ~$2.7bn net income — ~25× the ~$40m absolute scrutiny trigger and ~5× the 5%-of-NI hard-strike line; the same holds at the Ackman-personal level via his PSCM ownership. Formally PSCM is external, so the identical facts read as the dominant related-party arrangement. Either path is decisive → **FAIL**. Mitigants, real but non-curing: PSCM affiliates own ~28% outright (strong alignment); buybacks run at a ~29% discount to NAV — value-accretive, a P19 positive that never strikes.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers
- **Key-man:** the vehicle is Ackman — succession is undefined and his public profile adds idiosyncratic headline risk.
- **Fee drag:** 1.5%+16% has caused net returns to trail a zero-fee index on four of five standard windows; it is the binding constraint on shareholder value, not the portfolio.
- **Concentration / liquidity:** 12 names, several idiosyncratic or illiquid (GSEs, HHH-holdco, SPARC, Seaport) — NAV is less liquid than "large-cap" implies.
- **Discount persistence:** the ~30–35% gap is structural and has widened as often as narrowed; absent a corporate action it may never close.
- **Leverage:** modest (~20% debt/total capital) but adds beta to an already-concentrated book.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review
The verdict is structural, not price-driven — a wider discount only makes the stock cheaper to NAV without curing the SF fail. SF flips only on a fundamental fee restructuring (elimination or large permanent cut of the performance fee; the HHH-linked ~11% management-fee trim is immaterial). A control action collapsing the discount at/near NAV (US relisting, open-ending, liquidation) would realise a special-situation return — a trade to evaluate on its own terms, not a GSC verdict, and it leaves the structure fee-laden. **Next review:** FY2026 annual report (~Feb 2027), or any announced fee change or discount-affecting corporate action.
