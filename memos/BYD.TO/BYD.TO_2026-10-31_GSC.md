# BYD.TO — Boyd Group Services Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK (4.7% ROCE); Future WEAK (customer conc.); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (compensation; acquisitions also ADVERSE); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past + Future + SF)**.

Boyd is North America's largest independent collision-repair chain, built by buying hundreds of body shops that insurance companies route their claims to. The roll-up has grown sales from $1.0bn to $3.1bn in ten years, but operating income has not kept pace with the goodwill, shop equipment and leases bought to get there: the return on capital employed has fallen from about 11% to under 5%. Two insurers pay 31% of sales on agreements cancellable at short notice. In January 2026 Boyd paid $1.29bn for Joe Hudson's, a chain that earned $42.6m before interest in 2025, and the chief executive was paid $5.7m in a year the company earned $18.4m. At CAD 117.26 the stock trades at 11 times free cash flow but 44 times operating income, because lease and debt obligations of $2.0bn exceed seven years of operating income; no positive price meets the operating-income leg. A flip would need returns back above 10% on both windows, pay inside the proportionality line and acquisitions bought below worth.

## 2. Understanding

Boyd Group Services, a Winnipeg-headquartered company listed on the TSX and NYSE, operates about 1,300 company-owned collision-repair shops under the Gerber Collision & Glass, Boyd Autobody & Glass, Assured Automotive and Joe Hudson's names in 35 US states and five Canadian provinces, repairing accident-damaged cars and replacing auto glass for vehicle owners, with most of the bill paid by property-and-casualty insurers through direct-repair referral programmes (the five largest insurers pay about 54% of sales), plus third-party glass and claims-handling services for insurers (Gerber National Claim Services) and mobile ADAS calibration; it has grown mainly by buying independent and multi-shop operators. **PASS.**

The filer reports two operating segments, the United States and Canada, in the same business; Canada is about 10% of sales. Boyd is read as one business, not a group: the segments differ by geography only, and no part is a listed subsidiary or holding.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: one insurer pays about 19% of sales and a second about 12% (AIF), on direct-repair agreements usually cancellable on short notice. No strong moat carries the concentration: the insurers set rates, cycle-time and cost targets, and Boyd's margins fell in 2024–2025 while same-store sales were flat or falling. So customer power is a STRONG force. New entrants are a second STRONG force: private-equity-backed consolidators have built national chains inside the window, and a shop needs only a site, technicians and a referral programme. Suppliers and substitutes are WEAK. Two strong forces; no moat source identified; market growth MIXED. **WEAK** (customer conc.).
