# AME — AMETEK, Inc.: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding **FAIL** (one-level decomposition: both material segments are irreducible) → **DO NOT ANALYSE FURTHER**. Past, Future, shareholder-friendliness and cheapness were not graded.

AMETEK is easy to describe as a whole: a decentralised owner of niche instrument and electromechanical businesses. The difficulty is one level down. The filer reports each of its two segments as two product lines, and in each segment the two lines sell to different paying customers. In Electronic Instruments, process and analytical instruments (70%) go to process plants, laboratories, pharmaceutical and semiconductor makers. Aerospace and power instruments (30%) go to aircraft and engine makers, airlines and the military, utilities and data-centre operators. In Electromechanical, automation and engineered solutions (70%) go to equipment and medical-device makers. Aerospace (30%) sells thermal-management systems to aircraft makers and repair services to aircraft operators. Under MODEL v4.5.0 that makes both segments irreducible, and each is far above the materiality line, so Gate 0 fails. This is a parameter-driven result, not a finding of incomprehension. It flips back to a full analysis if the reporting stops mixing such lines inside a segment, or if a MODEL ruling changes how a diversified instrument segment is tested.

## 2. Understanding
**Group sentence.** AMETEK designs and makes differentiated electronic instruments and electromechanical devices. It sells them to process-industry plants, laboratories, aircraft and engine makers, airlines, utilities and equipment and medical-device makers. It is a decentralised, acquisition-built group of operating businesses headquartered in Berwyn, Pennsylvania, with about half its sales outside the US. It is a long-established NYSE company with $7.4bn of FY2025 sales, enlarged by the $5.0bn Indicor Instrumentation purchase of August 2026.

**Gate 0 test.** Both units fail the one-level decomposition.

- **EIG's sub-businesses are revenue lines the filer reports for the unit.** Process and analytical instrumentation is $3,464.3m (70.4%) and Aerospace and power $1,454.8m (29.6%) of FY2025 EIG sales. In H1 2026 the split is $1,835.6m and $750.1m (71.0% and 29.0%).
- **They differ in paying customer.** Process and analytical instruments serve "power generation, pharmaceutical manufacturing, medical and healthcare, research and development, water and waste treatment … semiconductor manufacturing … oil, gas, and petrochemical refining". The aerospace part of the second line is designed "to customer specifications" for "the world's leading commercial and military aircraft, jet engine and original equipment manufacturers and aerospace system integrators", served "primarily by direct sales engineers", plus spare parts and overhaul for commercial aerospace. The same line sells UPS and power-quality systems to utilities, grid and data-centre operators.
- **EMG's sub-businesses.** Automation and engineered solutions is $1,749.7m (70.5%) and Aerospace $732.4m (29.5%) of FY2025 EMG sales; in H1 2026 the split is $968.6m and $418.5m (69.8% and 30.2%). The first sells motion control, motors, surgical and implantable components and specialty metals to semiconductor, laboratory, medical-device and industrial equipment makers. The second sells motor-blower systems and heat exchangers for military and commercial aircraft and ground vehicles, plus third-party MRO services to aircraft operators.
- **Industry is read at the filer's segment description (v4.5.1).** EIG's description, "analytical, test and measurement instruments", may cover both of its lines as one industry. The paying customers differ in both segments anyway, and either difference is enough. Where a reading is open, the harsher governs (J13).
- **No exception applies.** These are not two sides of one marketplace, nor one product sold through different channels.

Both units are above the P22 line on all three measures, so each is a material irreducible unit. **FAIL → DNAF.** The lens test (STD on the evidence: no bank, insurer or finance part) and the group dependency check (no listed subsidiary or listed holding) were not needed for the verdict.
