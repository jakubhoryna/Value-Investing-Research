# SDZ.SW — Sandoz Group AG: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Gate | Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|------|---------------|------|--------|--------------------------|-----------|-------|
| Result | PASS | ADEQUATE | ADEQUATE | PASS | EXPENSIVE | **WATCH** |

Sandoz is the world's #1 biosimilars maker and a top-tier generics company — a real business worth owning for its biosimilar scale, leading 27-asset pipeline, and the multi-decade wave of biologic patent expiries it is built to harvest. What stands between here and a BUY is price, and the gap is enormous: after a +58.5% 2025 (the CHF line nearly doubled off its low), the stock trades at ~24–33× free cash flow and ~19–29× operating income. The framework would only call it cheap roughly two-thirds lower. **Verdict flips to BUY only on a catastrophic ~65%+ drawdown or a structural step-up in cash generation; verdict flips toward AVOID if generics price erosion overwhelms the biosimilar mix or standalone returns fail to consolidate above the 10% line on a clean (non-adjusted) basis.**

## 2. Understanding

Sandoz develops, manufactures and sells off-patent medicines — generics (70% of sales) and biosimilars (30%) — to wholesalers, pharmacies, hospitals and government payers across Europe (54%), International (24%) and North America (22%); a Swiss-domiciled AG spun off from Novartis in October 2023, listed on SIX (CHF) with a USD-reporting ADR, and the global leader in its category. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Two big caveats dominate: only ~2 years of clean standalone data exist (Oct-2023 spin), and the IFRS record (ROCE_xc 2.4–11.3%; FCFROCE_xc ~0–8.7%) was crushed in 2023–24 by separation, transformation and impairment charges now largely behind it. On a **core/adjusted** basis the picture clears the ADEQUATE bar and is improving: ROCE_xc ~14% (2024) → ~17% (2025), corroborated by the company's core ROIC of 12.3% → 14.5%; management FCFROCE_xc ~8.6% → ~12.3%; core OI/share +14%. Leverage is comfortable throughout (L/E 100–144%, net debt/core EBITDA 1.5×, BBB/Baa2). Returns are rising, not declining. **ADEQUATE** — held back from STRONG by thin standalone history and a borderline cash-return record. *(Judgment call; see Appendix C.)*

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS** with caveats: customers are broad (1bn+ patients, 100+ countries, diversified channels) though generics buyers (GPOs, government tenders) wield real pricing power; supply leans on Asian APIs (the 6-APA penicillin price-dumping episode is live), partly mitigated by biosimilar vertical integration (Slovenia, Toulouse). Forces are **MIXED**: generics is commoditised, price-eroding (−3%/yr) and rivalrous, while biosimilars enjoy higher development/manufacturing/regulatory barriers. Moat = **cost/scale plus biosimilar development & manufacturing capability**, reinforced by regulatory first-mover wins (first interchangeable denosumab, first MS biosimilar) — **strengthening in biosimilars, absent in generics**. Market growth favours the biosimilar mix (large LoE pipeline). Breadth PASS + forces MIXED + plausible, strengthening moat = **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Single class of shares, no controlling shareholder (Novartis distributed 100% at spin). Executive Committee aggregate pay ~CHF 37M (2024, ~2% of core OI), performance-linked via annual incentive + 0–200% PSUs. Related-party dealings are post-spin transitional/supply arrangements with Novartis — disclosed, arm's-length, winding down. Buybacks are essentially nil (no repricing-at-any-price issue). Dividend is conservative and rational at 27% of core net income (CHF 0.60 → 0.80), retaining cash for high-return reinvestment (capex peaking ~$1.1B in 2026); M&A is bolt-on, not empire-building. No strike. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Generics (70% of sales) faces relentless price erosion and tender/GPO buyer power; a bad cycle can swamp biosimilar gains.
- API supply dependence on Asia (6-APA penicillin dumping; tariff-driven distortions) pressures generic margins into H1 2026.
- Biosimilar economics compress as multiple entrants crowd each molecule; interchangeability advantages erode over time.
- Thin standalone track record — only ~2 clean years; reliance on "core" adjustments could mask weaker underlying cash conversion.
- US tariff/pricing-policy risk and FX translation (USD reporting, CHF listing) add earnings volatility.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

- **Toward BUY:** USD price into the high-$20s/low-$30s (trigger band), or a durable step-up in FCF (management FCF sustainably >$2B) and core OI that lifts the band.
- **Toward AVOID:** sustained ROCE_xc back below 10% on a clean basis, generics margin collapse, or a broken biosimilar launch cadence.
- **Next review:** FY2026 full-year results (~February 2027) to add standalone-history years and confirm cash conversion; sooner if price moves materially or a generic-semaglutide launch (excluded from 2026 guidance — potential upside catalyst) is confirmed.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth.** Customers: broad — generics/biosimilars sold to wholesalers, pharmacies, hospitals and government payers across 100+ countries; top 10 medicines = 33% of sales (no single-customer dependence). *Caveat:* generics purchasing concentrates in GPOs/government tenders, depressing price. Suppliers: broad commodity API base, but key intermediates (6-APA penicillins) depend on Asian (Chinese/Indian) supply — a live vulnerability (price dumping, tariffs); biosimilars increasingly vertically integrated (own drug substance: Slovenia, Toulouse). → **PASS** with flagged input/buyer caveats.

**Five forces.**
| Force | Threat | Reason |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Customer power | HIGH (generics) / MED (biosimilars) | Tenders, GPOs, payer formularies; −3%/yr price erosion |
| Supplier power | MEDIUM | Asian API dependence (6-APA); mitigated by integration |
| Substitutes | HIGH (generics) / MED-HIGH (biosimilars) | Generics are perfect substitutes; multiple biosimilars per molecule |
| New entrants | MEDIUM | Low barriers in generics; high dev/mfg/regulatory barriers in biosimilars |
| Rivalry | HIGH | Teva, Viatris, Hikma, Dr Reddy's, Indian firms; intense in both segments |
| **Overall** | **MIXED** | Tough generics offset by defensible, growing biosimilars |

**Moat.** Cost/scale + biosimilar development & manufacturing capability (global #1, 27-asset pipeline, vertical integration), reinforced by regulatory first-mover positions (first interchangeable denosumab; first/only MS biosimilar; broad launch cadence 2025). **Strengthening in biosimilars; absent in commoditised generics.** Not a wide, stable whole-company moat — concentrated in the 30% that is growing.

**Market growth.** Biosimilars: YES — large biologic loss-of-exclusivity wave (pipeline targets ~$420B originator sales; anti-VEGF ~$15B, pertuzumab ~$4.1B). Generics: flat/declining in value. Net MIXED, favouring the biosimilar mix.
