The brands are real, the balance sheet carries €4.1bn of net liquidity, and the company's own return on net operating capital is 31.5%. But on the model's capital definition returns have halved across the decade and FCF per share has compounded at **−9.4%** — while €1bn of stock was repurchased at €138.71 and €106.60. Two hard P19 strikes, each surviving every basis tested including a generous maintenance-capex read. Not a near miss: the verdict needs a decade of rebuilt cash returns, not a lower price.

## 2. Understanding

**PASS.** Beiersdorf is a Hamburg-headquartered, family-controlled (maxingvest ~51%) manufacturer of differentiated branded skin- and body-care products — NIVEA, Eucerin, Aquaphor, La Prairie, Chantecaille, Coppertone — sold to grocery, drugstore, pharmacy and e-commerce retailers who resell to consumers across Europe (44% of sales), the Americas (25%) and Africa/Asia/Australia (31%), plus a 17%-of-sales industrial adhesive-tape unit (tesa) selling to automotive and electronics manufacturers.

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized averages: **ROCE 13.5%** (end-of-period, with cash) and **FCFROCE 7.0%** — the first clears ADEQUATE but not STRONG, the second clears neither. Both decline across the window: ROCE 20.1% → 13.9% and FCFROCE 15.4% → 3.3% on beginning-of-period capital, with three-year blocks stepping down 12.1% → 8.8% → 5.1%. ΔOI/share compounds at +3.3% (just over the inflation floor); ΔFCF/share at **−9.4%**, failing P3's flat-to-positive test outright. Leverage is the one unambiguous strength: L/E 48.1%, far inside P5. **WEAK** — sustained FCFROCE decline plus negative FCF/share growth; the cause is real capital intensity (PP&E €1,046m → €2,845m), not cash drag — ex-cash ROCE fell just as far (30.9% → 18.5%).

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS** both sides: no IFRS 8.34 major-customer disclosure in the FY2025 report, and a broad chemical, packaging and contract-manufacturing supply base. Four forces: customer power **WEAK** (fragmented global retail and pharmacy base, must-stock brands, no backward integration into the brands themselves — consistent with the ULVR and NESN calls); supplier power **WEAK** (many vendors, low switching); new entrants **WEAK** (shelf access, marketing scale, Derma clinical dossiers are barriers to *success* even as DTC lowers entry); **substitutes STRONG** — private label, indie/DTC and retailer dermocosmetics at near-zero consumer switching cost, with NIVEA declining and China travel-retail off 14.9% in Q1 2026. Tally **1 strong → judgment zone**. Moat = **Brand/Ingrainedness**, **stable** (NIVEA pressured, Eucerin strengthening at +8.2% organic). Market growth **MIXED**. **ADEQUATE.** *Swing risk: call customer power STRONG on European retail concentration and the tally hits 2 → WEAK.*

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**FAIL — two P19 hard strikes.** The 2024 tranche (3,604,604 shares at €138.71) implies EV/OI of **20.7× year / 23.0× normalized** and MCap/FCF of 40.8× / 66.7× — both bases above 20× on both reads. The 2025 tranche (4,690,621 at €106.60) implies EV/OI 14.8× / 15.8× (flag) and MCap/FCF **75.8× / 47.8×** (strike). Neither reaches the ≤8×/≤7× rescue lines; both still strike on the P20 basis. With no clean execution in the window the pattern is independently a strike, and redemption fails all three limbs. P17 passes comfortably (CEO €2.41m/€13.19m against ceilings of €15.6m/€38.2m; top outside director €229.5k ≈ 1× the German chair norm). RPT clean.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- NIVEA is in organic decline, with China travel retail down 14.9% in Q1 2026.
- Capital intensity has stepped up (capex/sales 2.6% → 4.7%); if it does not revert, the FCF shortfall is structural, not cyclical.
- Working capital swung ~€430m against CFO in 2025; a €249m supply-chain financing programme flatters trade payables.
- The €4.1bn securities pile earns a financial return, not a business one, and drags every with-cash capital measure.
- Derma is the only growth engine; a stumble there removes the offset to NIVEA.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Two independent gates must move. **Past** requires FCFROCE rebuilt toward 8%+ on the worst-of-four read and FCF/share growth turned positive — multi-year, needing capex to normalize and working capital to stop consuming cash. **SF** requires the strikes to age out of the window (2034/2035) or the redemption clause satisfied in full: published worth-referenced criterion, a bargain-line or refusal year, *and* allocator turnover. The 2026 programme executes far lower but on FY2025 economics even €80 implies ~60× MCap/FCF — relief is arithmetically implausible. Next review: **FY2026 AR (~March 2027)**.

## C. Calculation notes

**FY2025 capital-employed build.** Total assets 12,758 − NIBCL 3,345 = **CE_wc 9,413**. NIBCL = trade payables 2,484 + income tax liabilities 167 + other current liabilities 148 + other current provisions (current portion) 546. **CE_xc** = 9,413 − (cash 1,200 + current securities 1,260) = **6,953**.

*NIBCL judgment call.* Other current provisions are included in full. The FY2025 Note 28 split shows the line is dominated by personnel accruals (bonuses, vacation, long-service) and marketing/selling accruals (returns, rebates) — unambiguously accrued expenses. Litigation and miscellaneous provisions (~€229m current, 1.8% of assets) are the genuinely ambiguous residual; the burden-of-proof rule would leave them in CE. **Sensitivity:** excluding the whole other-current-provisions line raises CE_wc to €9,959m and lowers FY2025 end-basis ROCE from 14.0% to 13.3% — not verdict-determinative in either direction.

*Non-current securities stay in CE on both variants.* CE_xc deducts cash and short-term investments only, per §2·1A. Beiersdorf's €1,845m of non-current securities is excess liquidity in substance (the company includes it in disclosed "net liquidity" of €4,088m), so ex-cash ROCE is understated by construction — noted, not adjusted, because adjusting it would flatter the gate the record already fails.

*Goodwill retained in CE.* Beiersdorf is acquisitive within the window (tesa 2018 €87m; Coppertone 2019 €452m; Chantecaille 2022 €545m; €115m in 2024), so the non-acquisitive deduction is unavailable.

**FY2025 TBV build.** BV 8,601 − intangible assets 822 = **TBV 7,779**. Intangibles are 9.6% of BV, so BV ≫ TBV does not hold and the Gate-1C acquisition audit is **not** mechanically triggered. Noted regardless: FY2024 carried a goodwill impairment at Chantecaille (disclosed in the FY2025 depreciation commentary) — the 2022 purchase has already been written down in part.

**FY2025 EV build (both variants).** MCap = €77.44 × 218,523,759 = **16,922**. Debt = non-current financial liabilities 117 + other current financial liabilities 164 = **281** (includes lease liabilities €77m current, short-term bank loans €23m, derivatives €17m; IFRS 16 in force throughout 2019–2025 so leases are already capitalised). Preferred nil; NCI 17.
- **No cash excess (conservative):** 16,922 + 281 + 17 = **17,220** → EV/OI **13.0×**
- **All cash & STI excess:** 17,220 − 2,460 = **14,760** → **11.2×**
- **All cash & all securities excess (most flattering):** 17,220 − 4,305 = **12,915** → **9.8×**

All three fail P8. The conservative variant binds for the band.

**Leases.** IFRS 16 adopted FY2019 (the Ten-Year Overview footnotes the +€64m EBITDA and +€177m PP&E artifact). For FY2016–FY2018, undisclosed operating-lease commitments were checked against P18: the balance-sheet artifact at adoption (+€177m PP&E on €8,871m of FY2018 assets ≈ 2.0%) is an order of magnitude below the 10%-of-assets capitalisation trigger, so no pre-adoption capitalisation was performed. Post-adoption lease principal repayments (€64–77m p.a.) sit in financing and are not deducted from FCF — this flatters FCF from 2019 onward by roughly €0.30/share, which works *against* the WEAK verdict and is therefore not a concern.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customer.** No IFRS 8.34 major-customer disclosure appears anywhere in the FY2025 report or segment note. IFRS 8.34 compels disclosure of any customer at or above 10% of revenue; its absence is the evidence that none exists. **PASS.** Noted for completeness: tesa (17% of sales) has genuine customer concentration in automotive and electronics — the FY2025 management report cites "inventory reductions by major customers" — but the multi-unit rule takes the dominant unit's call, and Consumer is 83% of sales. The Deutsche Bank supply-chain financing programme (€188m of participating-supplier liabilities, plus €61m via Itaú in Brazil) is a working-capital arrangement, not a supplier-dependency: participation is at supplier discretion and Beiersdorf bears no cost. Effective-supplier look-through finds no securitisation-style or single-ecosystem chokepoint. **PASS.**
