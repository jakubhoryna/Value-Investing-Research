## 2. Understanding

EA is a 44-year-old Redwood City developer-publisher of differentiated, licence-anchored interactive entertainment — sports annuities (EA SPORTS FC, Madden, F1) plus owned IP (Battlefield, The Sims, Apex Legends) — monetised as full-game sales, live-service in-game content and subscriptions, paid for by consumers transacting overwhelmingly through four platform channel-owners (Sony, Microsoft, Apple, Google) who are its contractual direct customers; a vertically integrated Nasdaq-listed prominent incumbent (~$7.5bn revenue), currently under a definitive take-private agreement. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four 10y normalized ROCE **14.7%** (end-of-period, with-cash) — comfortably over the 10% ADEQUATE line, just under the 15% STRONG bar; worst FCFROCE **19.9%**, excellent. But the shape matters: ROCE ran 16–27% through FY2020, then stepped down to 11–15% after the FY2021–22 acquisition spree ballooned capital employed; stable since, so no forced downgrade, but the step-down is real. ΔFCF/sh +7.9%/yr clears the floor; ΔOI/sh only +1.8%/yr (FY2026 endpoint depressed by merger fees; +5.0% through FY2025). L/E 94%, clean. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth **FAIL, sourced**: direct sales to Sony ≈39% and Microsoft ≈16% of FY2026 net revenue — both far over P15. Captive-customer waiver refused: concentration is structural (console duopoly), but both gatekeepers demonstrably backward-integrate (first-party studios; Microsoft–Activision) and EA bears the switching costs, not they. Forces: customer power **STRONG** (platform take-rates, rule-setting); supplier power **STRONG** (sports licensors are bilateral monopolies — NFL exclusivity, FIFA's exit demand); substitutes WEAK (Ultimate Team lock-in); entrants WEAK (licences + AAA scale). Tally 2 strong — the book's stop signal. Moat real — dominant source brand (EA SPORTS survived the FIFA split), stable — but it cannot cure a breadth FAIL. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK.**
