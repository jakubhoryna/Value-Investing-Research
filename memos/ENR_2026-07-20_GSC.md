## 1. Verdict

Siemens Energy is a genuinely improving business at the centre of a real electrification cycle: FY2025 orders €58.9bn, backlog €138bn, Grid Technologies and Gas Services both compounding, and an FY2026 outlook raised in May to ~€4bn net income and ~€8bn free cash flow pre tax. None of that is what the framework asks. The record it must be judged on — five clean post-spin fiscal years — produces cumulative operating income of **+€86m** against roughly €138bn of cumulative capital employed. Two of the four business areas earned that; Siemens Gamesa destroyed most of it. Nothing between here and a BUY is a price question: the model needs a decade-long return record this company does not yet possess, and it needs a repurchase policy that is not written to fund employee share awards. Both are multi-year repairs, and the shares trade at roughly ten times the level at which the arithmetic would even begin to engage.

## 2. Understanding

**PASS.** Siemens Energy AG is a Munich-headquartered German stock corporation, demerged from Siemens AG in September 2020 and now a DAX-40 constituent with ~103,000 employees, that engineers, manufactures, installs and services heavy energy equipment through four business areas of comparable size — gas and steam turbines plus their multi-decade service agreements (Gas Services, €12.2bn revenue), high-voltage transformers, switchgear and HVDC transmission systems (Grid Technologies, €11.3bn), industrial compression, electrification and steam plant (Transformation of Industry, €5.7bn), and wholly-owned Siemens Gamesa's onshore and offshore wind turbines and their service (€10.4bn) — selling under long-cycle, largely fixed-price project and service contracts to organisations that pay: electric utilities, independent power producers, transmission system operators, oil, gas and process-industry majors and wind-farm developers, with revenue split EMEA 53% / Americas 31% (US 22%) / Asia-Australia 17%.

## 3. Quality — past record

Five fiscal years only (FY2021–FY2025; the FY2020 closing balance sheet anchors beginning-of-period CE), so P1 is unmet and STRONG is unavailable by construction. It is not the binding constraint. Σ OI over the window is €86m; **worst-of-four normalized average ROCE is 0.1%**, identical to one decimal on all four bases — nowhere near P3's 10%. Worst-of-four FCFROCE is 5.7%, under P2's 8%. The shape is a deep trough (FY2023 ROCE −10.4%, the Siemens Gamesa quality crisis) and a sharp two-year recovery to 8.4%. OI/share crosses zero, so its geometric mean is n.m.; BV/share compounded at **−13.6%**, far below the 3% nominal floor. L/E 446% breaches P5 with no P6 conditions, though the ratio is dominated by €22.3bn of customer contract liabilities rather than debt — the group holds €8.0bn net cash. **WEAK**: the returns test fails by two orders of magnitude, not a margin.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS** on both legs: no IFRS 8.34 major-customer disclosure appears in Note 26, and no single supplier plausibly approaches 10% of the ~€36.9bn COGS-plus-opex base. Forces tally **three strong**. Customer power STRONG — competitively tendered, largely fixed-price long-cycle contracts, with the FY2021–23 losses as direct evidence of who sets terms. Supplier power STRONG — the annual report names capacity constraints, extended lead times, supplier-insolvency risk and "the lengthy process of developing and qualifying alternative suppliers, given the high complexity of our products." Substitutes STRONG at group level on Siemens Gamesa's weight (26% of segment revenue, −€1,364m profit before special items), against WEAK at Grid Technologies and in the Gas Services aftermarket. New entrants WEAK — installed base, certification and reference-plant requirements are real barriers outside China. The one identified moat is **switching costs** on the Gas Services installed-fleet service book (13.0% segment margin vs ~5.5% group), stable; Grid Technologies' lead-time advantage is a capacity position, not a barrier, and Siemens Gamesa has no moat. Market growth **YES**. **WEAK** — three strong forces, past P16's two-force stop, with a moat covering under a third of the business.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**FAIL — two independent P19 hard strikes.** First, dilution-sterilisation at any price: the company states the treasury shares bought under its buybacks "may be exclusively used for the purpose of issuing shares to employees and members of the Company's Executive Board... in the framework of share-based compensation or employee share programs." Second, the FY2025 tranche (2,030,920 shares, €170m, average €83.70) implies **32.2× EV/OI on the tranche year and 203.9× on the trailing-3y normalized read** — above 20× on both reads of the same basis. The FY2024 tranche is rescued below-worth at 4.2× EV/OI and neither flags nor strikes. P17 is clean: CEO Bruch's €6.605m awarded/due is 0.39% of net income against the 5% line and far under the indexed absolute trigger; chair Kaeser's €504,500 clears the director stop. RPTs are immaterial (0.6% of revenue; no executive transactions). Logged, not counted: the €13m Early Exit Component advanced to the Executive Board in FY2025 immediately after the federal counter-guarantee was terminated early.

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Siemens Gamesa remains loss-making (−€1,364m FY2025) with a €36bn backlog of contracts partly written in the loss-making era; further warranty or quality provisions would reopen the FY2023 wound.
- Roughly €27bn of contract liabilities funds the balance sheet — an order-intake slowdown reverses that cash flow violently, and FY2025 free cash flow flatters the underlying business for exactly this reason.
- Fixed-price long-cycle contracts in a tariff- and input-cost-volatile environment carry the same execution risk that produced FY2021–23; the €138bn backlog is a risk register as much as an asset.
- The in-flight €2bn buyback (€6bn authorised through FY2028) is being executed at 50×-plus EV/OI on the last completed year, which on current evidence adds a second, larger P19 strike at the next review.
- Demand concentration in grid and gas is policy- and data-centre-driven; a capex pause at hyperscalers or a change in permitting regimes would hit orders before it hits revenue.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

The verdict flips only on the record, not the price. Past moves off WEAK when the window carries enough profitable years to lift the worst-of-four normalized ROCE above 10% — mechanically, roughly four to five more years at FY2025-or-better returns, with the FY2023 trough rolling out of the window around FY2033. Shareholder-friendliness cannot clear while the repurchase authorisation is written for employee-share servicing; the P19 redemption clause requires all three of a published worth-referenced repurchase policy, a completed programme-year executed at or below the bargain lines, and turnover of the strike-era allocators — none of which is in evidence. Future would move to ADEQUATE if Siemens Gamesa were sold, closed or turned durably profitable, taking the substitutes call with it. Next review: the FY2026 annual report (~December 2026), the first clean post-turnaround year, which also carries the completed FY2026 buyback for a fresh P19 dual-read; earlier only on a Siemens Gamesa disposal or a fresh charge.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customer.** Question: does any single customer exceed a tenth of revenue? Evidence: Note 26 Segment information contains no IFRS 8.34 major-customer disclosure, which under IFRS is affirmatively required where a customer reaches 10%. Revenue is spread across EMEA 53% / Americas 31% / Asia-Australia 17% and four business areas with distinct buyer sets. **PASS.** Caveat recorded, not waived: individual projects are very large (backlog €138bn, 3.5× revenue) and transmission-system operators are few per geography, so project-level rather than customer-level concentration is real.

**Breadth — supplier.** Question: does any single supplier exceed a tenth of COGS + opex (≈ €36.9bn; the 10% line is ≈ €3.7bn)? No supplier of that scale is plausible for a business buying forgings, castings, electrical steel, cable, electronics and logistics across thousands of vendors, and none is disclosed. **PASS on the size test.** The qualitative failure — that qualified alternatives are not ready to serve — is carried into the supplier force call rather than counted here, per P15's structure. No captive-customer waiver used.

**Four forces (binary; segment-level calls, aggregated on materiality).**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | Sophisticated utilities, TSOs and IPPs procure by competitive tender on long-cycle, largely fixed-price terms; the FY2021–23 project losses are the evidence of who sets price and risk allocation. | Supplier bargaining power | **STRONG** | The annual report names capacity constraints, shortages, extended delivery times, supplier-insolvency risk and "the lengthy process of developing and qualifying alternative suppliers, given the high complexity of our products" — i.e. high input-side switching costs and few qualified sources for specialist forgings, electrical steel and high-voltage components. |
| Threat of substitutes | **STRONG** | Group call carried by Siemens Gamesa (26% of segment revenue, −€1,364m): solar-plus-storage has been out-competing wind on cost and auction volume, and the "doing-without" form bites as subsidy regimes shift. **Tally: 3 strong** — past P16's two-force stop signal. Future **WEAK** on the forces alone, before the moat question.

**Moat.** Single identified source: **switching costs**, and only in Gas Services — long-term service agreements on an installed turbine fleet, where the OEM holds the parts, the data and the outage-planning relationship, and re-qualification of a third-party servicer carries warranty and availability risk. Mechanism evidenced by the margin gap: Gas Services profit before special items 13.0% of segment revenue versus ~5.5% group operating margin. Direction **stable**. Grid Technologies' current advantage is a lead-time and capacity position in a shortage — real and lucrative today, but capacity is being added globally and it is not a barrier. Transformation of Industry: no identified moat. Siemens Gamesa: no moat, and the FY2021–23 record is what no-moat commodity manufacturing under fixed-price contracts looks like. A moat covering under a third of the business does not carry the tiering.

**Market growth. YES** — electrification, grid replacement and expansion, data-centre load and gas as firming capacity; FY2025 orders +19% comparable, backlog €138bn. Recorded with the guard §2·1B requires: market growth is the last and weakest of the four tools, and it is precisely the part of this case that is loudest.

**Future verdict: WEAK** (≥ 2 strong forces; moat confined to one of four units).
