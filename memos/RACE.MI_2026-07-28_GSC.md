Ferrari owns the best operating record in this register's automotive cohort: units capped near 13,600 while operating margin widened from 19.2% to 29.5% in ten years, on a brand capital cannot rebuild. What fails is not quality but the price management pays for its own shares. FY2025 repurchases imply 35.0x EV/OI and 51.6x MCap/FCF on the tranche year, 39.5x and 68.2x normalized; FY2024 was worse on every leg. Both bases breach 20x on both reads — a hard strike — and all three v3.5.0 redemption limbs fail. Compensation, related parties and dividends are clean; this is a one-test failure, and the new EUR 3.5bn 2026–30 authorization makes it a recurring one.

## 2. Understanding

Ferrari N.V. is a Dutch-incorporated, Italian-operating small-volume manufacturer that designs, builds and sells roughly 13,600 highly differentiated luxury sports cars a year, plus personalization options, spare parts and a captive US finance book, to affluent individual clients through 181 independent authorized dealers across EMEA, the Americas and Asia, while monetizing the same brand through Formula 1 sponsorship, its share of F1 commercial revenue, and lifestyle licensing. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ten-year averages: ROCE 23.6% and FCFROCE 12.7%, both on end-of-period with-cash CE, against P2 bars of 15% and 8%. Per-share growth compounds at 15.8%/yr for OI and 9.4%/yr for FCF; BV/sh at 32.7%/yr off a near-zero post-separation base; TBV/sh crosses from negative to EUR 8.32 (geometric mean n.m.). L/E is 146%, inside P5 and down from 431%. ROCE_wc fell FY2018–20, but OI rose in two of those years — denominator growth from the e-Building ramp, not return erosion, and FY2024 set a window high. **STRONG.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both sides: no customer above 10% is disclosed (dealer base of 181, no single-customer sentence), and no supplier approaches a tenth of the EUR 4.1bn cost base. Forces — customers WEAK (multi-year allocation waiting lists; clients compete for cars); suppliers **STRONG** (the 20-F states most key components are single-sourced, alternatives generally not qualified, several without long-term agreements); substitutes WEAK (rivals exist but deliver less to this buyer; losing allocation standing is a real switching cost); new entrants WEAK (entry is cheap, success is not — 78 years of racing provenance). Tally 1 strong, contained: components are a modest share of a ~52% gross margin. Moat: **brand**, single-sourced, stable — deliberate scarcity plus rising personalization mix. Market growth YES for the global UHNW cohort, though Greater China revenue fell from EUR 584m to EUR 491m across FY2023–25. One strong force caps this at **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Compensation is clean on the v3.6.0 dual-read: CEO total EUR 10.90m against a year line of EUR 70.3m and a normalized line of EUR 53.2m, and far below the ~USD 40m indexed absolute trigger; the highest-paid outside director takes EUR 110,816, a fraction of any norm. Related-party costs are EUR 21.1m on EUR 4,129.8m — 0.51%, itemized. Dividends are rational and rising. The failure is P19: FY2025 repurchases at EUR 406.63 imply 35.0x/39.5x EV/OI and 51.6x/68.2x MCap/FCF; FY2024 implies 39.3x/47.0x and 77.5x/91.4x. Both bases breach 20x on both reads — **hard strike**, no rescue (bargain lines are 7x/8x), no redemption. Exor's 32.3% voting stake via loyalty shares is a logged caveat, not a second strike. **FAIL.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Repurchase policy is the whole verdict: a EUR 3.5bn programme running 2026–30 at these multiples re-strikes P19 annually.
- Volumes are capped by design, so growth rests on mix, price and personalization continuing to rise — a demand-quality assumption.

- The electric transition risks the sensory content of a moat that is emotional, not technical.
- Single-sourced components with unqualified alternatives turn a supplier failure into a production stoppage.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: PASS.** No single-customer disclosure appears anywhere in the FY2025 20-F, including Note 31 Entity-Wide Disclosures where IFRS 8.34 would require it; per P15 the absence of the sentence indicates the absence of the condition. Structurally corroborated: sales run through 181 independent authorized dealers and 195 points of sale across EMEA, the Americas, Greater China and the rest of APAC, with the largest single country (the United States) at EUR 1,981m, 27.7% of revenue — geography, not a customer. Look-through check: no effective single buyer sits behind the dealer network; end demand is thousands of individual clients, and the allocation model means Ferrari, not the dealer, controls who receives cars.

**Breadth — suppliers: PASS, with the ready-alternatives exception unavailable.** No supplier is named or quantified. Reasoning from what the business must buy, per the book's method: aluminium and precious metals (palladium, rhodium) from global commodity markets; transmissions, brakes, driving-safety and navigation systems, electrical and electronic parts, castings and tyres from specialist automotive vendors; engines, chassis and final assembly in-house at Maranello. Against a FY2025 cost base of EUR 4,129.8m, a 10% supplier would represent over EUR 410m of spend — implausible for a 13,640-unit manufacturer sourcing across that many categories, and no risk factor hints at one. The threshold therefore passes. What does not survive is the exception clause: the 20-F states that most key components are purchased from single-source suppliers, that alternative sources are generally not qualified, and that long-term agreements are absent for a number of suppliers — so concentration is not held in check by ready alternatives. That finding is carried into the supplier-power force call rather than the breadth call.

**Forces (binary, P16):**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | WEAK | Multi-year allocation waiting lists, clients compete for cars and personalization is priced by Ferrari; no dealer or client can demand terms. |
| Supplier bargaining power | **STRONG** | Company's own disclosure: most key components single-sourced, alternatives generally not qualified, several suppliers without long-term agreements — Ferrari's switching costs are high by admission. |
| Threat of substitutes | WEAK | Direct rivals exist but deliver materially less to this buyer (resale, provenance, allocation standing); doing-without is real but the cohort is growing; no wholly-different-function substitute reproduces the object. |
| Threat of new entrants | WEAK | Barriers to entry are modest — EV supercar startups exist — but barriers to success are severe: 78 years of racing provenance and continuous F1 participation cannot be bought. |

**Tally: 1 strong, contained.** Containment rests on component spend being a modest share of a ~52% gross margin and on no single supplier being material to total spend; a supplier extracting better terms compresses margin at the edges rather than threatening the franchise. The alternative call — supplier power WEAK, on the grounds that Ferrari's pricing power lets it absorb any input increase and that bespoke suppliers to a prestige account have limited practical leverage — would give 0 strong forces and lift Future to STRONG. It is rejected because Marshall's test is the switching-cost limb, and Ferrari's filing answers that limb against itself. The choice does not affect the verdict (SF FAIL decides) but is logged for repeatability, and it does affect the P21 ADD-band eligibility should the name ever be held.

**Moat: brand, single identified source, stable.** Mechanism: an eight-decade racing record converted into artificial scarcity — Ferrari deliberately caps supply below demand, which sustains resale values, which sustains order books, which permits price and personalization increases without volume. Evidence of the mechanism working: units fell 0.8% in FY2025 while car and parts revenue rose 4.8% and operating margin reached 29.5%, up from 19.2% in FY2016. Direction: stable rather than strengthening — the personalization and mix engine is still gaining, but the electric transition puts the sensory content of the brand at genuine risk, and China has weakened.

**Market growth: YES, qualified.** The global UHNW population continues to expand, and Ferrari's addressable pool grows with it while its own volume is capped by choice. Qualification: Mainland China, Hong Kong and Taiwan revenue fell from EUR 584m (FY2023) to EUR 540m (FY2024) to EUR 491m (FY2025), a three-year contraction now under 7% of group revenue.

**Verdict: ADEQUATE.** Breadth passes both sides, exactly one force is strong and contained, and the moat is real, single-sourced and stable. STRONG is unavailable with a strong force on the board.
