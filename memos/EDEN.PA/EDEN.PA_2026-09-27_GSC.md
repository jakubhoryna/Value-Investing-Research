# EDEN.PA — Edenred SE: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; SF PASS (one flag — capital-allocation CAUTION); price FAIR — verdict **AVOID** on Past and Future.

Edenred owns a real two-sided network — over a million employer and fleet clients, 60 million users, two million merchants — with operating income per share compounding 11.7% a year and a buyback now executing below the bargain lines. Quality, not price, blocks a BUY: counting the client float it holds, the business earns 7% on capital employed on the worst reading, and statute is repricing the merchant side in Brazil and Italy while Brazil's open-loop mandate lowers the entry barrier. Price is near but moot — spot sits 17% above the all-cash bound. WATCH requires Past to clear (in practice only a MODEL-mode float treatment; the alternate reading is ≈23%) and Future to return to at most one strong force, with SF surviving the TMH re-test.

## 2. Understanding

Edenred SE — demerged from Accor in 2010, Paris-listed and French-headquartered, €3.0bn 2025 revenue — runs a two-sided prepaid payment network (meal and food vouchers and cards, other employee-benefit and engagement programmes, fleet fuel, toll and EV-charging cards) in which over one million employer and fleet clients pay issuance and service fees, over two million affiliated merchants pay redemption commissions, and the client float held between loading and redemption earns interest, across 44 countries with Brazil and Italy its largest markets (39% of 2025 operating revenue). **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 6.97% and FCFROCE 7.15% (end-of-period, cash-inclusive); cash-exclusive reads reach only 9.6–10.7%. One call decides it: €6.1bn of funds to be redeemed and €0.8bn of client funds owed by the PayTech arm stay in capital employed — client money is neither a payable nor deferred income, and the burden of proof sits with the item; deducting both reads ≈23% (App. C). Returns are stable-to-rising (end-of-period ROCE_wc 6.6% → 9.3%); per-share growth is excellent (ΔOI/sh +11.7%, ΔFCF/sh +13.7% geometric); book value is negative (ΔBV, L/E n.m.); net debt is 1.2× FCF and OI covers net finance costs 5.0×. **WEAK** — the level, not the trend.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth PASS: no customer exceeds 10% of operating revenue; inputs are labour and IT, with card schemes as ready alternatives. Forces: customer power **STRONG** — merchants have won statutory fee caps in Italy (since September 2025) and Brazil (since end-February 2026), together 39% of operating revenue; supplier power WEAK; substitutes WEAK — the tax wedge still makes benefits beat cash pay; new entrants **STRONG** — Brazil's decree forces large issuers onto open-loop networks and bans exclusivity, turning acceptance into shared rails. Two strong. Moat: network density, single source, eroding as acceptance is legislated open. Market growth YES (FY2025 like-for-like operating revenue +6.2%; new SME clients up over 10% in H1 2026). **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

Pay is proportionate: CEO Bertrand Dumazy's ≈€5.9m FY2025 total sits far below 5% of the lower of FCF and NI on both reads (€26.1m fiscal, €21.6m trailing-3y); directors earn ≤≈€0.15m a seat; no related-party concern found (auditors' special report outstanding). Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: the live €600m cancellation programme buys at ≈5–7× FCF and OI, cash-funded, but without a published price rule; the 2024 tranche ran at 11–14×, and Reward Gateway (2023) cost ≈25× even its favourable annualised EBIT. The competition-law record is logged, not scored (App. E). **PASS**, one flag.
