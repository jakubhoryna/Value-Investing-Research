A well-run insurance holding that fails the one gate its industry cannot argue with. Across Hannover Re, the retail books and industrial lines, customers renew annually, face negligible switching costs, and can back-integrate into captives or higher retentions; alternative capital substitutes for the reinsurance half more cheaply. The record agrees: ROE ran 6.6–10.4% in FY2016–20 and 16.6–19.7% in FY2023–25 on much the same assets. That is a price cycle, not a franchise. Execution and the balance sheet are sound; nothing flips this short of evidence that pricing power has turned structural.

## 2. Understanding

Talanx AG is a Hanover-headquartered German insurance holding company, 76.7% owned by the mutual HDI V.a.G. and itself holding ~50% of listed Hannover Rück SE, selling property/casualty and life/health insurance and reinsurance — plus asset management on ~€144bn of own-risk investments — for premiums paid by retail policyholders in Germany, Poland, Türkiye and Latin America, by industrial corporates and their brokers, and by ceding insurers worldwide, with revenue split USA 26% / Germany 14% / rest of Europe 13% / LatAm 12% / Asia 11% / CEE 10% / UK 9%. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Substitution rule applied (§2·1A, insurer): ROE replaces ROCE, FCFROCE n.m., leverage on interest-bearing debt. Worst of four normalized averages **11.4%** (Σ NI ÷ Σ equity, end-of-period, FY2016–25); the others read 11.5–12.1%. Clears P3's 10%, well short of P2's 15% — and the dispersion is the point: 8.5% through the soft cycle, 18.1% through the hard one. Per-share growth beats the 3% floor (EPS +11.6% p.a., BV/sh +4.3% p.a.). Leverage conservative: debt €7,899m ÷ equity ex-NCI = 58.7%. **ADEQUATE.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS** (millions of policyholders, thousands of cedants, no disclosed concentration; capital and retrocession from deep markets). Forces: customer power **STRONG** (annual renewal, negligible switching costs, demonstrated backward integration into captives and retentions); supplier power WEAK (deep capital and retro markets; broker oligopoly logged); substitutes **STRONG** (self-insurance, cedant retentions, and cheaper alternative capital, all low-friction to adopt); new entrants WEAK (licensing, ratings, scale). ## C. Calculation notes

**Substitution-rule treatment (§2·1A).** Talanx is an insurer whose balance sheet is float- and securities-dominated; the CE machinery (total assets − NIBCL) would treat €137bn of insurance-contract liabilities as either capital employed or non-interest-bearing current liabilities, and neither is economically true. Applied: ROE for ROCE, FCFROCE n.m., leverage on non-deposit interest-bearing debt, per-share growth on EPS and BV/sh, cheapness anchored on MCap/BV. Precedents: BRK.B (2026-06-24), INVE-B (2026-07-09), PSH (2026-07-09).

**TBV build (FY2025).** BV (equity ex-NCI) 13,468 − goodwill 1,578 − other intangibles 668 = **11,222**; ÷ 258.229m shares = **€43.46/sh**. The AR key-figures line "carrying amount per share excluding goodwill" of €46.05 deducts goodwill only and is not the framework's TBV.

**EV build (FY2025), both variants.** MCap 29,102 + total debt 7,899 + NCI 7,429 = 44,430 (no cash excess); − cash & equivalents 5,260 = **39,170** (all-cash-excess, lower). Total debt is the AR2025 debt-analysis total: subordinated bonds Talanx AG 1,247 + Hannover Rück SE 2,736 + subordinated loans 24 + notes payable Talanx AG 2,493 + Hannover Rück SE 748 + mortgage/infrastructure loans 651. **Both EV variants are marked informational, not gating:** NCI enters at €7,429m book while the ~50% Hannover Re minority carries a materially larger market value, so EV is understated and EV/OI (7.4×/8.4×) is flattered. No EV-based verdict is drawn.

**Goodwill.** Retained in book value throughout; Talanx is acquisitive (Liberty LatAm 2023; Warta/TU Europa minority buy-outs 2025), so no deduction is warranted beyond the TBV line.

**Leases.** No pre-IFRS 16 P18 capitalisation performed: Talanx adopted IFRS 16 in FY2019, and for FY2016–18 the operating-lease commitment disclosure was not reached within budget. Immaterial — the substitution rule removes CE, EV and the L/E ratio from the gating path, and lease PV on a €160bn balance sheet cannot move the ROE or MCap/BV readings.

**Bias check.** Live: **peculiarity** — the substitution rule makes this name's arithmetic unlike the register's operating companies, which invites the sense that ordinary standards need not apply; handled by deriving the MCap/BV anchor from P7's own required owner-earnings yield rather than inventing a threshold. Also watched: **miscontrast** — 2.16× book looks cheap against the register's US names but is not cheap against an 11.4% through-cycle ROE. **Cleverness** was checked on the backvaluing read and it did not carry the verdict; the verdict is a forces call. ## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth (P15).**
- *Customer:* PASS. No customer above 10% of revenue; none disclosed, and the business is structurally atomised — 45,207 registered shareholders is not the relevant number, but ~29,500 employees serving retail books in Germany, Poland (Warta, #2 in P&C), Türkiye and Latin America, industrial-lines corporates, and worldwide cedants is. Geographic revenue split: USA 26%, Germany 14%, rest of Europe 13%, LatAm 12%, Asia/Australia 11%, CEE 10%, UK 9%, Africa 1%.
- *Supplier:* PASS on available evidence, with a logged gap. The economic suppliers are capital, retrocession, and distribution. Capital and retro are deep markets with ready alternatives. Reinsurance brokerage is an oligopoly (three houses) but bears on the whole industry rather than on Talanx specifically.

**Forces (P16) — binary calls.**

| Force | Call | One-line reason |
|-------|------|-----------------|
| Customer bargaining power | **STRONG** | Annual renewal, negligible switching costs, and demonstrated backward integration — corporate captives, and cedants simply retaining more risk when reinsurance prices rise. |
| Supplier bargaining power | WEAK | Capital and retrocession are deep, substitutable markets; Talanx is itself a capacity supplier. Broker oligopoly logged as an industry-wide, not name-specific, drag. |

**Tally: 2 strong — the book's stop signal. Future WEAK per P16.** The memo is completed in full per §2.

**Moat.** Single identified source: **cost**, at Hannover Rück SE, whose administrative expense ratio has been the lowest among the major reinsurers for many years and is a genuine, mechanically-grounded advantage. Direction: **stable**. It is not group-wide — Retail Germany, Retail International and Industrial Lines have brand and ingrainedness positions (HDI in Germany, Warta in Poland, bancassurance channel ingrainedness) but no dominant moat. Per §2·1B a dominant moat is evidence for weak force calls, not an override; the cyclical ROE record is evidence against dominance, so no force call is revised.

**Market growth.** YES — insurance revenue €40.8bn (FY2022) → €58.4bn (FY2025), with structural growth in CEE and Latin America and a rising CSM (€9.6bn → €11.5bn).
