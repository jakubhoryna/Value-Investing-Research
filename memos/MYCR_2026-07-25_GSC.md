This is the best business the queue has turned up in weeks, and it fails anyway. Mycronic earns 22–36% on capital employed on every basis tested, grows operating income per share at 9.5% and free cash flow per share at 21%, carries SEK 2.0bn of net cash against SEK 3.8bn of total liabilities, and owns something genuinely rare — an effective monopoly in mask writers for display photomasks, where the customer set has no alternative supplier. Compensation is modest (CEO at 2.0% of free cash flow), related-party dealing is nil, and the customer base is disclosed as having no 10% name. What kills it is 99,448 shares. Mycronic repurchased them in 2025 for the stated purpose of delivering LTIP 2025 awards, and the AGM separately approved "hedging measures pertaining to LTIP 2025 by transfer of the company's own shares." P19 strikes dilution-sterilisation at any price, without a materiality threshold, and this register has already applied that rule to Atlas Copco, Epiroc, Hexagon, Boliden and Straumann on identical Nordic LTI-hedging facts. Consistency is not optional. The strike stands; the disproportion between the offence (SEK 19m, 0.05% of shares) and the verdict is a framework question, not an analysis question — flagged in §8.

## 2. Understanding

**PASS.** Mycronic is a Täby-based Swedish capital-equipment manufacturer that designs and sells high-precision production machines and their aftermarket service to the electronics industry, through four divisions: Pattern Generators (laser mask writers sold to the world's photomask makers and flat-panel display manufacturers — a near-sole-supplier position), PCB Assembly Solutions and High Volume (surface-mount pick-and-place, jet dispensing and inspection systems sold to electronics manufacturing services firms and OEMs), and Global Technologies (die bonding, plasma surface treatment, PCB test); 99% of sales are outside Sweden, 46% in China, and Bure Equity holds 22.8%.

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **22.3%**, worst-of-four FCFROCE **17.7%** across FY2019–FY2025 — comfortably through P2's 15%/8% STRONG lines on all four bases (best read 35.6%/28.4%). No sustained decline: the trough was FY2022 at 18.3% ROCE_wc, and FY2024 hit 32.6%. Per-share growth clears the 3% floor on every leg — ΔOI/sh +9.5%, ΔFCF/sh +21.1%, ΔBV/sh +15.6%, ΔTBV/sh +14.3% geometric. Leverage is trivial: L/E **53.6%**, with SEK 348m of interest-bearing debt against SEK 2,323m of cash. **ADEQUATE — and the cap is the reason, not the record.** Only 8 balance-sheet years (FY2018–FY2025) could be sourced; FY2016–FY2017 reports are not retrievable from Mycronic's site, so the window is 7 fiscal years against P1's 10 and §2·1A makes STRONG unavailable. Named re-test in §8.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS** both sides: the FY2025 segment note states zero customers exceed 10% of group sales; no supplier is disclosed above the ~SEK 550m line implied by 10% of cost of goods sold (structural argument, named re-test). Forces: customer power **WEAK** — Pattern Generators' buyer set is tiny but has nowhere else to go, and the assembly divisions sell to a broad EMS base; supplier power **WEAK**; substitutes **STRONG** — display makers can and do defer mask-writer replacement for years, which is the visible cause of Pattern Generators' lumpiness, and the assembly divisions face ASMPT, Fuji, Panasonic and Hanwha head-on; new entrants **WEAK** — the mask-writer market is too small to support a second developer of the optics. Tally 1 strong, contained. Moat: **switching costs**, **strengthening** in Pattern Generators (first Prexision 8000 Evo order booked in 2025; advanced packaging and multi-layer PCB complexity widen the base); no moat in assembly. Market growth **YES**. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

**FAIL — P19 hard strike.** During 2025 Mycronic repurchased 99,448 of its own shares "for delivery to participants in the LTIP 2025 long-term incentive program"; the AGM approved hedging of LTIP 2025 by transfer of own shares; treasury holdings stand at 562,876 shares (0.3% of capital, SEK 85m paid); repurchases recur in each of FY2021–FY2025 (SEK 16–26m per year) against share-based compensation of SEK 43m in 2025. ## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- China is 46% of sales; export controls, domestic-substitution policy or a display-capex pause would hit Pattern Generators and High Volume simultaneously.
- Pattern Generators revenue is order-lumpy by construction — a handful of multi-year mask-writer contracts drive the division, so any single year's operating income is a poor guide to run-rate earnings.
- The moat is confined to one division; PCB Assembly Solutions and Global Technologies compete on merit against far larger Asian rivals and earn materially thinner gross margins.
- Acquisition cadence is accelerating — five deals in 2025 alone — and goodwill has grown to 37% of book value, with purchase price allocations still preliminary.
- The LTIP repurchase pattern is small today but the board is seeking renewed authority for up to 5% of shares; scale-up without a worth-referenced price policy would compound the existing strike.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

The SF strike is the only thing between Mycronic and a WATCH row, and clearing it is a governance act, not a price move. Under the v3.5.0 redemption clause a hard strike downgrades to a standing flag only on all three limbs — a published, worth-referenced repurchase policy; a bargain-line execution year or a documented refusal year; and allocator turnover or a controlling-holder policy change. Mycronic satisfies none today: LTIP hedging is schedule-referenced by definition, there has never been a discretionary tranche to price, and management and Bure are continuous. The cleaner route is behavioural — LTIP awards settled by new issue or cash rather than by repurchased shares would remove the strike at source. Separately, **the disproportion here is worth a MODEL-mode conversation**: P19's sterilisation clause carries no de-minimis threshold, so a SEK 19m LTI hedge on a SEK 67bn company fails the same gate as a multi-billion EPS-flattering programme. That is the framework working as written, and possibly not as intended — but it is not a licence to soften this verdict. **Next review: FY2026 annual report (~Apr 2027)**, which re-tests P19, the acquisition cohort, and rolls a further year into the window. Independently, recovering the FY2016–FY2018 annual reports would extend the record to 10 years and lift the Past cap to STRONG on the current arithmetic.
