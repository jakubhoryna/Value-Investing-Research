# UNH — UnitedHealth Group Incorporated: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future WEAK, shareholder-friendliness FAIL and cheapness EXPENSIVE give **AVOID** (Past, Future and SF).

UnitedHealth is a national US health insurer joined to Optum's pharmacy-benefit, care-delivery and health-IT businesses, most of which sell to the insurer itself. It earned a steady 13–15% on capital until FY2023. Since then, Medicare Advantage rate and risk-model changes and medical-cost inflation have cut J1 operating income from $28.3bn to $13.3bn. One payer, the federal government through CMS, supplies 44% of revenue and sets its own prices. Management kept buying back stock at prices clearly above worth while returns fell, and it paid 38× pre-deal operating income for LHC Group. At $418.09 the stock trades at 36× EV/OI, and no CHEAP price exists on FY2025 figures. WATCH would need the ROCE decline to break, CMS concentration to be carried by evidenced pricing power, and repurchases to stop above worth.

## 2. Understanding
**Sentence.** UnitedHealth Group, a Minnetonka, Minnesota company with $447.6bn of FY2025 revenue, sells health-insurance cover (UnitedHealthcare) paid for mainly by the US federal government through CMS (44% of revenue, chiefly Medicare Advantage), by employers, by state Medicaid programmes and by individuals. Its Optum businesses run a pharmacy-benefit manager and pharmacies (Optum Rx), employ or contract physicians to deliver care (Optum Health), and sell data, software, claims and payment services (Optum Insight). ## C. Calculation notes

**C.1 Lens and units.**
- **FI fails.** UNH is the parent of state-licensed insurers, but it publishes no group capital ratio covering ≥ 90% of consolidated assets, and Optum's care, pharmacy and IT businesses are material non-financial parts. It is read on STD under v4.6.0 G2 cases (a) and (b):
  - insurance-contract liabilities (medical costs payable and unearned revenues) count in L/E and are never NIBCL;
  - the investment portfolio is never cash, and it is read on the G6 readings in Gate 1A;
  - investment income is financial income, out of OI at Gate 2.
- **Captive arm (R2).** Optum Bank (HSAs, CDs, payment and provider loans) is not a captive financier of group products; it serves consumers and organisations. It sits inside Optum Health in FY2025 and Optum Insight from 2026. It is immaterial on its own (deposits $15.5bn, 5.0% of total assets), so it is consolidated with the unit. Its deposits are debt-equivalents under STD (C.6).

## D. Future-performance worksheet

The integrated unit (P22 merge, C.1) is graded once, on its largest part by revenue, UnitedHealthcare (342.7bn of 443.6bn unaffiliated revenue). Optum Rx is named where a call runs the other way.

**Breadth.**
- **Customer.** Premium revenue from CMS was 44% of consolidated revenue in FY2025 and 40% in FY2024 and FY2023. That is concentration, flagged `customer conc.`. CMS is one payer under common control. State Medicaid programmes are separate payers; none is disclosed above 10%.
- **Supplier.** Care providers and pharmacies are fragmented, and no supplier above 10% of COGS + opex is disclosed. Optum Rx's drug purchasing names no single supplier; this runs the other way for Optum Rx only, and no figure is printed. No licence underpins more than 10% of revenue. No supplier concentration.

**Strong-moat test (R7) for the CMS concentration.**
1. *Dominance:* UNH is a national insurer with $352.2bn of FY2025 premiums. Its share against the nearest competitor is not printed in the documents read, so dominance is not evidenced.
2. *Stable or strengthening:* not met. UnitedHealthcare's operating margin fell from 5.8% (FY2023) to 5.2% (FY2024) and 2.7% (FY2025). The FY2025 10-K attributes the fall primarily to Medicare Advantage funding reductions and elevated medical-cost trend.
3. *Pricing power over CMS:* not met. CMS sets benchmark rates and the risk-adjustment model by rulemaking; the V28 phase-in and the 2024–2025 margin loss are the evidence.

The test fails, so the concentration is a STRONG customer-power force.

**Forces: 1 STRONG.**

| Force | Call | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | STRONG | CMS pays 44% and sets prices unilaterally; states (Medicaid) and large employers also negotiate hard |
| Suppliers | WEAK | Fragmented providers and pharmacies; Optum owns part of its own supply of care |
| Substitutes | WEAK | Coverage cannot be done without; traditional Medicare is the alternative, and MA penetration rose over the window |
| Entrants | WEAK | State licences, statutory capital, national provider networks and scale in claims data |

**Containment (P16): none.** The force reaches the moat's source, the MA premium pool. Returns did not hold against it: ROCE_wc incl. fell from 15.2% (FY2022) to 6.5% (FY2025). Future is **WEAK**.

**Moat:** cost and scale, plus ingrainedness of the national provider network and the Optum Rx–UnitedHealthcare integration. Direction: eroding.
- The FY2024–FY2025 margin collapse.
- Open DOJ criminal and civil investigations into Medicare billing (carried from v1, 2026-07-03; the FY2025 10-K lists the DOJ among its investigating agencies).
- A court-appointed monitor on the Amedisys divestitures (carried from v1, 2026-07-03).

**Market growth:** YES. UnitedHealthcare revenue grew 16% in FY2025, and Medicare enrolment ages upward.

**Decline cause (J6):** competitive and regulatory erosion (CMS rates and risk models, medical-cost trend), plus acquired capital (goodwill and intangibles rose from $52.8bn to $131.0bn over the window). Erosion means the moat is eroding.

**Optum Bank funding (P15 funding).** The funding test applies to a lender or deposit-taker that is the whole entity or a unit. Optum Bank is neither (C.1), so it is not run. Its insured HSA deposits would not form a channel.
