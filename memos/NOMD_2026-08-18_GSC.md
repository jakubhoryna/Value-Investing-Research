# NOMD — Nomad Foods Limited: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK; Future WEAK; shareholder-friendliness PASS with one flag; cheapness FAIR — **AVOID**, on two gates.

Europe's largest pure-play frozen-food group, assembled by debt-funded acquisition since 2015 — and the assembly is the problem. Some €4.6bn of goodwill and brands sit against €325m of operating income, so €5.3bn of capital employed earns 6.1–7.2% however measured. The equity looks cheap at 0.57× book and 9.9× free cash flow; that is the trap the returns test exists to catch, because the cheapness is a function of €2.31bn of debt rather than a mispriced franchise. Nothing flips on price. A flip needs the returns engine rebuilt — a far smaller invested base, or operating income some 60% higher — and the volume decline stopped.

## 2. Understanding

Nomad Foods Limited is a British Virgin Islands–incorporated, UK-headquartered holding company that manufactures and sells branded frozen savoury food and ice cream — fish and seafood, vegetables, poultry, ready meals, pizza, bakery — under owned household brands (Birds Eye, iglo, Findus, Ledo, Frikom, Aunt Bessie's, Goodfella's, La Cocinera) to grocery retailers, discounters and foodservice distributors, who pay, across roughly fifteen European markets, running nineteen owned factories and a cold chain, and standing as the region's largest pure-play frozen group after a decade of acquisitions. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized average ROCE over FY2021–FY2025 is **6.58%**, the best of the four 7.24% — every variant far below the 10% floor. FCFROCE worst-of-four is **3.78%**. There is no rescuing trend: annual ROCE_wc ran 6.09–7.20% with no direction, and FY2025 was the low. Per-share growth is thin and buyback-assisted — ΔOI/share +3.0% geometric, ΔFCF/share **−2.7%**, ΔBV/share +6.5%; tangible book has been negative throughout, so ΔTBV/share is n.m. Leverage passes: L/E 153.7%, inside the 200% flag. **WEAK** — the level, not the trend, decides it. Nine years of headline "record" adjusted EBITDA never converted into a decent return on the capital that bought it.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes narrowly on both sides: top-ten retail customers were 32% of FY2023 revenue with none disclosed above a tenth, and inputs are broadly sourced — though MSC-certified whitefish is an effective-supplier concentration the company says it could not replace at short notice. Forces: **customer power STRONG** (consolidated grocery, retailer-controlled shelf, annual trade terms, no long-term contracts — Q2-2026 volumes −5.9% on retailer disruption); supplier power WEAK; **substitutes STRONG** (private label on the same shelf, chilled and ambient alternatives, nil switching cost); new entrants WEAK. Tally **2 strong → WEAK**. Moat: brand plus cold-chain scale, single-sourced and **eroding** — 2025 sell-out +0.4% against organic revenue −1.9%, gross margin −250bp, 2026 guided −2% to −5%. Market growth MIXED.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 **INDETERMINATE**: this foreign private issuer discloses pay only in aggregate, so neither proportionality read runs; it forces a named re-test but cannot pair. Related-party dealing is confined to founder-entity advisory fees — €0.8m payable, 0.17% of operating expense — immaterial, though it costs both co-Chairmen their independence; caveat logged. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**, one flag: FY2025 returned €287m against €144m of free cash flow, part-funded by €193m of new borrowing weeks before a €75.9m refinancing loss. Derived tranches priced at 11.6× and 12.7× EV/OI — above the disciplined line, far from ≈25×; buybacks have since slowed sharply. **PASS.**

- New CEO from November 2025 with a value-creation plan not yet quantified — execution risk both ways.
- Whitefish quota and Russian-water sanction exposure is a live input-cost and availability risk.
