Fastenal is worth owning for a distribution network that earned 30–40% on capital in every year of the decade without leverage, because its vending machines and on-site stores sit inside customers' plants and make it the default supplier of the small parts nobody wants to manage. What stands between here and a BUY is price alone: at $50.75 the stock trades at 55× last year's free cash flow and 35× operating income, about seven times the ≈$7.31 band. ## 2. Understanding

Fastenal Company is a Winona, Minnesota-based US distributor, founded in 1967, that sells fasteners and a broad line of maintenance, repair and operations supplies — safety products, tools, cutting tools, janitorial items — largely commodity goods differentiated by service, to manufacturing, construction, government and other organisational buyers (national accounts 65% of 2025 sales) mainly in the United States, with Canada, Mexico and smaller international operations, through local branches, on-site stores inside customer plants and company-owned vending and bin devices fed by its own regional distribution centres and truck fleet; it is a large, mature, organically grown company (2025 sales $8.2bn). **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Return on capital is high and rising: worst-of-four normalized ROCE is 34.3% (year-end, with cash) and worst FCFROCE 21.6%; yearly ROCE on opening capital climbed from 32% in 2016 to 40% in 2025. Operating income per diluted share compounded 8.5% a year and free cash flow per share 10.0%, while the share count stayed flat. FCF swings with working capital — inventory built in 2021 and 2024, released in 2020 and 2023 — but the trend is up. Liabilities are 28% of equity and debt has fallen to $125m. **STRONG.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth passes both ways: no customer reaches 5% of sales, and one supplier exceeds 5% of inventory purchases among many Asian and North American sources. All four forces are WEAK. Customers are fragmented and bound by devices and stock inside their plants, though the growing weight of large national accounts has cut gross margin from 49.6% to 45.0% while operating margin held at 20%. Suppliers sell interchangeable parts; no substitute removes the need for consumables; an entrant would need a local inventory network and installed devices. The moat is switching costs, stable. Market growth is MIXED. **STRONG.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

The CEO's 2025 total of $4.18m is less than a tenth of the proportionality ceiling; director fees top out at $200k; no related-party transactions were reported. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**: dividends take 81–96% of free cash flow and debt has fallen, but every repurchase since 2016 ran far above the bargain lines, and 1.075M shares bought in 2026's first half at $46.33 read 46–51× FCF and 30–34× EV/OI, under an open-ended authorisation with no price criterion.
