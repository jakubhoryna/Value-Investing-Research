## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS; Past WEAK (growth); Future ADEQUATE (customer concentration, contained); Shareholder-friendliness FAIL (compensation strike); cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID (Past + SF)**.

CD Projekt owns two premium game franchises, The Witcher and Cyberpunk, and sold its GOG storefront at the end of 2025. Under the v4.5.0 rules the Future gate no longer fails on its platform payers. Valve and Sony take 44% and 25% of revenue; that concentration is flagged but contained, because the brands are the moat and every game ships on every platform. Two other gates now fail.

The company is spending record sums building Witcher 4 and Cyberpunk 2. Without the disclosed per-project split that would let the build count as growth, that spend counts as maintenance. That leaves FY2025 free cash flow near zero and the trailing figure negative, so the FCF-per-share trend turns negative and fails the Past gate. On that same thin cash flow, FY2025 management pay of about PLN 9–21m per board member is a proportionality strike. A Witcher 4 release that restores free cash flow would re-open both questions; at 54× operating income, price is far away either way.

## 2. Understanding

CD PROJEKT RED develops and publishes premium role-playing games from two owned franchises, The Witcher and Cyberpunk (FY2025 revenue: Cyberpunk 82%, Witcher 18%). They are monetised through launch spikes and long catalogue tails, sold digitally (87% of revenue) via Steam, PlayStation, Xbox, GOG and others, with the platforms as paying customers. Headquartered in Warsaw, it is founder-influenced, debt-free, and mid-build on Witcher 4 and Cyberpunk 2. It renamed itself from CD Projekt S.A. in 2026 after selling GOG on 31 Dec 2025. **PASS.** Lens STD. With GOG gone, one operating segment remains, so no group rules apply.
