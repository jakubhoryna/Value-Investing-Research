## Memo

Crocs, Inc. sells casual footwear under two brands: the Crocs Brand (~82% of revenue — molded Croslite clogs, sandals, and Jibbitz charms) and HEYDUDE (casual canvas/EVA shoes, acquired February 2022 for $2,047M cash per the cash-flow statement). FY2025 revenue was $4,040.6M (−1.5%): Crocs Brand +1.5%, HEYDUDE −13.3%. Gross margin 58.3% — exceptional for footwear and the clearest fingerprint of brand pricing power. Distribution splits 47.9% wholesale / 52.1% DTC across 85+ countries and 514 owned stores; ~8,010 employees.

**Past — ADEQUATE.** The decade is a turnaround story: operating income went from −$6M (2016) and $17M (2017) to $1,022M (2024). ROCE_xc on beginning capital cleared 15% in seven of ten years; the two turnaround years and impairment-hit 2025 (3.8% GAAP; 22.4% ex-impairment) are the misses, so STRONG (8/10) is narrowly missed on the official GAAP basis. The 2022→2023 step-down from 86% to 28% is a denominator reset — the HEYDUDE deal tripled the asset base — not operational decay; 2023–24 were stable in the mid-20s and FCF-ROCE_xc held 16.6% even in 2025. Leverage breached the 200% flag in several years (peak 460% in 2019 on lease adoption and buyback-shrunk equity; 450% in 2022 post-deal) but never the 700% cap, and is covered by the exceptional-returns clause; net borrowings/FCF is now 1.7× (2.3× including leases) and FY25 interest cover is 10.1× ex-impairment (1.7× GAAP).

**Future — ADEQUATE.** The Crocs Brand moat is real and stable: category-defining clog, icon status, Jibbitz personalization, disciplined pricing, and that 58% gross margin. Industry forces are nonetheless unfavourable (substitutes, entrants, and rivalry all HIGH; customers MEDIUM, blunted by 52% DTC; suppliers LOW). HEYDUDE (~18% of revenue) is eroding — down 13.3% in 2025 after a $738.1M trademark/goodwill impairment. Breadth passes: no >10% customer; top-two contract manufacturers make 73% of Crocs Brand output, but EVA molding capacity is fungible, Crocs owns the molds and compound, and HEYDUDE production was re-sourced from China (83% in 2023) to Vietnam (~44% by 2025) — a supply-chain risk, not supplier pricing power.

**Shareholder-friendliness — PASS, with the HEYDUDE caveat front and centre.** Comp is ~88% performance-based and actually fell with HEYDUDE's failure (58.6% STIP payout); Rees owns 2.0% (~$121M); single share class; clean governance; buybacks have been genuinely value-accretive — $577M in 2025 at an average $88.77 (~6.7× FY25 FCF) and $552M in 2024 at ~$117 (~7.6×), shrinking diluted shares 21% since 2020. Against that sits HEYDUDE: $2.0B of cash deployed in 2022, over a third impaired within four years. We score it a serious one-off misstep rather than empire-building (single bet, rapid self-funded deleveraging of ~$1.1B, no further M&A, incentives that punished failure). An analyst who scores it as the disqualifying strike would output AVOID — recorded in Appendix E. A 2025 securities class action and derivative suits over HEYDUDE wholesaler-inventory disclosures are a watch item, allegations only.

**Cheapness — EXPENSIVE on reported numbers; FAIR ex-impairment.** At $119.35 (MCap $5,972M; EV $7,073M on borrowings), MCap/FCF is 9.06 (average zone), EV/OI is 47.3 on GAAP FY25 OI — a clear fail — and 7.97 ex-impairment (average zone); MCap/BV 4.62 passes; TBV is negative (n.m.). The rubric permits one average-zone metric, so even the ex-impairment view is only FAIR. The stock has run from ~$78 in mid-March to $119 on a Q1'26 revenue beat ($921M vs $900.85M consensus), tariff mitigation ($50M of 2025 savings, incremental $100M identified for 2026), and HEYDUDE stabilization hopes.

**Verdict: WATCH.** Quality gates clear; price sits ~13% above the ≈$105 band. Below ≈$105 this becomes a BUY candidate, subject to no adverse HEYDUDE or litigation development.

2. **Leases as financing.** NIBCL = total current liabilities − short-term debt − current operating-lease liabilities (current lease liabilities treated as financing, excluded from NIBCL). NIBCL series ($M): 2021 345.31; 2022 559.45; 2023 612.70; 2024 671.66; 2025 614.36.
3. **EV debt definition.** EV = MCap + borrowings (LTD + ST debt) − cash; operating leases excluded (rent already burdens OI). Sensitivity: including total debt $1,614M gives EV $7,456M → EV/OI ex-imp 8.40× — still average-zone; verdicts unchanged.
4. **Old-year ROCE bounds (FY2017–21).** Exact NIBCL unavailable pre-2021. Lower bound = OI / (TA_prev − cash_prev), i.e., NIBCL = 0; upper bound = OI / (equity_prev − cash_prev), valid since NIBCL ≤ total liabilities = TA − E. Classification is safe wherever the bound clears or fails the threshold; FY2016 classified by OI sign alone (negative → fail, no denominator needed). Beginning-CE convention throughout.
5. **Macrotrends equity 2015–17** may include the Blackstone Series A convertible preferred (mezzanine; ~half repurchased Jul 2018, remainder converted to common by ~2020–21). Affects old-year L/E modestly; flagged, not adjusted.
6. **ROCE step-down 2022→2023** read as the HEYDUDE capital-base reset (TA $1.5B→$4.5B), not operational decay → no sustained-decline downgrade. 2023–24 stable mid-20s; 2025 GAAP dip is impairment-driven; FCF-ROCE 16.6% confirms cash economics.

## Appendix D — Future worksheet

**Gate-0 sentence (understanding test).** Crocs, Inc. is a Delaware footwear company that designs casual shoes under two brands — molded Croslite clogs/sandals (Crocs) and canvas/EVA loafers (HEYDUDE) — manufactured by third parties mostly in Vietnam and Asia, and sells them roughly half wholesale, half direct-to-consumer (digital 37.8% of revenue, 514 stores) across 85+ countries, where it is the category-defining leader in clogs; the Crocs Brand is growing modestly while HEYDUDE declines.

**Moat.** Brand moat in the Crocs Brand: icon status, category-defining clog, Jibbitz personalization attach, pricing discipline. Evidence: 58.3% gross margin (vs ~44% at large athletic peers), Crocs Brand +1.5% in 2025 on top of a multi-year run, sustained ROCE. Trajectory: durable and stable. HEYDUDE's brand is eroding (−13.3% in 2025; $738.1M impairment) but is ~18% of revenue with thin profit contribution; the company is rated on its dominant economics.

**Five forces (industry level).** Customers MEDIUM (large retailers carry weight; 52.1% DTC offsets). Suppliers LOW (fungible EVA contract manufacturing; Crocs owns molds and the Croslite compound; demonstrated re-sourcing — HEYDUDE from China 83% of production in 2023 to Vietnam ~44% by 2025; Crocs Brand Vietnam exposure trimmed 56%→45% over 2023–25). Substitutes HIGH (all casual footwear; fashion-driven). New entrants HIGH (copycat clogs endemic; brand is the barrier; constant design litigation). Rivalry HIGH (Nike, Deckers/HOKA-UGG, Birkenstock, Skechers, private label). **Industry verdict: UNFAVOURABLE.**

**Breadth.** Customers: PASS — no customer >10% of revenue; DTC majority; dispersed global wholesale. Suppliers: top two contract manufacturers = ~73% of Crocs Brand output (45% + 28% in 2025, per 10-K) — a concentrated sourcing *choice* for scale, in a broadly available manufacturing market. Scored PASS with a supply-chain-risk flag; recorded that the strictest literal reading of the breadth test could score this WEAK.

**Resolution of the rubric gap.** Stable, durable moat + breadth PASS sitting inside unfavourable forces has no exact rubric cell; resolved to ADEQUATE on dominant-economics reasoning (Crocs Brand ≈82% of revenue and substantially all profit). STRONG is unavailable (forces not favourable); WEAK would require an eroding core moat or breadth failure — neither holds.
