## 2. Understanding

AstraZeneca PLC, a UK company formed by the 1999 Astra–Zeneca merger and run from Cambridge, discovers, makes and sells patented prescription medicines — cancer drugs (Tagrisso, Imfinzi, Calquence, Enhertu with Daiichi Sankyo), heart, kidney and diabetes drugs (Farxiga), respiratory and immune drugs, and rare-disease antibodies from Alexion (Ultomiris, Soliris) — paid for mainly by drug wholesalers, hospitals, pharmacies and government health systems worldwide (42% of 2025 product sales in the US), plus royalties and milestones from partners; it is a large, growing incumbent with $58.7bn of 2025 revenue. **PASS.**

One reportable segment (the filer: "a single business activity of pharmaceuticals"). The only listed subsidiary, AstraZeneca Pharma India (75%), is immaterial; no listed holding reaches 10% of market value. Not a group.

## 4. Quality — future

Two US wholesalers each buy more than 10% of product sales (14.0% and 10.1% of revenue). No market share is printed, so dominance is not evidenced and the flag becomes a STRONG customer force, reinforced by payers: Farxiga and Calquence were selected for Medicare price negotiation. Substitutes are a second STRONG force: Soliris biosimilars entered in 2025 and Farxiga faced an authorised generic. Suppliers and entrants are WEAK. The moat is patents (government), stable across the portfolio, eroding product by product; market growth YES. **WEAK — 2+ forces.**
