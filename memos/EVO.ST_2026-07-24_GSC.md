Evolution is one of the highest-return operating businesses in this register — a worst-of-four ten-year ROCE of 24.6% and FCFROCE of 21.3%, no debt, and compound per-share operating income growth of 47% a year since 2016. It fails on two independent gates. Its largest customer is 12% of net revenues (13% in 2024, 13% in 2023, 14% in 2022), breaching P15's customer-breadth line on the company's own disclosure sentence in every year of the recent record; two of four forces call STRONG; and the moat is eroding, with net revenues now shrinking and operating margin down from 64.1% (FY2024) to 57.3% (H1 2026). Separately, the buyback record carries three P19 hard strikes (2021–23 tranches executed at 19–36× EV/OI and 21–44× MCap/FCF on the year read, worse on every normalized read), and the NetEnt acquisition was struck at 42–48× the target's pre-deal operating income. Nothing about the price flips this: quality and shareholder-friendliness are the binding constraints, not cheapness. What would need to change is structural — customer concentration below 10%, a stabilised competitive position, and a published worth-referenced repurchase policy backed by a bargain-line execution year.

## 2. Understanding

Evolution AB (publ) is a Swedish-listed, EUR-reporting, Malta-centred B2B supplier that builds and operates live-dealer casino games — streamed in real time from ~20 owned studios staffed by roughly 22,500 employees — plus RNG slot titles under the NetEnt, Red Tiger, Big Time Gaming and Nolimit City brands, licensing them on a revenue-share basis to approximately 870 online gaming operators worldwide who pay for the feed and integrate it into their own consumer-facing casinos. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized averages over FY2016–FY2025: ROCE **24.6%**, FCFROCE **21.3%** (both on the end-of-period, cash-inclusive basis) — comfortably clear of P2's 15%/8%. Per-share geometric means: OI/sh **+47.0%**, FCF/sh **+64.7%**, BV/sh +56.7%, TBV/sh +38.3%, all far above the 3% floor. L/E 35.5%, no interest-bearing debt beyond €90m of leases. The 2020→2021 return step-down (92%→22%) is NetEnt goodwill entering capital employed, not operational decay; returns then rose through FY2024. **STRONG** — but FY2025 is the first down year (OI/sh −8.1%) and LTM operating income has fallen further.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **FAIL**: largest customer 12% of net revenues, top five 39%. The captive-customer waiver is unavailable — operators demonstrably can and do multi-source or backward-integrate, and with ~870 customers the concentration reflects buyer size, not structural scarcity. Forces: customer power **STRONG** (concentrated, consolidating, multi-sourcing); supplier power WEAK; substitutes **STRONG** (Pragmatic Play, Playtech and others competing hard on price; RNG flat; players substituting to slots, sports and prediction markets); new entrants WEAK (licences, studio scale, 24/7 operations). Tally 2 STRONG = the book's stop signal. Moat: scale plus integration switching costs, **eroding** — share loss, declining revenue, compressing margin. Market growth MIXED. **WEAK** on three independent grounds.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 clean: CEO Carlesund's €8.60m total is 0.81% of the lower of FCF and net income (5% strike line), far below the ~$40m scrutiny trigger; chairman's fee €400k sits under the floor-default stop; founders hold 10.96% outright through Österbahr Ventures; related-party disclosure is clean. P19 is not. Three buyback tranches breach 20× on both reads of at least one basis — 2021 (35.6×/62.8× EV/OI), 2022 (23.7×/34.7×), 2023 (20.8×/28.0× MCap/FCF) — each a standalone hard strike. The v3.5.0 redemption clause fails all three limbs: no published worth-referenced price discipline (the stated rationale is capital-structure improvement), no completed bargain-line or refusal year (FY2025 at 10.4× EV/OI is below the flag lines but not below 7×), and continuous CEO and chair. The NetEnt purchase — deal EV €2,488m against pre-deal EBIT of SEK 529m (FY2019) / SEK 601m (FY2018) — reads **42–48×** on every build. **FAIL** (four independent hard strikes).

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Customer concentration is structural and the operator base is consolidating; the loss or repricing of one 12% relationship moves group revenue materially.
- Competitive erosion is now visible in the numbers — net revenues −1.4% in H1 2026, operating margin down ~7 points in two years.
- Regulatory: the UK Gambling Commission opened a Section 116 licence review of Evolution Malta Holdings in December 2024; the outcome is undisclosed and may carry sanctions.
- Asia (a large grey-market exposure) remains disrupted by cybercrime-related activity; regulated markets are only ~50% of revenue.
- Reported operating profit is flattered by non-cash earnout remeasurements — €151.1m in FY2024 and €51.7m in FY2025 ran through "other operating revenues"; clean FY2025 operating profit is nearer €1,206m.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Two gates must move together. Future flips only if the largest customer falls below 10% of net revenues **and** the force tally drops to ≤1 STRONG on evidence of stabilised share and margin. SF flips only on the full v3.5.0 redemption path — a published, board-level, worth-referenced repurchase criterion, plus a completed programme year at or below the bargain lines (EV/OI ≤7× or MCap/FCF ≤8×), plus allocator turnover. The 2026 programme is executing at 8.65× EV/OI — closer than any prior year, and worth watching, but neither complete nor below the line. Next review: FY2026 year-end report, 4 February 2027.

## C. Calculation notes

**NIBCL composition.** Accounts payable + accrued expenses and prepaid income + current tax liabilities. **"Other current liabilities" (€73,523k in FY2025) is left inside capital employed** under the burden-of-proof rule — Note 19 shows it contains a jackpot liability (€12,529k) and employee-related items, but the residual is not clearly a trade payable or accrual. Provisions (where separately shown, FY2020–22) also left in CE.

**Maltese tax gross-up.** Evolution carries very large gross current tax receivables (€740,643k) and current tax payables (€1,088,894k) from Malta's refundable imputation system. The payable is deducted as NIBCL per the definition; the receivable stays in assets. Net effect on FY2025 CE is a reduction of €348,251k — flattering ROCE by roughly 2 percentage points. Logged; it does not approach any threshold.

**FY2025 CE_wc build:** total assets 5,521,787 − NIBCL (10,991 + 59,748 + 1,088,894 = 1,159,633) = **4,362,154**. **CE_xc:** 4,362,154 − (cash 817,951 + bond portfolio 104,050) = **3,440,153**. The bond portfolio is classified non-current but is a liquid Level-1 holding; it is treated as a cash equivalent for CE_xc and for the all-cash-excess EV, and left in CE_wc.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers: FAIL.** AR2025: the largest customer was approximately 12% of net revenues in 2025 (13% in 2024); the five largest were about 39% (46%). Prior years: 13% (2023, top five 46%), 14% (2022). The disclosure sentence exists and reports above the 10% line in every year of the recent record — the P15 test is failed on the company's own words, not on inference.

**Captive-customer waiver: NOT AVAILABLE.** All three limbs must hold. (i) The concentrated customers can backward-integrate and face no prohibitive switching cost — operators routinely run several live-casino suppliers in parallel, and competitors have been built from a standing start inside this window. (ii) The moat is not stable (see below). (iii) The concentration is not structural — Evolution has ~870 customers; the top-five share reflects operator size, not an absence of possible buyers. Waiver refused; breadth FAIL stands.

**Breadth — suppliers: PASS (inferred).** Total supplier spend is ~11% of revenues per the AR2025 economic-value distribution, against total operating expenses of €860.9m; a single supplier above €86m is implausible across 20+ studio locations, property, telecoms and IT hardware. Not separately disclosed — logged in A3.
