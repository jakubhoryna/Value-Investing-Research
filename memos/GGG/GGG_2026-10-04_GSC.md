## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past STRONG, Future WEAK (customer breadth FAIL), shareholder-friendliness PASS (one capital-allocation flag), cheapness EXPENSIVE — **AVOID** (Future).

Graco earns exceptional returns on a small asset base selling premium fluid-handling equipment, with a net-cash balance sheet. It fails one gate. In each of FY2023–25 a single customer took more than a tenth of group sales, and that customer is a channel partner that could move shelf space to rival sprayers. Price would also block a BUY: the band sits near $28 against $78. The verdict flips to WATCH only if the annual report stops carrying the over-10% customer sentence, with returns intact.

## 2. Understanding
Graco designs and makes differentiated pumps, sprayers, proportioners and dispensing systems for hard-to-handle fluids, coatings and powders. Its paying customers are third-party distributors, paint-store and home-centre channel partners, integrators and OEMs, who resell to professional painters, contractors and manufacturers. It is a vertically integrated manufacturer that machines most parts in-house in Minnesota and sells mainly through distribution: Contractor 48% of FY2025 sales, Industrial 45%, Expansion Markets 7%. About 60% of sales are in the Americas. It is a century-old Minneapolis company with $2.24bn of sales, adding Valco Melton (adhesive dispensing, ~$145m sales) in August 2026. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**STRONG.** The worst-of-four average ROCE is 23.5% (end-of-period, cash in) and the best 30.9%. Worst FCFROCE is 17.9%. Per-share growth clears the floor: geometric OI/share +8.0%, FCF/share +16.1% (from a weak FY2015 base), BV/share +15.9%. Liabilities to equity were 23% at FY2025, with debt of $25m against $624m of cash. Judgment logged: returns stepped down in 2024 after a 2019–23 capex cycle tripled plant. The ex-cash beginning-of-period series fell four years running, 36.7% to 27.4%, but the other three variants turned up in 2023 or 2025. Without a sustained decline, P4 does not apply.

## 4. Quality — future
**WEAK.** Breadth fails on the customer side. The 10-K states that one customer, across Contractor and Industrial, individually represented over 10% of consolidated sales in 2025, 2024 and 2023, and that the Contractor segment depends on a few large channel partners. The waiver is refused. A paint and coatings reseller can give the shelf to competing sprayer brands, so switching costs are not prohibitive. Suppliers pass, with materials from multiple sources. Customer power is STRONG in Contractor; the other forces are WEAK. Tally: one strong force. The moat is brand: professional contractors specify Graco, and margins held at 23.5–29.5% outside the 2016 impairment year. It is stable. Market growth is mixed: sales rose 4% from 2022 to 2025.
