# RCO.PA — Rémy Cointreau SA: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict
Understanding PASS, Past WEAK, Future WEAK, shareholder-friendliness FAIL (flag pair), cheapness EXPENSIVE: **AVOID** (Past + Future + SF).

Rémy Cointreau owns an appellation-protected cognac franchise (Rémy Martin, Louis XIII) and a smaller liqueurs and spirits arm (Cointreau, Bruichladdich, The Botanist). That is what it would be worth owning for. The record does not support it. Operating income per share shrank 2.4% a year over the decade. Returns fell three years running, from 19.0% to 5.5% on capital. Free cash flow averaged under 3% of capital, because ageing eaux-de-vie absorbed most operating cash. Cognac price/mix fell 8.3% in FY2026 even as volumes rose. Before the de-rating, €464m went into buybacks at 21–37× operating income, and distributions ran at almost twice free cash flow. To move toward WATCH, returns would have to recover above 10% with per-share growth turning positive, and cognac pricing would have to stabilise.

## 2. Understanding
Rémy Cointreau ages, blends and markets premium spirits: cognac (Rémy Martin, Louis XIII; 61% of FY2026 sales of €935m) and liqueurs and spirits (Cointreau, Metaxa, Mount Gay, Bruichladdich, The Botanist). It sells them through its own distribution subsidiaries and partner wholesalers, importers and travel-retail operators, mainly in the Americas (39%, chiefly the US) and Asia-Pacific (38%, chiefly China). It is a vertically integrated French société anonyme headquartered in Cognac, carries €2.2bn of ageing inventory, is 58% owned (71% of votes) by the Hériard Dubreuil family through Orpar and Récopart, and is in a transformation plan under a chief executive appointed in June 2025. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record
**WEAK.** Worst-of-four normalized ROCE is **10.9%**, just above the floor, but FCFROCE is **2.7%**: inventory rose from €1.1bn to €2.2bn and consumed most operating cash. Operating income per share fell 2.4% a year and FCF per share fell 3.8% a year, both below flat. ROCE peaked at 19.0% in FY2023 on the post-COVID cognac boom, then fell to 11.9%, 7.9% and 5.5%. L/E is 79%, which is comfortable. Negative per-share growth and the sustained decline in returns each make Past WEAK.

## 4. Quality — future
Breadth **FAILS** on the customer side. The FY2026 accounts say the largest customer is "less than 14% of sales" (FY2025: less than 11%), which the company does not bring under 10%. Suppliers (cognac growers under multi-year contracts, glass, casks) pass. **Customer power is STRONG**: the FY2024–25 distributor destocking cut sales 36% from peak. **Substitutes are STRONG**: in FY2026 cognac volume rose 7.8% while price/mix fell 8.3% as drinkers traded down. Supplier power and new entrants are WEAK, protected by the appellation and aged stock. The moat is **brand**, judged **eroding**. Cognac sales are 18% below FY2020 on an organic basis, and US and Chinese duties are now permanent costs. Market growth: **NO** for cognac. **WEAK.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness
The FY2026 top total was €1.94m (Franck Marilly, nine months). That is under the fiscal-year line of €2.70m but above the €1.45m normalized line, so it is **one P17 flag**. Capital allocation is **CAUTION**. €464m of buybacks in FY2018–23 ran at 21–37× operating income and 34–642× free cash flow. FY2017–26 distributions of €1.11bn ran against €621m of free cash flow, and net debt rose from €390m to €690m. Since then buybacks have stopped, the dividend has been cut to €0.75 (partly paid in scrip) and a hybrid has been issued, so the course has changed. The Oeneo cask purchases (19% of capex) are logged INDETERMINATE. **FAIL** (flag pair: P17 normalized read + capital-allocation CAUTION).
