TI still earns a worst-of-four ROCE of 26.7% on the latest five years after doubling its capital into owned 300mm fabs. Under the v4.5.0–v4.8.0 rules, maintenance capex is built year by year; with revenue down in 2023–24 the build counts as maintenance, FCF per share shrinks and the returns decline fires, so Past turns WEAK and decides the verdict. Future improves to ADEQUATE: the 12% customer is one contained force in Analog. Past needs two clean years to recover, and the price is about twelve times the trigger.

## 2. Understanding

TI designs, and mostly in its own wafer fabs (300mm in Richardson, Sherman and Lehi), manufactures more than 80,000 analog and embedded-processing chips that it sells, more than 80% directly and the rest through one worldwide and a few regional distributors, to over 100,000 electronics makers in the industrial (33% of 2025 revenue), automotive (33%), personal-electronics (21%), data-center (9%) and communications markets, with 38% of revenue from US-headquartered customers and about a fifth from China-headquartered ones, as a Dallas-based integrated device manufacturer founded in 1930 that is ending a six-year capacity build and has agreed to buy Silicon Labs. PASS.

- **Analog (79% of 2025 revenue):** power-management and signal-chain chips (regulators, battery management, amplifiers, data converters, interface) sold to the same makers, mostly from TI's 300mm fabs.
- **Embedded Processing (15%):** microcontrollers, processors, wireless-connectivity and radar chips sold mainly to industrial and automotive makers, whose own software ties them to the product family.
- Other (6%: DLP projection chips, calculators, custom ASICs) is immaterial.

## 4. Quality — future

Two material units, weighted 90.5/9.5 on three-year segment profit. Analog carries the flag: one end customer took 12% of revenue in 2024 and 2025, recognised mainly in Analog, and distributor shares are not printed.
