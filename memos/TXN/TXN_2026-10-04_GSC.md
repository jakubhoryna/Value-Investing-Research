# TXN — Texas Instruments Incorporated: GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

Understanding PASS, Past ADEQUATE, Future WEAK, Shareholder-friendliness PASS with one flag (capital allocation CAUTION), Cheapness EXPENSIVE: **AVOID (Future)**.

TI is worth studying for a decade of worst-of-four ROCE above 30%, earned on chips it makes itself on 300mm wafers costing about 40% less per die than 200mm. Two things keep it off the shopping list. The FY2025 10-K discloses one end customer at 12% of revenue in 2024 and 2025; the filing names neither the customer nor any reason it could not switch, and with over 100,000 customers the concentration is a choice, not a property of the industry, so no waiver applies and Future is WEAK. The price is also roughly eight times the band. A FY2026 10-K with no customer at or above 10% would flip Future and move the name to WATCH; CHEAP would still need a seven-eighths price fall.

## 2. Understanding

TI designs and, mostly in its own 300mm fabs, manufactures more than 80,000 analog (power and signal-chain) and embedded-processing chips, selling them over 80% directly to more than 100,000 electronics manufacturers in the industrial (33%), automotive (33%), personal-electronics (21%), data-center (9%) and communications markets, with 38% of revenue from US-headquartered customers and 21% from China, as a 96-year-old Dallas-based integrated device manufacturer. PASS.

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four average ROCE is 30.2% and FCFROCE 19.7% (both end-of-period, with cash), far above the STRONG lines. The trend is the issue: ROCE fell from 40.9% in FY2022 to 16.7% in FY2024 and recovered to 18.8% in FY2025, FCFROCE from 23.9% to 4.6% and back to 8.1%, as capex ran at 26–31% of sales against a 5% pre-cycle norm. Two down years and an uptick are read as cycle plus build-out, not a sustained decline (judgment logged in Appendix C). Per-share growth FY2016→FY2025 by geometric mean: operating income +3.7%, book value +6.3%, tangible book +11.8%, but all-capex FCF −3.7%, which fails the 3% floor, so STRONG is unavailable. Liabilities-to-equity is 113%. **ADEQUATE**: top-decile returns, a build-out-depressed FCF record, three growth legs of four passing.

## 4. Quality — future

Customer breadth FAILS: one end customer took 12% of revenue in FY2025 and FY2024 (none at 10% in FY2023). Supplier breadth passes: manufacturing is in-house and essential materials are described as generally available. Forces: customer power WEAK (half of revenue from outside the top 50 customers); supplier power WEAK; substitutes WEAK (power and signal-chain functions cannot be done without; integration into customers' own silicon is the slow exception); new entrants WEAK (300mm scale, catalogue breadth and design-in switching costs; Chinese analog entrants are the contained pressure). Tally 0 strong. Moat: Cost, from owned 300mm capacity, strengthening as Sherman and Lehi ramp. Market growth YES. The breadth failure decides: **WEAK**.

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

CEO Haviv Ilan's FY2025 total was $22.7m against 5% of the lower of FCF and net income of $130m (fiscal-year read) and $91m (three-year read), below the ≈$40m indexed trigger; the top outside director earned $431.5k against a ≈$1.02m lead-seat stop. Insiders hold 0.6%; no control structure; no related-person transactions disclosed. Capital allocation is CAUTION: repurchases were throttled as the price ran away ($5.1bn in FY2018 at 15× EV/OI, $1.5bn in FY2025 at 28×, $185m in H1 2026), but dividends plus buybacks exceeded all-capex FCF by $3.5–4.2bn a year in FY2023–25 while $7.2bn of notes were issued, and the February 2026 agreement to buy Silicon Labs for a $7.5bn enterprise value, a target with operating losses in each of FY2023–25, is a single material misstep without a pattern. **PASS**, one flag.
