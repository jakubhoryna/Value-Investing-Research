## C. Calculation notes

**§2·1A substitution — treatment call re-affirmed.** Wise is an electronic-money institution: not a deposit-funded lender, not a securities-heavy holdco, not an insurer, so neither branch of the Gate-2 substitution text fits literally. v1 ran the quality gate on ROE and kept cheapness on operating income and owner free cash flow rather than book value, on the reasoning that Wise is an asset-light franchise rather than a securities portfolio whose net asset value is its worth. That call is re-affirmed. Book value here is regulatory capital supporting a fee-and-float business, not the asset being bought, and anchoring on MCap/BV would value the franchise at the cost of its licences. The anchor is stated per name as the rule requires: **EV/OI and MCap/FCF, with MCap/BV informational.**

**P20 not active.** Capital expenditure ran $13.4m, $44.1m and $19.6m against net revenue of $1,776m to $2,503m — no ramp, no expansion-labelled disclosure, no anchor to construct. The maintenance basis is unavailable and unnecessary, so the basis question that governed the Essity re-test does not arise here: every P17 and P19 free-cash-flow leg below runs on the ordinary owner-FCF proxy.

**Owner-FCF proxy.** Net income 498.7 + D&A 14.4 − capex 19.6 − intangibles 1.8 = **$491.7m**, unchanged from v1. Share-based compensation of $95.5m is deliberately not added back: it is settled in shares the company buys with cash through the employee trust, so adding it back would count the same dollars twice. That treatment is what makes the sterilisation finding coherent — the buyback *is* the cash cost of the compensation.

**EV build.** MCap $12,212m + interest-bearing debt $334.7m = **$12,547m** on the no-cash variant. The all-cash variant cannot be computed because own cash is not separable, but it is bounded: cash belonging to Wise rather than its customers cannot exceed stockholders' equity plus own debt, since customer money is matched by customer liabilities. That ceiling is $2,259.9m, so all-cash EV is at least $10,287m and all-cash EV/OI at least **17.4×**. The bound uses only filed figures and is stated as a bound, not an estimate.

### 3. Buybacks — **P19 HARD STRIKE (sterilisation, escalated on price)**

**Purpose and record.** Wise repurchases shares into an Employee Share Trust. The FY26 release states the purpose plainly — $470m for 35.9m shares "to eliminate shareholder dilution from historic share options" — and the new $500m-plus programme reserves roughly 40% for the same recurring purpose. The filing shows the mechanism running for three years:

| FY | Shares repurchased | Employee-trust holding | Repurchase cash |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 9,071,706 | 22.9m | not tagged |
| 2025 | 8,704,883 | 14.6m | not tagged |
| 2026 | 35,913,201 | 37.7m | **$470m** (disclosed) |

Diluted shares fell only 1.9% across the three years, from 1,048.9m to 1,029.7m, despite the spend — the sterilisation signature. Each year carries the **sterilisation flag**, which attaches at any size; flags aggregate within one test, so the three count as one.

**Escalation test — FY2026.** Average purchase price $470m ÷ 35,913,201 = **$13.09**. Implied MCap = 13.09 × 1,029.7m = $13,476m; implied EV (no-cash) = $13,811m, and at least $11,551m on the own-cash ceiling.

| EV/OI, year (all-cash floor) | **≥ 19.6×** | > 14× | breach |
| EV/OI, 3y normalized | **21.0×** | > 14× | breach |
| MCap/FCF, year | **27.4×** | > 16× | breach |

**Robust across the range.** The escalation does not depend on the derived price: WISE.L traded between $10.58 and a $13.12 average across FY2026 in dollar terms, giving 19.0× EV/OI at the year's low. No price in the year clears the line. **Sterilisation escalates to a hard strike.**

**FY2024 and FY2025 — INDETERMINATE, not relied on.** Repurchase consideration is not tagged for either year and the trading ranges straddle the escalation line: FY2024 reads 10.3× EV/OI at the low against 15.1× at the average, FY2025 10.5× against 15.5×. Under §2·1C a finding resting on undisclosed inputs is logged INDETERMINATE — it forces the named re-test and can never complete a pair. Both years keep their sterilisation flag; neither is scored as escalating.

**Rescue, de-minimis, worth check.** Rescue is tested first and fails by a factor of three — nothing approaches 7× EV/OI or 8× MCap/FCF. De-minimis is run as the register requires and fails on every computable denominator: FY2026's $470m is 96% of owner-FCF, 80% of that year's operating income and 72% of three-year normalized operating income; the three-year normalized FCF leg is not computable and would have to be over $9.4bn to matter. De-minimis does not reach sterilisation in any case. The v3.5.0 worth check clears flags, never hard strikes, and no contemporaneous below-worth arithmetic is published; v3.11.0 has since made explicit that a downgraded strike cannot then be cleared by the worth check either.

**Redemption clause — fails all three limbs, one structurally.** (a) No worth-referenced repurchase criterion; the published purpose is dilution elimination, which is the opposite of a price rule. (b) No bargain-line execution year and no refusal year — the mechanism ran in all three years and the successor programme reserves capacity for it. (c) No allocator turnover, and the July 2025 bundled vote extended founder supervoting to roughly 2036, which forecloses the limb for a decade rather than merely failing it today.

### 4. Governance — second independent negative

The dual-class structure itself is a documented caveat under P17, not a flag. What counts separately is the **bundling**: the extension of 9:1 Class B supervoting by ten years was put to shareholders tied to the US primary listing, over a section 314 statement from co-founder Hinrikus objecting to the bundling and with both major proxy advisers flagging it. That is a documented governance negative in its own right. Paired with the sterilisation flag it would fail the gate on the two-flag rule even if no escalation had occurred — so the FAIL rests on two independent footings.

### 5. Dividends & capital allocation

No dividend; retention is appropriate at these returns and there is no empire-building M&A. The allocation failure is specific and quantified: capital returned as compensation funding at 23× operating income.
