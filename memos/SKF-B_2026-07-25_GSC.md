# SKF-B — AB SKF (publ): GSC Memo

## 1. Verdict

| Understanding | Past | Future | Shareholder-friendliness | Cheapness | FINAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | ADEQUATE | ADEQUATE | PASS | EXPENSIVE | **WATCH** |

A 119-year-old bearings franchise with a genuine spec-in moat, an unusually clean owner record — no buybacks in ten years, no dilution, modest pay, a Wallenberg anchor — and returns that never quite justify the reputation: worst-of-four ROCE 11.3% and FCFROCE 5.5% across FY2016–25, with operating income ending the decade below where it began. Nothing stands between here and a BUY except price, and the gap is enormous: the shares trade at 25.5× FCF and 17.3× EV/OI against a band near SEK 80. The complication is structural, not valuational — the Automotive separation targeted for Q4 2026 means the ten-year record below describes a company that will not exist in January 2027. Verdict flips only on a price collapse of roughly two-thirds, or on the post-separation Industrial entity demonstrating that its 15%-margin economics survive standing alone.

## 2. Understanding

**PASS.** SKF designs and manufactures rolling bearings, seals, lubrication systems and condition-monitoring hardware and services — differentiated, engineered components sold to industrial OEMs, heavy-industry and aerospace end-users, and vehicle manufacturers, both directly and through a worldwide industrial distributor network; a vertically integrated Swedish public manufacturer, headquartered in Gothenburg, operating across roughly 130 countries with the USA (19%), China (16%) and Germany (8%) its largest customer locations; the industry's incumbent since 1907, presently splitting itself in two. Dominant unit is Industrial (76% of FY2025 segment assets); Automotive is carried through Gate 1B separately.

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ROCE **11.3%** (end × with-cash) clears P3's 10% but not P2's 15%; worst-of-four FCFROCE **5.5%** falls well short of 8%. Cash conversion is the weak leg — capex ran at 5.0–5.8bn in FY2022–24 against 2.2bn in FY2016, and FCF collapsed to 428m in FY2022. Per-share growth is flat: OI/sh +0.3%/yr, FCF/sh −0.9%/yr, both under the 3% floor; BV/sh +8.3% and TBV/sh +22.1% reflect retained earnings and the amortisation of legacy goodwill rather than earnings power. Leverage is the bright spot — L/E 94.8%, down from 183% in FY2017. **ADEQUATE** — returns comfortably above the floor, nowhere near strong, and ROCE_wc has now fallen two straight years (12.1% → 11.1% → 7.8%); a third fires P4.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS** both sides: no IFRS 8 major-customer disclosure appears in the segment note, which for an IFRS filer is affirmative evidence no customer reaches 10%; direct materials are steel and the supplier base is broad with no concentration disclosed. Forces, on the dominant Industrial unit: customer power **WEAK** (fragmented industrial base plus distributors, re-qualification costs, no backward integration); supplier power **WEAK** (commodity steel, many vendors); substitutes **WEAK** (rotating machinery cannot do without bearings); new entrants **STRONG** (Chinese volume producers — C&U, Wafangdian, LYC — have taken commodity share for two decades). Tally 1, contained in the premium and aerospace segments. Moat: **switching costs / ingrainedness** — bearings are specified into machine designs and sold through an entrenched 17,000-location channel; **stable**, not strengthening. Market growth **MIXED** (aerospace strong, wind soft). **ADEQUATE.** Automotive read separately fails — OEM customer power and Chinese entry both STRONG, Future WEAK — and it is leaving.

Largest in-window acquisition MSEK 668 (John Sample Group, 2024) — far below the audit trigger. **PASS**, zero strikes, zero flags; dual-class (A 1 vote, B 1/10) logged as a caveat, not a strike. ## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customer.** PASS. SKF's Note 2 segment disclosure carries no major-customer statement. IFRS 8.34 requires disclosure of any single customer at or above 10% of revenues; its absence in an audited IFRS filing is affirmative evidence that none exists. Corroborating structure: Industrial sells directly and through a distributor network of ~17,000 locations; Automotive's largest customer industry is light vehicles at 52% of that segment, itself spread across many OEMs.

**Breadth — supplier.** PASS. Direct materials are dominated by bearing-grade steel, a globally traded input with many qualified producers; SKF discloses a broad responsible-sourcing programme covering tier-one through tier-three suppliers and no concentration. No effective-supplier chokepoint of the fab-ecosystem type is present.

**Forces (dominant unit = Industrial; Automotive read separately):**

| Force | Industrial | Reason | Automotive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | WEAK | Fragmented industrial base plus distributor channel; re-qualification cost after spec-in; backward integration implausible | **STRONG** — concentrated vehicle OEMs, sophisticated buyers, annual price-down conventions |
| Supplier bargaining power | WEAK | Commodity steel, many qualified vendors, no forward-integration threat | WEAK |
| Threat of substitutes | WEAK | Rotating machinery cannot do without bearings; plain and magnetic bearings serve narrow niches | WEAK |
| Threat of new entrants | **STRONG** | Chinese volume producers named in SKF's own reports have taken commodity share over two decades; barriers to success hold only at the premium and aerospace end | **STRONG** — same entrants, lower qualification barrier |

Tally — Industrial **1 strong, contained** → ADEQUATE zone. Automotive **2 strong** → WEAK; that unit exits in Q4 2026 and is logged rather than aggregated, per the §2 dominant-unit rule (Industrial = 76% of FY2025 segment assets: 62,489 of 82,508).

**Moat.** Single source: **switching costs, shading into ingrainedness.** Bearings are engineered into machine designs and re-qualification carries certification and downtime cost; the industrial distributor network is the channel's default stock. Direction **stable** — it has neither widened (ROCE has not trended up in ten years) nor visibly broken (premium and aerospace pricing holds). Not dominant: a dominant moat would not produce 11.3% worst-of-four ROCE.

**Market growth.** MIXED. Aerospace strong; industrial drives and heavy industry low-single-digit; wind soft through FY2025 and cited by management as a volume drag; automotive flat with EV transition reshaping rather than expanding bearing content.
