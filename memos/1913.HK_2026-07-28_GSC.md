Prada is a genuinely improving business that fails the framework on its own decade. What is worth owning here is a rare thing in luxury: a controlled, undiluted equity — 2,558,824,000 shares outstanding unchanged since 2015, no repurchases ever, no option or SBC dilution — attached to a real brand moat and a second brand, Miu Miu, compounding at 30%+ while the industry shrinks. What stands between here and a BUY is two separate distances. The quality distance: FY2016–FY2020 were poor enough (ROCE 0.3% in FY2020, sub-6% through FY2018–19) that the ten-year normalized average sits below the ADEQUATE floor even though FY2024–25 print 13–17%. The price distance: the binding trigger band is ≈ HKD 14.8 against HKD 38.90, i.e. the shares would have to fall ~62%. The verdict flips on time rather than on news — as FY2016–18 roll out of the window the same arithmetic gives 10.1% (FY2018–25 basis) and 10.6% (FY2019–25), so a mechanical upgrade to ADEQUATE is arriving on the calendar, not on merit. That is worth stating plainly rather than pre-empting.

## 2. Understanding

Prada S.p.A. is a Milan-headquartered, family-controlled (≈80% via Prada Holding S.p.A.), Hong Kong-listed and vertically integrated luxury house that designs and manufactures in 25 owned factories, and sells differentiated leather goods, footwear and apparel — Prada 66%, Miu Miu 31%, Versace (acquired 2 Dec 2025), Church's and Marchesi 1824 — mainly through its own stores (89% of revenue is retail) to affluent individual consumers worldwide, with a secondary, non-obvious business licensing its trademarks to third parties for eyewear and beauty. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ten-year average ROCE **9.4%** (end-of-period, cash included), against 11.1% on the flattering beginning/ex-cash basis; worst-of-four FCFROCE 10.0%. The FCFROCE clears P2's 8% comfortably; ROCE does not clear P3's 10%, and the worst basis governs. The shape is collapse-and-recovery, not decline: 7.1% (FY2016) → 0.3% (FY2020) → 17.2% (FY2024) → 13.3% (FY2025, diluted by Versace's mid-year consolidation). Per-share growth is strong — OI/sh +11.5% and FCF/sh +5.3% geometric over nine years on a constant share count, both above the 3% floor. Leverage is comfortable: L/E 137%, inside P5. It fails on the level of the decade average alone. **WEAK.**

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth **PASS** both sides: the HKEX disclosure states the five largest customers or suppliers are each under 30% of sales/purchases with no single counterparty influential, and 89% direct retail to individual consumers makes customer concentration structurally nil; supplier side is backstopped by 25 owned factories. Forces: customer bargaining power **WEAK** (millions of consumers, no backward integration); supplier bargaining power **WEAK** (fragmented Italian artisan base, vertical integration); threat of substitutes **STRONG** (a €3,000 handbag's function — status signalling — is served equally by jewellery, watches, travel, and above all by simply deferring the purchase); new entrants **WEAK** (barriers to *success* in luxury are decades of heritage). Tally 1 strong, contained → judgment zone. Moat: **Brand**, single source, stable at group level — Prada steady, Miu Miu strengthening, Versace currently impaired. Market growth **MIXED**. **ADEQUATE.**

## 5. Shareholder-friendliness

P17 clean on both reads: top individual pay €21.18m (Patrizio Bertelli; Miuccia Prada Bianchi identical) vs 5% of lower(FCF, NI) = €42.6m fiscal-year and €39.4m trailing-3y; absolute total ≈ US$24.8m is below the ≈US$40m indexed trigger; highest outside director €218k, far under any local stop. Buybacks: none, ever — no P19 exposure and no dilution to sterilise. RPT is the negative: 20+ related parties and, in three successive years, the controlling holder acquiring buildings the Group occupies (Milan 2023, New York, London Jun 2025) — €240m of related-party lease liabilities. Mitigated because each lease was struck with third parties *before* the family bought the freehold. One flag, no pair. **PASS.**

## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Versace is loss-making on €684m of revenue, management guides to similar losses in FY2026, and €1,002m of provisional goodwill — 80% of the consideration — is exposed to a purchase-price allocation that is not final until 2026.
- The acquisition was debt-funded: financial debt went from €404m to €1,732m in one year, and total lease-inclusive debt to €4,824m, so the balance sheet is no longer the fortress the pre-2025 record implies.
- Concentration in one brand's momentum: Miu Miu is 31% of revenue and effectively all of the growth; the Prada brand itself printed −1% retail in FY2025.
- Personal luxury demand is soft and China-exposed; the FY2020 record shows how completely operating income can evaporate (EBIT €20m on €2.4bn revenue) when stores close.
- Controlled company at ≈80% — minorities have no mechanism to force capital-allocation change, and the family has been steadily moving the Group's occupied real estate onto its own balance sheet.

## 8. Flip conditions & next review

Upward: the ten-year window rolling past FY2016–18 lifts worst-of-four ROCE mechanically through 10% within roughly two annual cycles — Past should reach ADEQUATE around FY2027–28 without any operational change, at which point the name becomes a WATCH at an unchanged band. Sustained group ROCE above 15% with Versace absorbed would do it faster. Downward: an SF pair — a second flag joining the RPT flag would FAIL the gate; the most likely candidate is the Versace audit resolving adversely once the PPA is final and a full year of Versace economics is visible. A goodwill impairment on Versace, or debt-funded further M&A, would also count. Next review: **FY2026 results (~Mar 2027)**.

## C. Calculation notes

**NIBCL composition (burden-of-proof calls).** NIBCL = trade payables + all tax payables + the whole "other current liabilities" line. Note 24 shows that line is entirely operating — short-term employee benefits, payables for capital expenditure, accrued expenses and deferred income, provision for customer returns, customer advances. Both income taxes and other taxes (VAT) are deducted, applied consistently across all ten years because pre-2025 reports present a single combined "tax payables" line. Left **in** CE, deliberately: lease liabilities, financial payables and bank overdrafts, derivative instruments, and related-party payables (ambiguous, and immaterial at €21k in FY2025). This treatment is the *generous* one — a stricter NIBCL would lower ROCE further, so the Past WEAK call is not an artifact of an aggressive deduction.

**FY2025 CE build (EUR k).** Total assets 11,012,937 − NIBCL 1,224,385 (trade payables 628,166 + income tax 41,603 + other taxes 87,404 + other current liabilities 467,212) = **CE_wc 9,788,552**; less cash & equivalents 1,261,676 = **CE_xc 8,526,876**.

**FY2025 TBV build.** Equity attributable to owners 4,644,229 − goodwill 1,517,902 − other intangibles 382,085 = **TBV 2,744,242** (EUR 1.0725/sh). Goodwill retained in CE by default; the "non-acquisitive company" deduction option is expressly unavailable now that Versace has been bought.

**P18 lease capitalisation, FY2016–FY2018 (mandatory, not discretionary).** Undiscounted operating-lease commitments were €3,002m (FY2016), €2,617m (FY2017) and €2,497m (FY2018) against total assets of €4.66bn, €4.74bn and €4.68bn — **53% to 64%**, far above P18's 10% trigger. Method: PV of the maturity schedule, the beyond-five-years lump spread forward at the year 2–5 annual rate; discount rate **2.0%, derived** from Prada's own FY2019 IFRS 16 lease interest of €48,980k ÷ average lease liability ≈ €2,458m (no incremental borrowing rate is separately disclosed). Resulting PVs: 2015 €2,477m, 2016 €2,733m, 2017 €2,391m, 2018 €2,318m. Sanity check: the FY2018 PV of €2,318m against Prada's actual FY2019 closing IFRS 16 lease liability of €2,415m. ROU asset recognised at **0.97× the liability**, the ratio Prada actually reports post-adoption (FY2019 2,362,841/2,415,298; FY2025 2,983,620/3,091,879). Per §2·1A the adjustment shapes every number: OI = published OI + imputed interest; FCF = CFO − capex + fixed minimum lease payments − imputed interest; the PV enters CE, total liabilities and L/E.

**Logged alternate.** Without the P18 adjustment the worst-of-four ROCE is **10.2%** — i.e. marginally ADEQUATE. The adjustment is what carries the verdict, so it is stated openly rather than buried: P18's condition is met by a factor of five to six, the treatment is mandatory under the framework, and it is also the economically correct read for a company whose store estate was entirely off-balance-sheet before 2019.

**Post-IFRS 16 years.** Reported balance sheets already capitalise; lease liabilities are treated as debt-equivalent in CE, EV and L/E. Prada classifies interest paid *within* CFO (the statement shows it as a deduction between "Cash flows from operating activities" and "Net cash flows from operating activities"), so **no IFRS interest reclassification is required** — FCF = net CFO − capex directly. Lease principal repayments sit in financing on both bases, so the pre- and post-adoption FCF definitions are consistent.

**FY2025 EV build (EUR k).** MCap 11,163,626 (2,558,824k sh × EUR 4.3629) + financial liabilities 1,732,366 (current 439,861 + non-current 1,292,505) + lease liabilities 3,091,879 (current 524,699 + non-current 2,567,180) + NCI 21,538 + preferred 0 = 16,009,409 **(no cash credit, conservative)**; less cash & STI 1,261,676 = **14,747,733 (all-cash-excess)**.

**No captive finance arm, no financial-institution substitution, no P20 maintenance basis.** Backvaluing not used — personal luxury is cyclical but not "definitively cyclical" in the book's sense, and the FY2020 trough is a pandemic artifact rather than a cycle low.

**Bias check.** One live: **hotness** — Miu Miu's +34% and the FY2024–25 ROCE recovery create real pull toward weighting the last two years over the decade, which is exactly the judgment the worst-of-four decade average exists to prevent; handled by refusing to shorten the window and by stating the rolling-window flip openly instead of pre-empting it. **Miscontrast** considered and rejected as live: Prada looks strong beside register neighbours LVMH and Kering (both AVOID), but Past was tested against P2/P3 absolutes, not against them. **Confirmation** guarded by computing and publishing the no-P18 alternate (10.2%) that would have flipped the verdict.

## D. Future-performance worksheet

**Breadth — customers.** *Is the customer base broad and unlikely to consolidate?* **YES.** The FY2025 Directors' Report states the percentage of sales attributable to the five largest customers is under 30% and that the Directors consider no customer influential. 89% of revenue is retail sold directly to individual consumers; the wholesale remainder is spread across department stores and multi-brand accounts. The disclosure sentence exists and is affirmative — P15's test is met.

**Breadth — suppliers.** *Broad and unlikely to consolidate?* **YES.** The same sentence covers the five largest suppliers at under 30% of purchases. Looked through to the effective supplier: Prada operates 25 owned factories, so a material share of leather goods and footwear is made in-house; the external base is the fragmented Italian *façonista* network. The FY2025 10% equity investment in Rino Mastrotto Group (leather) is a deliberate step *toward* securing input access, not a dependency. No waiver required.

**Forces (binary, one line each):**

| Force | Call | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Customer bargaining power | **WEAK** | Millions of individual consumers, no possibility of backward integration, purchase is brand- not price-driven |
| Supplier bargaining power | **WEAK** | Fragmented artisan base, 25 owned factories, ready alternatives, upstream equity stakes being taken |
| Threat of substitutes | **STRONG** | All three forms live: direct (any rival maison), doing-without (wholly discretionary and indefinitely deferrable — the FY2020 EBIT collapse to €20m is the proof), wholly-different-function (jewellery, watches, travel, experiences compete for the same status spend) |
| Threat of new entrants | **WEAK** | Barriers to *success* are decades of heritage and archive; new houses rarely scale — barriers to entry are low, barriers to success very high |

**Tally: 1 strong → judgment zone → Future capped at ADEQUATE.**

**Moat.** Single identified source: **Brand**. Mechanism: archive, heritage and creative-director continuity permit pricing far above production cost and confer pricing power that survives cost inflation; the moat is brand-specific, not group-specific. Direction **stable** overall — Miu Miu strengthening decisively, Prada brand stable-to-flat (−1% retail FY2025), Versace currently eroding and the subject of a repositioning that management expects to be loss-making through FY2026.

**Multi-unit note.** Two dominant units (Prada 66%, Miu Miu 31% of FY2025 revenue; pro-forma with a full year of Versace roughly 59/28/11). Both share identical six-parameter economics, so one set of force calls covers them; Versace is carried at group level pending a full year of reported economics.

**Market growth.** **MIXED.** Personal luxury demand has been soft, with a China-exposed customer base and a global aspirational cohort trading down; the long-run direction remains positive but no directional confidence is available for the medium term.

**Verdict: ADEQUATE** — breadth PASS, one strong force contained, a plausible and stable brand moat.

The concern is the **pattern in the property leg**: in three consecutive years the controlling holder has acquired freeholds the Group occupies — Immobiliare Rivalsa (Milan) bought by a Prada Holding subsidiary in 2023; PH-RE Llc and '720 Fifth USA Llc (New York) carrying €146.8m of ROU and €168.8m of lease liabilities; and Prada RE UK Ltd acquiring the London property in June 2025, a building Prada UK had leased since 2010. Each transfers the landlord economics of the listed company's own flagship estate to the family.

*Mitigant, and it is a real one:* in every disclosed case the lease was entered into with an independent third-party lessor **before** the family acquired the freehold, and the family simply succeeded as lessor on unchanged terms. No rent was rewritten. Individual expense shares are low single digits. **Scored as one flag** — recurring, directional and documented — **not a hard strike**.

**3. Buybacks (P19).** **None, ever.** The FY2025 Directors' Report states that neither the Company nor any subsidiary purchased, sold or redeemed any listed securities during 2025 and that no treasury shares were held at year-end; the share count is 2,558,824,000 in every year of the window. There is consequently no tranche to test against the 14×/16×/20× lines, no dilution-sterilisation exposure, and no equity-based dilution to sterilise in the first place — AR2020 confirms no arrangements exist enabling directors to acquire shares. On the framework's most common failure mode for large-cap luxury, Prada is clean where LVMH and Kering are not.

Dividends paid to owners FY2025 €419,647k = €0.164/sh, a **49% payout** of net income; €0.166/sh proposed for FY2026. Consistent with the doctrine for a mature business whose ROCE is good but not exceptional.

Derived deal EV including assumed lease liabilities of €491,254k and financial liabilities of €9,000k, less cash: **≈ €1,698m**; excluding leases ≈ €1,207m.

*Pre-deal normalized OI: negative.* Versace's pro-forma FY2025 contribution would have been €684.4m of revenue, and the 5 Mar 2026 press release states Versace incurred operating losses in FY-25 and is expected to incur losses of similar magnitude in FY-26. P19's 15×/20× mechanical lines apply only to a profitable target; **pre-profit targets remain judgmental**.

*Judgment, logged.* The price is not prima facie empire-building: ≈2.5× EV/revenue for a top-tier luxury house, materially below the ≈$2.1bn Capri itself paid in 2018 for a then-larger Versace, i.e. a distressed asset bought from a distressed seller at well under the previous transaction price. Against that: the deal was **debt-funded** — financial debt rose from €404m to €1,732m, with €1,216.6m of new long-term borrowings drawn in FY2025 — it is dilutive to group EBIT margin on management's own account, and 80% of the consideration sits in provisional goodwill on a brand that is currently shrinking.

*Classification.* Because Versace's standalone pre-deal normalized operating income is **not disclosed** by either party, any multiple here rests on estimates of undisclosed inputs. Per §2·1C [v3.4.0] the finding is logged **INDETERMINATE**: it forces heightened scrutiny and a named re-test, and **never completes a pair**. Named re-test: **FY2026 results (~Mar 2027)** — finalised purchase price allocation, first full year of Versace economics, and any impairment.

**Strike tally: one flag (RPT), one INDETERMINATE (Versace audit), no hard strike, no pair → SF PASS.** The flag stands on the record and will be re-tested at the next REVIEW.
