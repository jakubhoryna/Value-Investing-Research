Bilia is a well-run consolidator of a structurally poor business. It buys dealerships cheaply — the 2025 cohort at 5.6×–11.3× pre-deal operating income — and runs a dense service network at a superior aftermarket margin. None of that survives the gates. Capital employed has more than doubled since 2016 while operating income has not: worst-of-four ten-year ROCE 9.7%, under the adequacy line; ten years of all-capex free cash flow sum to SEK 1.1 bn against SEK 13.3 bn of operating income, a 0.8% FCFROCE; liabilities 304% of equity. Three independent P4 triggers. Nothing here flips on price.

## 2. Understanding

Bilia AB is a Gothenburg-headquartered, Nasdaq Stockholm-listed automotive retail and service group that sells new and used cars, transport vehicles and trucks for 18 manufacturer brands under revocable dealer authorisations, and — through 185 largely leased facilities in Sweden, Norway, Luxembourg and Belgium — sells workshop service, parts, tyres, wheel storage, damage repair, car rental and brokered financing and insurance, directly to private motorists and to corporate fleets (about 60% of new-car demand), alongside a small Swedish fuel-station operation. **PASS.**

## 3. Quality — past record

Worst-of-four normalized ten-year averages (FY2016–25, pre-IFRS 16 years lease-capitalised per P18): **ROCE 9.7%**, **FCFROCE 0.8%**. Both miss — P3 requires 10% ROCE, P2 requires 8% FCFROCE. The trend is worse than the level: ROCE_wc fell from 13.6% (2021) through three consecutive declines to 6.9% (2024), with a marginal 7.5% uptick in 2025, as right-of-use assets and a larger leased-vehicle fleet entered capital employed. Operating income per share compounded 4.8%, above the 3% floor; FCF per share is n.m., six of ten years negative. **L/E 304%** breaches the P5 flag with no P6 relief. **WEAK** — level, trend and leverage each fail independently.

## 4. Quality — future

Breadth: customers PASS (≈100,000 cars delivered to fragmented private and fleet buyers). Suppliers **FAIL** — FY2025 new-car deliveries were 29.5% BMW, 22.7% Volvo, 13.2% Toyota, and new-car purchases dominate cost of goods; the ready-alternatives exception is denied, since brand supply is not fungible at dealership level and the supplier holds the termination right. Forces: customer power **STRONG** (identical product at any authorised dealer; the 3.2% margin is the evidence), supplier power **STRONG**, substitutes **STRONG** (OEM agency and direct sales, online used-car platforms, independent workshops), new entrants **WEAK**. Tally 3 — past the P16 stop. Moat: none owned — scale and network density are real but licensed, and **eroding** as brands migrate to agency. Market MIXED. **WEAK.**

Buybacks: five tranches, 2021 (19.5× MCap/FCF) and 2025 (15.9× EV/OI) above the flag lines, none breaching 20× on both reads, flagged not dominant → **one flag**; no dilution-sterilisation. ## 7. Risks & thesis-breakers

- Any general agent recalling an authorisation removes a revenue block outright, as Volvo Cars demonstrated in Germany in 2020.
- SEK 5,490 M of recourse liabilities for associates (principally Ziklo Bank) sits off balance sheet behind a thin equity base.
- Agency-model migration transfers new-car margin to the manufacturer, leaving Bilia a fee-taking service operator.
- EV mix structurally reduces service hours per vehicle, attacking the one segment earning 11.8%.
- Capital employed and leverage move with fleet and lease-term decisions rather than with trading.
