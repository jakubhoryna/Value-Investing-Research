## Bottom line

HelloFresh is my worst investment. Counting every euro I put in and took out, the position is worth 0.46× what I paid, a money-weighted return of −39% a year, at the 6 Nov 2026 close of €2.99 [J, C].

I bought first at €24.50 in December 2022 and kept buying all the way down to €5.61 in May 2024. Then, in August 2025, I made the largest purchase of all at €7.16: about half of all the money I ever put into the stock [J]. Three mistakes drove it:

- I was underwriting a margin the company had shown once, in a lockdown year.
- I treated every lower price as a better deal, even though every annual report showed a worse business.
- I made my biggest bet last, after the accounts already showed an operating loss.

Under the rules I run now, none of those purchases would have been allowed. HelloFresh fails the first quality gate on every record that was available when I bought. A company that fails a gate has no add zone, so averaging down is off the table.

My fix is structural, not a promise to be more careful. The rules are now:

- written down and versioned;
- impossible for me to change in the middle of an analysis;
- applied to my holdings exactly as they are applied to companies I don't own;
- built so that averaging down into a failing business is impossible.

I still hold the shares, by a logged decision. That is the open question here, and section 6 sets it out.

## 1. The trade

The thesis had three parts [J]:

1. **Margin.** The model buys customers with discounts. Run efficiently, I believed it could earn 10% on €8bn of revenue, which is about €800m of operating income.
2. **Catalyst.** An activist shareholder was pushing an efficiency programme. It did push one through.
3. **Downside.** I counted the large fulfilment network built during COVID as a floor under the value.

Three facts were visible each time I bought:

- **The 10% margin existed only in 2020, the lockdown year.** My €800m target was nearly twice the best operating income the company has ever reported (€426m).
- **The margin fell every year from 2020 to 2024.** Each add bought a worse business at a lower price, not the same business more cheaply.
- **The "floor" was capacity that demand didn't need.** Owned property, plant and equipment rose from €134m in FY2020 to €794m in FY2023 [M], 5.9 times as much [C]. Impairments in FY2024–25 wrote part of it off [M]. Book value is not earning power. At €2.99 the shares trade at 0.66× book [C], and that makes them neither cheap nor safe while operating income is negative.

Since then the decline has continued:

- Orders fell 12.6% in the first half of 2026 [M].
- In Q3 2026, revenue fell 11.3% at constant currency and orders fell 17.9% ([Grocery Gazette, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.grocerygazette.co.uk/2026/11/05/hellofresh-boosts-profitability-despite-falling-q3-sales/)) [P].
- Full-year guidance is for a revenue decline of 9–11% at constant currency [P]. Applied to FY2025 revenue, that is about €6.0–6.15bn [C].

Management guides to adjusted EBITDA of €350–370m [P]. The lowest reported operating line for FY2025 was −€2.0m [M]. My thesis depended on the gap between those two numbers.

## 4. Would today's framework have let me buy?

No, not at any price I paid. Below is the rule chain applied to the record in the current memo. This is my application of the rules, not a back-dated memo.

- **Past fails on growth.** HelloFresh lost money at the operating line in FY2016–18: −€86m, −€84m and −€78m [M]. Because the starting years are negative, per-share growth in operating income is undefined on both of the framework's growth measures. An undefined growth metric fails the Past test (MODEL §2·1A, P3). That holds for every ten-year window up to FY2026.
- **Past also fails on decline.** Return on beginning capital fell from 104% in FY2020 to 35% in FY2021, 14% in FY2022 and 6% in FY2023 [C]. Three consecutive declines, with returns below the 15% bar, make Past WEAK (P4).
- **Future fails.** Substitutes and new entrants are both STRONG forces in each unit, and the moat is narrow and eroding [M].
- **Shareholder-friendliness fails on two flags together** [M]:
  - executive pay well above the token level in loss years;
  - buybacks in FY2025 and Q1 2026 at prices clearly above worth.

The verdict has been AVOID, with all three of those gates failing, in every memo version since 10 June 2026, v1 to v7 [M].

A failed gate also removes the add zone. The framework allows adding only when all of these hold (MODEL §7.2, P21):

- the price is at or under the trigger;
- a current review confirms every gate;
- the bias check is clean.

HelloFresh has had no trigger at all, because its operating income is at or below zero [M].

## 5. What changed in the framework

| Failure | The rule now | Effect on HelloFresh |
|---|---|---|
| Underwriting a hoped-for margin | Price is judged on the latest audited year, or on the trailing twelve months when that is lower. A three- or five-year average is allowed only for a one-off down year that passes a strict test. Guidance and "temporarily depressed" claims never count (§2 Gate 2, P28) | No trigger while operating income ≤ 0, and no price makes it cheap |
| Reading the flattering number | Operating income is the lowest reported operating subtotal, with no adjusted lines (J1). FCF deducts maintenance capex, and the figure after all capex is printed beside it (R6) | Read at −€2.0m, not at €350–370m of adjusted EBITDA |
| Choosing the kinder reading | Where a rule allows two readings, the harsher one governs (J13) | Applied throughout the memo |
| Averaging down | Add only at or under the trigger, with every gate confirmed by a current review and a clean bias check. "Incumbency is not a discount" (§7.2, P21) | No add zone since the first memo |
| Kinder readings for holdings | HELD is a register state, never a verdict, and owning a stock never softens a gate. Every memo on a held name logs a consistency check (§2) | Seven AVOIDs in a row |
| Bending rules mid-analysis | Rules change only in a separate MODEL session. Each change is versioned, recorded in the change log and scanned for consequences. It is committed only on the words "commit model" and applies from each company's next run on, never retroactively (§0, §8.4) | The rules I run today are the rules that judged it |
| Sizing by conviction | Doctrine: 10% of the portfolio or nothing (§7.2, context) | It would have been 10% at most, never a concentrated bet |
| Silent overrides | A hold against a sell flag is a dated decision line in `queue/TODO.md`, with the realised outcome to be logged (§7.1) | Logged 18 Aug and 20 Oct 2026 |

Not every rule was written because of HelloFresh. Most come from the book, or from the 29 framework versions committed since July 2026. HelloFresh is the case that shows why each rule has to bind.

The clearest evidence that the rules do bind: I committed v4.5.0 on 6 Oct 2026 knowing its consequence scan rated my largest winner, CROX, about 80% likely to turn AVOID with a sell flag (v4.5.0 change log, kept in `model/archive/MODEL_v4.7.0_2026-10-27.md`). CROX did turn AVOID.

- The board-linked activist fund kept buying shares at €2.34–2.38 in October [M].

**The case against me.** The framework says no price makes HelloFresh cheap while operating income is at or below zero. A skeptic would call this hold loss aversion with a thesis attached. On top of that, the €350m 5.5% bond issued in July 2026 adds debt while operating income is close to zero [M].

**What would change the decision:**

- a takeover bid;
- two consecutive quarters of order growth, which is the memo's condition for a different verdict;
- FY2026 operating income compared with the roughly €107m that would give the stock a positive trigger [M].
