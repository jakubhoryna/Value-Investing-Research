## Bottom line

Counting every dollar I put in and took out, the position has returned 1.43× in USD, a money-weighted return of 30% a year. That runs from my first purchase on 22 Jun 2023 to the 6 Nov 2026 close of $151.86 [J, C]. Crocs has never paid a dividend [M]. Most of the return came from the part of the thesis I had right. The clog franchise kept producing $650–920m of free cash flow a year, and the company used it to buy back about a quarter of its shares, much of that at single-digit multiples of free cash flow. HEYDUDE was the part I had wrong: in 2023 I called it "a major success", and since then it has shrunk and been written down by $737m. I also accepted a balance sheet that my current rules reject. Under those rules I could not have bought in 2023 at all.

Today the framework rates CROX AVOID on three gates and flags the position for sale. I hold it by a logged decision dated 20 Oct 2026, and I don't add to it. The price is about 18% below the framework's conservative-worth line.

## 1. The 2023 thesis

I built the position between June and August 2023 at $92–109 and made it my largest [J]. My write-up from that year (not published here) rested on four claims:

1. **Brand moat.** Visible in a 56% gross margin.
2. **Returns.** ROCE averaged 26% after Andrew Rees became CEO.
3. **Capital allocation.** Buybacks funded from free cash flow at about 8× operating income and 8× free cash flow.
4. **HEYDUDE.** Bought for $2.5bn at roughly 12× operating income, which I called "a major success", with growth "imminent".

At $96 I had the stock at 7.8× trailing EV/OI and 7.7× MCap/FCF. My bands were: buy at $86–100, add at $100–150, and a sell zone at $310–380 [J]. Two risks were in that write-up, and I accepted both:

- Liabilities at 287% of equity. I wrote: "I am especially okay with these solvency numbers".
- Two Vietnamese suppliers that made 42% and 27% of production.

**The clog delivered.** The Crocs Brand earns 33–36% segment margins on $3.3bn of revenue [M]. From 2023 to September 2026 the company bought back 16.9m shares for $1,804m, an average of about $107 a share [C, from M]. That is 29.9% of its average diluted share count [M].

Over FY2023–25 the buybacks used 54% of cumulative free cash flow, and the rest went to paying down debt [C]. The $2.0bn term loan taken to buy HEYDUDE in February 2022 is down to $500m, and total borrowings at face value are $1,313m [M].

**The buybacks were mostly cheap.** The table reads each year's purchases the way the board could see them at the time, on the three fiscal years published before the purchase [M]. The framework puts conservative worth at about 16× FCF or 14× EV/OI.

| Purchases | MCap/FCF | EV/OI (no cash) | Framework reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 15.2× | 11.1–17.4× | about worth |
| 2024 | 12.3× | ≤ 12.7× | below worth |
| 2025 | 6.4× | ≤ 8.0× | below worth |
| 9M 2026 | 7.1× | 9.9× | below worth |

Only the 2025 purchases were made at the single-digit multiples the thesis assumed. The 2023–24 purchases were made closer to worth.

**HEYDUDE did not deliver.** Crocs paid 11.1× HEYDUDE's operating income for CY2021 ($225.6m), the only pre-deal year it disclosed [M]. By the framework's yardstick that was a fair price. Then HEYDUDE's revenue fell 25% in two years. In Q2 2025 Crocs wrote off $737m, $430m of trademark and $307m of goodwill. That was 29.6% of the goodwill and intangibles booked for the deal [M]. Measured against HEYDUDE's average segment income for 2023–25 ($141.1m), the price paid was 16.5× [M]. Q3 2026 was the brand's first quarter of growth since then, at +6% ([8-K, 29 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1334036/000133403626000058/croxq32026-pressrelease.htm)).

**The re-rating.** On 29 Oct 2026 Crocs reported Q3 results: revenue up 4.6%, helped by a one-off tariff refund of $64.1m [M]. The stock rose 25.5%, from $119.69 to $150.26 [Q]. Management raised its 2026 guidance for adjusted EPS to $14.45–14.65 [P]. The framework uses neither adjusted figures nor guidance.

At $151.86, on normalised earnings [C]:

- MCap/FCF is 8.9× on three-year averages and 10.3× on five-year averages.
- EV/OI is 11.9× on three-year averages and 11.7× on five-year averages.

The framework's conservative-worth line is 14× trailing three-year operating income, less debt-equivalents. That comes to about $184 a share [M], and the price is 17.6% below it [C].

**What the framework catches that I let through.** All three failures were visible in 2023, and my write-up mentions each of them: the leverage, the supplier concentration, and an acquisition priced on a single year. When I bought, the latest annual balance sheet was FY2022, with liabilities at 450% of equity [C]. Today's rules would have failed Past on that and blocked the purchase.

That has a real cost: the rules would have kept me out of a position that has returned 1.43×. I accept that cost. The rules are built to keep me out of companies like HelloFresh. Judging a rule on a single outcome tells you nothing about it.

## 5. Decision, and what would change it

**The decision on record:** HOLD, dated 20 Oct 2026, in `queue/TODO.md` [J]. The note reads: "flag acknowledged and the analysis agreed; held while no better business is in sight". I make no adds: with three gates failing, the framework gives no ADD band.

**Why I hold rather than sell [J].** On normalised earnings the price is still below conservative worth, so each buyback still adds value per share. The margin is thinner than in 2025. The framework raises the flag and I make the decision. The decision is logged, and I re-examine it at every review.

**What has to be true:**

1. Fashion substitution does not reach the clog, and Crocs Brand margins stay above 30%.
2. Free cash flow of $650m or more keeps paying for buybacks and brings liabilities back under 200% of equity.
3. HEYDUDE takes no second impairment. Its $1.14bn trademark had less than 10% headroom at the last impairment test [M].

**The bear case.** The clog is a fashion item and it has gone out of fashion before. In FY2016–17 the group's ROCE fell to low single digits [M]. On top of that:

- a second HEYDUDE write-down;
- a disruption at the manufacturer that makes 45% of output;
- an IRS challenge to the Maltese IP structure (the long-term tax payable is $641.6m [M]);
- $1.7bn of buyback authorisation with no price criterion [M].

**What would change the decision.** The next check is the FY2026 10-K, due in mid-February 2027. If liabilities stay above 200% of equity and the supplier is still unnamed, the flag stands and I have to argue the hold again in writing. If the price gets near the conservative-worth line, the main reason to hold disappears.
