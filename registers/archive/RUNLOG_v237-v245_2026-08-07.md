## Open MODEL items — one of these now blocks clean French analysis

- **The French OI-line convention must be decided, not noted.** SGO.PA (05 Aug) used the line
  *labelled* "Operating income" and Past survived at 10.07%, where the *Business income* line would
  have given 7.70–9.26% → WEAK. ML.PA (06 Aug) applies the same label-based rule and Past dies at
  8.39%, where *segment* operating income gives 10.09% → WATCH. The names pull in opposite
  directions, and the counter-argument is sharp: SGO.PA's stated *reason* was that the line equalled
  revenue − COGS − SG&A, and Michelin's segment OI is exactly that while its labelled "Operating
  income" is SGO's "Business income" under another name. A label rule and a definition rule give
  opposite economics on the same two filers. **SW.PA is now the third case** — 9.34% labelled vs
  10.45% underlying — and the first where the divergence is *not* verdict-determinative, so it is a
  free calibration point. All three French names land within 70bp of the 10% floor on at least one
  reading, and Sodexo adds the strongest evidence yet that the charged line is the economic one:
  management has **guided ≈€300m** of "other operating income and expenses" for FY2026 against
  −129/−58/−154 in FY23–25. **BVI.PA is the fourth case and the sharpest test** — 15.15% statutory
  vs 16.62% adjusted, ADEQUATE on both, so it costs nothing to decide here — because the adjustment
  is dominated by *amortisation of intangibles arising on acquisitions*, an asset already sitting
  inside capital employed, which is a structurally different object from restructuring charges or a
  business-income line. A serial acquirer is where the label rule and the definition rule diverge
  most sharply. **FR.PA is the fifth instance and the first where the *definitional* rule has no
  clean object at all** — Valeo's "Operating margin" line is not the revenue − COGS − SG&A construct
  SGO.PA's reasoning relied on, because €2,273m of R&D sits above it. Valeo also adds a point for the
  label rule: its wedge is dominated by *recurring* restructuring (€78m in H1-2026 alone against a
  standing programme), and a charge recurring nine years in ten is an operating cost. Four of the
  five instances have now been free calibration points — only SGO.PA and ML.PA were actually decided
  by the convention. **MMB.PA is the sixth and a new failure mode: Lagardère presents no line
  labelled "operating income" at all**, so the label rule has no object and the definitional rule
  has none either — a different defect from the SGO.PA/ML.PA label-versus-definition conflict and
  from Valeo's case. **TRI.PA is the seventh and the first where the convention does not bite at
  all** — the wedge is €0.4–1.7m, moving worst-of-four ROCE by **6bp**, three orders of magnitude
  smaller than Saint-Gobain or Michelin. That is the most useful data point of the seven, because it
  suggests the conflict is a **large-cap phenomenon** — standing restructuring programmes, serial
  acquisition amortisation, disposals — rather than an inherent feature of French IFRS presentation.
  **The rule should probably be scoped by a materiality test rather than by filer nationality**,
  which would dissolve five of the seven instances without deciding the two that actually matter
  (SGO.PA and ML.PA). The France block is now closed, so this is no longer blocking — but it is ripe.
- **The OI-line item is no longer a French question.** 6501.T is the eighth instance and the first
  non-French one, and it breaks in a new way: Hitachi presents no line labelled "operating income"
  *and* presents a line that is exactly revenue − COGS − SG&A — "Adjusted operating income", which
  excludes impairments, restructuring and disposals, i.e. a clean definitional object that §2·1A
  simultaneously forbids as an add-back. The label rule has no object; the definitional rule has a
  prohibited one. Seven of the eight instances have been free calibration points, which is what makes
  **Trigano's materiality-test proposal** look right: scope the rule by how much the wedge moves the
  reads, not by where the company is domiciled. **7011.T is the ninth instance and it inverts the
  sign**: MHI presents 事業利益 and no line labelled 営業利益, so the label rule has no object while
  the definitional rule has one — but here the labelled subtotal is the *more conservative* number,
  the exact opposite of Hitachi. Taken together the two Japanese names suggest restating the rule
  substantively as **"the reported subtotal including the most operating charges"**, scoped by
  materiality, rather than by label or nationality. MHI also documents the J-GAAP 特別損益 placement
  problem on a single filer reported twice: FY3/2018 shows ¥126,530m of operating income under
  J-GAAP and ¥58,176m under IFRS — a **2.2× difference from placement alone**.
- **The Siemens captive-finance item is answered in the negative for Hitachi, not extended.** Hitachi
  Capital was deconsolidated in October 2016 and exited in 2021, so it is consolidated for one
  half-year of the window (2.0% of revenue) and the symmetric-exclusion test moves worst-of-four by
  **2bp**. Useful because it shows the SIE.DE item is a *live-captive* question, not a conglomerate
  question — the two should not be bundled at the MODEL touch.
- **§2·1A substitution scope** — WISE.L v3 was the third extension by precedent rather than text (a
  licensed e-money institution fits neither literal branch); sits alongside the standing ZIP.AX item.
