### Gate 0 — Understanding

Write the **understanding statement**: the best single unambiguous sentence in plain language that the filings allow, covering all six parameters — **products** (goods/services; commodity vs differentiated; hunt for non-obvious products) · **customers** (who *pays*, not who uses; consumers or organisations, as specific as filings allow) · **industry** (including non-obvious ones) · **form** (legal *and* operational — REIT, franchisor/franchisee, vertically integrated, multilevel) · **geography** (customers, operations, HQ — emphasise whichever is most enlightening) · **status** (prominence, age, transformation, size).

**Try hard [v4.6.0; G1].** Gate 0 describes the business; it is never a filter on how the filer reports. Read the business review, the segment note and the product and customer disclosures until the sentence can be written. Where the business has several products, paying customers or industries, the sentence names them, as specifically as the filings allow; layered industries and non-obvious products are the book's own cases (Ch5 Avon, Vivint). Prefer the plain, specific word to the filer's label. **A business that can be described as a whole in one sentence passes Gate 0** — a long sentence, a list of businesses, a conglomerate, a trading house or a holding company included.

Avoid the four traps: product familiarity, marketing messages, mission statements, aspirations — describe what the business **is**, not what it could become. **Units [v4.6.0; G3].** Multi-unit companies write the group sentence and then one six-parameter sentence for each material unit (P22: ≥ 10% of revenue, assets or Σ positive segment results), the best the filings allow, and carry the split through Gate 1B (Ch5 multi-unit rule; Ch12 Kone): each material unit gets its own Future block. A unit that mixes businesses — several products, paying customers or industries; an "Other" column; a residual of "all other" activities — is described as it is, naming its parts. Where the filer does not say what a material "Other" holds, its sentence says so and its Future block counts WEAK, as an ungradeable part (§2 Group grades). A mix inside a unit is a fact for Gate 1B (breadth, segment-level force calls), never a Gate-0 test. An "Other" that carries operating businesses is a unit; reconciliation, corporate and elimination columns are not units, and their content stays in the consolidated record. A column that mixes operating businesses with holding, corporate or real-estate companies is tested on revenue and Σ positive segment results, and on assets only net of the holding companies' assets where the filer shows them [v4.5.1]. Immaterial units are named in one line.

**The only Gate-0 FAIL [v4.6.0; G2; P30]** is a listed business that cannot be understood from its filings after the best attempt. The test reads the latest annual report's business description, segment note and revenue disclosures (with the interim and the filer's investor materials where they add disclosure), and asks one question: **do the filings show what the company sells, and who pays for it, for more than half of its revenue?**
- **Incomprehension** — the filings disclose, but what they disclose does not let the analyst state the products or the paying customers of more than half of revenue in plain words.
- **Obfuscation** — the filer withholds what would answer the question: no segment, product, customer or related-party disclosure lets more than half of revenue be described by product and paying customer.

Both are observable facts about the filings, never a judgment of intent or of how profitable the business is. Below the P30 line nothing fails: a material unit, an "Other" or a part that the filings do not describe is described as far as they allow and counts WEAK as an ungradeable part (§2 Group grades). The memo names the parameter that could not be determined and the documents read (§6.4). A stretch invites learning; genuine incomprehension or obfuscation is a hard stop. Reporting structure is never a ground: a unit that mixes businesses, a material "Other", a group whose parts differ, and a bank, insurer or holding company that the FI lens does not fit all go on to be graded. A company with no listed security takes no memo: its register row becomes RETIRED (§7.1), and a queue name found without a listed security is marked off the queue (`[x] no listed security`; `routines/ANALYSE.md`).

**Lens [v4.5.0; R4; too-hard pile retired v4.6.0, G2].** Before Gate 1 the memo states `lens:` STD or FI and logs the test, with its evidence, in Appendix C. There are two lenses. FI is tested first, and every company that does not take FI is read on STD.
- **FI**: the listed entity is a licensed bank or insurer, or the parent of a banking or insurance group supervised on a consolidated basis. It must publish a regulatory capital ratio covering ≥ 90% of consolidated total assets (P22, FI fit), and have no material non-financial part. A ratio counts when it is published for the listed group: a banking consolidated-situation ratio, a financial-conglomerate ratio, or a group solvency ratio (Solvency II group SCR, RBC). A group holding both a bank and an insurer may reach the 90% with its two sector ratios together, and must then pass both capital tests (P24). Coverage is shown from the filer's own figures; where it is not, FI fails. The test is legal and regulatory form (Ch5), never business model.
- **STD**: every other company, however financial the product. This covers non-bank and BNPL lenders, payment and e-money institutions (including those whose licence covers only the company's own payment, e-money or brokerage product), unlicensed brokers, leasing companies, asset managers, exchanges and utilities (Introduction L226; Ch5 Vivint; Ch12 Delta). It also covers every non-financial group with a captive finance arm (R2). **Since v4.6.0 [G2] it also covers the cases the retired too-hard pile stopped:** (a) a licensed bank or insurer, or the parent of a banking or insurance group, that does not fit FI; (b) a group with a material (P22) unit or controlled subsidiary that is a licensed insurer, or a licensed bank funded mainly by customer deposits, and is not a captive finance arm; (c) a company whose total assets are more than half unlisted non-controlled stakes, or securities that are neither cash-like (S1/S2) nor material listed holdings. STD reads them with its own conservative rules, never a softer one:
  - deposits, policyholder and insurance-contract liabilities and every other funding of a deposit-taking or insurance activity count in L/E as liabilities;
  - the deposits and other funding of a lending, leasing or deposit-taking activity are debt-equivalents (Gate 2), with the funding pairing;
  - the cash and securities that back deposits, policyholder liabilities or other customer funds are never cash: a bank's liquidity portfolio stays in CE as part of its operating book, and an insurance activity's investment portfolio is read with its income on the G6 readings in Gate 1A (§2·1A, P31);
  - an insurance activity's investment income is financial income (§2·1A J3): in Gate 1A it is read with the portfolio that earns it (G6, P31), and at Gate 2 it stays out of OI and FCF;
  - unlisted stakes, and securities that are neither S1/S2 nor material listed holdings, are S3: in Gate 1A they are read with their income on the G6 readings (§2·1A, P31), and at Gate 2 they earn no credit.

  The book accepts that the industrial lens gives harsh readings on financials (Introduction L226): a harsh reading is a verdict, never a stop.

**Captive finance arm [R2].** A finance part (lending, leasing, deposit-taking or insurance) of a non-financial group, licensed or not, whose business is financing, leasing or insuring the group's own products for the group's customers, dealers or suppliers. It is a unit like any other: consolidated and graded on STD. Its OI, assets and debt stay in, and it never takes a lens or a capital-ratio test of its own. Its OI is its own result after its funding cost (§2·1A): the segment result where the filer reports it after funding cost, else the arm's reported result less the interest the filer attributes to its funding; where the consolidated OI line leaves the arm's interest income and funding cost in financial items, the arm's result is added to OI and that interest stays out of the financial-income removal, so nothing counts twice (logged in Appendix C). For retail loans and leases originated through dealers the borrower is the customer; a dealer that itself borrows (wholesale or floorplan finance) is a customer under P15, with no exception (R7). A finance part is not captive when the filer's figures show that more than half of its book (receivables, leases or premiums) is unrelated to the group's products. Where the split is not shown, it is captive if the filer describes it as supporting the group's sales, and not captive otherwise.

No holding company is ever valued on NAV [J4; V14].

**Units [P22].** P22 sets the materiality of units (a captive finance arm included), controlled listed subsidiaries and listed holdings.
- A unit is the filer's reportable segment. Never split a segment to bring a piece under the line, and never merge segments to hide one.
- A segment that consists of non-controlled listed holdings is not a unit: each material holding is a part in its own right (P22, V14), and nothing is weighted twice. A segment of unlisted stakes or other S3 securities is a unit, graded from the filer's description of its investees; where the filer does not describe them, it is ungradeable [v4.6.0].
- A unit that takes more than half its revenue from sister units is merged with its main internal customer. Below that line, sister-unit revenue is internal. It stays out of the unit's breadth test, just as intra-group revenue stays out of the group's own P15 test.
- Immaterial units stay in the consolidated record, each named in one line. Every finance activity of a group stays consolidated (R2); a material non-captive bank or insurer part is consolidated and read on STD with the group, unless the whole listed entity fits FI (Lens, above) [v4.6.0; G2].

**Group Gate 0 [R3, R5; v4.6.0, G4].** A group, for every group rule, is a listed entity with two or more material units, or with any material controlled listed subsidiary or material listed holding. Conglomerates, trading houses and holding companies are groups, and are analysed as one company at the parent: the consolidated Past (ROCE), each material part's Future (forces, moat, market) and the parent's shareholder-friendliness, its capital allocation across the parts included (§2·1C·3). Gate 0 writes one group sentence, plus one six-parameter sentence for each material unit (a captive finance arm included), each material controlled listed subsidiary and each material listed holding: a listed part's sentence comes from its own current memo where one exists, otherwise from the parent's filings. **Gate 0 is decided on the group sentence: a part never fails the group [G4].** A part that cannot be graded — a material "Other" the filer does not describe, or a listed part whose own memo is DNAF, missing, stale or ungradeable — counts WEAK in the weighted Future, adds nothing to the look-through Past and earns no credit (§2 Group grades, Group dependencies). Cross-holdings, the company's own stock and its parent's stock are S3 (no credit, Gate 2). A cycle of holdings (A holds B, B holds A) is never a Gate-0 ground on its own; where it lets management or a controller vote shares that the group itself owns, it is read under votes versus economics (P23, V4).

Verdict **PASS / FAIL**. On FAIL the final verdict is **DO NOT ANALYSE FURTHER**: produce the abbreviated memo (§6.4), output the register row, and stop. Sections 1–4 are skipped only in this case.

### Gate 1 — Is it good?

#### 1A. Past performance (P1 window)

**Fewer than P1 years:** proceed on the record that exists and flag it — STRONG is unavailable; the verdict is ADEQUATE or WEAK. **Label the shortfall [v3.9.0].** **History-limited** — the fiscal years do not exist (IPO, spin-off, restructuring); a permanent property of the name. **Record-limited** — the years exist and are audited but could not be retrieved this session. Both carry the same cap. Record-limited additionally carries a mandatory rebuild flag in Appendix A and a register open item, so a later REVIEW knows the cap is liftable. Audited carve-out statements start the record; earlier years without audited statements are history-limited [v4.5.0].

The seven metrics [BOOK]: ROCE, FCFROCE, ΔOI/FDS, ΔFCF/FDS, ΔBV/FDS, ΔTBV/FDS, liabilities-to-equity. Definitions (compute — these are not standard screens):

- **Capital employed** — subtractive and conservative. `CE_wc = Total assets − NIBCL`, where NIBCL defaults to accounts payable + accrued expenses (incl. accrued compensation) + income taxes payable + deferred income/revenue. Burden of proof rests with the item: leave ambiguous items (gift-card float, deferred rent, "other") in CE when material, and log the call. Short-term debt and the current portion of long-term debt always stay in CE. An insurance activity's insurance-contract liabilities, unearned premiums included, are never NIBCL (float funding, §2·1A G6) [v4.6.0]. `CE_xc = CE_wc − (cash & equivalents + short-term investments + S1/S2 securities, Gate 2)`. Goodwill and acquired intangibles always stay in CE [J13; Ch7].
- **Leases.** Fiscal years under ASC 842 / IFRS 16: reported balance sheets already capitalise; treat lease liabilities as debt-equivalent in CE, EV, and L/E, and flag the adoption-year artifact. Pre-adoption years in the window: when undiscounted operating-lease commitments exceed P18, capitalise — liability = PV of the scheduled lease payments (allocate the beyond-five-years lump along the visible downtrend) at the company's disclosed recent cost of debt; adjustments shaping one number must shape the others: OI = published OI + lease expense − imputed depreciation (depreciation = lease expense − imputed interest, interest = rate × PV); FCF = CFO − maintenance capex (P20) + lease payments − imputed interest. J-GAAP filers without lease capitalisation are pre-adoption in every window year: apply the P18 test to each year [v4.5.0].
- **Operating income** as reported — no add-backs of impairments, litigation, or other "one-offs". Comment on distortions in prose; let multiyear averaging dilute them. Price reads a one-off down year on the 3-year and 5-year averages of this same line (P28, Gate 2) [R1]; no year is ever tweaked, with one named exception: the J6 limb (b) re-read of a P28-qualified year (sustained decline, below), which tests a decline and never sets a grade's level.
  - **Operating-income line [J1, never the flattering number].** OI is the **lowest** reported operating subtotal: of all the candidates (face statement, segment note, APM reconciliation), the one carrying the most operating charges. There is no label preference and no materiality exception.
    - A subtotal that excludes a recurring operating charge is never OI. Recurring charges include restructuring, acquisition amortisation, impairment, share-based pay, and "special", "non-underlying" or "adjusted" items.
    - One OI serves the whole memo: Gates 1 and 2, the band, P9, P17 and P19; in Gate 1A, above the P31 line, the G6 include reading's OI is read beside it (§2·1A), and nowhere else [v4.6.0]. Gate 2, the band and P9 read its P28 average in a one-off down year [R1], and so does the J6 limb (b) re-read (the one quality use, an extension of R1); no other test reads it (P17, P19 and P6 read their own years). The one exception is the funding pairing of a lending, leasing or deposit-taking business read on STD (Gate 2 Funding pairing), which applies wherever EV meets OI: the band, the P9 sell line and the P19 worth reads [v4.6.0].
    - Where the accounting standard books operating charges below its operating subtotal (J-GAAP extraordinary losses), charge them to OI. The matching gains are never added.
    - Non-service pension and other post-employment components (interest cost, expected return, remeasurement, mark-to-market): a net income leaves OI wherever the OI line carries it (financial income); a net cost stays where the filer books it and is never charged to OI from below the operating line, because the net unfunded obligation is a Gate 2 debt-equivalent. Service cost is always in OI.
    - With no operating subtotal, OI = pre-tax income before net financial items, with the build logged.
    - A lending, leasing or deposit-taking business graded on STD, as a unit of a group (a captive finance arm included) or as the whole entity, is graded on OI after its funding cost (Ch12 Delta).
  - **Financial income [J3; STD; FI follows the FI gains rule below].** These items leave OI wherever the OI line carries them, and leave FCF wherever CFO carries them:
    - dividends and interest received;
    - equity-accounted results;
    - fair-value changes;
    - gains on financial assets or on disposals of businesses or stakes.

    A removal never raises OI or FCF: a net loss stays in, and the asymmetry is logged (in Gate 1A the G6 readings below replace this rule for the G6 classes; it stands everywhere else, Gate 1C and Gate 2 included). Removals net per bullet class and per fiscal year, never across classes or years. An item of ambiguous character leaves when positive and stays when negative, logged. Interest and fees earned on the operating book of the product the Gate 0 sentence names (a lending, leasing, deposit-taking or brokerage activity) are revenue, not financial income; for an insurance activity the operating book is premiums and fees only, and investment income on its float is financial income outside the FI lens. Interest on the securities that back a deposit-taking activity's deposits (its liquidity portfolio, never cash and kept in CE) is part of that activity's operating book [v4.6.0]. Interest on the company's own cash and securities is financial income. Where CFO does not show interest and dividends received separately (US GAAP indirect method), the income-statement interest and dividend income is removed from CFO, labelled `derived`. For equity-accounted associates and joint ventures (H excepted), S3 stakes and securities, and an insurance activity's investments, the income and the capital that earns it are read together in Gate 1A on the G6 readings (below; P31); at Gate 2 they earn no credit and the income stays out under this rule [v4.6.0].
- **Income and its capital, read together [v4.6.0; G6; P31].** For the classes below, the capital moves with the income that J3 takes out of OI and FCF, because adjustments shaping one number shape the others (Ch7; D19, D41). The list is closed. Any other asset whose income J3 or the J1 pension rule removes keeps its reading, logged in Appendix C: cash, S1 and S2 the four-way cash variants; a defined-benefit surplus, derivative and other financial assets in CE (subtractive, Ch7). The classes are:
  - associates and joint ventures accounted for by the equity method, with the loans and other long-term interests that form part of the net investment in them (IAS 28.38) and their interest (a material listed holding, H, keeps its own reading: §2·1A Groups, V14);
  - S3 stakes and securities (Gate 2), and loans receivable outside an operating book the Gate 0 sentence names;
  - the investment portfolio of an insurance activity read on STD (§2 Lens), a captive insurance arm's included: every investment and the cash its insurance entities hold, never split into float-backed and surplus parts. A listed holding in it is part of the class in Gate 1A and earns no credit at Gate 2 (Never cash), while P22 still makes a material one a part for Gate 0 and the Future.

  On both readings, an insurance activity's insurance-contract liabilities, unearned premiums included, are float funding and never NIBCL (they count in L/E). Otherwise the exclude reading would deduct the funding as well as the assets it funds.

  On STD (the FI lens grades ROE on NI and is unchanged), Gate 1A (Past) reads the classes two ways, and every Gate 1A test takes the harsher; Gate 1B, Gate 1C (P17, P19 and P27) and Gate 2 read OI and FCF with the classes' income out, J3's one-way rule standing, and the §2·1C·3 allocation test reads the filer's segment measures as that test sets them:
  - **Exclude both:** the income leaves OI and FCF (J3), and the carrying value leaves CE in both cash variants.
  - **Include both:** the equity-accounted result as reported (after the investee's interest and tax, never grossed up) and the interest and dividend income are in OI, added where the filer books them below its operating line. The cash they brought in is in FCF: what CFO carries stays, and dividends, distributions or interest the filer classifies in investing are moved into CFO where the cash-flow statement shows them. A distribution booked as a return of capital, and discount accretion collected at maturity, are not added. Where the filer does not show where a receipt sits, nothing is added (J13), logged. The carrying value stays in CE in both cash variants. Interest the filer does not attribute to a class stays out on both readings (J13), logged with its bound.
  - Fair-value changes and disposal results of class assets stay out of OI and FCF, except that on the include reading a net loss on them (a fair-value, disposal or impairment loss on the carrying value) is in OI, added where the filer books it below its operating line or in net financial items, as the include reading's income is (J3's one-way rule; J13).

  The readings feed the tests as follows:
  - ROCE and FCFROCE take, on each window, the worst of eight normalized averages (the four of Denominators, below, on each reading); the level bar takes the lower of the two windows' worst of eight.
  - ΔOI/sh and ΔFCF/sh take the lower of the two readings, each on its harsher-of read.
  - The J6 decline runs on each reading: limb (a) on each of its per-year series, and limb (b) on its own latest-three-year and window worst-of-four averages, a P28 re-read taking that reading's own OI and FCF. A decline that fires on either reading binds, and it clears only after two clean years on every series that fired.
  - J3's one-way rule (a removal never raises OI) yields to the two readings for the classes' income: the exclude reading removes the income and the fair-value, disposal and impairment results of class assets whatever their sign; on the include reading a net loss on them is in OI (added where booked below the operating line) and a net gain stays out.
  - L/E, BV and TBV are unchanged.
  - Tax paid on realised gains of class assets, and interest paid on debt that funds them, stay in CFO on both readings (J13), logged in Appendix C.

  **The P31 line.** A class is the category summed: all equity-accounted associates and JVs (H excepted), all S3 stakes and securities, an insurance activity's investment portfolio. The classes are tested together. The exclude reading alone is used where both limbs hold:
  - at every balance-sheet date B2 uses (the eleven of the P1 window, the opening one included; the record that exists, if shorter), the classes' summed carrying value is under 5% of consolidated total assets; and
  - in every fiscal year of the window, the absolute value of the include reading's OI less the exclude reading's OI (the classes' income, with any net fair-value, disposal or impairment loss on class assets that the include reading keeps) is under 5% of the exclude reading's OI. A year whose exclude-reading OI is ≤ 0 fails this limb unless that difference is nil.

  Below the line, a class's income and its fair-value, disposal and impairment results leave OI and FCF whatever their sign: by the income limb, none of them reaches 5% of OI in any window year.

  Above the line every class is read both ways together: exclude takes all of them out, include keeps all of them in, and no class is mixed between readings. Cash, S1 and S2 keep the four-way cash variants. Captive finance arms, lending books and a deposit-taker's liquidity portfolio stay operating: include both, one reading. A captive arm's insurance float is the exception: it is a G6 class, since include-both is the R2 reading and the harsher of the two is never softer.

  **Gate 2 does not change.** The price pays for the whole business, only cash-like assets are deducted from EV (Ch14 L2324: when in doubt, not cash), the classes earn no credit, and their income stays out of OI and FCF under J3's one-way rule (a net loss stays in). That is harsher than either consistent pairing where the income is positive.

  B2 prints the per-year series and the summary rows on both readings, the harsher flagged (J6 reads the series). Appendix C logs each class, its carrying value and income per year, where the filer books the income and the cash received, and the P31 test.
- **FCF (levered, derived) = CFO − maintenance capex [R6].** Capex is every investing outflow for property, plant and equipment and for intangible assets, capitalised development and software included [J13]. Maintenance capex is the part that sustains current operations, set per fiscal year by the P20 hierarchy below. **Every FCF test runs on maintenance-capex FCF, because reinvestment for growth never makes a company look worse.** The places:
  - Gate 1A: FCFROCE on both windows, the J6 decline series, ΔFCF/sh on both reads, both P3 paths, and net debt ÷ FCF as equity ≤ 0 context;
  - Gate 1C: the P17 fiscal-year and trailing reads and the group P17 denominator; P27 materiality; the P19 worth reads of repurchases, sterilisation and holding-share purchases; the acquisition stop's pre-deal price ÷ FCF (the target's maintenance-capex FCF where P20 can be built from its last annual report before the deal, otherwise all capex, labelled); and the distributions and funding record;
  - Gate 2: MCap/FCF and the FCF leg of the trigger band (so also the ADD band), P12, the P28 averages and the 3-year and 5-year readings, backvaluing's prior year, and each holding's own lines inside the V14 credits (from its own memo).

  The all-capex reading (CFO − all capex, nothing excluded as growth) is always printed beside it (B1, B2, Appendix F) and never governs, including where a rule takes the less flattering of two readings [R6 over J13]. IFRS filers that classify interest paid in financing: move it into CFO before deriving FCF.
  - **Maintenance capex (P20) [R6; Ch9 L1345–1375; Ch14 Flowserve L2472–2480].** For each fiscal year, use the first of these that is available:
    1. **Disclosed.** A company figure for maintenance, replacement or sustaining capex. Or a company figure for growth, expansion or named-project capex (a new plant, fab or store openings, with their spend), with maintenance = capex − growth, floored as in tier 2 (never below that year's depreciation and amortisation of the capex base). Sources: the annual report or 10-K, or earnings-call Q&A. Labelled `disclosed`.
    2. **Constructed (real-growth method).** `growth capex_t = k × max(0, Rev_t − (1 + π_t) × Rev_{t−1})`, where `k = Σ net fixed capital ÷ Σ revenue` over the P1 window (the record that exists, if shorter), and the deflator `π_t` is the higher of 3% (the inflation floor) and the fiscal year's consumer-price inflation in the reporting currency's home economy (the euro-area HICP for EUR; a named official source, logged), so growth is real, never price-led. Where the filer prints organic volume growth for the year, the real growth is the lower of `Rev_t − (1 + π_t) × Rev_{t−1}` and that volume rate × Rev_{t−1}. Net fixed capital is year-end net PP&E (right-of-use assets excluded) plus capitalised development and software; goodwill and acquired intangibles are excluded. Revenue growth is organic: where the filer discloses organic growth, or the revenue of businesses acquired in the year (IFRS 3 B64(q); ASC 805), the acquired revenue is taken out; otherwise reported revenue is used, logged. In a fiscal year whose deals sum above 1% of EV (§2·1C·3) and whose filings disclose neither organic growth nor the acquired revenue, the constructed tier is unavailable and that year takes all capex (tier 3), because a deal would otherwise create growth capex that never went through capex. `Maintenance capex_t = capex_t − growth capex_t`, never below the year's depreciation of PP&E plus amortisation of capitalised development and software (right-of-use depreciation and acquired-intangible amortisation excluded), and never above capex_t. Labelled `constructed`.
    3. **Otherwise all capex is maintenance** (Ch9: knowingly too dark). Labelled `= all capex`.

    Growth must be evidenced: revenue growth above the deflator π_t (never below the 3% inflation floor), or disclosed project spend (tier 1). Nominal growth up to π_t is maintenance, because inflation raises replacement cost (Ch9). Depreciation is never the estimate (Ch9); it is only the floor of a constructed or netted figure. Pre-cycle ratios, peer norms and guided capex are never anchors. A TTM figure (Gate 2) takes a split the interim filings disclose, otherwise the constructed method on TTM figures (TTM revenue against the TTM a year earlier, π on the latest twelve months). Appendix C shows k, each year's deflator and real growth, the growth capex and whether the floor bound.
  - **Lending and leasing books [R2, R6].** The net change of the book of a lending or leasing activity read on STD (finance receivables and assets leased to customers; a captive finance arm included) is that activity's capital investment or release. Where the cash-flow statement carries it, in CFO or as capex, the change beyond ±3% of the prior year-end book leaves FCF in both directions: growth above 3% is growth investment and leaves the deduction; a shrinkage beyond 3% is run-off, a liquidation inflow and not cash generation, and leaves FCF too. A change within ±3% stays where the filer books it. Book additions booked as capex (equipment leased to others) follow this rule instead of the P20 build, and their assets leave net fixed capital. Funding principal booked in CFO (customer deposits, securitisation or warehouse draws and repayments) is not cash generation and leaves FCF; the funding is a debt-equivalent (Gate 2). For an insurance activity read on STD, the change in insurance-contract liabilities net of reinsurance that CFO carries is float funding, not cash generation: beyond ±3% of the prior year-end liability it leaves FCF in both directions, as a book change does [v4.6.0]. Logged in Appendix C.
- **Denominators.** B2 tabulates the YoY series on beginning-of-period CE (prior FY-end balance; an 11th balance-sheet year is required), both cash variants. The rubric tests the **worst of four normalized averages** [BOOK]: average ROCE = Σ OI ÷ Σ CE computed four ways (beginning/end of period × with/without cash); likewise FCFROCE. Growth metrics average by geometric mean. Never tweak individual years. Where G6 applies (P31), the four averages are taken on both of its readings; on each window the worst of the eight governs, and the level bars take the lower of the two windows' worst of eight [v4.6.0]. **Level bars [Ch11 L1631].** Every ROCE, FCFROCE and ROE bar (P2, P3, P4, P24) is tested on the lower of two worst-of-four averages (worst-of-two for ROE: beginning and end of period): the P1 window, and the latest five fiscal years. B2 prints both summary rows.
- **Per-share growth (YoY %):** Δ operating income/share, Δ FCF/share, Δ book value/share, Δ tangible book value/share — all on **fully diluted weighted-average** shares. `BV = equity excluding noncontrolling interests`; `TBV = BV − goodwill − other intangibles`. Read ΔBV and ΔTBV only in the context of dividends and buybacks. **Harsher of two reads [J13].** ΔOI/sh and ΔFCF/sh (ΔNI/sh and ΔTBV/sh under FI) take the worse of:
  - (i) the geometric mean of the year-on-year rates;
  - (ii) the compound rate between the average per-share value of the first three years and that of the last three years, compounded over n − 3 years for a series of n fiscal years (mid-point to mid-point: 7 years in a 10-year window).

  If an endpoint average is ≤ 0 the read is n.m., and an n.m. read never clears a floor.
- **Leverage:** `L/E = total liabilities ÷ BV` — all liabilities, interest-bearing or not; most recent year only (never normalised); capitalised-lease PV belongs in the numerator. L/E above P5 means Past WEAK unless P6 holds. "All other metrics great" means every other Past metric is at the P2 level. Customer advances, deferred revenue and every other non-interest-bearing liability count in L/E, and they are never a P6 argument (Ch11 L1615).
  - **Equity ≤ 0** is beyond the P6 cap. It passes only if all other metrics are great **and** the deficit came from distributions, or from repurchases made at or under the strict CHEAP line, which is clearly below worth on the P19 yardsticks [J14]. Net debt ÷ FCF and interest coverage are logged as context, never used as a way to pass.
- **Groups [R2, R3, R5].** Past is graded once, on the listed entity's consolidated accounts. Every controlled business is in, including a captive finance arm (R2) and a controlled listed subsidiary (R5), together with corporate costs and eliminations. Financial income leaves OI and FCF (J3), and S1/S2 securities count as cash, as built. A material listed holding that the parent does not control is not consolidated: its carrying value leaves CE in both variants, its income leaves OI and FCF, and it is valued at Gate 2 (V14). An ungradeable holding (§2 Group dependencies) is read as a G6 class instead (an equity-accounted one as an associate, any other as S3): on the G6 readings in Gate 1A (P31), with no credit at Gate 2 [v4.6.0]. The consolidated return is the capital-weighted average of the divisions' returns, so no division is graded on its own figures. B4 prints the divisions' segment figures for information and for the Future weights, never for a grade.
  - **Look-through record [R5, V14; review fix B1, 2026-10-07].** Past is never a weighted grade. Where material non-controlled listed holdings exist, it is still graded once, on one record: the consolidated record (holdings out, as above) plus, for each window year, the parent's economic share at that year-end of each material STD holding's J1 OI, maintenance-capex FCF, CE (both variants), total liabilities, BV and TBV, taken from the B record of the holding's own current memo (its fiscal year ending in, or last before the end of, the parent's fiscal year; translated at the parent's year-end reference rate; logged in Appendix C). Where the holding's own memo reads G6 classes above its P31 line, its exclude-reading record is added to the parent's exclude reading and its include-reading record to the parent's include reading. A holding memo with one reading adds that record to both [v4.6.0; G6]. That is proportionate consolidation, the record R5's "equivalent to consolidated" describes. Per-share series use the parent's diluted shares, and P2–P6 and J6 are tested once on that record.
  - **Guards.** (i) The consolidated record's own leverage, equity and decline findings bind the group: where it alone has L/E above P5 without P6, or equity ≤ 0 outside its pass, the group Past is WEAK; where it alone shows a J6 decline, that decline's outcome (the ADEQUATE cap, or WEAK) binds the group; no holding lifts either, because the parent's own debt is carried in full by its owners. (ii) A holding adds nothing for a year its memo's B record lacks, and nothing at all while it is read on FI (an FI memo's B record carries no STD record; its own Past is printed in B4 for information) or is ungradeable (§2 Group dependencies); for those years it is read as a G6 class (an equity-accounted holding as an associate, any other as S3) on the G6 readings in Gate 1A (P31) [v4.6.0; G6].
- **FI lens [J2, J4, R4; PROJECT, softer than the book, which accepts the misses (Introduction L226)].**
  - ROE = Σ NI ÷ Σ attributable equity, at beginning and end of period; the worse governs.
  - NI excludes gains on securities and disposals when their sum over the window is positive, and includes them when it is negative. Banks keep trading income.
  - Grades follow P24. Capital tests replace P5/P6; a group holding a bank and an insurer passes both. An undisclosed capital ratio gives Past WEAK `capital`. All-liabilities L/E is logged.
  - An insurer whose combined ratio over the window is above 100% is capped at ADEQUATE.
  - FCFROCE is n.m., and the sustained-decline rule [J6] applies to ROE.

Flags: L/E above P5 fails the leverage test unless the P6 conditions hold. BV ≫ TBV, or any deal (or fiscal year's deals together) above 1% of EV in the window, runs the §2·1C·3 acquisition stop.

**Sustained decline [P4; J6].** A sustained decline is either:
- (a) three consecutive fiscal-year declines in ROCE or FCFROCE (ROE under FI), on any per-year series the memo computes, with the last decline in one of the two latest years; or
- (b) a pooled worst-of-four average over the latest three years that is more than one third below the window's.

It clears only after two consecutive years without a decline on every series that fired. If the P2 level bars hold on both windows and the P3 growth and leverage conditions are met, the decline caps Past at ADEQUATE. Otherwise Past is WEAK. There is no cyclical or acquisition exemption. The FCFROCE series run on maintenance-capex FCF (P20) [R6], and a capex build-out still counts where it lowers ROCE or that FCFROCE: R6 governs the FCF numerator only, and capital employed stays the book's, so a build-out's assets still weigh in CE (the reading taken). **One-off down year [R1; P28].** Limb (b) does not fire when a single fiscal year that passes the P28 down-year test (Gate 2) produces it alone: re-read with that year's OI and FCF at their P28 governing values (the lower of each one's 3-year and 5-year averages), the latest-three-year average is not more than one third below the window's. Both readings are logged; limb (a) is unchanged. This re-read is an extension of R1 beyond the three uses R1 names (cheapness, the trigger, the sell line), read from R1's "mitigating facts go into the evaluation"; it is on Jakub's veto list, with Kenneth's dissent recorded (BOOK_EXTRACT D30, D42). Gate 1B names the cause.

Verdict **STRONG / ADEQUATE / WEAK** per the §3 rubric (P2–P6 on the consolidated record, or on the look-through record where material non-controlled listed holdings exist, the consolidated record's own guards binding (§2·1A Groups); P24 under FI), with one paragraph of explanation.

#### 1B. Future performance — four qualitative tools; no quantitative forecasting

- **Breadth analysis [BOOK, P15; R7: Future judges market position; it is not a strict judge].** The test runs exactly, with no exceptions, and its result is a finding, never a grade by itself.
  - **Customer [J7; Ch5 L695].** The customer is whoever pays: distributors, wholesalers, retailers (supermarkets included), OEMs, platforms, marketplaces and governments. Entities under common control count as one customer, and so does a buying alliance. Concentration means any paying customer above 10% of revenue. For a business selling to organisations (organisations pay 10% or more of its revenue) it also means silence: no filer statement or customer table that rules such a customer out (Ch12). Silence in a segment note is not a statement, even where the accounting standard would require a major customer to be disclosed, and a statement about credit-risk concentration, receivables or counterparties is not a customer statement. An "economic customer" argument never substitutes for this test.
  - **Supplier.** Concentration means any single supplier above 10% of COGS + operating expenses, unless several potential vendors stand ready to serve (Ch12): named and qualified, by disclosed dual sourcing or a switch actually made within the window. The ready-vendor exception never covers sole-source or specified inputs. A single licence underpinning more than 10% of revenue is supplier concentration. Look through nominal counts to the effective supplier (a securitisation market, a single fab ecosystem); "over 100 suppliers" proves nothing.
  - **Concentration found is FLAGGED (`customer conc.` / `supplier conc.`), never an automatic Future WEAK [R7]. It is weighed in the judgment:**
    - **Strong moat.** Future may pass with the concentration as a caveat, capped at ADEQUATE, when Appendix D evidences all three of the following:
      - (1) the company is dominant: the leading supplier of its product or process by evidenced share with no competitor of comparable share, or the sole or one of two qualified suppliers, with a moat from one identified source (moat identification, below);
      - (2) the position is stable or strengthening. An announced or ongoing in-house replacement or second-sourcing by the concentrated customer means it is not;
      - (3) for a customer concentration, pricing power over the concentrated customers themselves (price increases they accepted, margins held or rising through their negotiations, prepayments or co-investment), so the customer-power force is WEAK; for a supplier concentration, the supplier-power force is WEAK on named evidence (mutual dependence, the company as the supplier's dominant customer, ownership, or a long-term contract with price terms).
    - **Otherwise** the concentration is called a STRONG customer-power (or supplier-power) force for that reason, and P16 decides: ADEQUATE only with named containment and no other STRONG force, otherwise WEAK. Evidence that a distributor is pass-through (the company sets the price or contracts with the end customer, and the distributor holds neither that relationship nor the specification), that at least two named alternatives of comparable reach carry the product or volume has moved between distributors within the window without lost revenue, and that there is no dependence on it for credit, stocking, rebates or price protection is containment evidence, not an exception.
  - **Funding is supply (Ch12 Delta) — strict; R7 leaves it unchanged.** Under FI, for a lending or leasing unit of a group (a captive finance arm included), and for a lender or deposit-taker read on STD, as a whole or as a unit [v4.6.0], `funding conc.` is found if one counterparty exceeds 10% of liabilities, or one channel exceeds 50%, and it means Future WEAK; no moat carries it. The channels are exhaustive: the capital market, one channel (securitisation, CP, bonds and notes of any tenor, bank loans and facilities, interbank and other wholesale funding, and deposits above the deposit-insurance limit or from corporates — in a freeze they close together, Ch12 Delta); the central bank; the parent or group treasury. Insured retail deposits are many counterparties, not a channel; a deposit platform or broker is one counterparty. For a captive finance arm the parent or group treasury is the group itself, never a channel or counterparty: its funding of the arm is looked through, pro rata, to the group's own external channels and counterparties, and the test runs on that mix (strict as ever; the group's bonds, CP and bank lines are the capital-market channel).
- **Forces analysis** [BOOK, P16] — four forces, each called **STRONG or WEAK; medium isn't useful**. Customer bargaining power (weakened by many customers, improbable backward integration, high switching costs) · supplier bargaining power (many suppliers, improbable forward integration, low switching costs) · threat of substitutes (three forms: direct, doing-without, wholly-different-function; kept low by lower substitute value or high adoption switching costs) · threat of new entrants (= barriers to entry **plus barriers to success**; any one of scale, customer switching costs, or hard-to-get permits dulls it). One-line reason per call. Multi-unit businesses get segment-level calls. Tally: 0 strong = favourable; exactly 1 strong = judgment zone; ≥ 2 strong = the book's stop signal (the project completes the memo; Future is WEAK). One STRONG force is ADEQUATE only when Appendix D names the mechanism that contains it: the force does not reach the moat's source, and returns have held against it across the window. Otherwise Future is WEAK (Ch12 L1834: when in doubt, quit). A concentration without a strong moat is a STRONG customer-power or supplier-power call (breadth, above) [R7].
- **Moat identification.** Moats are rare and have a **single identified source** among six: Government · Cost · Brand · Network · Switching costs · Ingrainedness (brand for the channel). Describe the mechanism in 1–3 sentences and state whether it is **strengthening / stable / eroding**. A dominant moat is evidence for weak force calls, and a strong moat can carry a concentration flag (breadth, above) [R7]; there is no other override. A channel holding more than 10% of revenue is not the company's ingrainedness unless the filings show the channel cannot drop the company.
- **Market growth.** YES / NO / MIXED — a basic directional sense suffices; exact rates are neither required nor useful. Guard against the glow an idea develops this late in the pipeline. NO or MIXED caps Future at ADEQUATE.
- **Declines [J6].** Appendix D names the cause of a sustained decline (§2·1A): cycle, capex build, acquired capital, cash accumulation, or competitive erosion. Erosion means the moat is eroding. An unexplained decline bars STRONG.
- **Groups [R3, R5].** Appendix D grades each material unit's Future on this section's rubric, a captive finance arm included. A listed subsidiary's or holding's Future comes from its own current memo, and one without a current memo is ungradeable (§2 Group dependencies). The group Future is the weighted grade (§2 Group grades). An unexplained consolidated decline, or any material part graded WEAK, bars a group STRONG. **A unit that mixes businesses [v4.6.0; G3]** is graded as one unit, from its own Gate-0 sentence: the material unit is the grain of the analysis. Its breadth, force, moat and market calls read the unit on its largest part by revenue, and each reason line names any part on which the call runs the other way; where the parts are unsized, or within ten percentage points of each other, and their calls differ, the harsher call governs [J13]. A part the filer reports with its own figures may get its own block in Appendix D, for information; the unit's grade stays the unit's, and analysing a part never stops grading the unit or the group.

Verdict **STRONG / ADEQUATE / WEAK** [PROJECT tiering of the book's rules]:
- STRONG = breadth with no concentration flag + 0 strong forces + a stable or strengthening moat + market growth YES.
- ADEQUATE = at most 1 strong force, with its containment named + a plausible moat. A concentration flag (carried by a strong moat, or a contained STRONG force), market growth NO/MIXED, or an unexplained decline caps here [R7].
- WEAK = ≥ 2 strong forces (a concentration called STRONG counts), an uncontained strong force, no moat, an eroding moat, or `funding conc.`.

#### 1C. Shareholder-friendliness — defined by the worst; never average

The burden sits with the company everywhere — it is not for the analyst to find a reason to call a factor bad; it is for the company to prove the factor is good (Ch13 L2207). The capital-allocation forward call (·3) is the one deliberate exception: an ADVERSE that rests on behaviour continuing needs the analyst to name the forward evidence [J14; restored from v4.0.0]. Assess all three (depth proportionate to what's glaring); verdict **PASS / FAIL** (FAIL names the strike, the ADVERSE capital-allocation finding, the flag pair, or the single flag that fails at the alignment step).

**Strike logic — three kinds of finding (applies across all three tests) [J13; round 2, 2026-10-07: R8, R9, V3, V4, V13, V17].**
- **Hard strike.** SF FAIL on its own: an egregious pay finding or an egregious outside-director fee (·1; egregious = more than twice the line), a glaring RPT (·2), or a capital-allocation ADVERSE (·3; the worth test and the acquisition stop define it [J14]).
- **Flag.** A scrutiny-level negative: the executive-pay flag (·1), the director-pay flag (·1), a capital-allocation CAUTION (P19), an RPT concern (·2), a controlled listed subsidiary's caution or a material non-controlled listed holding's SF FAIL (Group 1C, §2 Group grades), or another documented governance negative named in the memo.
- **Judgment input.** Never a flag and never a strike: the alignment call (P23), votes versus economics, insider purchases, sales and pledges, family succession, and the dividend posture. Inputs are recorded in Appendix E and act only at step 3.

**The verdict — read in this order; the first step that applies decides:**
1. A hard strike → **FAIL**, naming it. Nothing offsets a strike: not a large insider stake, not disciplined repurchases (Ch13: Swatch's large insider holding did not rescue it, L2185–2209).
2. Two or more flags on distinct behaviours → **FAIL** (`flag pair`).
3. Exactly one flag → **PASS**, with the flag logged as the caution (`sf_reason` = its token). The exception: if the alignment call is NOT ALIGNED **and** votes are separated from economics (P23), the result is **FAIL** (`alignment`). The people who run the company then bear little of the flagged behaviour's cost, and the outside owners cannot check it.
4. No flag → **PASS**, whatever the inputs say. An adverse input is a logged caveat.

Inputs that point different ways are never netted. A favourable input (ALIGNED, open-market buying, worth-disciplined repurchases) never cancels a flag or a strike. An adverse input never fails a company that has no flag. Within the alignment call, the economic holding decides; votes, pledges and sales do not move it. A single flag forces heightened scrutiny and a named re-test, and is never averaged away.
- **Flags aggregate per behaviour.** Repeated observations of one behaviour by the same actors are one flag. Distinct behaviours count separately, even within one test. All executive-pay findings across the three-year pay table are one behaviour and one flag; outside-director fees are a second behaviour. One deal's pre-deal price, its withheld terms and its later write-down are one behaviour. Aggregation never demotes a strike or an ADVERSE.
- **INDETERMINATE [Ch13 Costco L2103].**
  - **(a) Company opacity**: the company withholds what a check needs (scope, rates, counterparties, a per-person pay total). This is a negative. It completes a pair with any determinate flag, but never fails alone and never pairs with another INDETERMINATE. Undisclosed insider holdings are not INDETERMINATE: the alignment call is then NOT ALIGNED (the burden sits with the company).
  - **(b) Input gaps**: figures that do not exist. These never count; name the re-test.

  An input that could have been retrieved but was not is INCOMPLETE, never INDETERMINATE.

1. **Compensation & ownership** [P17, P23].
   - **The hairiest number [R9; Ch13 L2055–2059].** For every executive officer and executive director, and for each fiscal year of the three-year pay table, read every per-person total that the primary documents print, on any basis:
     - grant-date value of awards (the US Summary Compensation Table, a grant-basis remuneration table);
     - realised or vested pay (the UK single total figure, "paid" or "received" tables);
     - the home statutory table (§162 AktG report, SRD II report, Swiss compensation report, Japanese securities-report table, or the named equivalent);
     - any other per-person total the primary documents print, the US pay-versus-performance "compensation actually paid" included.

     Add pension or service cost printed for that person and year where the total excludes it. A **year total** is the largest of these totals for any one person in that year; the **hairiest number** is the largest year total in the table, and it is the number judged. Caps, targets and maxima are not totals. A one-off, front-loaded or multi-year award counts in full, at grant-date value, in the year it was granted; it is never spread. Where no grant-date value is printed, use target shares × the grant-date share price, labelled.

     Where only aggregates are printed, aggregate ÷ members is a floor and the aggregate is a ceiling. A breach of the floor, or a pass under the ceiling, is determinate; anything in between is INDETERMINATE (a).
   - **Adequacy tests [R9, V3]. Each breach is a flag; it is a strike only when egregious.** Egregious means more than twice the line, the factor the book itself puts between its director norm and its stop (Ch13 L2065). Every fiscal year of the table is a test year, read on its year total, so the hairiest number is always read and a smaller total in a thinner year is not missed.
     - **Proportionality** (Ch13 L2061).
       - Line: 5% of the lower of FCF (maintenance-capex basis, P20, R6; the all-capex reading printed beside, never governing) and NI. Under FI the leg is NI.
       - Two reads: the fiscal-year read (the test year's legs, as reported, a P28 down year included: P28 governs price only, and the pay committee paid in that year), and the trailing read (the simple average of each leg over the three fiscal years ending with the test year; in-window years only, fewer at the window edge, logged).
       - Flag: either read above the line.
       - Strike: the fiscal-year read above 10% **and** the trailing read above 5%. One bad year raises a flag, never a strike on its own.
     - **Loss years.** Where the fiscal-year leg (the lower of the two) is ≤ 0, more-than-token pay is a flag; more than token means a year total above the outside-director cap in use (below: the lower of 2× the home seat norm and ≈ US$666k). It is a strike when the trailing leg is also ≤ 0 and the year total rose on the prior year's.
     - **Absolute.** Line: US$30m in 2017 dollars, CPI-indexed (≈ US$40m in 2026), or a lower home benchmark from a named source; the US figure is the ceiling everywhere. Until P17 has the home row, log "home benchmark not set". Flag above the line; strike above twice the line in use (≈ US$80m on the US line).
     - **Big grants.** An award outside the regular annual cycle (special, one-off, retention, make-whole, sign-on, front-loaded or multi-year) whose grant-date value is at least the recipient's regular annual total is a flag. The regular annual total is the average of that person's other years in the table; for a new hire, it is the contracted target total. A big grant is a strike when the grant alone is above twice the proportionality line on the trailing read, or above twice the absolute line.
     - **Home average.** A top-executive total above the home average for the role, from a named source, is a flag (Ch13: Swatch's pay read against its national context, L2067). It is never a strike. Until P17 carries home rows, it is raised only on a source the memo names.
     - **Outside directors [V3; Ch13 L2063–2067].**
       - Cap: the lower of 2× the home norm for the seat and the CPI-indexed book stop (US$500k in 2017, ≈ US$666k in 2026).
       - Flag above the cap; strike above twice it (the lower of 4× the home seat norm and ≈ US$1.33m).
       - Employee representatives are excluded (Ch13 L2063). A chair counts as an outside director unless the filings state a full-time executive role.

     Executive findings, across every limb and every year, are one behaviour: one flag, or a strike where any limb is egregious. Outside-director fees are a second behaviour.
   - **Ownership and alignment [R8, V2, V4; Ch13 L2045, L2077–2085].**
     - **Holdings.** Credit the outright shares that executive officers and executive directors hold, directly or through entities they control, on economic interest only (Ch13 Swatch, L2185–2189).
       - Unvested awards and options earn nothing.
       - Investment funds are ignored (L2079).
       - A parent's stake is never alignment for a subsidiary's outside owners.
       - Shares whose beneficial ownership the insider disclaims (trusts for family members, family members' own holdings) are excluded; the percentage is of shares outstanding at the ownership table's record date.
       - Pledged shares count in full: a pledge is a private financing choice, and the economic exposure stays with the insider.
     - **Alignment call (P23).** A judgment input, never a flag. Holdings are valued at the memo's `px`. The memo prints the figure, not only the band, and more is better. Management is the executive officers and executive directors together, and its holding is their holdings summed. The pay yardstick is the hairiest number (R9, above: the largest year total in the three-year table, whoever earned it; for a team with no year in the table, the top executive's contracted target total). A zero holding never meets a multiple.
       - **ALIGNED:** management holds ≥ 1% of shares outstanding, or management's holding is worth ≥ 10× the hairiest number.
       - **WEAKLY ALIGNED:** management's holding is worth ≥ 3× the hairiest number.
       - **NOT ALIGNED:** below both, or holdings undisclosed.
     - **Insider transactions.**
       - Open-market buying is a positive and is logged (L2083).
       - Option exercises are neutral.
       - Sales and pledges are never a flag or a caveat; insiders may simply need the money (Ch13 L2085). They count only through the holding that remains.
     - **Votes versus economics [V4].** A judgment input, never a flag. Votes are *separated from economics* when a holder, family or pool commands ≥ 20% of the votes, through share classes, loyalty shares, voting agreements or caps, with a voting share ≥ 1.25× its economic share. This acts only at strike-logic step 3. A documented control-related governance negative (not the voting structure itself) is a flag under "another documented governance negative" (`control`).
2. **Related-party transactions.** Number, size, and opacity are inversely correlated with SF. Search the proxy for *related / relationships*; expect leasing, consulting, hiring. Blunt fairness test: the insider entity's share of the relevant total expense line. Dynasties are questionable, not disqualifying — bad nepotism surfaces in ROCE or comp.
   - **Glaring RPT, hard strike [Ch13 Swatch L2197–2245; V17 confirmed 2026-10-07].** A payment that is material under P27, made to an entity of an executive, director or controller (or their family), for functions management or the board is already paid to perform: management, executive, advisory, support, or licensing the controller's name.
     - A material RPT with thin detail is one flag.
     - A checkable RPT that passes the share-of-expense test is a caveat.
   - **Controller as counterparty.** A controller or insider entity that sells shares to the company, or deals in assets with it, is an RPT. Its price is also judged against worth under ·3 (P19 yardsticks): above worth it is a CAUTION there, clearly above worth ADVERSE [J14]. Dealings with subsidiaries are excluded from the parent's test only.
3. **Capital allocation — repurchases, dividends, M&A, reinvestment [P19; the worth test and the acquisition stop, J14].** The question the test answers: **is management allocating capital, today and on its known intentions, so as to sustain the historical ROCE/FCFROCE — and does it treat the minority holder as a partner?** The historical record is the yardstick; the verdict weighs current and expected behaviour most.

**Evidence — collected in full.** Testing is mandatory in every ANALYSE and REVIEW: repurchase cash from the financing section of the cash-flow statement, the share count from the treasury-share movement in the equity statement or share-capital note; where the count genuinely cannot be derived, bound the tranche-year across the fiscal year's full trading range. A window with no repurchases is itself a finding and is recorded as one. Implied MCap = average repurchase price × diluted shares; implied EV adds the debt-equivalents (Gate 2), with and without cash & STI. Every tranche-year is read on that year's OI and maintenance-capex FCF (P20; the all-capex reading printed beside, never governing) and on the trailing-3-fiscal-year averages (§2·1A normalization; in-window years only, fewer at the window edge, logged), on both EV variants; for the live year, that year's figures are the latest fiscal year's, replaced by TTM only when lower (Gate 2). The live programme: the authorisation, its execution to date and management's stated price criteria. The acquisition record: every deal in the P1 window, each analysed as the target stood pre-acquisition — deal EV (price paid incl. assumed debt) ÷ pre-deal normalized OI, and equity price ÷ pre-deal FCF (§2·1A FCF: maintenance-capex basis where the target's figures allow; otherwise all capex, labelled as an upper bound on the multiple, with the EV/OI read governing). The dividend and payout record vs ROCE (read against maintenance-capex FCF), reinvestment vs the returns trend, and the funding source (free cash flow vs debt) complete the picture. All of it goes to Appendix E.

**Worth yardsticks [J14] — calibration for a judgment, never triggers.** Price paid is judged against conservative worth *as knowable at execution* — hindsight re-rating neither convicts nor acquits. The buy line sits clearly below worth: J14 takes the margin of safety behind it as above 50%, so conservative worth is about twice the buy line, and the P9 sell line is where price has clearly flown past worth (Ch17 L2904).
- **Worth ≈ 2× the lens's buy line:** STD EV/OI ≈ 14 (2 × P8) and MCap/FCF ≈ 16 (2 × P7), on the J1 OI and maintenance-capex FCF (P20) [R6]; FI 2 × the P25 band (MCap ÷ NI ≈ 16 on the NI basis); with holding credits, each material holding at its own worth on its figures of that year (look-through, V14; §2 Gate 2).
- **Clearly above worth ≈ the P9 sell line:** EV/OI ≈ 25 and MCap/FCF ≈ 28.6 (25 × 8/7); FI the P25 sell line (28.6 × the higher NI read); holdings at their own sell-line values (V14).

Where a tranche-year's readings (the year and the trailing-3 averages, both EV variants, both primaries) straddle a yardstick, the analyst states which reading governs and why; when in doubt, the less flattering governs. The FCF basis is never one of the straddling readings: maintenance-capex FCF governs, and the all-capex reading is printed, never governing (R6: reinvestment for growth never makes a company look worse).

**The judgment — repurchases: the worth test [J14; Ch13 L2117–2133, and the book's Fossil case, L2219–2243, is its source].** Repurchases below worth are good and at worth acceptable; above worth they are bad, and clearly above worth a red flag (Ch13: at other-than-discount prices they are overpriced acquisitions). The record is read with explicit recency weighting: the trailing three fiscal years, the live programme and any published capital-allocation criteria dominate; the earlier window is context and pattern evidence. **Material** = about 1% of average diluted shares across the trailing three fiscal years plus the live year, counted gross of reissue; below it, tranches are logged, never flagged; sterilising tranches count toward it and take the same floor [V13]. Only executed repurchases form the record; authorisations, pauses and stated intentions are forward evidence. Dividend doctrine is unchanged: dividends are most shareholder-friendly from fully priced companies in mature industries with long strong-FCF histories; rational allocation retains when ROCE is high and distributes when it is not; no empire-building M&A. Verdict, one of three:

- **SOUND.** Current allocation is disciplined: recent material repurchases at or below worth, funded from free cash flow (or none); a dividend posture rational for the return profile; no deal above worth; reinvestment consistent with sustaining the returns record (for a group, read by the allocation-across-the-parts test below, which replaces a separate judgment on the same capital [v4.6.0]). Old tranches above worth do not defeat SOUND by themselves (the earlier-window rule below aside); they sharpen the forward analysis and are said out loud in the log.
- **CAUTION — one flag under the §2·1C pair rule (one per distinct behaviour).** Material repurchases above worth in the recent record, or repurchase pricing drifting above worth while cash generation still covers it; material repurchases clearly above worth in the recent record whose behaviour is no longer live; any CAUTION finding below. Forces heightened scrutiny and a named re-test.
- **ADVERSE = SF FAIL.** Material repurchases clearly above worth in the recent record **and** the behaviour live or expected to continue: the programme executing or freshly authorised without a worth-referenced price discipline, and the stock still above worth. A disclosed pause weakens but does not by itself defeat a live, price-blind authorisation (IDEAS A8, the harsher reading). Also: any ADVERSE finding below; serial value-destructive M&A under the same allocators with no course change; debt-funded distributions that impair the returns engine.

The forward call carries the burden of naming its evidence — current price against worth, the live authorisation and its execution to date, management's stated criteria, the funding source — never bare pattern extrapolation.

**Read on the same yardsticks [J14]:**
- **Sterilisation [Ch13 L2131–2133; V13].** Purchases up to the number of shares issued under equity pay in the same years are read on this limb; any purchases beyond that are ordinary repurchases.
  - The ≈ 1% materiality floor applies. Below it, tranches are logged and never flagged.
  - Material tranches above worth, or clearly above worth, are CAUTION.
  - They are ADVERSE only when **egregious and dilutive**:
    - egregious = tranches clearly above worth in the trailing three fiscal years or the live year, the programme still running (it counts as live while equity pay runs), and the stock still above worth;
    - dilutive = the latest fiscal year's average diluted shares are above those of three fiscal years earlier.
- **Earlier window.** Material repurchases clearly above worth earlier in the P1 window (sterilising tranches included, subject to the same floor) are CAUTION while the CEO, CFO or chair who oversaw them is still in office; otherwise, and for tranches merely above worth, they are context.
- **Purchases of a listed holding's shares** (open market or from third parties) are tranches judged against that holding's own worth yardsticks on its own figures; the cash spent counts toward materiality as cash ÷ the parent's average MCap in the year.
- **Acquisition stop [Ch10 L1488; read against worth under J14, V1 confirmed 2026-10-07; the private-target and write-down limbs are PROJECT].** Every deal completed in the trailing three fiscal years or the live year, and worth more than 1% of the acquirer's EV, is read as it stood before the deal: deal EV ÷ pre-deal normalised OI, and equity price ÷ pre-deal FCF (the target's maintenance-capex FCF where P20 can be built from its last annual report before the deal; otherwise all capex, labelled as an upper bound on the multiple, with the EV/OI read governing) [R6]. A listed target is read from its last annual report before the deal. Where pre-deal OI is undisclosed, the acquiree profit or pro-forma contribution the acquirer must disclose (IFRS 3 B64(q); ASC 805), annualised, is the OI proxy, labelled. Deals are also read per fiscal year in aggregate: when a year's deals sum above 1% of EV, their total consideration plus assumed debt ÷ their aggregate acquiree profit (annualised) is read as one deal, and deals disclosed only in combination are read as one deal.
  - **Price against worth:** at or below worth is sound; above worth is CAUTION; clearly above worth (pre-deal EV/OI ≈ 25 or price/FCF ≈ 28.6) is ADVERSE (`acquisitions`). A disclosed EV/EBITDA or EV/EBITA is a lower bound on EV/OI.
  - **No positive pre-deal OI:** worth is not demonstrated — at least CAUTION.
  - **A listed target below the P3 level** on its own pre-deal record, as its last annual reports show it, is ADVERSE (Ch10 L1488: would I have paid that price?).
  - **Write-downs:** a write-down of ≥ 25% of a deal's goodwill and acquired intangibles, within the trailing three fiscal years, while the CEO or CFO who made that deal is still in office, is evidence of overpayment — CAUTION; ADVERSE when it is large relative to the acquirer or repeated.
  - Consideration or contribution withheld in every primary document, for a deal or a year's deals above 1% of EV, is one determinate flag (CAUTION), never INDETERMINATE; only deals below the acquirer's disclosure threshold are INDETERMINATE (b). Deals clearly above worth earlier in the window, under a CEO or CFO still in office, are CAUTION.
- **Groups: allocation across the parts [v4.6.0; G4; PROJECT].** Every group (§2 Group Gate 0) with two or more material units — conglomerates, trading houses and holding companies included — is judged as an allocator of capital among its units: the question of this section, read across the parts. Purchases of listed holdings' shares are read on their own limb (above).
  - **Units and span.** The reportable segments material at the latest fiscal year-end (P22), on the filer's latest segmentation. The window runs from the earliest fiscal year-end the filer reports, or restates, on that segmentation to the latest, within P1. A window shorter than three fiscal years is INDETERMINATE (b).
  - **Return and line.** Each unit's segment result ÷ its segment assets, on the filer's own measures (equity-method results and carrying values included where the filer puts both in the segment; where it puts one in and not the other, the other is moved to match where the note shows it, else the unit is read as filed, logged; positive fair-value changes and disposal gains on G6 class assets come out of the segment result where the note shows them, a net loss staying in under J3's one-way rule [v4.6.0; G6]), pooled over the latest three fiscal years (Σ result ÷ Σ year-end assets). The line is 10%, P3(b)'s ROCE level; on a segment result struck after tax it is 10% × (1 − the home statutory tax rate).
  - **Growth.** Each unit's change in segment assets from the window's opening year-end to the latest. Growth is the sum of the positive changes; a unit that shrank takes no share.
  - **Finding.** When more than half of the growth went to units below the line, the reinvestment is inconsistent with sustaining the returns record: CAUTION (`reinvestment`), one flag under the pair rule. Capital moved out of units below the line into units at or above it is logged as a favourable input. A deal inside a unit is read on the acquisition stop. A `reinvestment` CAUTION and an acquisition-stop CAUTION on deals into the same below-line units are one behaviour and one flag; an acquisition-stop ADVERSE governs where it applies.
  - **Evidence gaps.** Without segment assets the test is INDETERMINATE (b), logged with its re-test. Segment results and capex by unit, where printed, go into the log as context.

**Logging discipline.** Appendix E states: the verdict and the strike-logic step that decided it (the SF ladder); the pay, director and alignment worksheets; the recency-weighted reading in ≤ 10 lines; the worth table (every tranche-year against the worth and clearly-above-worth yardsticks on both reads and both EV variants, the governing reading where they straddle and why, shares gross, the allocators in office); the acquisition worksheet; for a group, the allocation-across-the-parts table [v4.6.0]; the named forward evidence; every INDETERMINATE item with its class (a)/(b) and named re-test; and confirmation that the consistency hazard was checked — a held or admired name gets no friendlier reading than a stranger would, a disliked one no harsher. Two sessions disagreeing must be able to locate the disagreement in one named judgment line — that is the test of an adequate log.

Source all three from the latest proxy (DEF 14A or local equivalent), the 10-K notes, and the cash-flow and equity statements.

#### Group grades [R3, R5, V14; P29]

**Scoring.** STRONG = 3, ADEQUATE = 2, WEAK = 1. The group score is S = Σ wᵢ × scoreᵢ over the material parts named at Group Gate 0 (§2 Gate 0). The grade is STRONG when S > 2.5 and no material part is graded WEAK (an ungradeable part counts WEAK), ADEQUATE when 1.5 < S ≤ 2.5 or when S > 2.5 with a WEAK part, and WEAK when S ≤ 1.5, so a score on a cut-off takes the lower grade [J13]. A group carrying a WEAK material part therefore never reaches STRONG, and never opens P12. S and every weight are printed in B4. These scores grade the Future only; Past is never weighted (§2·1A Groups).

**Weights.** wᵢ is the part's share of the summed weight base. The base is the average OI of the latest three fiscal years:
- a unit: its segment result after the J1 charges where the segment note allocates them (restructuring, impairment, acquisition amortisation, share-based pay), else the segment result as the filer reports it, logged;
- a controlled listed subsidiary: its segment result, or its own J1 OI where it is not a segment of its own, at 100% (it is consolidated). Where it sits inside one or more reportable segments, those segments' bases are reduced by its J1 OI, pro rata to their revenue unless the filer shows the split (logged);
- a non-controlled listed holding: its J1 OI from its own memo × the parent's economic share (an FI holding: its NI basis ÷ (1 − its statutory tax rate)).

If any material part's base is ≤ 0 or undisclosed, every weight uses the latest-three-year average revenue on the same shares. Immaterial units carry no weight. Corporate costs sit in the consolidated record, never in a weight.

**Future** is the weighted grade of the material parts: each unit graded in Appendix D on the §2·1B rubric, each listed subsidiary and holding taken from its own current memo. **Past** is never a weighted grade: it is graded once, on the consolidated record, or on the look-through record where material non-controlled listed holdings exist, the consolidated record's own leverage, equity and decline findings binding (§2·1A Groups). Parts carry grades, never verdicts; only the whole is bought (Ch16 miscontrast).

**Group 1C** runs once, on the listed entity.
- A controlled listed subsidiary's SF FAIL is the parent's (`inherited`). Its caution counts as one flag. A material listed holding that is not controlled: its SF FAIL is one flag for the parent. An ungradeable listed part (§2 Group dependencies) is INDETERMINATE (b) here, with its re-test named; it is never read as clean, and the parent's own SF tests run in full on the consolidated evidence [v4.6.0].
- The P17 and P27 denominator is the lower of:
  - consolidated NI attributable to the parent's owners (after NCI and the financial-income rule); and
  - consolidated maintenance-capex FCF (P20) × the parent's share (NI attributable ÷ total NI; where total NI ≤ 0, equity attributable ÷ total equity), labelled, plus dividends actually received from material non-controlled listed holdings. A pure holding company whose holdings are not consolidated thus reads its own FCF plus the dividends it actually received.
- P27 materiality is tested against that denominator and against the expense line of the entity that bears the payment; material on either is material.

**Group dependencies [v4.6.0; G4].** A material controlled listed subsidiary or material listed holding is read from its own current memo: no more than 15 months old and written under MODEL v4.5.0 or later (P26); an older-version memo is stale, and so is a DNAF memo written below v4.6.0 (its ground may be one v4.6.0 retired). **A missing or stale memo never stops the parent.** The parent's run completes with that part ungradeable, and the key is queued in the `queue/TODO.md` block `▶ Group dependencies`.
- The dependency key is the subsidiary's or holding's register key when a register row matches on company name or ISIN; otherwise the §7.1a key of its primary line, fixed at Gate 0 by the parent run. A dependency found as a prior under another key is rewritten to that key.
- **Ungradeable** (Ch5: no part of what cannot be graded): a dependency whose memo is missing or stale, whose own memo is DNAF, whose own run ends `[!] incomplete` or `[!] stalled`, or whose venue the price endpoint does not serve. It counts WEAK in the weighted Future with its weight (the harsher reading, J13); `sub dependency` is the Future reason where it decides. A non-controlled holding is read as a G6 class throughout (an equity-accounted one as an associate, any other as S3): on the G6 readings in Gate 1A (P31), it adds nothing to the look-through Past, and it earns no credit, so the band prints `credit owed` and `holding_keys` lists only the memos read (n.m. if none). A controlled listed subsidiary sits inside the consolidated Past in any case. Its SF is INDETERMINATE (b) for the parent (Group 1C).
- **No verdict rests mainly on unread parts.** While ungradeable parts (listed parts and undescribed units together) carry more than a third of the P29 weight base, the group Future is WEAK (`sub dependency`, or `unit dependency` where an undescribed unit carries the larger share). The parent still completes with that Future, so no BUY, WATCH or ADD rests mainly on what was not read.
- When a dependency's memo lands, each parent that read it as ungradeable is listed for REVIEW priority 1c (`` `PARENT` — parent re-run owed (HKEY memo {date}) ``, `routines/INTAKE.md`), and the re-run grades the part from that memo. When a dependency is found RETIRED or without a listed security, the parent is listed the same way (`` (HKEY retired {date}) `` or `` (HKEY no listed security {date}) ``), and the re-run reads the part from the parent's own filings: a controlled one consolidated like any other controlled business, a non-controlled one as a G6 class (an equity-accounted one as an associate, any other as S3) [v4.6.0; G5].

### Gate 2 — Is it cheap?

At the current price. **EV = MCap + debt-equivalents − cash & short-term investments**, computed **both ways** — all cash excess and none — and for CHEAP both versions must pass. **MCap** counts every economic share class.

**Debt-equivalents [v4.5.0; list extended J13]:**
- borrowings including current maturities, at the higher of carrying amount and principal or redemption amount;
- lease liabilities (§2·1A);
- preferred and hybrid capital at 100% whatever its accounting classification;
- NCI of unlisted subsidiaries at book (put value where redeemable); a listed subsidiary's NCI at the higher of market value at `px_date` and book [R5; Ch14]. Where MCap already counts every economic unit (Up-C paired shares), the NCI those units represent is not added again;
- earn-out and acquisition put/call liabilities at carrying fair value, the maximum payable logged;
- obligations to a controlling holder that are frozen or deferred;
- net unfunded defined-benefit and other post-employment obligations (never net of tax; a surplus is never deducted);
- supplier-finance liabilities (also taken out of NIBCL);
- decommissioning provisions whose unwinding sits in finance costs;
- guarantees of non-consolidated debt;
- under STD, the deposits and other funding of a lending, leasing or deposit-taking activity, a captive finance arm included [R2].

**Funding pairing.** Where that funding sits in EV, EV/OI pairs it with OI before the funding cost (financing-blind); the reading with the funding left out of EV pairs with OI after it. Both consistent pairings are computed and the lower band governs; a mixed pairing (funding in EV, OI after its cost) is never used. The same holds wherever else EV meets OI [v4.6.0]: the P9 sell line takes the pairing that gives the lower sell-line price, and each P19 worth read takes the pairing that gives the higher multiple. Gate 1 grades on OI after funding cost (§2·1A).

**Never cash:** restricted, pledged, escrowed or regulatory cash; segregated client funds; cash under capital controls; under STD, the cash and securities that back deposits, policyholder liabilities or other customer funds (a bank's liquidity portfolio, an insurer's investment portfolio), which stay out of every cash deduction: in CE as a deposit-taker's operating book, and on the G6 readings in Gate 1A for an insurance activity's investments (§2·1A, P31) [v4.6.0].

|Metric                    |Threshold|Rank      |Notes                                        |
|--------------------------|---------|----------|----------------------------------------------|
|MCap / FCF                |≤ 8 (P7) |**Primary**|Levered FCF on the P20 maintenance basis (§2·1A); latest audited FY, an interim TTM figure replaces it only when lower (Ch11 L1579), or the P28 down-year average|
|EV / Operating income     |≤ 7 (P8) |**Primary**|Sage metric — financing-blind; both EV variants; the J1 OI, latest audited FY, an interim TTM figure replaces it only when lower, or the P28 down-year average|
|MCap / Book value         |≤ 3 (P10)|Qualifier |Dead-company metric                            |
|MCap / Tangible book value|≤ 3 (P11)|Qualifier |Dead-company metric                            |

**Primaries decide.** CHEAP requires both primaries at or under threshold (with both EV variants in the bargain bin); when Past **and Future** are STRONG, one primary may run to ≤ 1.25× on the latest-FY maintenance-capex basis (never on a P28 average or a backvalued year), with the other passing on both EV variants (P12).

**Qualifiers investigate.** A qualifier breach never blocks mechanically; it mandates a named check whose adverse finding blocks: BV ≫ TBV → run the Gate-1C acquisition audit; TBV depleted/negative from buybacks → both qualifiers are distorted identically — mark inapplicable and say so; both qualifiers high and similar with modest goodwill → check for understated tangible assets (informational); qualifier breach on an asset-light franchise with STRONG returns → log and proceed (Nike-at-3×-book precedent). Where the consolidated entity holds parent stock, state gross and look-through readings; the more conservative bears the verdict [v4.5.0].

**Securities [J3; every lens] (class table in Appendix C).**
- **S1, cash-like** (deposits, bills, government money-market funds and bonds maturing within 12 months, at the lower of carrying amount and market; other money-market funds, longer bonds and bond funds at market × 0.9, Ch14 L2324: cash only as far as it converts at balance-sheet value), and **S2, free listed equity that is immaterial** (never an equity-accounted associate or JV: significant influence is not free equity, so it is H where material, else S3 at Gate 2 and a G6 class in Gate 1A [v4.6.0; G6]): both count as **cash**. They stay in CE_wc, are deducted in CE_xc and in the all-cash EV variant, and stay out of the no-cash variant and MCap/FCF (Ch14 L2324: when in doubt, not cash).
  - S2 = market × (1 − t) × (1 − h).
  - t = statutory tax on the gain over the disclosed tax base. An undisclosed base counts as zero; a participation exemption applies only where the parent's own filing invokes it for that holding; use the deferred-tax liability if it is larger.
  - h = 10% for a stake of ≥ 5% of the investee, and 25% for a controlling stake or where the investee's free float is under 25% (P26). h applies to S2 only; H carries no haircut (V14).
- **H, a material listed holding the parent does not control (P22) [V14]: credited at intrinsic value, never at market price or NAV.** The stake is valued on the holding's own current memo (its core numbers, and its own holding credits at the same yardstick), at the yardstick of the parent's line it enters (look-through):
  - **buy-line legs:** the holding's no-cash buy line, min(8 × FCF, 7 × OI − debt-equivalents) (FI: its P25 band), which is its operative trigger × its diluted shares;
  - **worth reads (§2·1C·3):** its worth, min(16 × FCF, 14 × OI − debt-equivalents) (FI: 16 × NI basis);
  - **sell line:** 25 × OI − debt-equivalents + cash (FI: 28.6 × the higher NI read).

  v is the parent's economic share of that line, floored at 0, converted into the parent's reporting currency at the reference rate of the parent's `px_date`. H = v − max(t × max(0, v − tax base), the deferred-tax liability on the stake), never below 0, with t and the tax base as for S2. There is no block haircut. A holding without a current memo earns no credit (§2 Group dependencies). A material holding the filer calls strategic, core or long-term, or one that is a customer, supplier or partner of the group, is H, never S3.
- **S3, no credit**: unlisted stakes; pledged, blocked or locked-up portions; immaterial strategic holdings (strategic as defined under H: the filer calls the stake strategic, core or long-term, or the investee is a customer, supplier or partner of the group; any other immaterial listed equity is S2); cross-holdings; own or parent stock; anything whose saleability is in doubt. In Gate 1A the stake and its income are read together on the G6 readings (§2·1A, P31); at Gate 2 it earns no credit, and its income stays out of OI and FCF [v4.6.0].

**Listed subsidiaries [R5].** A material controlled listed subsidiary stays consolidated. Its OI, FCF, debt and cash are the group's, its minority's claim enters EV at the higher of market and book (debt-equivalents, above), and it earns no credit. It is analysed under its own key, where the parent counts as a controlling holder: it gets no alignment credit, and its dealings with the subsidiary are RPTs.

**Gate 2 with holding credits [V14].** C = Σ H at the buy line, N = diluted shares, and FCF, OI, the debt-equivalents D and the cash K are the consolidated figures with the holdings' income out (§2·1A Groups).
- MCap/FCF = (MCap − C) ÷ FCF, which is 0.0 when MCap ≤ C and FCF > 0. EV/OI no-cash = (MCap + D − C) ÷ OI; the all-cash variant also subtracts K.
- Legs: P_FCF = (8 × FCF + C) ÷ N; P_nc = (7 × OI − D + C) ÷ N; P_ac = P_nc + K ÷ N. The trigger and the ADD band take these legs as for any STD name (§7.1, P21).
- The sell line uses Σ H at their sell-line values: (25 × OI − D + K + Σ H) ÷ N. The worth reads use Σ H at worth.
- No market price moves the credit, so no scaling applies (§7.1b). Appendix F prints the credit table: each holding's three values before and after tax.

**Gate 2 under FI [P25].** NI basis = the lower of latest-FY NI (its P28 average in a one-off down year) and P1-average NI attributable to the parent's owners, under the FI gains rule; N = diluted shares. For an insurer, V = cash and fixed income at fair value + listed equities (S2/H rules) − insurance liabilities net of reinsurance − debt + 8 × the after-tax underwriting basis. This is J2's Berkshire method: the float is the prior claim on the securities. Insurance liabilities are every insurance and investment-contract liability (IFRS 17: the contract liabilities with their CSM and risk adjustment; otherwise claims, unearned-premium and life reserves), net only of reinsurance recoverable from rated, unrelated reinsurers; assets held at policyholders' risk and their matching liabilities net to zero. The underwriting basis is the insurance result before investment income (premiums or fees less claims, benefits and expenses; for a unit-linked or fee insurer, its fee result), the lower of the latest FY and the P1 average, taxed at the statutory rate, and negative if negative.
- Band = min(8 × NI basis, 3 × TBV) ÷ N for a bank, and min(8 × NI basis, V) ÷ N for an insurer. TBV (or V) binds inside the band; it is not a qualifier here. CHEAP = price at or under the band, which also means MCap ÷ NI ≤ 8 on both the latest and the normalised reads. The ADD band is the same figure.
- EV/OI is n.m.
- Sell line = 28.6 × the higher NI read ÷ N.

**One-off down year [R1, V11; P28].** Cheapness reads the latest fiscal year. When the latest fiscal year is a one-off down year, the lower of the 3-year and 5-year average OI governs instead: for cheapness, the trigger band, the ADD band and the sell line [R1]. FCF is tested separately in the same way. Both averages are simple averages ending with, and including, the latest fiscal year, on the J1 OI and the maintenance-capex FCF as the B1 record prints them (the filer's restated figures where it restates). The latest year is a one-off down year only when the memo shows all of the following:
1. **Down:** the latest-FY figure is below the lower of the two averages.
2. **Named cause:** identified events, quantified from the filings, explain more than half of the shortfall against the 5-year average: an impairment or write-down, a restructuring programme, or a disruption (strike, outage, disaster, recall, a one-time litigation or regulatory charge).
3. **One year:** the prior fiscal year was not itself down under tests 1–2, and no charge of the same kind exceeded 10% of OI in any of the four prior fiscal years (a one-off that recurs was never one, Ch11 L1641).
4. **Recovery evidenced, nothing structural [review fix B2, 2026-10-07].** The evidence depends on the cause:
   - **a disruption** (strike, outage, disaster, recall, a one-time litigation or regulatory charge): the cause has demonstrably ended (the plant restarted, the strike settled, the recall closed, the charge paid or settled), or interim results published after the down year are at or above the same periods of the last fiscal year before it;
   - **an impairment, write-down or restructuring:** the booked charge proves nothing, since an impairment is the filer's own evidence of lower expected cash flows (IAS 36; ASC 350), so recovery needs operating evidence on both of these: the down year's OI before the named charges (a test reading only, never OI: J1) is at or above the lower of the two averages; and interim results published after the down year are at or above the same periods of the last fiscal year before it. Where the impaired or restructured business is a reportable segment, its own segment result passes the interim test too. With no interim yet published, the test is unmet;

   and in every case the moat is not eroding, no STRONG doing-without or different-function substitute exists, no perimeter change in the window removed the profit source, and Future is not WEAK.

In doubt, the latest year governs [J13]. On the averaged path the TTM-replacement rule does not apply (test 4 replaces it). The average never feeds P12. Where backvaluing also qualifies, the lower of the two readings governs. Under FI the test runs on NI (FI gains rule) in place of OI and FCF: a qualifying year's NI is replaced by the lower of its 3-year and 5-year averages inside the P25 NI basis, which still takes the lower of that and the P1 average. Appendix F prints the test, its evidence and both readings; `core_oi_basis` and `core_fcf_basis` carry ` · down-year avg` (§6.1).

**Averaged readings [R10].** Every memo prints MCap/FCF and EV/OI (both EV variants) at px on the 3-year and 5-year average OI and FCF: simple averages ending with, and including, the latest fiscal year, on the same basis and with the same credits as the primaries, against the current MCap, debt-equivalents and cash. Under FI the reading is MCap ÷ average NI (FI gains rule), and EV/OI is n.m. A reading is n.m. when the record has fewer fiscal years than its average needs, or its base is ≤ 0. The readings are context, never a route to CHEAP, unless P28 makes one govern. The register carries MCap/FCF and the no-cash EV/OI of both (§7.1, §7.1b).

**Backvaluing [Ch14 L2338–2344].** Backvalue only when both of these hold:
- the industry is definitively cyclical: demand is exogenous, and a full trough and recovery is visible in the company's own window OI;
- the latest year is below the prior year.

Use the **immediately prior year's** FCF (maintenance-capex basis, P20) and OI, never a peak or an average. Every Flowserve proviso must be shown: the acquisition audit is clear; there is no change in perimeter or capital intensity; and there is no secular decline (a STRONG doing-without or different-function substitute bars it). Any doubt means no backvaluing. It applies to price only. The result prints "CHEAP (backvalued)" and needs both primaries on both EV variants.

Verdict **CHEAP / FAIR / EXPENSIVE**: CHEAP = primaries pass as above and qualifier investigations (if triggered) clear. FAIR = primaries above threshold (beyond any applicable P12 edge) but within ~75% above it. EXPENSIVE = beyond. A primary that is n.m. because its base (FCF, OI or the NI basis) is ≤ 0 reads EXPENSIVE.

**Trigger band (always compute).** The limit price: the share price at which **MCap/FCF ≤ 8 and EV/OI ≤ 7 simultaneously hold** — the lower of the FCF leg, `P = 8 × FCF ÷ diluted shares`, and the EV/OI leg, on the governing OI and FCF (the latest FY, TTM only when lower, or the P28 average). **The EV/OI leg is computed on both EV variants and reported as a labelled range:** `P_no-cash = (7 × OI − debt-equivalents) ÷ diluted shares` and `P_all-cash = (7 × OI + cash & STI − debt-equivalents) ÷ diluted shares`. FI names use the P25 band; names with holding credits use the credited legs (above). Excess cash is unknowable (§2·1A), so the bracket that governs CE carries through to price; where the two bounds converge within ~5% print the single figure and say they converge; where they diverge the width is itself the finding — the verdict turns on a cash judgment, which the P12 edge can bridge when Past and Future are STRONG. **Print both bounds labelled `no-cash / all-cash`; never a bound without its label, never one bound without the other.** CHEAP requires **both** variants to pass, so **the no-cash bound — the lower of the FCF leg and the EV/OI no-cash leg — is the strict all-pass line and the operative trigger** (the buy-zone price) [R10; Ch3]. The all-cash bound, capped by the FCF leg (IDEAS C6), is printed beside it, labelled, as context; it never governs. The band prints the trigger first. State which metric binds. **Where a band leg admits no positive price (`n.m.` — debt-stack exceeds 7×OI, or no positive OI), state the operating-income level that restores the no-cash bound [v4.2.0]:** net-cash dominance, no positive OI, a structurally unattainable OI and a near miss all print identically as `n.m.`, and the restoring level is what distinguishes them. Where Past and Future are STRONG, also state the P12 outer edge. Report the qualifier-implied prices (3 × BV/sh, 3 × TBV/sh) for context — they are not band inputs except 3 × TBV in the FI band. Mechanical threshold arithmetic from the framework, not a price target.

**Sell-flag line (HELD names only — always compute).** The share price at which the *conservative* reading of P9 trips: `P_sell = (25 × OI + cash & STI − debt-equivalents) ÷ diluted shares` — the inversion of EV/OI = 25 on the all-cash-excess (lower) EV variant, so the flag fires only when even the flattering multiple exceeds 25. FI names use the P25 sell line; names with holding credits use the credited sell line (above). The OI is the governing OI: the latest-FY J1 line, or the P28 down-year average [R1]. **Mitigating facts go into the evaluation (Ch17 L2904) [V11].** Beyond P28, a fact counts only when it is evidenced in filings or a binding announcement and quantified: a signed disposal or acquisition, read pro forma as the filer discloses it, with OI and EV moved together (an acquisition brings the target's OI in with the consideration, the cash paid out of cash & STI or the new debt, and the debt it assumes; a disposal takes the disposed OI and the debt that leaves with it out and brings the agreed proceeds into cash, net of the tax the filer states; a one-sided pro forma never counts); a business consolidated for only part of the year (the filer's annualised acquiree result); or a debt-equivalent that reverses (a disputed tax or litigation amount held in EV). When the mitigated reading is within the line (EV/OI ≤ 25 on the all-cash variant; under FI, price within 28.6 × the mitigated NI read ÷ N; with holding credits, the credited line), the flag does not fire: the zone is HOLD, labelled `SR1 mitigated`, with the facts and both readings in Appendix F. Guidance, forecasts and "temporarily depressed" without a named cause are never mitigating facts. In doubt, the flag fires [J13].

### Psychology check — before finalising any verdict, and before any ADD / SELL-FLAG zone statement

Tick through the 18 cognitive biases (affinity, reciprocity, anchoring, authority, availability, cleverness, incomprehensibility, consensus, peculiarity, intermixing, consistency, confirmation, hope, lossophobia, scarcity, hotness, miscontrast, windfallapathy) and the two forms of akrasia (impetuosity, weakness). The tick-through is required before any buy **or sell** order; zone outputs on held names inherit the requirement. A judgment is invalid only when *born of* a bias. Log any live one in Appendix C with how it was handled; otherwise one line: "Bias check: none live." For held names, the two named hazards: **consistency** (adding to a holding whose real flaws have surfaced) and **lossophobia** (selling into a price drop while fundamentals hold — a price drop is never itself a worth drop).

## §4. DATA PROTOCOL

- **Source hierarchy** [BOOK: filings beat data services — legally accountable, uninterpreted]: (1) company filings — latest 10-K / 20-F / annual report, and the latest proxy for Gate 1C; quarterlies for numbers that must be timely more than audited (current share count, latest balance sheet for EV); (2) one dense aggregated-fundamentals page (e.g., stockanalysis.com, macrotrends, or the SEC XBRL companyfacts API) for the multi-year series — verify judgment-sensitive items (NIBCL composition, lease tables, comp, RPT) against the primary filing; (3) any quote source for current price and market cap. IR e-mail is a legitimate tool for factual questions where feasible; note that the quality of the reply is itself information. List every source in Appendix A.
- **Tier-2 acceptance test [v3.9.0].** An aggregated multi-year series is accepted only on an **exact tie-out**, at the reporting unit, against the primary filings for the latest covered year and one earlier year, on four anchors: revenue, operating income, equity, and **one derived composite that reconciles only if several underlying fields are simultaneously correct** (e.g. net financial position rebuilt from cash + short-term investments − total debt + lease liabilities, which validates the debt/lease split that drives CE, EV and L/E). Exact means exact: a rounding break means the source has restated something, and the series is rejected and rebuilt from filings. On a pass the series is accepted **for the covered years only** — years the source does not carry are a data gap, never an accepted series. Record the anchors and the years tested in Appendix A.
- **Primary-interim-debt rule [v4.2.0].** At every REVIEW touch, the EV inputs that move bands — debt, lease liabilities, cash & STI, preferred, NCI — refresh from the primary interim balance sheet; aggregator figures never update EV inputs (adopted after four logged drift instances, latest NET/ZBRA 2026-08-09).
- **Extraction-conflict rule [v4.2.0].** Never accept a single extraction of a figure that a second document class also carries; where two extractions disagree, rebuild from the primary, ring-fence the consequences, and disclose the conflict in the memo front matter (Ørsted/BH precedents).
- **Excluded hosts [v4.5.0].** A host or path excluded by robots.txt is unretrievable in a routine run (log the host and rule once); an attended session uses such a document only when the user supplies or approves it.
- **Never invent a number.** Write "n/a — not disclosed" rather than guessing. Label every derived figure (e.g., "FCF derived = CFO − maintenance capex, P20 constructed"). Flag every data gap prominently in Appendix A and in the memo body if it affects a verdict.
- **Currency & listings:** keep the reporting currency throughout; note ADR ratios. Fewer than P1 years: proceed with what exists, flag it, and apply the §2·1A cap with its history-/record-limited label.
- **Budget (the P14 rule; §3 records the numbers).** The cap counts **exploratory search only** — search-engine queries and speculative page fetches. **Primary-document retrieval is uncounted**: filings, proxy / remuneration / governance / RPT documents, the IR index pages needed to locate them, and price or quote endpoints — this hierarchy mandates them, so the cap must not fight it. GitHub calls (git and `gh api`, §7.4a) never count, and RECONCILE does no web work at all (§8.5). Routine runs (§8.7) follow the same rule; their per-job numbers — including REVIEW's due-check operations and QUEUE-SEED's discovery and decay-pass queries — are recorded in P14 [v4.4.0]. A **directed enquiry** — a user question arriving after a memo is delivered — carries its own allowance, logged separately, and never draws down the originating ANALYSE budget. An aggregator rejected on the tier-2 acceptance test licenses the primary rebuild at no exploratory cost. Report the document count in Appendix A; more than ten documents on one name is itself a disclosure-quality observation worth a line in the memo. Never economise on primary sources; prefer one dense source over many shallow ones; never re-fetch data already in a prior memo (that is REVIEW's delta approach) — but write it out again in full: a REVIEW memo is self-contained (§6.6) [v4.5.0].

### §6.4 Abbreviated memo (Gate 0 failure only)

Front matter (verdict DNAF; the §6.1 machine fields with `n.m.` where no gate ran) + body sections 1 (Verdict) and 2 (Understanding: the best sentence the analyst could attempt, the parameter that could not be determined, and the documents searched) + the register row. ### §6.5 After every memo

Run the §7.4a session-end sequence: print the register diff and file list, take the confirm, commit memo (+ the move of a superseded prior memo to `archive/memos/<KEY>/`, §7.4a step 3) + §7.5 snapshot pair + `register.csv` and the re-rendered `_REGISTER.md`, both through `routines/tools/gsc_register.py` [v4.5.0], in one commit, merge it by PR, report the URLs — plus the portfolio row if the name is HELD. ### §6.6 REVIEW memo — one complete, self-contained current memo [v4.5.0]

Every REVIEW writes **one complete memo that stands alone**: a reader holding only this file has every figure, worksheet and judgment the verdict rests on. It folds the prior memo's findings and the new evidence into one current analysis; nothing in it depends on reading an earlier version.

- **Complete.** Full §6.1 front matter — `memo_version` the prior's number + 1, `supersedes:` naming the archived prior — and every §6.2 body heading and every §6.3 appendix heading A–G, each exactly once. The body keeps the P13 cap.
- **Current worksheets.** B1–B3 (and B4 for groups) carry the full P1 window: the new fiscal year appended, the oldest dropped as the window rolls, the four-way summaries recomputed. C, D, E and F are complete worksheets at this version's figures — E with the pay, director and alignment worksheets, the full worth table, the acquisition worksheet and the SF ladder, F with both EV variants (and the holding credits).
- **Sources.** Appendix A lists every source any figure rests on; a source carried from an earlier version keeps its original retrieval date.
- **No pointers.** Carried content is restated in full; a provenance tag `(carried from v{n}, {YYYY-MM-DD})` may follow it. Pointers to an earlier version — "unchanged from v{n}", "see v{n}", "as in v{n}", "incorporated by reference from v{n}", "delta only" or "not repeated" against v{n} — and any memo file name are forbidden in the body and Appendices A–F; memo file names appear only in `supersedes:` and Appendix G.
- **Delta fetch, full write.** "Fetch only the delta" (§8.2) limits web work, never what is written: figures the prior chain already holds are reused, not re-fetched, and written out again. Inputs §4 refreshes at every REVIEW touch (primary-interim-debt rule) are re-derived, never copied.
- **Legacy prior.** A prior memo that points to an earlier version is resolved from `archive/memos/<KEY>/` (repo reads, never P14). A routine roll-up that cannot be finished within the run's time budget is `INCOMPLETE (roll-up exceeds budget)` and routes an attended roll-up to `queue/TODO.md`; routine REVIEW skips the key while that item is open (`routines/REVIEW.md` step 2).
- **Gate 0.** A Gate-0 outcome writes the §6.4 abbreviated memo plus `supersedes:` and Appendix G.
- **Move, never edit.** The same commit or PR moves the prior memo, unchanged and under its own filename, to `archive/memos/<KEY>/` (§7.4a step 3, §7.5); `memos/<KEY>/` then holds only the new memo. Memos are never edited in place.
- **Checked.** The commit-time compliance re-measure runs the self-containment check (§7.4a; the exact checks are in `routines/README.md` §5).

## §7. REGISTER, PORTFOLIO & ECOSYSTEM

### §7.1 Register

`_REGISTER.md` **in the GitHub repo (§7.4)** is the master watchlist and the **source of truth** on state; the project-knowledge copy is a convenience mirror. **`register.csv` beside it is its machine twin [v4.5.0]:** every register column plus the core numbers (§7.1b) and the position record, one line per key, in the rendered order. Both change in one commit and only through `routines/tools/gsc_register.py`: `register.csv` is updated (`apply`, `position`, `rekey`, `retire` [v4.6.0]) and `_REGISTER.md` is rendered from it (`render`) — never edited by hand (§7.4a generation rule). One row per company, from its current memo only; a REVIEW replaces the row.

**Lean format [v4.5.0] — the register summarises the research; it holds no prose.** A front-matter block, the H1, a legend of at most two lines, and tables. Master-table columns:

```
| Ticker | Company | Memo date | Ver | Verdict | Past | Future | SF | Px | Trigger | MCap/FCF | EV/OI | MCap/FCF 3y · 5y | EV/OI 3y · 5y | Next review |
```

- **Ticker** — the §7.1a key only. Aliases live in the memo's `aliases:` and in `register.csv`, never in the table.
- **Verdict** — one word.
- **Past · Future · SF** — the grade, then ` · ` and the deciding reason in at most three words from the vocabulary below: `STRONG · 28% ROCE`, `WEAK · ROCE decline`, `WEAK · breadth`, `FAIL · buybacks`, `PASS`. A PASS carrying one flag (an SF caution) prints `PASS CAUTION · {reason}`. A grade with no established reason prints alone, and the gap is a backfill item in `queue/TODO.md`.
- **Px · Trigger** — plain numbers in the listing's quote unit (`px_unit`, the price endpoint's `currency`; GBp, ZAc, ILA and KWF are minor units), with no currency symbol and no thousands separator. Trigger is the operative trigger (below); `n.m.` where none exists.
- **MCap/FCF · EV/OI** — one decimal, computed at the Px shown (§7.1b); EV/OI is the no-cash, strict reading. `n.m.` where undefined; `-` where the core numbers are owed.
- **MCap/FCF 3y · 5y · EV/OI 3y · 5y [R10]** — the averaged readings at the Px shown (§2 Gate 2, §7.1b), `{3y} · {5y}`, EV/OI no-cash; `n.m.` per side; `-` where owed (a cell with both sides owed prints `-`). Context only: the order still follows EV/OI.
- **Next review** — `YYYY-MM-DD`, `Qn YYYY` or `Mon YYYY` (`n.m.` for DNAF; `retired YYYY-MM-DD` for RETIRED, below): the memo's `next_review_short`. The full trigger stays in the memo's `next_review`.
- **Format contract [v3.3.0; lean v4.5.0].** A cell is a token, never a sentence; no cell exceeds **160 characters**. **The register points, the memo explains:** bands and their legs, basis tags, flags, force tallies, distances and rationale of any kind live in the memo (§6.2 body, §6.3 appendices). Anything a cap forces out is, by construction, already in a memo; if it is not, it goes into the memo at the next touch — never back into the register.

**Reason vocabulary [v4.5.0] — fixed; a new token goes to `queue/IDEAS.md` before use.** Past: `{x}% ROCE` (the worst-of-four average, the worst of eight where G6 applies, that decided the grade; `{x}% ROE` under the FI lens), `ROCE decline`, `FCFROCE`, `leverage`, `losses`, `growth`, `record-limited`, `capital` (FI capital test failed or undisclosed) [v4.5.0]. Future (WEAK; or the caveat of an ADEQUATE carried with a concentration flag, which prints `ADEQUATE · customer conc.` or `ADEQUATE · supplier conc.` [R7]; STRONG and every other ADEQUATE print the grade alone): `breadth`, `customer conc.`, `supplier conc.`, `funding conc.` [v4.5.0], `no moat`, `moat eroding`, `2+ forces`, `forces: customers` · `forces: suppliers` · `forces: substitutes` · `forces: entrants`, `dependency` (an ungradeable part, counted WEAK in the weighted Future; printed `sub dependency` for a listed subsidiary or holding and `unit dependency` for an undescribed material unit [v4.6.0]) [R5; a Future token since the review fix of 2026-10-07, when Past stopped being weighted]. SF (a FAIL, or the one-flag caution on a PASS): `compensation`, `director pay`, `buybacks`, `acquisitions`, `distributions`, `reinvestment` (a group's allocation across its parts, §2·1C·3), `RPT`, `control`, `dilution`, `alignment` (a single flag failed at strike-logic step 3, §2·1C), `flag pair`, `inherited` (a controlled listed subsidiary's FAIL) [v4.5.0; round 2: `pledging` retired, `alignment` new, R8; `reinvestment` new v4.6.0, G4]. `alignment`, `flag pair` and `inherited` occur only on a FAIL. `control` names a documented control-related governance negative, never the bare voting structure (V4). **Group reasons [R3, R5]** may carry one prefix, `unit` or `sub`, within the 3-word limit (`unit breadth`, `unit 2+ forces`, `sub dependency`, `sub moat eroding`). A weighted WEAK prints the reason of the heaviest-weighted WEAK part, and a weighted ADEQUATE the caveat of the heaviest-weighted part carrying one (`unit customer conc.`), else the grade alone; a Past reason never carries a prefix (Past is graded once, on the consolidated or look-through record, §2·1A Groups). **Precedence.** Where several reasons decide one grade, the cell prints the first failing one in this vocabulary's order (Past: a failing `{x}% ROCE` level first, then the list as written; Future and SF: the list as written); the memo body names the others. Part grades never enter the register; only the group's grade and reason do. The memo carries them as `past_reason`, `future_reason` and `sf_reason` (§6.1), `n.m.` where the grade prints alone.

**Sections and order [v4.5.0; replaces the v3.2 Dist % rule; RETIRED v4.6.0].** PORTFOLIO (§7.2) → BUY → WATCH → AVOID - one gate failed → AVOID - two or more gates failed → DNAF → RETIRED (printed while a RETIRED row exists). BUY, WATCH and both AVOID sections sort by **EV/OI ascending**; `n.m.` rows follow, then `-` rows; ties sort by key. DNAF and RETIRED sort by key. Gates failed = the count of Past WEAK, Future WEAK and SF FAIL; an AVOID row with none, or a BUY or WATCH row with one, is a register-integrity breach (§2 verdict mapping). A Gate 0 failure is DNAF (§2 Gate 0). Section counts and the verdict split live in the front matter, derived from the rows; the order recomputes at every regeneration. Distance to the buy zone (Px ÷ Trigger − 1) is computed by SWEEP for its PR body and by any session that needs it, never stored in the register.

**Px [v4.5.0].** Held, BUY, WATCH and one-gate AVOID rows carry the latest price: the newest SWEEP close INTAKE has applied (§8.3), else the memo's `px`. Two-or-more-gate AVOID and DNAF rows carry the memo's `px` and are never refreshed; RETIRED rows keep the Px they carried when retired (below). The multiples always match the Px shown.

**RETIRED [v4.6.0; G5].** A key whose listed line has ceased to trade is RETIRED: a take-private or merger has closed, a squeeze-out is registered, or a delisting is effective. RETIRED is a register state, like HELD (§2 verdict mapping); it is never a verdict and never a Gate-0 outcome, because DNAF is Gate 0 only.
- **How.** An attended session retires the key with `gsc_register.py retire --key KEY --date YYYY-MM-DD --source "…"`, on the standard §7.4a confirm, citing the primary notice of the close or delisting. The date is the last trading day. The guard hook refuses `retire` in routines.
- **The row.** The Verdict cell reads `RETIRED`, and the grades and reasons stay as the row held them when retired. Next review reads `retired YYYY-MM-DD`. Px, its date and the multiples stay as they stood when the key was retired, and are never refreshed. The memo stays in `memos/<KEY>/` as the record. A RETIRED row is never priced, reviewed or re-verdicted. `apply` refuses a RETIRED key. `retire --undo` takes the verdict, section and Next review from the memo the key was retired on (memo wins) and keeps every other field; where another memo is current by then, or the kept fields cannot carry the memo's verdict (a register hold), it rebuilds the row from the memo as `apply --memo` does. A held name is retired only after its position event (§7.3). The RETIRED section, and its count in the front matter, print only while a RETIRED row exists.
- **Identity.** The row's company name and ISIN keep QUEUE-SEED and ANALYSE from taking the name again (§8.1 step 1).
- **Re-entry.** A re-listing of the company or its successor is an attended ANALYSE, after `retire --undo --key KEY` (The row, above).
- **Routines** that find a closed take-private or an effective delisting (QUEUE-SEED's discovery or decay pass, REVIEW's due-check) raise it as an open item, a delisting awaiting retirement, and never retire the key themselves.

**Operative trigger [R10].** The memo's strict all-pass line: the lower of the FCF leg and the EV/OI no-cash leg of the binding band (§2 Gate 2), which the band prints first. Where the headline prints as a labelled range or slash pair (`X–Y`, `X/Y` no-cash/all-cash), the lower, no-cash bound governs [R10; reverses the upper-bound reading of IDEAS C4(b)]. A split dual-line band (two share classes) takes the leg of the line the key prices. FI names use the P25 band; names with holding credits use the credited legs (§2 Gate 2). The all-cash bound, allowances, alternates and P12 edges never govern. For a held name the trigger equals the ADD band (P21). The memo's `trigger:` carries the result in `px_unit` and `trigger_basis: no-cash` records it; the register never re-derives it. A `register.csv` row still carrying a pre-R10 trigger (the all-cash bound capped by the FCF leg, IDEAS C6) reads `trigger_basis: all-cash` until its memo is re-read by the one-time attended `gsc_register.py retrigger` (the guard hook refuses it in routines) or replaced at the next ANALYSE or REVIEW.

**Open items leave the register [v4.5.0].** The two admissible classes [v4.2.0] — pending user decisions and register-integrity holds (pending re-keys, delistings awaiting retirement, §7.1 RETIRED) — one line each, hard cap **7 entries**, live in the `queue/TODO.md` head block `▶ Open items (register)`. A held SELL-FLAG line stays in the block with Jakub's logged decision (`decision HOLD [user, YYYY-MM-DD]` or a dated deferral) until the flag clears (§8.7 item 11) [v4.6.1]. Everything else routes at the commit that surfaces it: work (re-tests, rebuilds, backfills, corporate actions) → `queue/TODO.md`; framework ideas, calibration questions and standing-method proposals → `queue/IDEAS.md`; a per-name re-test lives in its memo's `next_review` and appendix. **The register carries no change log:** each version's one-line entry lives in its commit and PR body (and INTAKE's run note), and `archive/registers/{YYYY}/` holds every full state (§7.5).

When memory and the register disagree, **trust the register**; when the register and the current memo (the one file in `memos/<KEY>/`) disagree, trust the memo — except on the two register states the memo does not carry, HELD (§7.2) and RETIRED (below) [v4.6.0]. Bands computed under pre-v3 parameters are stale by construction — recompute at the next REVIEW touch (v3 movers: P17/P19 re-tests of SF verdicts; P20 bands; P9 sell lines for held names).

Every memo sets a `next_review` trigger — the earliest of: next annual results, a named event, or price moving into/near the trigger band. Strategic intent: AVOID rows save future time; WATCH rows with trigger bands are the downturn shopping list — EV/OI ordering puts the cheapest WATCH names first; HELD rows carry the standing sell discipline below.

**Memo filenames track the key.** A re-key renames every memo file with it: `memos/<old-key>/` becomes `memos/<new-key>/` and `archive/memos/<old-key>/` becomes `archive/memos/<new-key>/`, each file renamed `<new-key>_…`. The current memo's `ticker:` front matter and body H1 are rewritten to match, so filename, front matter and register row never disagree; archived files are renamed but their bytes stay unchanged — the one sanctioned path change inside `archive/` besides a revert (§7.5) [archive v4.5.0]. The rename is executed inside the RECONCILE that performs the re-key and is logged in its PR body with the file count; §7.4a's deterministic `memos/<KEY>/` load is the reason folder, filename, current front matter and register row must agree.

**§7.1b Core numbers and recomputation [v4.5.0].** Every memo carries the inputs that reproduce its two primaries (the §6.1 `core_` fields). `register.csv` carries them for every key — from the memo when the memo has them, from the one-time v4.5.0 rebuild otherwise (`core_source`), and `-` when neither established them: never invented, never carried from an older memo version. The rebuild is `gsc_register.py rebuild` — attended only (the guard hook refuses it in routines): it rebuilds `register.csv` and the lean `_REGISTER.md` from the outgoing register and the memo each row cites, and takes core numbers, reasons and overrides only from an attended assisted file in which every value carries a verbatim memo quote, accepting core numbers only where they reproduce the memo's own printed multiples within 5% at the memo price (`core_source: rebuild-assisted`). Its report — rules, every normalisation and owed field, the assisted file verbatim — sits beside the outgoing snapshot in `archive/registers/{YYYY}/`; `--assisted` reads that report back for a deterministic re-run.

At price X in `px_unit`, for a monetary core value v: **ps(v) = v × scale(core_unit) × core_fx × minor(px_unit) × core_ratio ÷ (core_shares_diluted × 10⁶)**, where scale is k 10³, m 10⁶ and bn 10⁹; minor is 100 for GBp, ZAc and ILA, 1,000 for KWF and 1 otherwise; and `core_fx` is the major-unit rate, px currency per 1 `core_currency` (1 when they are the same currency). Then **MCap/FCF = X ÷ ps(core_fcf)**, **EV/OI no-cash = (X + ps(core_debt_eq)) ÷ ps(core_oi)** and **EV/OI all-cash = (X + ps(core_debt_eq) − ps(core_cash)) ÷ ps(core_oi)**, each to one decimal, half up. FCF ≤ 0 makes MCap/FCF `n.m.`; OI ≤ 0, or an FI name (`core_oi: n.m.`), makes EV/OI `n.m.`; any `-` input makes its outputs `-`. **Averaged readings [R10]:** the same formulas, with the same credit, with `core_oi_3y` and `core_fcf_3y` (then `core_oi_5y` and `core_fcf_5y`) in place of `core_oi` and `core_fcf`, give `mcap_fcf_3y`, `ev_oi_3y`, `mcap_fcf_5y` and `ev_oi_5y` (EV/OI no-cash), recomputed wherever the primaries are. When a SWEEP supplies a fresher rate for the pair (its `## FX`), INTAKE uses it and writes it to `core_fx` and `core_fx_date`. `routines/tools/gsc_register.py` is the only implementation: `metrics` for SWEEP, `apply` for INTAKE and attended register writes, `lint-memo` for the §6.1 self-consistency check.

**Credits [v4.5.0; V14].** credit = `core_credit`, a constant: holdings are credited at their own buy-line value, which no market price moves (§2 Gate 2, holding credits).
- MCap/FCF = (X − ps(core_credit)) ÷ ps(core_fcf). It is 0.0 when the numerator is ≤ 0 and core_fcf > 0.
- EV/OI no-cash = (X + ps(core_debt_eq) − ps(core_credit)) ÷ ps(core_oi). The all-cash version also subtracts ps(core_cash).
- With `core_credit` at 0 these are the plain formulas above. Under FI the MCap/FCF column reads MCap/NI. At the printed trigger the binding multiple equals 8.0 (MCap/FCF) or 7.0 (EV/OI no-cash), as the memo legs are computed with the same credit.

### §7.2 Portfolio layer — HELD names

The register carries a **PORTFOLIO table above the master table** (empty until the first purchase):

```
| Ticker | Zone | Px | ADD band | Sell line | Last zone check | Next review |
```

Compact numbers [v4.5.0]: **Zone** is the zone word, with the sell-reason code on a flag (`SELL-FLAG SR2`: SR1 = P9 price, SR2 = a quality re-verdict, SR3 = a buyout); **ADD band** (P21) and **Sell line** (P9) are numbers in `px_unit`, or `n.m.`; **Px** is the row's master-table Px; **Last zone check** is the close date (`YYYY-MM-DD`) of the price the zone was last checked at. Basis tags and arithmetic live in the memo's Appendix F. Entry dates, average cost and position size stay out of the table: `avg_cost` (number and currency) lives in `register.csv` and the held memo's front matter. The zone changes only through a REVIEW; a SWEEP moves Px, and moves Last zone check only when its portfolio-table zone equals the Portfolio `Zone` — a changed held zone leaves the date, raises a review request and is listed under Decide in the weekly report (J11; §7.4, §8.7 item 11) [v4.6.1]. The held flag is part of the position record (§7.3): INTAKE never sets or clears it, and a memo whose `position` disagrees with it is an open item for Jakub.

HELD names also keep their master-table row; HELD is a state, never a verdict. Three zones, computed at every REVIEW and SWEEP touch:

- **ADD** — price at or below the trigger, the trigger band's **no-cash bound**, which is the ADD band (the strict all-pass line, both primaries passing on both EV variants [R10]; the P12 outer edge widens CHEAP for a first purchase only and never the ADD zone, so a held name CHEAP only through P12 is HOLD — P21) **and** the latest REVIEW confirms all gates (a stale memo cannot support an ADD statement) **and** the bias tick-through is clean, with the consistency hazard named. The book's buy-side rebalancing logic applies: a holding whose weight fell without sales means a cheaper price — but adding is a buy and passes the full discipline; incumbency is not a discount.
- **HOLD** — the wide middle between the band and the sell-flag line. The default state, on purpose: the twin-portfolio tax arithmetic (churn stunts compounding), the scarcity of alternatives, and the option value of cash all argue for doing nothing. Action is not progress.
- **SELL-FLAG** — any of: (1) P9 trips on the governing OI (the latest-FY J1 line, or the P28 average) — even the lower-EV EV/OI exceeds 25 (FI names and names with holding credits use their own lines); evidenced, quantified mitigating facts that bring the reading within the line hold the flag as `SR1 mitigated` (HOLD, logged in Appendix F; in doubt it fires) [V11]; (2) sell-reason 2 — a REVIEW re-verdict turns Past or Future WEAK or SF FAIL (the analysis was wrong or the context changed; fight the consistency bias, and say so plainly); (3) a buyout is announced (effectively involuntary; note the tax consequence). The framework flags; the user decides. "A clearly better opportunity" (sell-reason 4) is never framework output — it is user judgment held in extreme suspicion (hope bias; Marshall reports never having used it). Rebalancing, memorialising success, and agent compensation are not reasons and must never appear as ones.

Doctrine context, never memo advice: sizing is 10%-of-portfolio-or-nothing with a preference not to exceed a tenth; concentration runs to a dozen names at most, usually far fewer; several years of living expenses stay sequestered outside the portfolio in home-currency insured cash — the mechanism that makes concentration and full drawdowns survivable; dividends and buyouts generate cash passively, and yield-on-cost compounds invisibly.

### §7.3 Position events

When the user reports a transaction ("bought X @ Y", "added", "sold"): record it with `routines/tools/gsc_register.py position` (the held flag and `avg_cost` in `register.csv`; recompute avg cost on adds) and re-render, set `position`/`zone` fields in the next memo touch, and print the replacement row(s) [tool v4.5.0]. The held flag and `avg_cost` in `register.csv` are the record; mirroring the holding into claude.ai memory is optional and done from the project [v4.4.0]. A sale clears the held flag, which removes the PORTFOLIO row (log it in the commit and PR body, with the realised outcome noted as user input); the master row and verdict remain.

One commit carries both, so the pair lands atomically and no ordering rule is needed [git form v4.4.0]. Snapshots are append-only history, the register's counterpart to `model/archive/`; git history is the secondary net.

Each version's one-line entry — `vN — date — KEY(s) action: verdict (deciding gate); note`, ≤ 200 characters — lives in its commit body and PR body (and INTAKE's run note); every full register state is one snapshot pair in `archive/registers/{YYYY}/`, and git history is the secondary net. A memo that supersedes it (a REVIEW, or a same-day rerun) moves the outgoing file unchanged, under its own filename, to `archive/memos/<KEY>/` in the commit that writes its successor (§7.4a step 3); a same-day supersession is renamed `…_GSC_superseded-v{n}.md` on the way. ## §9. STYLE

*The resident guardrails — no price targets, no invented numbers, plain Markdown content, no credential handling, commit gates (PR merge — gsc-automerge for merge classes, Jakub otherwise; "commit model") — live in `CLAUDE.md` (and, for read-only project sessions, the project-settings bootstrap) and win on any conflict [v4.4.0]. Plain Markdown applies to content — memos, the register, queue and run files — with one exception, `register.csv`, the register's machine twin (§5, §7.1) [v4.5.0]; the guard and automation configuration under `.claude/` and `.github/` (hook settings, guard scripts, the gsc-guard, gsc-automerge and gsc-notify workflows, their scripts and tests) and the Python tools under `routines/tools/` (the register tool, the register page generator and the weekly report tool) are configuration and framework-locked; the register page and the weekly report are generated, never committed (§7.4). This section carries only what is model-layer.*

- Analytical and direct; no padding, no hedging beyond genuine data uncertainty. Verdicts stated plainly and never softened. Avoid making excuses for unremarkable companies — or for companies already owned.
- Quantitative material in tables; qualitative reasoning in tight prose. The memo body must be readable in under three minutes.
- Flag every estimate, derivation, and data gap explicitly, at the point of use.
- **The memo explains the company, not the framework [v3.9.0].** Body prose carries data, verdicts and reasoning only — never a restatement of framework mechanics, thresholds, strike logic or rule text. The reader has the model. Tags (P19, §2·1C, "worst-of-four") are references, not explanations; rule text in the body is wasted words against the P13 cap.
- Same data ⇒ same verdict. Where judgment is unavoidable, apply §3 and record the call in Appendix C.
